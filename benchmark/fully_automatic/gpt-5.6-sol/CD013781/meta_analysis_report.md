# Perioperative Levosimendan Versus Placebo for All-Cause Mortality in Adults Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Report

## Abstract

**Background:** Perioperative levosimendan has been proposed to improve outcomes after cardiac surgery. This report evaluates its effect versus placebo on all-cause mortality.

**Methods:** The prespecified outcome was all-cause mortality, analyzed using trial-level deaths and randomized denominators. The intended effect measure was the risk ratio (RR), with RR < 1 favoring levosimendan. Double-zero-event trials were to be retained qualitatively but excluded from RR pooling without continuity correction.

**Results:** The supplied dataset indicated 4 studies but contained only 1 effect-size record. That record, labeled `study_3_R3`, reported Hedges’ g = -0.643402 (95% CI -1.212 to -0.075; n = 50). No arm-level death counts, randomized denominators, individual study risk ratios, or meta-analytic model output were supplied. Consequently, the requested pooled risk ratio, its 95% confidence interval, and heterogeneity statistics could not be calculated or verified. The reported standardized effect cannot be treated as a mortality risk ratio, and its clinical direction cannot be established from the available metadata.

**Conclusions:** The provided results are insufficient to determine whether perioperative levosimendan reduces all-cause mortality compared with placebo in adults undergoing cardiac surgery. A valid synthesis requires deaths and randomized totals in both arms for every trial, together with mortality timepoints and procedures for handling multiple reports.

## Effect Sizes

The prespecified mortality effect measure was the **risk ratio**, calculated as:

\[
RR = \frac{d_L/n_L}{d_P/n_P},
\]

where \(d_L\) and \(n_L\) denote deaths and randomized participants in the levosimendan arm, and \(d_P\) and \(n_P\) denote the corresponding placebo-arm values. An RR below 1 would favor levosimendan.

The intended analysis used randomized denominators on an intention-to-treat basis where available. Trials with no deaths in either arm were to remain in the qualitative review but be excluded from RR pooling without a continuity correction.

However, the supplied results contained no arm-specific numbers of deaths or randomized participants. The only effect-size record was:

| Record | Reported statistic | 95% CI | Total n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `study_3_R3` | Hedges’ g = **-0.643402** | **-1.212 to -0.075** | **50** |

This standardized effect is not the prespecified dichotomous mortality effect measure. Hedges’ g ordinarily summarizes a standardized difference in a continuous outcome and cannot be converted to a trial mortality RR without additional information and assumptions. The underlying outcome and sign convention were not documented. Although the protocol states that a positive value on a deliberately harmonized signed standardized scale would indicate benefit from levosimendan, the available record does not establish that this transformation was applied. Its direction should therefore not be interpreted as favoring either arm.

## Discussion

The supplied dataset does not permit an estimate of the effect of perioperative levosimendan versus placebo on all-cause mortality after cardiac surgery. Although four studies were reported as included, the numerical information comprised only one standardized effect, Hedges’ g = -0.643402 (95% CI -1.212 to -0.075; n = 50). That statistic is not a mortality RR and lacks sufficient metadata to establish the underlying outcome or effect direction.

This distinction is clinically and statistically important. All-cause mortality is a dichotomous endpoint, and its analysis requires the number of deaths and participants at risk in each randomized arm. Replacing an RR with a standardized mean difference changes the estimand and may produce a misleading impression of precision or benefit. The reported confidence interval excludes zero on its own standardized scale, but this does not establish a statistically significant or clinically beneficial effect on mortality.

A valid updated analysis should obtain, for each independent trial, the randomized allocation and death count in both arms at a clearly defined follow-up timepoint. Multiple reports from the same trial should be linked to avoid double-counting. Distinct mortality timepoints should either be prespecified or analyzed separately. Trials with zero deaths in both groups should be described but excluded from conventional RR estimation without continuity correction, consistent with the requested method. Trials with a zero event count in only one arm require a prespecified computational approach, and sensitivity analyses should assess the effect of sparse-event methods and model choice.

Risk of bias should also be evaluated using a domain-based tool appropriate for randomized trials, covering randomization, deviations from assigned intervention, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, and selective reporting. Certainty of evidence should then be judged with attention to risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias.

7. **No reproducible search record:** Search sources, dates, terms, and screening decisions were not supplied.
8. **No quantitative synthesis:** A pooled effect, heterogeneity statistics, sensitivity analyses, and publication-bias analyses could not be calculated.

Because of these limitations, the available standardized effect should not be used to guide clinical practice or to support a conclusion about survival benefit or harm.

## Conclusion

Based on the provided results, there is **insufficient evidence to quantify or determine** the effect of perioperative levosimendan versus placebo on all-cause mortality in adults undergoing cardiac surgery. The requested pooled risk ratio cannot be calculated from one unclassified standardized effect. Completion of the meta-analysis requires trial-level deaths and randomized totals for both arms, explicit mortality timepoints, trial-report linkage, and risk-of-bias information.
