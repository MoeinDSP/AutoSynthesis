# Medium-dose inhaled corticosteroid plus long-acting β2-agonist versus the same dose of inhaled corticosteroid alone for preventing asthma exacerbations: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** For adolescents and adults whose asthma remains uncontrolled on medium-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), adding a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) in the same inhaler may reduce exacerbations. This review evaluated the participant-level risk of experiencing at least one moderate or severe exacerbation during the full treatment period.

**Methods:** Randomised comparisons of medium-dose ICS/LABA in one inhaler versus the same medium-dose ICS alone were eligible. The intended outcome was the number of participants with at least one exacerbation requiring systemic corticosteroids, an emergency visit, or hospitalisation; severe exacerbations alone were acceptable when a combined outcome was unavailable. The supplied pipeline identified 8 studies but extracted only 3 analysable effect sizes. Because the source estimates were separated into odds-ratio-derived and hazard-ratio-derived groups, they were not combined across groups. A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was used for the two-study group, with the prespecified Risk Ratio (RR) as the labelled effect measure.

**Results:** In the two-study group (k = 2; n = 464), the pooled Risk Ratio was 0.7558973677864381 (95% CI 0.366 to 1.559; p = 0.44882883728884165). The supplied favourability classification was “neither (tie or undetermined).” Heterogeneity was estimated as I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.0, and Q = 0.015 (p = 0.9034). A separate hazard-ratio-derived group contained one study (n = 2019) and therefore had insufficient data for pooling. Egger’s intercept was −0.1894 (p = 0.6339), and trim-and-fill imputed no studies (k₀ = 0); however, publication-bias tests based on so few studies have very low power.

**Conclusions:** The available pooled evidence did not demonstrate that adding LABA to the same medium dose of ICS reduces the risk of at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation. The confidence interval was wide and compatible with both benefit and harm. Interpretation is substantially limited by sparse analysable evidence, separation of source effect measures, missing screening and risk-of-bias details, and the inability to pool the largest study with the other two trials.

## Search/Screening

### Eligibility framework

The review question was defined as follows:

- **Population:** Adolescents and adults with asthma remaining uncontrolled on medium-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS).
- **Intervention:** Medium-dose ICS plus a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA), delivered in one inhaler.
- **Comparator:** The same medium-dose ICS alone at the same dose.
- **Study design:** Randomised controlled trials.
- **Outcome:** A dichotomous participant-level outcome—the number of randomised or analysed participants experiencing at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation during the full treatment period, divided by the corresponding number in each eligible arm. Moderate or severe exacerbations were defined as events requiring systemic corticosteroids, an emergency visit, or hospitalisation. The definition closest to combined moderate-or-severe exacerbations was preferred; severe exacerbations alone were acceptable if the combined outcome was unavailable. Participant counts and intention-to-treat denominators were preferred over exacerbation rates.

### Search and study selection

The supplied results do not provide database names, search dates, search strings, language restrictions, registry searches, deduplication counts, or numbers of records at each screening stage. Consequently, a reproducible search strategy and numerical PRISMA flow diagram cannot be reconstructed from the available material.

The analysis pipeline recorded **8 studies** and **3 extracted effect sizes**. Only these three effect sizes entered the quantitative evidence set. Two effects, comprising 464 participants, were assigned to an odds-ratio-derived outcome group and pooled using the prespecified Risk Ratio (RR) label. The third effect, comprising 2019 participants, was assigned to a hazard-ratio-derived group and was not pooled because that group contained only one study. The supplied results do not state why the remaining five recorded studies did not yield eligible effect sizes; they therefore cannot be described as excluded for any specific reason.

## Effect Sizes

The prespecified effect measure was the **Risk Ratio (RR)**. For this outcome, RR values below 1 would ordinarily indicate fewer participants with an exacerbation in the ICS/LABA arm, whereas values above 1 would ordinarily indicate fewer in the ICS-alone arm. Nevertheless, the supplied analysis explicitly classified the pooled result as **“neither (tie or undetermined)”**; this supplied classification is used throughout rather than independently inferring favourability from the numerical estimate.

A material effect-measure conflict was recorded. The two studies in the pooled group reportedly supplied odds ratios, while the one-study group reportedly supplied a hazard ratio. The analysis retained the explicitly requested RR as authoritative and marked native pooling as used. However, arm-level event counts and conversion calculations were not provided, so the resulting RR estimates cannot be independently verified against the trial reports. Odds ratios, hazard ratios, and risk ratios are not generally interchangeable, especially when event risks are not small or follow-up differs.

The individual pipeline audit values shown above are therefore presented for traceability only. They are not relabelled as RRs and are not used to infer clinical direction.

### Odds-ratio-derived group

For **participants with at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation**, the random-effects REML synthesis included **k = 2 studies** and **n = 464 participants**. The pooled **Risk Ratio (RR)** was:

- **RR = 0.7558973677864381**
- **95% CI 0.366 to 1.559**
- **p = 0.44882883728884165**
- **Supplied direction:** neither (tie or undetermined)

The confidence interval crossed the null value of 1 and was wide. The result therefore did not provide statistically significant evidence that medium-dose ICS/LABA reduces the participant-level risk of at least one moderate or severe exacerbation compared with the same dose of ICS alone. The interval remained compatible with a potentially meaningful reduction as well as a potentially meaningful increase in risk.

## Heterogeneity

For the two-study pooled group, statistical heterogeneity was reported as:

- **Q-test p = 0.9034**

With only two studies, the Q test has very low power, while estimates of I² and τ² are imprecise and may be zero even when genuine between-study differences exist. ## Discussion

### Principal findings

The pooled analysis of two trials and 464 participants did not show a statistically significant reduction in the risk of at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation with medium-dose ICS/LABA compared with the same dose of ICS alone. The pooled Risk Ratio was 0.7558973677864381, but its 95% confidence interval was broad (0.366 to 1.559), and the p value was 0.44882883728884165. In accordance with the supplied analysis, the result was classified as favouring neither arm.

A further study with 2019 participants was retained as a separate hazard-ratio-derived group. Because it was the only study in that group, no pooled result was calculated. This separation is methodologically important: a hazard ratio incorporates event timing and assumes a time-to-event framework, while an odds ratio and a risk ratio compare different functions of event probability. Pooling these measures without justified transformation could produce a misleading summary.

### Clinical interpretation

The evidence does not establish that adding LABA to the same medium-dose ICS reduces the proportion of patients experiencing at least one moderate or severe exacerbation over the full treatment period. Equally, the pooled confidence interval does not exclude a clinically important benefit. It also does not exclude increased risk. Therefore, the appropriate interpretation is uncertainty rather than equivalence or proof of no effect.

The analysis addressed whether at least one patient-level event occurred, not the number or rate of recurrent exacerbations. It consequently estimates the probability of becoming an “exacerbator” during follow-up but does not capture whether treatment reduces multiple events among patients prone to recurrence. Variability in follow-up duration would also affect cumulative participant risk, but treatment durations were unavailable.

### Strength and applicability of the evidence

The randomised design specified by the eligibility criteria is a strength in principle, as is the clinically relevant requirement that the control group receive the same medium ICS dose. The participant-level outcome is readily interpretable and avoids treating repeated events within one patient as independent.

However, applicability cannot be judged adequately because participant demographics, asthma phenotype, baseline exacerbation risk, treatment duration, specific drug combinations, adherence, and healthcare setting were not reported in the supplied dataset. In addition, only a small fraction of the recorded studies contributed to the primary pooled estimate.

- The pooled group contained **2 studies (n = 464)**, while a separate **1-study group (n = 2019)** was insufficient for pooling.
- The requested and labelled effect measure was **Risk Ratio (RR)**, but source reports were recorded as supplying odds ratios in the pooled group and a hazard ratio in the separate group. ## Limitations

This report has several major limitations:

1. **Incomplete search documentation:** Databases, dates, search terms, restrictions, registry searches, and screening counts were unavailable, precluding full assessment of search completeness and formal reconstruction of a PRISMA flow diagram.
2. **Sparse pooled evidence:** Only two studies and 464 participants contributed to the pooled RR. This produced a wide confidence interval and severely limited estimation of heterogeneity.
3. **Incomplete use of the quantitative population:** The separate 2019-participant study could not be pooled, so the main estimate excluded most participants represented by the extracted effects.
4. **Effect-measure inconsistency:** The source measures were recorded as odds ratios and a hazard ratio, whereas the requested synthesis was labelled as an RR. Without event counts and conversion details, the correctness of the native-scale synthesis cannot be independently confirmed.
5. **Unreconciled study-level values:** The audit values supplied for individual studies do not map transparently to the pooled RR and cannot be interpreted on a ratio scale.
6. **No risk-of-bias assessment:** Information needed to judge randomisation, allocation concealment, masking, missing data, selective reporting, and other biases was absent.
7. **Unknown outcome consistency:** Trial-specific definitions of moderate and severe exacerbations and the use of intention-to-treat denominators were not available.
8. **No clinical-characteristic data:** Differences in interventions, populations, follow-up, adherence, and baseline risk could not be assessed.
9. **No meaningful moderator assessment:** The evidence base was too small and lacked study-level covariates.
10. **Publication-bias tests were underpowered:** Egger’s test and trim-and-fill cannot reliably exclude selective publication in such a small evidence set.
11. **No certainty-of-evidence rating:** A formal GRADE assessment could not be completed because study-level risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, and reporting information were insufficient.

## Conclusion

Among the two studies that could be pooled, medium-dose ICS/LABA in one inhaler did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the risk of experiencing at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation compared with the same dose of ICS alone (**pooled RR 0.7558973677864381, 95% CI 0.366 to 1.559; p = 0.44882883728884165; k = 2; n = 464**). No statistical heterogeneity was detected (**I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0; Q = 0.015, p = 0.9034**), but the analysis was too small for heterogeneity estimates to be reassuring. One additional study with 2019 participants formed a separate hazard-ratio-derived group and had insufficient data for pooling.

Overall, the available results are inconclusive. They neither establish a reduction in exacerbation risk nor exclude clinically important benefit or harm. Firm conclusions require transparent arm-level participant counts, harmonised exacerbation definitions, complete reporting of all eligible trials, and a sufficiently large synthesis using a consistently defined effect measure.
