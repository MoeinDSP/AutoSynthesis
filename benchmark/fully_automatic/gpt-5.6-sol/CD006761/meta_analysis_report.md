# LigaSure Versus Conventional Excisional Haemorrhoidectomy for Operating Time: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis

## Abstract

### Background
LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy may shorten operating time compared with conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy.

### Objective
To estimate the effect of LigaSure versus conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy on haemorrhoidectomy operating time in adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids.

### Methods
Eligible comparisons evaluated LigaSure against scissors, diathermy, Milligan–Morgan, Ferguson, or another conventional excisional technique. The prespecified outcome was operating time in minutes, and the effect measure was the mean difference (MD), calculated as LigaSure minus conventional; therefore, MD < 0 favours LigaSure. A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was used. The supplied pipeline contained 21 studies, of which 8 comparisons (594 participants) contributed to the meta-analysis.

### Results
The pooled Mean Difference (MD) was −13.460133678706196 minutes (95% CI −18.946 to −7.974; p = 1.5172270428954423 × 10⁻⁶), favouring LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy. Heterogeneity was considerable (Q = 229.111, I² = 98.28%, τ² = 60.147156; reported Q-test p = 0.0). Egger’s intercept was 0.116 (p = 0.0083), indicating funnel-plot asymmetry. Trim-and-fill estimated one potentially missing study, but its adjusted value (−2.2059) was supplied on an unspecified or standardized scale and cannot be interpreted as an adjusted MD in minutes.

### Conclusions
LigaSure was associated with a statistically significant reduction in operating time of approximately 13.46 minutes compared with conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy. However, extreme between-study heterogeneity and evidence of small-study effects or publication bias substantially limit confidence in the magnitude and transportability of the pooled estimate.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was extreme:

- **Cochran’s Q = 229.111**
- **Q-test p = 0.0**, as reported by the pipeline
- **I² = 98.28%**
- **τ² = 60.147156 minutes²**

An I² of 98.28% indicates that nearly all observed dispersion beyond sampling error was attributed to between-study heterogeneity under the model. The estimated between-study standard deviation is √60.147156 = **7.755459 minutes**. This dispersion is large relative to the pooled reduction in operating time and indicates that the average effect should not be assumed to apply uniformly across settings, surgeons, or conventional techniques.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced:

- **Intercept = 0.116**
- **p = 0.0083**

This statistically significant result indicates funnel-plot asymmetry consistent with possible small-study effects or publication bias. However, asymmetry is not specific to publication bias and may also arise from clinical heterogeneity, methodological differences, selective outcome reporting, or chance. Interpretation is particularly uncertain with only 8 comparisons and extreme heterogeneity.

Trim-and-fill estimated **1 potentially missing study** and reported an adjusted value of **−2.2059**. This adjusted value is not labelled as a native-scale MD in minutes and is numerically aligned with the supplied standardized effect-size outputs rather than the pooled MD. It must therefore **not** be interpreted as an adjusted operating-time difference in minutes or directly compared with the pooled MD of −13.460133678706196 minutes.

The pipeline classified the overall risk of publication bias as **high**. No funnel-plot path was supplied.

## Discussion

### Principal findings

This random-effects meta-analysis found that LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy was associated with a statistically significant reduction in operating time compared with conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) was −13.460133678706196 minutes (95% CI −18.946 to −7.974), favouring LigaSure.

The effect is potentially important in operative practice because shorter procedures may improve theatre efficiency. Nevertheless, operating time is a process outcome rather than a direct measure of patient benefit, and this review did not evaluate pain, blood loss, wound healing, complications, length of stay, recurrence, or return to work. No conclusions about those outcomes should be inferred from the present analysis.

### Interpretation of heterogeneity

The pooled estimate represents an average across highly heterogeneous comparisons. With I² = 98.28% and τ² = 60.147156 minutes², differences among studies were much larger than would be expected from sampling error alone. Such heterogeneity may reflect variation in the conventional technique, surgeon familiarity, procedural extent, case complexity, operating-time definition, or data transformation methods. Therefore, the estimated 13.46-minute average reduction should not be treated as a universal effect.

### Certainty and robustness

The result was statistically strong, but statistical significance does not resolve concerns about inconsistency or reporting bias. Egger’s test was significant (p = 0.0083), and the pipeline rated publication-bias risk as high. Moreover, the absence of arm-level extraction data prevents independent verification of procedure assignment, units, conversions, summary-statistic transformations, and study-level MD calculations. These issues lower confidence in the exact magnitude of benefit.

## Data Quality and Validation Notes

Several validation issues are apparent from the supplied results:

1. The pipeline listed 21 studies but only 8 effect sizes; reasons why 13 studies did not contribute were not provided.
2. Study-level native MDs in minutes and arm-level means, SDs, and sample sizes were absent. Consequently, the pooled native-scale analysis cannot be independently reconstructed from the context.
3. The supplied study-level estimates were standardized, whereas the confirmed pooled measure was a native-scale Mean Difference (MD) in minutes. They are not on the same numerical scale.
4. The trim-and-fill adjusted value of −2.2059 was not confirmed as a native-scale MD and must not be reported as minutes.
5. Search methods, study citations, risk-of-bias assessments, and transformation records were unavailable.
6. The reported Q-test p value was 0.0, which should be understood as the pipeline’s numerical output rather than proof that the mathematical probability is exactly zero.

## Limitations

This report is limited by the incompleteness of the supplied review record. First, the search strategy and screening flow were unavailable, preventing assessment of search completeness and study-selection reproducibility. Second, study identities and baseline characteristics were not supplied. Third, arm-level operating-time data were unavailable, so it was impossible to verify assignment by procedure, exclusion of anaesthesia or theatre time, unit conversions, SE-to-SD conversions, or any median-to-mean and range/IQR-to-SD estimations. Fourth, study-level risk of bias was not reported. Fifth, the extreme heterogeneity substantially limits generalisability of the mean effect. Sixth, Egger’s test has limited reliability with only 8 studies and in the presence of substantial heterogeneity, although its significant result remains a reason for caution. Finally, the trim-and-fill result was not available on a confirmed minute scale and could not provide a valid bias-adjusted MD for this outcome.

## Conclusion

Across 8 comparisons involving 594 participants, LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy reduced operating time relative to conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy. The REML random-effects pooled **Mean Difference (MD) was −13.460133678706196 minutes** (95% CI −18.946 to −7.974; p = 1.5172270428954423 × 10⁻⁶), favouring LigaSure.

The magnitude of this reduction should be interpreted cautiously because heterogeneity was extreme (I² = 98.28%; τ² = 60.147156), funnel-plot asymmetry was statistically significant (Egger p = 0.0083), publication-bias risk was rated high, and the study-level native data required for independent verification were not supplied. The evidence supports a shorter average operation with LigaSure, but it does not establish that the same reduction will occur consistently across clinical settings.
