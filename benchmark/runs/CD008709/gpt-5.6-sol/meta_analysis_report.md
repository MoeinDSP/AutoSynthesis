# Single-Incision Versus Transobturator Mid-Urethral Slings for Patient-Reported Cure or Improvement at 12 Months: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

## Background
Single-incision mid-urethral slings (SIS) were developed as less invasive alternatives to transobturator mid-urethral slings (TOT/TVT-O) for women undergoing surgery for stress urinary incontinence or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence. This review evaluated patient-reported subjective cure or improvement at approximately 12 months.

## Methods
Eligible comparisons evaluated a commercial SIS other than TVT-Secur (including Ajust, Needleless/Contasure, MiniArc, Ophira, Altis, or Solyx) against an inside-out or outside-in TOT/TVT-O. The outcome was the number of women reporting subjective cure or improvement divided by the number analysed in each arm. Study-defined patient-reported criteria were accepted. Twelve-month results were preferred; where unavailable, the closest earlier assessment of at least 6 months was eligible. SIS was coded as treatment and TOT as control. Risk ratios (RRs) were pooled using a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood model.

## Results
The supplied evidence pipeline identified 14 studies, of which nine effect estimates contributed to the quantitative synthesis. The nine comparisons included 1,461 analysed participants. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 0.998731486977945 (95% CI 0.965 to 1.033; p = 0.9418442167759403), favoring neither intervention and indicating essentially identical patient-reported success. Heterogeneity was absent (I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0; Q = 3.071, p = 0.9298; k = 9). Egger’s intercept was 0.1878 (p = 0.4859), although trim-and-fill estimated two potentially missing studies; publication-bias risk was therefore classified as moderate.

## Conclusions
Eligible SIS devices excluding TVT-Secur and transobturator slings produced essentially the same probability of patient-reported cure or improvement at approximately 12 months. The narrow pooled confidence interval and absence of detected heterogeneity support consistency across the analysed comparisons. Nevertheless, incomplete source-level reporting, unavailable risk-of-bias assessments, and limited power to detect small-study effects restrict certainty. The findings should not be interpreted as formal proof of noninferiority because no noninferiority margin was prespecified.

# Introduction

Stress urinary incontinence (SUI) is commonly treated surgically when conservative management is unsuccessful or unsuitable. Transobturator mid-urethral slings (TOT/TVT-O), inserted by either an inside-out or outside-in route, are established procedures. Single-incision mid-urethral slings (SIS) were introduced to reduce the extent of dissection and avoid passage through the obturator foramen. Their clinical value depends in part on whether reduced procedural invasiveness is achieved without compromising outcomes meaningful to patients.

Earlier evaluations of SIS have sometimes combined substantially different devices, including TVT-Secur. Because sling design, fixation, and operative technique vary, pooling all devices without restriction can obscure the performance of currently relevant systems. The present review therefore excluded TVT-Secur and focused on eligible commercial SIS devices such as Ajust, Needleless/Contasure, MiniArc, Ophira, Altis, and Solyx.

The review question was: among women undergoing surgery for SUI or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence, what is the effect of an eligible SIS, compared with TOT/TVT-O, on patient-reported subjective cure or improvement at approximately 12 months?

# Search/Screening

## Eligibility criteria

The prespecified eligibility framework was as follows:

- **Population:** Women undergoing surgery for SUI or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence.
- **Intervention:** An eligible commercial SIS, excluding TVT-Secur. Eligible examples included Ajust, Needleless/Contasure, MiniArc, Ophira, Altis, and Solyx.
- **Comparator:** A transobturator mid-urethral sling (TOT or TVT-O), inserted by either an inside-out or outside-in route.
- **Outcome:** Patient-reported subjective cure or improvement, expressed as the number reporting success divided by the number analysed in each arm. Accepted definitions included no self-reported stress leakage on a questionnaire, ratings of “very much improved” or “much improved” on a global impression scale, or satisfaction-based success.
- **Timing:** Twelve-month data were preferred, including 12-month observations reported within longer-term publications. If 12-month data were unavailable, the closest earlier assessment at or beyond 6 months was eligible.
- **Exclusions:** Objective cure alone was not eligible. TVT-Secur arms were excluded.

SIS was required to be coded as treatment and TOT as control according to the device description rather than table position. The intended handling of multi-arm trials was to retain each eligible SIS comparison, ignore any TVT-Secur arm, and divide events and totals from a shared TOT group equally across eligible comparisons. Multiple reports from the same trial were to be linked, with the trial represented once and preference given to the report containing the 12-month subjective result.

# Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood included nine comparisons and 1,461 participants. The pooled **Risk Ratio (RR)** was **0.998731486977945** with a **95% CI from 0.965 to 1.033** and **p = 0.9418442167759403**.

According to the supplied favorability classification, this result **favored neither intervention** and represented essentially a tie. The point estimate indicates almost exactly the same probability of patient-reported subjective cure or improvement with eligible SIS and TOT. On the relative scale, the reported confidence limits extend from approximately 3.5% lower to 3.3% higher success with SIS, but the corresponding absolute difference cannot be determined without arm-level baseline risks.

The confidence interval includes the null value of 1, and the test of the pooled effect was not statistically significant. Thus, the synthesis provides no evidence that either eligible SIS or TOT yields a higher subjective-success proportion at approximately 12 months.

The cumulative meta-analysis is available at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/cumulative_plot.png`. It should be interpreted as a display of how the summary estimate evolves across the ordering used by the analysis, not as proof of temporal stability unless that ordering is confirmed to be chronological.

These findings do not constitute a formal demonstration of noninferiority or equivalence. Such a conclusion would require a prespecified clinical margin and an analysis designed to test that margin. The result instead shows a very small estimated difference and a relatively narrow confidence interval around the null under the supplied analysis.

# Heterogeneity

No statistical heterogeneity was detected:

- **Cochran’s Q:** 3.071
- **Degrees of freedom:** 8
- **Q-test p value:** 0.9298
- **I²:** 0.0%
- **τ²:** 0.0

The Q statistic was smaller than its degrees of freedom, and the estimated between-study variance was zero. These results indicate that the observed dispersion was compatible with within-study sampling variation. The heterogeneity summary is shown at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/heterogeneity_summary.png`.

An I² of 0.0% should not be interpreted as proof that all trials had identical underlying effects. With only nine comparisons, heterogeneity statistics have limited precision. Clinically relevant variation could remain undetected, particularly across sling designs, transobturator routes, outcome definitions, and participant populations.

# Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **0.1878** with **p = 0.4859**. This was not statistically significant and therefore provided no statistical evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Nevertheless, only nine comparisons were available, so the test had limited power.

Trim-and-fill estimated **two potentially missing studies** and returned a trim-and-fill output of **0.0522** on the pipeline’s standardized scale. Because this value was not supplied as an adjusted native RR, it must not be interpreted as a corrected risk ratio or directly compared numerically with the pooled RR of 0.998731486977945.

Considering the nonsignificant Egger test together with the two imputed studies, the supplied assessment classified publication-bias risk as **moderate**. Publication bias and selective outcome reporting therefore cannot be ruled out.

# Discussion

## Principal findings

Across nine comparisons involving 1,461 participants, eligible SIS devices excluding TVT-Secur and transobturator mid-urethral slings had essentially identical patient-reported subjective cure or improvement at approximately 12 months. The pooled RR was 0.998731486977945 (95% CI 0.965 to 1.033), with no statistically significant treatment difference. The result favored neither intervention according to the supplied classification.

The absence of detected heterogeneity is notable. It suggests that the included comparisons were statistically consistent despite the probable inclusion of different SIS products, transobturator approaches, and patient-reported definitions. However, this interpretation remains provisional because device and outcome details were not available for trial-level examination.

## Clinical interpretation

Patient-reported cure or improvement captures the outcome most directly experienced by women after surgery. It can integrate leakage frequency, bother, perceived improvement, and satisfaction in ways that objective stress tests do not. At the same time, combining study-defined subjective criteria may introduce conceptual variation: complete absence of stress leakage is more stringent than satisfaction or ratings of “much improved.” The lack of observed statistical heterogeneity does not remove this measurement concern.

The pooled estimate indicates no meaningful directional advantage in subjective success for either procedure over the observed period. Treatment selection may therefore depend on other outcomes not analysed here, including perioperative pain, operative time, recovery, mesh exposure, urinary retention, repeat surgery, de novo urgency, groin pain, sexual function, costs, surgeon experience, and patient preferences. No conclusions about these outcomes can be drawn from this analysis.

The result applies specifically to eligible SIS devices other than TVT-Secur. It should not be generalized to TVT-Secur, and it does not establish that all SIS devices are interchangeable. Device-level effects could not be assessed because subgroup data were not supplied.

## Statistical interpretation

Native RR pooling was used, consistent with the binary outcome. The estimate is highly precise around the null in relative terms, but clinical interpretation would be strengthened by absolute risks and risk differences. Without the pooled TOT event rate, the number needed to treat or harm cannot be calculated.

Although τ² was estimated as 0.0 and I² as 0.0%, use of a random-effects framework remained appropriate because clinical diversity across devices and definitions was expected. The evidence does not support superiority of either intervention. It also cannot by itself establish formal equivalence or noninferiority.

# Data Quality & Validation Notes

- The pipeline contained 14 studies but only nine effect estimates. The reasons that five studies did not contribute to the quantitative synthesis were not provided and could not be audited.
- One document (`manual:6f25cf61`) was excluded as a near-duplicate or split fragment of `6c877e8a_jwh.2015.5416` (`manual:77df9e82`) after full-text similarity detection. This exclusion reduced the risk of counting the same trial evidence more than once.
- Trial names, citations, devices, arm-level event counts, and exact follow-up times were unavailable. Correct SIS-versus-TOT coding, shared-control splitting, TVT-Secur exclusion, report linkage, and selection of 12-month data therefore could not be independently verified from the supplied records.
- The primary synthesis used the confirmed native **Risk Ratio (RR)**. Study-level standardized values were also supplied, but they are not interchangeable with individual RRs and were not used to reinterpret the native pooled result.
- The source metadata noted a conflict in which papers reportedly presented odds ratios while the user-specified analysis required RRs. The requested RR was retained as authoritative. Without arm-level events, the consequences of that discrepancy cannot be independently checked.

# Limitations

First, the search and screening record was incomplete. Databases, dates, search strategies, screening counts, and full-text exclusion reasons were not available, preventing full reproducibility and a complete PRISMA flow accounting.

Second, source-level characteristics were absent. It was not possible to determine which SIS products were represented, whether TOT procedures were inside-out or outside-in, how many women had mixed incontinence, or whether baseline prognostic factors were balanced.

Third, arm-level event counts and exact subjective-outcome definitions were unavailable. Consequently, individual study RRs, absolute effects, baseline risk, and numbers needed to treat could not be independently reproduced. Differences in the stringency of subjective-success definitions may also limit clinical comparability.

Fourth, risk-of-bias assessments were not supplied. The pooled estimate may therefore be affected by weaknesses in randomization, allocation concealment, attrition, selective reporting, or outcome measurement that cannot be quantified here.

Fifth, publication-bias assessment was based on only nine comparisons. Egger’s test has low power with such a small evidence base, while trim-and-fill relies on assumptions that may not hold. The classification of moderate publication-bias risk is appropriately cautious.

Sixth, no moderator data were available. Potential differences among SIS devices, comparator routes, populations, outcome definitions, and follow-up times remain unresolved. A pooled class effect should not be assumed to apply identically to every device.

Finally, the analysis addresses only patient-reported success at approximately 12 months. It does not determine longer-term durability or comparative safety, and it cannot establish formal noninferiority in the absence of a prespecified margin.

# Conclusion

Among women undergoing surgery for SUI or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence, eligible single-incision mid-urethral slings excluding TVT-Secur produced essentially the same probability of patient-reported subjective cure or improvement as transobturator mid-urethral slings at approximately 12 months. Across nine comparisons and 1,461 participants, the pooled **Risk Ratio (RR) was 0.998731486977945 (95% CI 0.965 to 1.033; p = 0.9418442167759403)**, favoring neither intervention. Statistical heterogeneity was absent (I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0), but publication-bias risk was considered moderate.

The available evidence does not support superiority of either sling approach for this outcome. Clinical decisions should incorporate adverse events, durability, procedural factors, surgeon expertise, and patient preferences. Better-reported device-specific trials with transparent subjective definitions, complete arm-level data, and longer follow-up are needed.
