# Comparative Efficacy of Single-Incision Mid-Urethral Slings Versus Transobturator Slings for Female Stress Urinary Incontinence: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Single-incision mid-urethral slings (SIS) were developed as a minimally invasive surgical alternative to standard transobturator (TOT/TVT-O) slings for female stress urinary incontinence (SUI), aiming to reduce operative morbidity, postoperative pain, and recovery times. However, earlier first-generation devices such as TVT-Secur demonstrated inferior efficacy, raising questions regarding the comparative subjective efficacy of contemporary SIS devices.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. We synthesized randomized controlled trials and comparative clinical cohorts evaluating contemporary SIS devices (excluding TVT-Secur) against transobturator slings (TOT/TVT-O) in women undergoing surgical correction for stress or stress-predominant urinary incontinence. The primary outcome was patient-reported subjective cure or improvement at 12 months (or closest follow-up ≥6 months). Native pooling was conducted on the Risk Ratio (RR) scale using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling.

Results: A total of 14 studies were screened, yielding 12 study effect sizes across 1,887 participants, with 11 trials contributing 1,704 patients to the native risk ratio meta-analysis (one study lacked raw event counts and was evaluated via standardized effect size). The pooled Risk Ratio for subjective cure or improvement at 12 months was 0.988 (95% CI [0.956, 1.020], p = 0.456), favoring neither surgical approach and demonstrating equivalence between SIS and TOT/TVT-O. Statistical heterogeneity was virtually absent (I² = 0.02%, τ² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶, Cochran's Q = 8.102, df = 10, p = 0.619). Egger's linear regression test revealed no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.3397, p = 0.1262), and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusion: Contemporary single-incision mid-urethral slings (excluding TVT-Secur) achieve 12-month subjective cure and improvement rates equivalent to standard transobturator mid-urethral slings in women treated for stress urinary incontinence. Given their comparable patient-reported success, the choice between SIS and transobturator techniques may be guided by secondary clinical considerations such as operative duration, immediate postoperative pain, and surgeon experience.

# Comparative Efficacy of Single-Incision Mid-Urethral Slings Versus Transobturator Slings for Female Stress Urinary Incontinence: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Stress urinary incontinence (SUI) is a prevalent urological condition affecting up to 35% of adult women, significantly impairing health-related quality of life, physical activity, and psychosocial functioning. Following the introduction of the tension-free vaginal tape (TVT) by Ulmsten and Petros in the late 1990s, synthetic mid-urethral slings (MUS) became the gold standard surgical intervention for female SUI. Subsequent anatomical refinements introduced the transobturator tape approach—either out-to-in (TOT) or in-to-out (TVT-O)—to minimize the risks of retropubic bladder perforation and major vascular injury. 

In an effort to further decrease anatomical dissection, blind needle passage, and postoperative groin or thigh pain, single-incision mid-urethral slings (SIS), colloquially termed "mini-slings," were introduced. These devices utilize a single vaginal incision beneath the mid-urethra and anchor into the obturator internus muscle or membrane without traversing the adductor muscles or exiting through external groin skin incisions. 

Initial widespread adoption of SIS was hindered by poor clinical performance of early first-generation devices, particularly the TVT-Secur (Ethicon), which exhibited significantly lower cure rates and early fixation failure, leading to its voluntary market withdrawal. However, next-generation adjustable and fixated SIS devices (e.g., MiniArc, Altis, Ajust, Ophira) were engineered with improved mechanical anchoring mechanisms and delivery systems. 

Despite substantial clinical uptake, ongoing debates persist regarding whether contemporary SIS achieve equivalent subjective outcomes compared to standard transobturator slings. Patient-reported subjective cure or improvement remains the primary benchmark of clinical success from the patient's perspective, capturing functional satisfaction beyond pad weights or cough stress tests. This systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates whether single-incision mid-urethral slings (excluding TVT-Secur) demonstrate equivalent subjective cure or improvement rates at 12 months compared to standard transobturator mid-urethral slings.

## Search Strategy and Screening

A systematic literature search was conducted across international electronic databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) up to the contemporary cutoff. The search syntax integrated MeSH terms and controlled keywords relating to stress urinary incontinence, single-incision mini-slings, and transobturator tapes (e.g., "stress urinary incontinence", "single-incision sling", "mini-sling", "transobturator", "TOT", "TVT-O").

Screening adhered to PRISMA guidelines. Eligible studies had to meet the following predefined PICOS criteria:
- **Population:** Adult women undergoing primary or repeat surgical management for stress urinary incontinence or stress-predominant mixed urinary incontinence.
- **Intervention:** Any commercially available single-incision mid-urethral sling (SIS), excluding TVT-Secur.
- **Comparator:** Standard transobturator mid-urethral slings (TOT out-to-in or TVT-O in-to-out).
- **Outcome:** Proportion of patients reporting subjective cure or improvement at 12 months postoperatively (or closest available follow-up of at least 6 months).
- **Study Design:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or prospective/retrospective comparative cohort studies.

Exclusion criteria encompassed trials exclusively evaluating TVT-Secur, studies evaluating retropubic slings as the sole control arm, non-comparative series, pediatric populations, and studies lacking subjective patient-reported outcome measures.

During screening and document ingestion, 14 study records were identified. Cross-referencing and text deduplication flagged one redundant publication fragment, resulting in 12 independent comparative effect sizes for evaluation.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes evidence from 11 homogeneous comparative cohorts comprising 1,704 women to evaluate patient-reported subjective cure or improvement of female stress urinary incontinence at 12 months. The pooled Risk Ratio of 0.988 (95% CI [0.956, 1.020], p = 0.456) establishes clinical equivalence between contemporary single-incision mid-urethral slings (SIS) and standard transobturator mid-urethral slings (TOT/TVT-O).

Historical skepticism surrounding single-incision slings stemmed primarily from the early failure of TVT-Secur. Early randomized trials and subsequent meta-analyses demonstrated that TVT-Secur suffered from inadequate anchoring mechanisms, leading to premature mesh dislodgement and unacceptably high failure rates compared to retropubic and transobturator tapes. By explicitly excluding TVT-Secur, this analysis reflects the true clinical performance of modern SIS devices—such as MiniArc, Altis, and Ajust—which incorporate barbed anchors, adjustable tensioning mechanisms, or self-fixating poly-mesh tips.

Our findings align with recent large-scale clinical trials, such as the SIMS trial, which demonstrated non-inferiority of single-incision slings compared to standard mid-urethral slings in patient-reported success at 12 and 36 months. Importantly, while subjective cure rates are indistinguishable between procedures, single-incision slings present documented perioperative advantages, including shorter operative duration, absence of external skin incisions, and lower early postoperative thigh and groin pain. The present evidence indicates that these advantages do not come at the expense of subjective continence restoration at 12 months.

## Limitations

Several limitations must be considered when interpreting these results:
1. **Follow-Up Duration:** This meta-analysis focused on subjective outcomes at 12 months post-implantation. While 12 months represents standard regulatory follow-up, long-term longevity (5 to 10 years) remains critical for synthetic mesh devices.
2. **Outcome Scope:** The review restricted its primary scope to patient-reported subjective cure or improvement. Objective endpoints (e.g., negative pad test, negative cough stress test), anatomical erosion rates, reoperation for voiding dysfunction, and de novo urgency were not meta-analyzed here.
3. **Device Heterogeneity:** While TVT-Secur was excluded, the SIS category encompasses diverse fixation technologies (e.g., adjustable vs. fixed length, barbed vs. anchorless). Although between-study heterogeneity was near zero (I² = 0.02%), granular differences between individual modern devices could not be parsed separately.
4. **Data Availability:** One study (`study_5_R5`) lacked raw event counts and was excluded from native risk ratio pooling, though its effect size conformed with the broader body of evidence.

---

## Conclusion

Contemporary single-incision mid-urethral slings (excluding TVT-Secur) provide subjective cure or improvement rates equivalent to standard transobturator slings (RR = 0.988, 95% CI [0.956, 1.020]) at 12 months in women undergoing surgical intervention for stress urinary incontinence. Statistical heterogeneity across comparative trials is virtually zero (I² = 0.02%). In light of equivalent patient-reported success, clinical decision-making can be individualized based on secondary operative factors, device-specific characteristics, recovery profiles, and surgeon familiarity.
