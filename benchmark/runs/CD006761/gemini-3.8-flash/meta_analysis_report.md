# Operating Time in LigaSure Versus Conventional Diathermy Haemorrhoidectomy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Haemorrhoidectomy is the definitive treatment for advanced symptomatic haemorrhoids; however, traditional excisional techniques can be prolonged and technically demanding. The LigaSure vessel sealing system has emerged as an alternative to conventional diathermy haemorrhoidectomy.
Objective: To evaluate the comparative efficacy of LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy versus conventional diathermy haemorrhoidectomy on operating time in adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids.
Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. Ten studies were screened, yielding seven comparative cohorts (N = 447 patients). Operating time was analyzed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Because native mean differences in raw minutes were not uniformly confirmed across the primary reports, standardized effect sizes (Hedges' g) were synthesized as a standardized fallback metric. Heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.
Results: LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy was associated with a statistically significant reduction in operating time compared to conventional diathermy (pooled Hedges' g = -3.86, 95% CI: [-6.305, -1.413], p = 0.0020). Extreme between-study heterogeneity was observed (I² = 98.57%, Q = 242.33, p < 0.0001; tau² = 10.638). Egger's regression test demonstrated marked asymmetry (intercept = 1.9998, p = 0.0006), indicating high risk of publication bias and small-study effects.
Conclusions: LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy significantly reduces operating time compared with conventional diathermy. However, given extreme statistical heterogeneity, absence of raw minute scale pooling, and significant funnel plot asymmetry, these findings must be interpreted with caution.

# The Effect of LigaSure Versus Conventional Diathermy Haemorrhoidectomy on Operating Time: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Haemorrhoidal disease represents one of the most common proctological conditions encountered in surgical practice, affecting millions of adults worldwide. While low-grade haemorrhoids (grades I and II) are frequently managed with conservative lifestyle interventions and outpatient non-excisional modalities, advanced symptomatic haemorrhoids (grades III and IV) typically necessitate surgical excision.

Conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy—most notably the Milligan-Morgan open technique and the Ferguson closed technique—has traditionally utilized cold scalpel, scissors, or conventional monopolar/bipolar diathermy. Although conventional diathermy allows concurrent tissue division and coagulative hemostasis, lateral thermal spread and the frequent need for additional suture ligation can extend operative duration and contribute to postoperative pain and tissue trauma. 

The LigaSure™ vessel sealing system (Medtronic/Covidien) utilizes precise feedback-controlled electrothermal energy combined with mechanical pressure to denature collagen and elastin within vessel walls, achieving complete autologous sealing of vessels up to 7 mm in diameter with minimal lateral thermal dissipation (typically < 1–2 mm). By obviating the requirement for extensive suture ligation and providing immediate hemostasis, LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy is hypothesized to substantially curtail operative duration.

The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the comparative effect of LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy versus conventional diathermy haemorrhoidectomy on operating time (measured in minutes from the initial excision to final hemostasis or wound closure) in adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids.

---

## Search and Screening Strategy

A comprehensive literature search was executed across major biomedical citation indexes, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), alongside trial registries. Search strings incorporated combinations of controlled vocabulary terms (Medical Subject Headings) and text words encompassing "LigaSure", "bipolar vessel sealing system", "haemorrhoidectomy", "hemorrhoidectomy", "diathermy", and "operating time".

Eligibility criteria were defined *a priori*:
1. **Population**: Adult patients (≥ 18 years) undergoing surgical treatment for symptomatic haemorrhoids (grades III–IV or mixed internal/external disease).
2. **Intervention**: Excisional haemorrhoidectomy performed primarily with the LigaSure vessel sealing system.
3. **Comparator**: Conventional diathermy haemorrhoidectomy (open or closed techniques utilizing standard electrocautery).
4. **Outcome**: Total operating time (measured in minutes).
5. **Study Design**: Comparative prospective randomized controlled trials and non-randomized comparative cohort studies.

Across the search pipeline, 10 studies were identified and evaluated for eligibility. After full-text verification, 7 independent study cohorts satisfied all quantitative inclusion criteria and reported adequate parameters for effect size calculation, comprising a total sample size of 447 surgical patients.

## Effect Size Calculation

The primary outcome of interest was the difference in operating time between the intervention and control arms. Because raw continuous metrics across all included studies could not be harmonized into confirmed native-scale units (minutes with identical variance reporting), standardized mean differences were calculated. Hedges' $g$ was selected to adjust for small-sample bias. 

Negative values of Hedges' $g$ reflect a shorter operating time in the LigaSure group compared with conventional diathermy, whereas positive values indicate prolonged operative duration in the LigaSure cohort.

## Heterogeneity Assessment

Statistical heterogeneity across the included cohorts was substantial:

- **Cochran's $Q$ statistic**: 242.33 ($df = 6$, $p < 0.0001$)
- **Inconsistency index ($I^2$)**: 98.57%
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$)**: 10.6382

An $I^2$ value of 98.57% denotes that nearly all observed variance is attributable to genuine differences across trial populations, procedural definitions, or surgical techniques rather than sampling error. The dispersion of effect sizes is visualized in the Heterogeneity Summary (`outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/heterogeneity_summary.png`) and the Bubble Chart (`outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/bubble_chart.png`).

Potential sources of this extreme heterogeneity include:
1. **Differences in Surgical Technique**: Variations between open (Milligan-Morgan) and closed (Ferguson) conventional diathermy approaches across control arms.
2. **Number of Columns Excised**: Tri-columnar versus bi-columnar excisions, or inclusion of circumferential prolapse.
3. **Surgeon Learning Curve and Experience**: Differences in familiarity with the LigaSure handpiece versus traditional diathermy.
4. **Definition of Operative Start/Stop Times**: Variations in whether time measurements commenced at anal dilatation, local anesthetic infiltration, or the first incision, and whether time ended at pedicle division or mucosal closure.

## Moderator and Subgroup Analyses

Given the limited number of studies ($k = 7$), meta-regression with multiple covariates was statistically constrained. However, visual inspection of the effect distributions indicates two discernible clusters:
- Trials with moderate effect sizes (`study_6_R6`: $g = -0.879$; `study_9_R9`: $g = -0.818$)
- Trials with extreme effect sizes (`study_3_R3`: $g = -7.481$; `study_8_R8`: $g = -9.307$)

Studies reporting very high effect sizes may have utilized open diathermy with routine manual transfixion suture ligation for all vascular pedicles, contrasting with rapid sutureless LigaSure applications. Furthermore, variations in perioperative analgesia (e.g., pudendal nerve blocks or local infiltration) may have influenced surgical field clarity and operative speed.

---

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were evaluated using quantitative modeling:

- **Egger's Linear Regression Test**: Intercept = 1.9998, $p = 0.0006$.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Analysis**: Estimated missing studies ($k_0$) = 0; adjusted pooled estimate remained $g = -3.8593$.
- **Overall Risk of Bias**: High.

The Egger's regression test demonstrated highly statistically significant asymmetry ($p = 0.0006$). While the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), this frequently reflects the algorithm's vulnerability when severe between-study heterogeneity ($\tau^2 = 10.64$) and clustering are present, rather than genuine absence of publication bias. Small-study effects, selective outcome reporting, or unpublished non-significant trials represent a major risk to the validity of the effect magnitude.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

Across the 10 records captured in the initial pipeline retrieval, 3 documents were excluded from the quantitative synthesis due to duplication of patient cohorts, review format, or absence of extractable continuous operating time data. Seven independent comparative cohorts were retained for meta-analytic pooling. 

Crucially, native-scale pooling was not feasible (`native_pooling_used: false`): variance reporting and summary scales across the original reports precluded direct, unstandardized pooling of mean minutes. Consequently, all calculations were executed on a standardized fallback scale (Hedges' g). Readers must recognize that this pooled metric represents a standardized effect index rather than an absolute difference in minutes.

## Discussion

This meta-analysis demonstrates that LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy yields a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in operative time compared to conventional diathermy (pooled Hedges' $g = -3.86$, $p = 0.0020$). In surgical practice, LigaSure streamlines haemorrhoid excision by delivering instantaneous electrothermal coaptation and cutting, eliminating the requirement for sequential clamping, manual transfixion ligation, and extensive oversewing of vascular haemorrhoidal cushions.

The clinical implications of reduced operative duration in proctological surgery are notable:
- Decreased overall exposure to general or regional anesthesia, which is particularly beneficial in elderly patients or individuals with cardiopulmonary comorbidities.
- Enhanced operating theater efficiency and reduced cumulative procedural turnover times.
- Diminished collateral tissue thermal injury, which has been correlated in secondary literature with reduced early postoperative pain scores and faster return to normal daily activities.

Nonetheless, the magnitude of the standardized effect size ($g = -3.86$) is unusually large for surgical interventions. This extreme effect is driven primarily by studies such as `study_8_R8` ($g = -9.31$) and `study_3_R3` ($g = -7.48$). When studies report standard deviations that are exceptionally tight relative to mean differences, standardized metrics inflate disproportionately. In trials where standard deviations were broader (`study_6_R6`, `study_9_R9`), effect sizes stabilized at moderate-to-large values ($g \approx -0.8$ to $-0.9$).

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations must be considered when interpreting these findings:

1. **Standardized Fallback Metric**: Because native pooling could not be validated across studies, results could not be expressed as a confirmed absolute mean difference in minutes. Standardized effect sizes reflect relative separation scaled by sample variance rather than direct clinical units.
2. **Extreme Statistical Heterogeneity**: An $I^2$ of 98.57% and a $\tau^2$ of 10.638 reflect substantial variability in baseline patient selection, haemorrhoidal grade, surgical technique (open Milligan-Morgan vs. closed Ferguson), and intraoperative timing protocols.
3. **High Risk of Publication Bias**: Egger's test revealed substantial funnel asymmetry ($p = 0.0006$). Smaller studies tended to report dramatically larger effect sizes, suggesting selective dissemination or methodological differences in smaller trial environments.
4. **Technical Confounders**: The studies did not uniformly control for surgeon experience with the LigaSure device. The initial learning curve associated with novel energy platforms can skew early procedural timings.

---

## Conclusion

LigaSure haemorrhoidectomy achieves a statistically significant reduction in operating time compared to conventional diathermy haemorrhoidectomy in adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids (pooled Hedges' $g = -3.859$, 95% CI: [-6.305, -1.413], $p = 0.0020$). However, given the presence of extreme statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 98.57\%$), reliance on a standardized fallback scale rather than confirmed raw minutes, and high risk of publication bias, the pooled magnitude must be interpreted with caution. Future large-scale, methodologically rigorous randomized controlled trials adhering to standardized operative timing protocols and transparent reporting of raw minutes are warranted to confirm these observations.
