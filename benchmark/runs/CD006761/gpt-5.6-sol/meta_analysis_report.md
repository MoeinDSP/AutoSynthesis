# LigaSure Versus Conventional Excisional Haemorrhoidectomy for Operating Time: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis

## Abstract

## Abstract

**Background:** LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy may reduce operating time compared with conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of LigaSure versus conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy on operating time in adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids.

**Methods:** Eligible comparisons involved LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy and a conventional excisional technique, including scissors, diathermy, Milligan–Morgan, Ferguson, or a comparable procedure. Operating time was defined in minutes from incision or commencement of haemorrhoidectomy to completion; anaesthesia time, total theatre time, and time to haemostasis alone were outside the prespecified outcome. The supplied dataset contained 10 studies and six effect-size records. Four studies had the raw data required for native-scale pooling and contributed 214 participants. A random-effects model estimated by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was used. The raw mean difference (MD) was defined as LigaSure minus conventional, so a negative MD represents a shorter operation with LigaSure.

**Results:** The pooled mean difference was **−10.231251161470396 minutes** (95% CI **−19.108 to −1.354**; p=**0.023886253991479565**; k=4; N=214), favoring LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy. Heterogeneity was extreme (Q=**386.973**, I²=**99.45%**, τ²=**81.517241 minutes²**; Q-test p recorded as **0.0**). Six supplied standardized effect estimates were all negative, but two records lacked the raw inputs needed for native MD pooling and were excluded from that analysis. Egger’s intercept was **2.7825** (p=**0.0022**), indicating funnel-plot asymmetry; trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀=0). Publication-bias risk was rated high.

**Conclusions:** LigaSure was associated with a statistically significant reduction in operating time of approximately 10.23 minutes, but the estimate was highly inconsistent across studies and potentially affected by small-study effects or publication bias. The limited reporting supplied for study methods, risk of bias, and operating-time derivation materially restricts certainty and clinical generalizability.

## Introduction

Excisional haemorrhoidectomy is an established treatment for symptomatic haemorrhoids requiring surgery. Conventional excision may use scissors or diathermy and may follow an open Milligan–Morgan, closed Ferguson, or comparable excisional technique. LigaSure uses bipolar vessel sealing during tissue division and could shorten operating time by combining dissection and haemostasis.

This review addressed the following question: among adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids undergoing excisional haemorrhoidectomy, what is the effect of LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy, compared with conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy, on operating time in minutes?

The prespecified outcome was duration from skin incision or the start of haemorrhoidectomy to completion of the procedure. Anaesthesia time, total theatre or operating-room time, and time to haemostasis alone were not eligible. The intended native effect measure was the raw mean difference (MD), calculated as the mean for LigaSure minus the mean for conventional excision. Accordingly, MD <0 denotes a shorter operation and favors LigaSure.

## Search and Screening

### Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies compared:

- **Treatment:** LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy.
- **Control:** conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy using scissors, diathermy, Milligan–Morgan, Ferguson, or a comparable conventional excisional technique.
- **Population:** adults with symptomatic haemorrhoids undergoing excisional haemorrhoidectomy.
- **Outcome:** eligible operating time reported or derivable in minutes.

Stapled, laser, Doppler-guided, and other non-excisional or otherwise ineligible intervention arms were not to be included. In multi-arm studies, only eligible LigaSure and conventional excision arms were to be retained. Other outcomes—including blood loss, postoperative pain, wound healing, complications, hospital stay, recurrence, and return to work—were outside this review.

### Data handling rules

The planned extraction used means, standard deviations (SDs), and numbers randomized or analysed in each arm. The protocol required conversion of seconds or hours to minutes; conversion of SE/SEM to SD using SD = SE × √n after checking table footnotes; and, when necessary, estimation of means and SDs from medians with ranges or interquartile ranges using recognized methods such as those of Wan et al. or Luo et al. Arm assignment was based on the procedure performed rather than table position. Where two eligible conventional arms existed, they were to be combined using standard Cochrane formulas when clinically appropriate, or the closest comparator was to be selected with justification.

The supplied results did not provide study-level extraction sheets, original units, conversion logs, arm means and SDs, or imputation annotations. It was therefore not possible to verify whether any time conversion, SE-to-SD conversion, median-based estimation, range-based estimation, or combination of conventional arms occurred.

Four records had sufficient raw inputs for the requested native-scale MD meta-analysis; two additional effect-size records lacked the raw inputs needed for native MD pooling. ## Meta-Analytic Result

A random-effects model estimated using restricted maximum likelihood produced a **pooled mean difference of −10.231251161470396 minutes** (95% CI **−19.108 to −1.354**; p=**0.023886253991479565**; k=4; N=214).

The effect favors **LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy**, as specified in the supplied analysis output. Under the raw convention of LigaSure minus conventional, the negative estimate indicates that LigaSure procedures were, on average, approximately **10.23 minutes shorter**. The confidence interval excludes zero but is wide: values compatible with the model range from approximately 1.35 to 19.11 minutes shorter with LigaSure.

Native pooling was used, and the effect measure was confirmed as a mean difference in minutes. The two studies lacking raw arm-level inputs were not included in this MD estimate. No overall estimate combining native MDs and standardized effects was calculated, because doing so would mix incompatible units.

A cumulative meta-analysis graphic was generated at `outputs/HShuwD5_xJc0KHeDZGpcYg/cumulative_plot.png`. Because the ordering variable and cumulative numerical results were not supplied, the cumulative plot should be treated as a visual supplement rather than evidence of a particular temporal or precision-related pattern.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was extreme:

- **Cochran’s Q:** 386.973
- **Q-test p-value:** recorded by the pipeline as 0.0
- **I²:** 99.45%
- **τ²:** 81.517241 minutes²
- **τ:** approximately 9.0287 minutes

An I² of 99.45% means that almost all observed dispersion, under the assumptions of the statistic, was attributed to between-study heterogeneity rather than sampling error. The estimated between-study SD of approximately 9.03 minutes is large relative to the pooled reduction of 10.23 minutes. Consequently, the pooled MD is an average across highly inconsistent effects and should not be treated as a universally expected reduction for every surgical setting.

The heterogeneity summary figure is available at `outputs/HShuwD5_xJc0KHeDZGpcYg/heterogeneity_summary.png`.

Potential explanations could include differences in the conventional technique, open versus closed excision, number or grade of haemorrhoidal cushions, surgeon experience, definitions and timing of the operating-time outcome, haemostatic practice, and whether reported values were means or converted summaries. These possibilities are clinically plausible but could not be tested with the supplied data.

## Publication Bias

The supplied publication-bias analysis reported:

- **Egger’s intercept:** 2.7825
- **Egger’s test p-value:** 0.0022
- **Trim-and-fill estimated missing studies:** k₀=0
- **Trim-and-fill adjusted standardized estimate:** −4.5895
- **Overall publication-bias risk:** high

Egger’s test was statistically significant and indicates funnel-plot asymmetry consistent with possible small-study effects or publication bias. However, asymmetry may also arise from the extreme clinical or methodological heterogeneity observed here. Moreover, asymmetry tests are generally unreliable with very few studies; conventional guidance advises particular caution when fewer than 10 studies are available.

Trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies, but this does not negate the significant Egger result. Trim-and-fill can perform poorly under high heterogeneity and with sparse evidence. The trim-and-fill value was supplied on a standardized scale and is therefore **not directly comparable** with the pooled MD in minutes. It must not be interpreted as an adjusted operating-time reduction of 4.5895 minutes.

No funnel-plot path was included among the generated figures. Overall, the supplied high-risk publication-bias classification is appropriate, and the pooled MD warrants cautious interpretation.

## Discussion

### Principal findings

In four studies comprising 214 participants, LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy reduced operating time relative to conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy by a pooled average of **10.231251161470396 minutes**. The 95% CI (**−19.108 to −1.354 minutes**) and p-value (**0.023886253991479565**) indicate statistical significance under the fitted REML random-effects model.

The direction of all six supplied standardized study-level estimates was consistent with shorter operating time for LigaSure, although only four studies could be represented on the prespecified native scale. Directional consistency does not resolve the very large differences in estimated magnitude. With I²=99.45% and τ²=81.517241 minutes², the pooled estimate should be understood as an average of markedly heterogeneous results rather than a precise expectation for an individual hospital, surgeon, or patient.

### Clinical interpretation

A reduction of approximately 10 minutes could be operationally meaningful for a short elective procedure, potentially affecting theatre scheduling and resource use. Its importance depends on the baseline duration of conventional surgery, which was not supplied. The available analysis also does not address whether any time saving is accompanied by differences in safety, postoperative pain, wound healing, recurrence, equipment cost, or other outcomes; those outcomes were deliberately outside this review.

Because conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy encompasses several techniques, variation in comparator practice may explain part of the heterogeneity. A difference against scissors-based Milligan–Morgan excision, for example, may not generalize to an efficient diathermy-based or closed Ferguson procedure. Likewise, a learning effect for LigaSure or variation in surgeons’ experience could materially change operating time.

### Certainty and robustness

The point estimate is statistically significant, and the native scale is clinically interpretable. Nevertheless, confidence in a single summary value is weakened by four considerations: only four studies contributed to native pooling; heterogeneity was extreme; publication-bias risk was rated high; and no study-level risk-of-bias information was available. No prediction interval, influence analysis, leave-one-out analysis, or alternative τ² estimator was supplied. Robustness to individual studies and modeling choices therefore remains unknown.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

- The pipeline reported **10 studies**, but only **six effect-size records** were available and only **four** contributed to the native MD meta-analysis.
- Two records—identified in the validation output as `study_6` and `study_7`—were excluded from native MD pooling because they lacked raw inputs. Their standardized effects were not converted into or treated as minute differences.
- The six descriptive standardized effects and the four-study mean-difference pool are different statistical representations and were kept separate.
- The publication-bias trim-and-fill result was also on a standardized scale and was not interpreted as minutes.
- Study reports and extraction sheets were unavailable, so participant overlap, duplicate reports, arm assignment, original time units, SD derivations, summary-statistic conversions, and conventional-arm combinations could not be independently validated.
- The Q-test p-value was supplied as **0.0**, most likely reflecting numerical rounding or underflow; it should be understood as extremely small rather than as a literal probability of zero.

## Limitations

First, the search strategy and screening history were not supplied. Database coverage, unpublished-study searches, language restrictions, search dates, duplicate removal, and reasons for exclusion cannot be assessed, preventing full independent reproduction of the review.

Second, bibliographic and clinical characteristics were absent. The precise conventional procedures, population severity, surgeon expertise, trial design, and operating-time definitions could not be compared. This is particularly consequential given the extreme heterogeneity.

Third, only four studies and 214 participants informed the native-scale result. Two additional records could not be included because arm-level data needed to calculate an MD were unavailable. Selective availability of raw data could itself bias the native-scale result.

Fourth, study-level risk of bias was not reported. Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding of outcome timing, incomplete data, selective reporting, and deviations from intended surgery could not be evaluated. No formal certainty-of-evidence assessment was possible from the supplied information.

Fifth, the publication-bias analysis was based on sparse evidence and is unstable. Although Egger’s test was significant, both regression-based asymmetry testing and trim-and-fill have important limitations with few, highly heterogeneous studies.

Finally, no prediction interval or sensitivity analyses were provided. The extent to which the pooled result depends on a single influential study, alternative correlation assumptions, or the method used to estimate between-study variance remains unknown.

## Conclusion

LigaSure vessel-sealing haemorrhoidectomy was associated with a statistically significant shorter operating time than conventional excisional haemorrhoidectomy. The REML random-effects **pooled mean difference was −10.231251161470396 minutes** (95% CI **−19.108 to −1.354**; p=**0.023886253991479565**; k=4; N=214), favoring LigaSure.

The result should not be interpreted as a uniform 10-minute benefit across settings. Heterogeneity was extreme (I²=99.45%), the native-scale evidence base was small, and publication-bias risk was high. Better-reported trials and individual-study data are needed to clarify whether the magnitude of time saving depends on the conventional comparator, surgical expertise, disease severity, or the definition of operating time.
