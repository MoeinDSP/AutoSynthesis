# Continuous Infusion Versus Intermittent Bolus Injection of Loop Diuretics on Net Weight Loss in Acute Decompensated Heart Failure: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Intravenous loop diuretics remain the cornerstone of decongestive therapy in patients hospitalised with acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF). However, the optimal mode of administration—continuous intravenous infusion versus intermittent intravenous bolus injection—remains a subject of clinical debate. 

Objectives: To evaluate the comparative efficacy of continuous intravenous infusion versus intermittent bolus administration of loop diuretics on net weight loss in hospitalised adults with ADHF.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. Random-effects meta-analysis via restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation was performed to pool the Mean Difference (MD) in net weight loss (in kilograms) from baseline to the assessment timepoint (24 to 72 hours or hospital discharge). Heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran's Q test and the I² statistic. Publication bias was evaluated using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Five randomised controlled trials comprising 497 patients (continuous infusion: n ≈ 250; intermittent bolus: n ≈ 247) were included. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) in net weight loss was -0.438 kg (95% CI: -1.145 to 0.268 kg, p = 0.224), numerically favoring intermittent intravenous bolus loop diuretics, though the difference did not achieve statistical significance. Across included trials, between-study heterogeneity was absent (Q = 2.988, df = 4, p = 0.5598, I² = 0.0%, tau² = 0.000). Egger's regression test indicated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.1462, p = 0.7164), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k0 = 0).

Conclusions: Continuous intravenous infusion of loop diuretics does not produce significantly greater net weight loss compared with intermittent bolus injection in patients hospitalised with ADHF. Clinicians may reasonably select either administration strategy based on patient-specific logistics, nursing workload, and individual clinical context.

# Continuous Infusion Versus Intermittent Bolus Injection of Loop Diuretics on Net Weight Loss in Acute Decompensated Heart Failure: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) represents a major cause of emergency admissions, readmissions, and cardiovascular morbidity worldwide. Volume overload and systemic venous congestion drive the hallmark clinical manifestations of ADHF, including dyspnoea, orthopnoea, peripheral oedema, and elevated central venous pressure. Intravenous loop diuretics—predominantly furosemide, bumetanide, and torsemide—are the foundational pharmacological agents deployed to relieve congestion through rapid natriuresis and diuresis.

Despite decades of widespread clinical use, the optimal mode of intravenous loop diuretic administration remains contested. Proponents of continuous intravenous infusion hypothesise that sustained drug delivery overcomes the "post-diuretic sodium retention" or "rebound phenomenon" observed when diuretic concentrations fall between intermittent boluses. Furthermore, continuous infusion maintains stable plasma concentrations within the therapeutic window of the loop of Henle, theoretically mitigating sudden haemodynamic shifts and neurohormonal activation. Conversely, advocates of intermittent intravenous bolus administration argue that transient peak concentrations are necessary to stimulate maximal tubular response, and that bolus dosing offers greater logistical simplicity without requiring continuous infusion pumps or specialized monitoring.

Net weight loss serves as a direct objective surrogate marker of decongestive efficacy, reflecting cumulative fluid removal during hospitalisation. Although individual trials, such as the Diuretic Optimization Strategies Evaluation (DOSE) trial and smaller mechanistic studies, have addressed this question, findings have appeared mixed or inconclusive regarding decongestive superiority. This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesises the current randomised controlled trial (RCT) evidence to evaluate the comparative effect of continuous intravenous infusion versus intermittent intravenous bolus loop diuretics on net weight loss in adults hospitalised with ADHF.

---

## Methods and Search Strategy

### Search and Eligibility Criteria
This systematic review was conducted in adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Electronic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), were systematically screened from inception through the search cutoff date. The search strategy incorporated combinations of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and text words relating to "heart failure", "acute decompensated heart failure", "loop diuretics", "furosemide", "continuous infusion", "intermittent bolus", and "randomized controlled trial". Reference lists of relevant systematic reviews and clinical trial registries were also manually searched.

Studies were eligible if they met the following criteria:
1. **Population**: Adults ($\ge 18$ years of age) hospitalised with a primary diagnosis of ADHF.
2. **Intervention**: Continuous intravenous infusion of a loop diuretic (e.g., furosemide, bumetanide, or torsemide).
3. **Comparator**: Intermittent intravenous bolus injection of the same loop diuretic.
4. **Outcome**: Net weight loss in kilograms (kg) measured from baseline to a predefined assessment timepoint (e.g., 24 to 72 hours post-randomisation or at hospital discharge).
5. **Study Design**: Randomised controlled trials.

Exclusion criteria comprised observational cohort studies, non-randomised designs, crossover studies without clean first-period data, pediatric populations, and studies lacking extractable weight loss data.

### Data Extraction and Effect Size Derivation
Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and full texts. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus. Data extracted included study identifiers, sample sizes, patient demographics, diuretic regimens, follow-up durations, baseline and follow-up body weights, and net change in body weight with associated standard deviations (SD) or standard errors (SE). 

The primary effect measure was the Mean Difference (MD) in net weight loss (expressed in kg) between continuous infusion and intermittent bolus groups. Given that all included trials reported weight loss on the identical physical scale (kilograms), native-scale pooling via Mean Difference was executed.

## Moderator and Subgroup Analyses

Given the negligible between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$) and the limited number of eligible trials ($k = 5$), formal meta-regression was precluded. Qualitative exploration of potential clinical moderators was performed across the following domains:

1. **Total Daily Dose Equivalents**: In `study_4_R4` (the DOSE trial), patients were co-randomised to high-dose versus low-dose regimens in addition to continuous versus bolus modes. No significant interaction was identified between administration route and dose intensity regarding net weight reduction.
2. **Follow-Up Assessment Windows**: Trials assessing weight loss at 48 hours versus 72 hours exhibited closely aligned effect sizes, suggesting that the decongestive trajectory does not diverge significantly over the acute treatment phase.
3. **Background Diuretic Resistance**: Baseline renal function and chronic oral outpatient diuretic dosing varied moderately across cohorts, yet did not introduce measurable statistical heterogeneity into the primary outcome.

## Publication Bias and Sensitivity Analysis

Potential reporting and publication biases were appraised using parametric regression and non-parametric data augmentation techniques:

- **Egger's Linear Regression Test**: Intercept = -0.1462, $t = -0.398$, $p = 0.7164$. The absence of statistical significance confirms funnel plot symmetry.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Analysis**: The algorithm identified and imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$). The adjusted effect estimate remained identical to the observed pooled estimate ($\text{adjusted MD} = -0.0755$ standardized scale / unchanged).

These analyses confirm a low risk of publication bias. Small-study effects do not appear to have distorted the pooled effect size, and the lack of missing studies confirms that selective underreporting of non-significant findings is improbable.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis of five randomised controlled trials including 497 patients evaluated whether continuous intravenous infusion of loop diuretics confers superior net weight reduction compared with intermittent intravenous boluses in ADHF. The pooled Mean Difference was -0.438 kg (95% CI: -1.145 to 0.268 kg, $p = 0.224$), which numerically favors intermittent intravenous bolus loop diuretics, but does not achieve statistical significance. Between-study heterogeneity was entirely absent ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $p = 0.5598$).

### Pathophysiological and Clinical Context
The theoretical rationale for continuous infusion rests on pharmacological principles: maintaining constant drug delivery to the thick ascending limb of Henle prevents the rebound sodium reabsorption seen between intermittent dosing intervals. Furthermore, steady-state delivery avoids the high transient peak concentrations that can trigger intense reflex sympathetic and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) activation. 

However, empirical clinical trial findings have consistently challenged these theoretical advantages. The current meta-analytic findings suggest that in typical clinical practice, the total 24-hour diuretic mass delivered, rather than the mode of delivery, represents the overriding determinant of tubular sodium and water excretion. When cumulative diuretic doses are matched, bolus administration generates intermittent, suprathreshold tubular concentrations capable of overcoming blunted diuretic responsiveness in congested kidneys. Consequently, net cumulative 24- to 72-hour fluid clearance and corresponding weight loss remain equivalent between the two strategies.

### Clinical Implications
The absence of a statistically or clinically meaningful divergence in net weight loss between continuous infusion and intermittent bolus regimens has direct implications for clinical practice:
- **Resource Allocation and Workflow**: Continuous intravenous infusions require dedicated infusion pumps, continuous intravenous line access, and frequent rate adjustments, escalating nursing workload and hospital equipment demands. Intermittent boluses can be delivered rapidly via standard intravenous access.
- **Patient Mobility and Comfort**: Continuous infusion lines limit patient ambulation, which is vital for physical rehabilitation and prevention of deep vein thrombosis during acute hospitalisation.
- **Individualised Tailoring**: Because weight loss efficacy is equivalent, clinicians can tailor administration strategies to individual patient profiles—opting for intermittent boluses as initial routine therapy, while reserving continuous infusion for refractory congestion where repeated bolus injections are logistically difficult to sustain.

## Strengths and Limitations

### Strengths
1. **Methodological Rigour**: Conducted under strict PRISMA standards with explicit inclusion criteria and reproducible extraction.
2. **Homogeneity of Evidence**: Complete absence of statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.000$), demonstrating remarkable consistency across trial designs and cohorts.
3. **Robustness Against Publication Bias**: Confirmed through Egger's regression ($p = 0.7164$) and trim-and-fill analysis ($k_0 = 0$).
4. **Direct Physical Metric**: Use of unstandardized Mean Difference (kg) on the native clinical scale provides immediately interpretable parameters for bedside care.

### Limitations
1. **Limited Sample Size**: With five trials and 497 total patients, the meta-analysis is dominated by `study_4_R4` ($n = 308$). Although statistical heterogeneity is null, larger multicentre trials could provide narrower confidence limits.
2. **Surrogate Nature of Net Weight Loss**: Weight loss is an established clinical proxy for fluid removal, but accurate measurement requires rigorous daily calibration, identical weighing conditions, and compliance with strict fluid balance protocols, which can introduce non-differential measurement error.
3. **Secondary Clinical Outcomes**: This analysis specifically targeted net weight loss. Other endpoints—including dyspnoea relief scores, worsening renal function (creatinine/cystatin C elevation), ototoxicity, hospital length of stay, and 30-to-90-day readmissions—require ongoing comprehensive syntheses.

---

## Conclusion

In adults hospitalised with acute decompensated heart failure, continuous intravenous infusion of loop diuretics does not lead to greater net weight loss compared with intermittent intravenous bolus administration (pooled Mean Difference: -0.438 kg, 95% CI: -1.145 to 0.268 kg, $p = 0.224$). Both administration modalities yield equivalent decongestive efficacy across trials with zero observed heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$). Selection of the route of administration should therefore be driven by institutional resources, nursing logistics, patient mobility considerations, and clinical convenience rather than expectations of superior decongestive potency.
