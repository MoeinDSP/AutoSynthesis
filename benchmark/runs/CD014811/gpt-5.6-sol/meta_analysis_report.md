# Continuous versus intermittent intravenous loop-diuretic administration for in-hospital weight loss in adults with acute heart failure: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Continuous intravenous infusion and intermittent intravenous bolus injection are both used to administer loop diuretics to adults hospitalised with acute heart failure. This review evaluated their comparative effect on net weight loss during in-hospital treatment.

**Methods:** Eligible studies directly compared continuous infusion with intermittent bolus administration of the same intravenous loop diuretic in hospitalised adults with acute heart failure and reported net weight loss. The intended outcome was kilograms lost, expressed as a positive quantity, at the longest reported assessment remaining within the hospital stay. Five studies contributed five effect sizes and 497 participants. A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) pooled the native-scale mean difference (MD), defined as continuous infusion minus bolus injection. Positive values favour continuous infusion.

**Results:** Across five studies, the pooled mean difference was **0.7219560695936074 kg** (95% CI **0.015 to 1.429**; p = **0.045414855721821405**), favouring continuous intravenous infusion. Statistical heterogeneity was absent (Q = **0.464**, df = 4, p = **0.9769**; I² = **0.0%**; τ² = **0.0**). Egger’s intercept was **0.1183** (p = **0.7837**), and trim-and-fill imputed no studies (k₀ = **0**), providing no detected evidence of small-study effects, although power was limited with only five studies.

**Conclusions:** Continuous intravenous loop-diuretic infusion produced a statistically significant, modest increase in in-hospital net weight loss relative to intermittent bolus injection. The estimate was precise enough to exclude no difference only narrowly, and its clinical importance remains uncertain. Interpretation is limited by the small evidence base and incomplete study-level reporting in the supplied dataset.

## Introduction

Acute heart failure frequently requires hospitalisation for intravenous diuretic therapy to relieve congestion. Loop diuretics—most commonly furosemide, but also torsemide or bumetanide—may be given as a continuous intravenous infusion or as intermittent intravenous bolus injections. Continuous administration could theoretically maintain tubular drug exposure and reduce post-diuretic sodium retention, whereas bolus treatment is simpler and may permit easier dose adjustment. Whether either delivery strategy produces greater decongestion remains clinically relevant.

This systematic review and meta-analysis addressed the following question: among adults hospitalised with acute heart failure, what is the effect of continuous intravenous infusion of a loop diuretic, compared with intermittent intravenous bolus injection of the same loop diuretic, on net weight loss during the in-hospital treatment period?

The prespecified outcome was net weight loss in kilograms, represented as a positive quantity for kilograms lost. The target contrast was the mean difference (MD), calculated as continuous infusion minus intermittent bolus injection; by definition and as confirmed in the supplied analysis, a positive MD favours continuous infusion.

## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects meta-analysis using **restricted maximum likelihood (REML)** included **5 studies** and **497 participants**. The **pooled mean difference was 0.7219560695936074 kg** (95% CI **0.015 to 1.429**; p = **0.045414855721821405**). The analysis record states that this result favours **continuous intravenous infusion of a loop diuretic**.

Thus, on average, continuous infusion was associated with approximately **0.72 kg greater net in-hospital weight loss** than intermittent bolus injection. The lower confidence limit of 0.015 kg lies only slightly above the null, indicating that statistical significance was marginal. The interval remains compatible with effects ranging from a negligible difference to approximately 1.43 kg of additional weight loss.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **0.1183** with p = **0.7837**. This was not statistically significant and therefore provided no detected evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0**, meaning that no potentially missing studies were imputed. The supplied publication-bias assessment classified the risk as **low**.

The trim-and-fill output also reported an adjusted value of **0.167** under the field name `trim_fill_g`. Because this value is presented on a standardized-effect field whereas the primary synthesis used a native-scale MD, it should not be interpreted as an adjusted kilogram difference or substituted for the primary estimate.

These diagnostics should be interpreted cautiously. With only five studies, tests for funnel-plot asymmetry have low power, while trim-and-fill can be unstable. The findings therefore indicate an absence of detected asymmetry, not proof that reporting or publication bias is absent.

## Discussion

This synthesis found that continuous intravenous infusion of a loop diuretic was associated with greater net weight loss during hospital treatment than intermittent intravenous bolus injection. The pooled MD was **0.7219560695936074 kg**, with a 95% CI from **0.015 to 1.429 kg**, and the result narrowly reached conventional statistical significance (p = **0.045414855721821405**). Statistical heterogeneity was not detected.

The magnitude of the pooled effect is modest. Weight change is a practical marker of decongestion, but it is influenced by measurement timing, scale calibration, oral intake, insensible losses, and non-fluid changes. Furthermore, a statistically detectable difference in weight loss does not by itself demonstrate improvement in symptoms, organ function, length of stay, readmission, survival, or patient-reported outcomes. Those outcomes were intentionally not included in this review and should not be inferred from the present result.

The absence of detectable heterogeneity may suggest broadly consistent comparative effects, but confidence in that interpretation is constrained by the small number of studies and missing clinical descriptors. The pooled CI barely excludes zero, so modest changes in data, assumptions, or trial availability could alter the statistical conclusion. The result is best understood as evidence of a small average advantage in weight loss for continuous infusion rather than proof of broad clinical superiority.

No detected publication-bias signal materially weakens the result, but it also does not substantially strengthen it because only five comparisons were available. Egger’s test and trim-and-fill are not reliable safeguards against selective publication in such a small evidence base.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

A material internal reporting inconsistency is present in the supplied dataset. The five individual effects are labelled **Hedges’ g**, whereas the pooled analysis is explicitly labelled a native-scale **Mean Difference (MD)** in kilograms and reports `native_pooling_used = true`. The pooled MD has therefore been reported exactly as specified, but the individual standardized values cannot be reconciled with it without the underlying arm-level means, SDs, and sample sizes.

In addition, the supplied dataset did not provide arm-specific extraction data or assessment periods. It is therefore impossible to verify sign reversal, pound-to-kilogram conversion, SE-to-SD conversion, median/IQR estimation, substitution of randomised for analysed samples, or pooling of multiple dose arms. These omissions affect auditability and reproducibility, even though they do not change the numerical pooled result reported by the analysis pipeline.

## Limitations

1. **Small evidence base:** Only five studies and 497 participants were included, limiting precision, heterogeneity assessment, and publication-bias diagnostics.
2. **Incomplete study identification:** Bibliographic citations, recruitment settings, eligibility criteria, and treatment protocols were unavailable.
3. **Missing arm-level outcome data:** Means, SDs, arm-specific sample sizes, and selected measurement periods were not supplied, precluding independent recalculation of the MDs.
4. **Effect-scale inconsistency:** Study-level estimates were labelled Hedges’ g, but the pooled result was reported as a native-scale MD. This inconsistency could not be resolved from the available data.
5. **No risk-of-bias assessment:** Randomisation, allocation concealment, blinding, missing outcome data, selective reporting, and other trial-level biases could not be evaluated.
6. **No moderator analysis:** Clinical and methodological sources of effect modification could not be assessed.
7. **Outcome limitations:** Weight loss is an indirect marker of decongestion, and no conclusions can be drawn here regarding symptoms, safety, renal outcomes, rehospitalisation, or mortality.
8. **Borderline statistical result:** The lower CI limit was only 0.015 kg and the p value was 0.045414855721821405, making the inference sensitive to analytical assumptions or small data changes.
9. **Incomplete review-process reporting:** Search methods and screening counts were unavailable, preventing full verification of PRISMA compliance and study completeness.

## Conclusion

In five studies involving 497 adults hospitalised with acute heart failure, continuous intravenous infusion of a loop diuretic resulted in a **pooled mean difference of 0.7219560695936074 kg greater net in-hospital weight loss** than intermittent intravenous bolus injection (95% CI **0.015 to 1.429**; p = **0.045414855721821405**), favouring continuous infusion. No statistical heterogeneity was detected (I² = **0.0%**; τ² = **0.0**), and no small-study effect was detected by Egger’s test or trim-and-fill. Nevertheless, the effect was modest and only narrowly statistically significant. Missing study-level details, absent risk-of-bias information, and inconsistent labelling of individual versus pooled effect scales limit confidence and reproducibility. Better-reported trials with standardised weight-assessment periods and complete arm-level data are needed to establish the clinical importance of this difference.
