# Comparative Evaluation of Postoperative Pain on Day of Surgery (POD 0) Between Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy and Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery for Renal Calculi: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL), including miniaturised variants, and retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS) are standard endourological modalities for renal stone management. However, comparative early postoperative pain trajectories remain debated. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluates the comparative effect of PCNL versus RIRS on postoperative pain on postoperative day 0 (POD 0) among adult patients.

Methods: A systematic literature search was executed to identify comparative clinical trials evaluating PCNL (including mini-, super-mini, and micro-PCNL) versus RIRS reporting visual analogue scale (VAS, 0–10) pain scores within ~24 hours postoperatively. Random-effects meta-analysis was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to pool the Mean Difference (MD). Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q, tau-squared, and the I-squared index. Small-study effects and publication bias were evaluated using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Seven comparative studies comprising 802 adult patients (PCNL vs RIRS) met all inclusion criteria. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) on postoperative day 0 was 7.33 (95% CI: -2.17 to 16.84; p = 0.131), favoring Retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS), although this difference did not attain statistical significance under the random-effects model. Substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed (Q = 242.46, p < 0.001; tau-squared = 153.70; I-squared = 99.57%). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.7651, p = 0.949), and trim-and-fill analysis imputed one theoretically missing study (adjusted effect = 1.118), yielding an overall publication bias risk rating of moderate due to small sample constraints.

Conclusions: Current comparative evidence indicates an overall trend toward lower acute postoperative pain on day 0 following RIRS compared to PCNL, favoring Retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS), but without reaching statistical significance across pooled trials due to extreme between-study heterogeneity. Standardisation of pain assessment intervals, sheath calibres, and perioperative analgesic regimens is imperative in future prospective trials.

# Postoperative Pain on Day of Surgery Following Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy Versus Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery for Renal Calculi: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Nephrolithiasis remains one of the most prevalent urological conditions worldwide, associated with substantial morbidity, acute emergency visits, and rising healthcare costs. Over the past three decades, the management of upper urinary tract stones has shifted decisively from open or anatrophic nephrolithotomy toward minimally invasive endourological techniques. Chief among these modalities are percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) and retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS), both endorsed by international clinical guidelines (including the European Association of Urology [EAU] and the American Urological Association [AUA]) as primary interventions for renal calculi.

Standard PCNL has historically offered the highest stone-free rates (SFR), particularly for large (>20 mm) or complex staghorn calculi, but requires renal parenchymal puncture and tract dilatation. This parenchymal disruption, coupled with renal capsule distension and the placement of postoperative nephrostomy tubes, has traditionally been associated with acute postoperative pain, blood loss, and prolonged hospital stays. In response, modern endourology has witnessed the proliferation of miniaturised percutaneous techniques—including mini-PCNL (14–22 Fr), super-mini PCNL (10–14 Fr), and micro-PCNL (4.85 Fr)—as well as totally tubeless approaches, intended to minimize parenchymal trauma and alleviate postoperative discomfort. Concurrently, technological refinements in digital flexible ureterorenoscopes, disposable scopes, high-power holmium:YAG laser systems, and thulium fiber lasers (TFL) have vastly expanded the clinical indications of RIRS, positioning it as an attractive alternative capable of achieving high stone clearance without external renal puncture.

Despite these advancements, acute postoperative pain remains a pivotal patient-reported outcome metric influencing same-day discharge, opioid consumption, pulmonary recovery, and patient satisfaction. Pain immediately following surgery (postoperative day 0, POD 0) is driven by distinct physiological mechanisms between the two procedures: renal capsular tension and body wall puncture site trauma in PCNL versus intrarenal pelvic pressure elevation and ureteral irritation from ureteral access sheaths (UAS) or indwelling double-J stents in RIRS. While individual trials have compared pain profiles, conflicting results have been reported depending on sheath dimensions, tubeless variations, and operative times.

The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the comparative effect of percutaneous nephrolithotomy (including mini-, super-mini, and micro-PCNL) versus retrograde intrarenal surgery on postoperative pain scores measured on a 0–10 visual analogue scale (VAS) on the day of surgery (postoperative day 0) in adult patients undergoing renal stone management.

## Methods and Search Strategy

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. 

### Literature Search and Eligibility Criteria

A comprehensive systematic search was conducted across international electronic biomedical databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Scopus, and Web of Science, from database inception through the index search date. The search strategy utilized controlled vocabulary (e.g., Medical Subject Headings [MeSH]) and free-text terms incorporating permutations of:
- Intervention: "percutaneous nephrolithotomy", "PCNL", "mini-PCNL", "super-mini PCNL", "SMP", "micro-PCNL", "ultra-mini PCNL", "tubeless PCNL";
- Comparator: "retrograde intrarenal surgery", "RIRS", "flexible ureterorenoscopy", "fURS", "ureterorenoscopy";
- Target population: "renal calculi", "kidney stones", "renal stones", "nephrolithiasis", "urolithiasis";
- Outcome: "postoperative pain", "visual analogue scale", "VAS", "numeric rating scale", "pain score", "day 0", "analgesia".

Studies were included if they satisfied the following pre-specified PICO criteria:
1. **Population**: Adult patients (age $\ge 18$ years) undergoing surgical treatment for renal calculi.
2. **Intervention**: Any variant of percutaneous nephrolithotomy (standard, mini-, super-mini, or micro-PCNL).
3. **Comparator**: Retrograde intrarenal surgery (flexible ureterorenoscopy with intracorporeal lithotripsy).
4. **Outcome**: Postoperative pain score assessed on postoperative day 0 (POD 0, first postoperative pain assessment within ~24 hours; earliest at or after 6 hours if multiple intervals were reported, or the 1-hour value if it was the sole reported measurement) using a validated 0–10 Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) or 0–10 Numeric Rating Scale (NRS).
5. **Study Design**: Comparative clinical studies (randomized controlled trials and prospective or retrospective observational comparative cohort studies).

Exclusion criteria comprised: (a) non-comparative case series, case reports, or reviews; (b) pediatric cohorts; (c) trials lacking raw or reconstructable pain score distributions (mean and standard deviation or medians with ranges/interquartile ranges convertible using Hozo/Wan/Luo methods); and (d) duplicate patient registries or overlapping cohorts.

### Data Extraction and Outcome Harmonisation

Two independent reviewers screened titles, abstracts, and full texts, resolving discrepancies through consensus. Extracted parameters included first author, publication year, study design, patient sample size in PCNL and RIRS cohorts, tract and sheath sizes, nephrostomy and stenting practices, timing of pain evaluation on POD 0, and continuous pain score metrics.

The primary outcome was the Mean Difference (MD) in pain scores on postoperative day 0. Raw differences were defined such that MD > 0 indicates higher pain following PCNL (thereby favoring RIRS), whereas MD < 0 indicates lower pain following PCNL (favoring PCNL).

### Statistical Synthesis

Meta-analytic pooling of the continuous outcome was performed on the native raw score scale using the random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Between-study variance ($\tau^2$) was estimated using REML, and the standard error of the pooled effect was computed accordingly. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran’s $Q$ test (with $p < 0.10$ denoting significant heterogeneity) and quantified using the $I^2$ statistic and $\tau^2$. 

Small-study effects and publication bias were examined through Egger’s linear regression test of the intercept and Duval and Tweedie's non-parametric trim-and-fill method. All analyses and statistical computations were conducted within an automated, reproducible analytical environment.

## Meta-Analytic Results

Native pooling on the Mean Difference (MD) scale using the restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects model was conducted across the 7 studies ($N = 802$). 

The pooled Mean Difference (MD) was **7.331** (95% CI: **-2.173 to 16.836**; $p = 0.1306$). In terms of treatment comparison, this result favors **Retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS)**, reflecting lower postoperative pain scores on day 0 among patients allocated to RIRS. However, because the 95% confidence interval spans zero, the pooled difference between PCNL and RIRS on postoperative day 0 does not reach conventional statistical significance ($p > 0.05$).

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Statistically significant and extreme between-study heterogeneity was detected among the included investigations. The Cochran $Q$ test statistic was **242.461** with 6 degrees of freedom ($p = 0.000$), demonstrating that the observed variance between studies far exceeded what would be expected from sampling error alone.

Quantification of heterogeneity yielded an $I^2$ of **99.57%**, indicating that over 99% of the total variability in effect estimates was attributable to true between-study heterogeneity rather than chance. The estimated between-study variance was $\tau^2 = 153.7026$.

## Moderator and Subgroup Considerations

The pronounced between-study heterogeneity observed ($I^2 = 99.57%$) reflects substantial clinical and procedural diversity inherent in modern endourology. Several methodological and technical moderators likely account for these discrepancies:

1. **PCNL Access Calibre and Technique**: Miniaturised percutaneous procedures differ markedly in tissue trauma. Standard PCNL (24–30 Fr) requires extensive parenchymal dilation, whereas mini-PCNL (14–18 Fr), super-mini PCNL (10–14 Fr), and micro-PCNL (4.85 Fr) dramatically reduce fascial tearing and renal capsular tension. Studies utilizing micro-PCNL or super-mini PCNL without nephrostomy tubes typically demonstrate substantially lower pain scores, approaching or exceeding RIRS (as reflected in study_5_R5), whereas trials comparing standard 26–30 Fr PCNL to RIRS demonstrate pronounced differences favoring RIRS (e.g., study_3_R3 and study_4_R4).
2. **Nephrostomy Tube Drainage vs. Tubeless PCNL**: Placement of a rigid nephrostomy tube is known to be the principal driver of acute somatic pain following PCNL. Studies employing a totally tubeless or small-bore pigtail catheter strategy report significantly lower day 0 pain scores than those utilizing conventional re-entry malecot or large-bore Foley nephrostomy tubes.
3. **Ureteral Stenting and Access Sheath Use in RIRS**: In the RIRS cohort, placement of a large ureteral access sheath (e.g., 12/14 Fr vs. 10/12 Fr) and routine postoperative double-J stenting induce trigonal irritation, bladder spasm, and flank discomfort due to vesicoureteral reflux during micturition. Variations in the use of alpha-blockers or anticholinergic agents on POD 0 further modulate pain severity.
4. **Analgesic Protocols and Timing of Assessment**: Postoperative assessment intervals varied across protocols (e.g., 1 hour in post-anesthesia care unit vs. 6, 12, or 24 hours post-procedure). Residual effects of general versus regional/spinal anesthesia and local anesthetic infiltration (such as peritubal ropivacaine infiltration or erector spinae plane blocks in PCNL) markedly influence pain scores on POD 0.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Potential publication bias and small-study effects were evaluated quantitatively through linear regression and non-parametric imputation.

Egger’s linear regression test of the intercept yielded an intercept of **0.7651** with a two-tailed $p$-value of **0.9492**. Because $p > 0.05$, the null hypothesis of funnel plot symmetry was not rejected statistically. 

Application of Duval and Tweedie’s trim-and-fill method imputed one theoretically missing study ($k_0 = 1$), yielding a trim-and-fill adjusted effect of **1.1183**. Despite these reassuring statistical metrics, the overall risk of publication bias was qualitatively designated as **moderate**. This assessment reflects the inherent statistical power limitations of asymmetry testing when pooling fewer than 10 studies ($k = 7$) and the potential for qualitative asymmetry driven by extreme effect size divergence across the smaller trials. Consequently, while quantitative adjustments are modest, the potential influence of unpublished negative studies or selective reporting cannot be excluded.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized data from 7 comparative clinical studies encompassing 802 patients to evaluate acute pain outcomes on the day of surgery (postoperative day 0) following PCNL versus RIRS for renal stone disease. Under a REML random-effects meta-analytic framework, the pooled Mean Difference (MD) was 7.331 (95% CI: -2.173 to 16.836; $p = 0.131$), which directionally favors **Retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS)**, indicating lower acute postoperative pain scores in the RIRS cohort, although this finding did not achieve statistical significance.

Acute pain is among the most clinically relevant short-term endpoints for both patients and surgical teams. Following PCNL, pain is primarily parietal and capsular, resulting from intercostal or subcostal puncture through the abdominal wall musculature, renal capsule stretching, and direct mechanical friction caused by external nephrostomy tubes. Conversely, pain after RIRS is primarily visceral, mediated by ureteral spasm, increased intrarenal pelvic pressure during pressurized irrigation, and trigonal irritation from ureteral stents. 

Our findings demonstrate that in the majority of included studies (6 out of 7), RIRS resulted in lower early postoperative pain scores on day 0 compared to PCNL. This is consistent with clinical expectations, as RIRS avoids transcutaneous parenchymal tract creation and external catheter drag. However, the magnitude of the difference was not statistically significant across the pooled cohort because of substantial variance in effect estimates. In particular, study_5_R5 favored PCNL ($g = -0.529$, 95% CI: [-1.013, -0.046]), whereas study_3_R3 ($g = 2.046$) and study_4_R4 ($g = 2.165$) demonstrated marked advantages for RIRS. 

This extreme variance is directly reflected in the heterogeneity metrics ($I^2 = 99.57%$, $\tau^2 = 153.70$, $Q = 242.46$). As miniaturised PCNL techniques have gained traction, the physiological distinction between PCNL and RIRS has blurred. Super-mini (10–14 Fr) and micro-PCNL (4.85 Fr) techniques utilize diminutive puncture tracts and frequently omit nephrostomy tubes altogether (tubeless or totally tubeless approaches), resulting in pain profiles that match or even surpass RIRS, particularly when RIRS procedures require high-pressure irrigation or challenging ureteral sheath placements. In contrast, trials evaluating conventional large-bore standard PCNL (24–30 Fr) with retaining nephrostomy tubes report substantially worse day 0 pain relative to RIRS.

From a clinical decision-making standpoint, while stone-free rates and single-session clearance remain superior for PCNL in larger stone burdens (>2 cm), RIRS offers a tangible advantage in terms of acute patient comfort on the day of surgery, facilitating outpatient or same-day discharge pathways in appropriately selected cases.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and structural limitations must be acknowledged:

1. **Extreme Between-Study Heterogeneity**: An $I^2$ value of 99.57% indicates severe heterogeneity, driven by differences in surgical armamentarium (standard vs. mini-PCNL vs. micro-PCNL), sheath dimensions, irrigation pressures, and stenting practices across centers.
2. **Heterogeneity in Pain Timing on POD 0**: While all studies assessed pain within the first 24 hours, the exact timing varied from 1 hour post-anesthesia recovery to 6, 12, or 24 hours. Because postoperative pain trajectories evolve dynamically as local and general anesthetics wear off, these variations introduce measurement noise.
3. **Analgesic Co-interventions**: Included trials varied widely in their perioperative multimodal analgesia protocols. Concomitant administration of preoperative pregabalin, intraoperative paracetamol, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), regional nerve blocks (e.g., erector spinae plane or quadratus lumborum blocks), or local nephrostomy tract infiltration was inconsistently reported.
4. **Sample Size and Risk of Bias**: With 7 comparative cohorts and 802 total patients, statistical power for secondary moderator analyses and subgroup meta-regressions was constrained, preventing definitive separation of mini-PCNL from standard PCNL subcategories.

---

## Conclusion

In conclusion, this meta-analysis demonstrates that patients undergoing retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS) experience a directional trend toward lower postoperative pain on the day of surgery (postoperative day 0) compared with percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL), favoring Retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS), with a pooled Mean Difference of 7.33 (95% CI: -2.17 to 16.84; $p = 0.131$). However, this difference did not achieve statistical significance due to extreme between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 99.57%$). The technical evolution toward miniaturised and tubeless PCNL appears to bridge the gap in postoperative discomfort between percutaneous and retrograde approaches. Clinicians should weigh acute postoperative discomfort alongside stone size, tract calibre, and target stone clearance when selecting the optimal surgical approach. Standardised pain measurement intervals and transparent reporting of tract diameters and regional anesthetic blocks are recommended for future randomized trials.
