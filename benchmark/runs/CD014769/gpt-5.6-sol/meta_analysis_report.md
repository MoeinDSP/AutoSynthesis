# Statins for Primary Prevention and Deep Vein Thrombosis: A Random-Effects Meta-analysis With Unresolved Statin-Specific Subgrouping

## Abstract

**Background:** This review evaluated whether rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, or pravastatin, compared with placebo, affects the number of adults experiencing deep vein thrombosis (DVT) during primary-prevention trial follow-up. Venous thromboembolism (VTE) or a composite of “DVT or pulmonary embolism” was eligible only when a separate participant-level DVT count was unavailable.

**Methods:** The prespecified analysis was a random-effects odds-ratio synthesis, using restricted maximum likelihood (REML), conducted separately by statin type and without an overall estimate across statins. The supplied dataset identified six studies but contained three effect-size records (total N = 31,112). No bibliographic citations, treatment-to-study mapping, arm-level event counts, endpoint definitions, or statin-specific meta-analysis groups were provided.

**Results:** The supplied analysis reported one pooled **Odds Ratio (OR)** across three effects: OR = 0.4726480093266312 (95% CI 0.319 to 0.700; p = 0.0001874925940706744). Heterogeneity statistics were Q = 0.447 (p = 0.7997), I² = 0.0%, and τ² = 0.0. The supplied `favors` classification was “neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).” This estimate cannot be presented as the requested statin-specific result because the supplied data do not identify separate rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and pravastatin groups and appear to pool all three available effects. Egger’s intercept was −0.4812 (p = 0.044), while trim-and-fill imputed no studies (k0 = 0) and produced an adjusted value of −0.4368 on an unspecified standardized scale. With only three effects, publication-bias analyses are highly unstable.

**Conclusions:** The supplied numerical output suggests a ratio below 1, but its favorability was not confirmed in the supplied analysis, and the pooled estimate violates the protocol requirement to avoid combining different statins. Consequently, no valid statin-specific conclusion can be drawn. Arm-level participant counts, exact eligible outcome definitions, study identities, and statin assignments are required for a definitive analysis.

# Introduction

Venous thromboembolism (VTE), encompassing deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), is an important cause of morbidity and mortality. Statins have established cardiovascular benefits, but their potential effect on venous thrombosis remains uncertain and could differ by statin agent, dose, population, and follow-up duration.

The review question was: among adults receiving statins for primary prevention, what is the effect of rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, or pravastatin versus placebo on the number of participants experiencing DVT during follow-up? The intended outcome hierarchy was participant-level DVT where separately reported, followed—only when separate DVT data were unavailable—by participant-level VTE or the composite “DVT or pulmonary embolism,” with the exact substitute definition recorded. The prespecified effect measure was the **Odds Ratio (OR)**, pooled using a random-effects model separately for each statin. No overall estimate across statins was permitted.

# Search and Screening

## Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies were placebo-controlled randomized trials of rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, or pravastatin used for primary prevention in adults. The outcome was the number of randomized participants experiencing at least one eligible venous-thrombosis event during trial follow-up, using randomized arm denominators.

The prespecified endpoint hierarchy was:

1. A participant-level DVT count, preferably adjudicated or definite.
2. If no separate DVT count was reported, a participant-level VTE count.
3. If neither was available, a participant-level count for the composite “DVT or pulmonary embolism.”

Participants, rather than episodes, were to be counted. Explicitly reported zero-event cells were eligible. PE alone, bleeding, myopathy, mortality, and unrelated adverse events were ineligible. A trial was to be excluded from the outcome synthesis when no venous-thrombosis outcome appeared anywhere in the full report, including the main results, safety or adverse-event tables, secondary-outcome tables, and appendices.

Although the protocol states that OR < 1 would represent treatment benefit when the event is correctly coded as DVT or an eligible VTE endpoint, the information needed to verify endpoint and arm coding was not supplied.

The research question required independent random-effects analyses for rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and pravastatin, with no overall estimate across statins. # Heterogeneity

For the supplied three-effect combined analysis, Cochran’s **Q was 0.447** with **p = 0.7997**, **I² was 0.0%**, and the REML estimate of **τ² was 0.0**. These values indicate that no statistical heterogeneity was detected in the analyzed set.

This result should not be taken as evidence that the effects of rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and pravastatin are interchangeable. Heterogeneity tests have very low power with only three effects, and clinical or methodological differences may remain even when I² and τ² are estimated as zero. Moreover, pooling different statins was outside the stated protocol.

# Publication Bias

The supplied publication-bias analysis reported an Egger’s intercept of **−0.4812** with **p = 0.044**. Trim-and-fill estimated **k0 = 0**, meaning that no missing studies were imputed, and reported an adjusted value of **−0.4368** on an unspecified standardized scale. Because this adjusted value is not labeled as an Odds Ratio (OR), it should not be interpreted as an adjusted OR or compared directly with the pooled OR of 0.4726480093266312.

The supplied interpretation classified publication-bias risk as high because Egger’s test was statistically significant. This finding warrants caution, but it is not robust: regression tests for funnel-plot asymmetry are generally unreliable with very few studies, and only three effects were available here. Funnel asymmetry can also arise from chance, outcome-definition differences, selective outcome reporting, or other small-study effects rather than publication bias alone. Conversely, trim-and-fill imputing no studies does not exclude bias.

# Discussion

## Principal findings

The supplied analysis produced a statistically significant pooled Odds Ratio (OR) of 0.4726480093266312 (95% CI 0.319 to 0.700; p = 0.0001874925940706744) across three effect records and found no detectable statistical heterogeneity. Nevertheless, the supplied `favors` classification was “neither,” and the endpoint and treatment coding necessary to resolve direction were unavailable.

The central issue is not merely uncertainty around the numerical estimate. The analysis combined effects despite an explicit requirement to pool rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and pravastatin separately and not calculate an overall estimate across statins. The combined estimate is therefore not an eligible primary result for the research question. Statin-specific evidence remains unresolved.

## Clinical interpretation

If the outcome was consistently coded as the occurrence of DVT or an eligible substitute VTE endpoint, and if the treatment arm was consistently in the numerator, an OR below 1 would conventionally indicate lower event odds with statin therapy. However, those prerequisites cannot be verified from the supplied data, and the provided favorability designation must be retained. Absolute effects, such as events prevented per 1,000 treated, also cannot be calculated because control-arm event risks and arm-level counts were not supplied.

The evidence cannot establish whether any association applies equally to all three statins. Differences in pharmacologic properties, doses, study populations, and endpoint ascertainment could yield distinct effects. Separate statin-specific estimates are essential to answer the stated question.

## Certainty and applicability

A formal certainty-of-evidence assessment was not supplied and cannot be completed reliably. Certainty would likely require downgrading considerations for unverifiable outcome definitions, unavailable risk-of-bias information, inability to reproduce the ORs from participant counts, failure to preserve statin-specific groups, and possible reporting or small-study bias. Applicability to primary prevention also cannot be confirmed without trial-level population descriptions.

# Data Quality and Validation Notes

Several data-quality issues materially constrain interpretation:

1. Six studies were reported by the pipeline, but only three effect-size records were available; reasons for the discrepancy were not supplied.
2. Trial identities and statin assignments were absent, preventing the required rosuvastatin-, atorvastatin-, and pravastatin-specific analyses.
3. Exact endpoint definitions were absent, so use of DVT versus VTE versus “DVT or pulmonary embolism” could not be verified.
4. Randomized arm denominators and participant-level event counts were absent, preventing independent reconstruction of odds ratios and confirmation that participants rather than episodes were counted.
5. The listed study-level values were standardized quantities, whereas the pooled analysis was labeled Odds Ratio (OR); their relationship was not documented.
6. The metadata noted that papers explicitly reported hazard ratios, while the analysis used OR at the user’s request. Without binary counts, the native OR calculation cannot be checked.
7. The only pooled estimate appears to combine three effects, contrary to the requirement not to pool across statin types.
8. The supplied direction field classified the result as favoring neither, indicating unresolved event or arm coding despite the numerical OR being below 1.
9. The trim-and-fill adjusted value was reported on an unspecified standardized scale and is not a confirmed adjusted OR.

# Limitations

This report is limited by the incompleteness and internal inconsistency of the supplied results. Search methods, citations, selection decisions, study characteristics, outcome definitions, arm-level counts, follow-up times, and risk-of-bias judgments were unavailable. The study-level standardized entries cannot be transparently connected to the pooled OR. The use of a single pooled result across potentially different statins conflicts with the protocol and could obscure genuine between-agent differences.

Statistical limitations are also substantial. With only three effects, estimates of τ² and I² are imprecise, Cochran’s Q has low power, and Egger’s regression and trim-and-fill are not dependable. No sensitivity analyses could be conducted for outcome definition, adjudication status, zero-event handling, model choice, risk of bias, or exclusion of potentially non-primary-prevention populations. No absolute risk estimates could be derived.

Finally, the absence of bibliographic records prevents verification against full trial reports and appendices. It is therefore unknown whether separate DVT counts existed but were overlooked, whether substitute composite endpoints were used appropriately, or whether outcome reporting was selective.

# Conclusion

The supplied three-effect REML analysis reported a pooled Odds Ratio (OR) of 0.4726480093266312 (95% CI 0.319 to 0.700; p = 0.0001874925940706744), with Q = 0.447 (p = 0.7997), I² = 0.0%, and τ² = 0.0. The supplied direction classification favored neither arm, and a significant Egger’s test (intercept −0.4812; p = 0.044) raised concern about asymmetry, although publication-bias methods are unreliable with k = 3.

This pooled value cannot answer the prespecified research question because it is not separated by rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and pravastatin. No valid agent-specific estimate can be reported from the available information. A definitive analysis requires study citations, statin assignments, exact eligible endpoint definitions, participant-level event counts, and randomized arm denominators, followed by separate random-effects OR models for each statin and no overall cross-statin estimate.
