# Intranasal Corticosteroids for Persistence of Otitis Media With Effusion in Children at Up to 6 Weeks: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Otitis media with effusion (OME) is common in childhood, but the short-term effect of intranasal corticosteroids on persistence of middle-ear effusion remains uncertain.

**Objective:** To evaluate intranasal corticosteroids, with any co-treatment administered equally in eligible arms, versus no intranasal corticosteroid treatment, excluding placebo nasal-spray controls, for persistent OME at the first post-baseline assessment no later than 6 weeks after treatment initiation.

**Methods:** Eligible data concerned a dichotomous persistence outcome at the child level where available, or aggregated across both ears when only ear-level data were available. Five studies contributed five effect sizes and 757 participants. The prespecified native effect measure was the risk ratio (RR). A random-effects model estimated by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was used. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochran’s Q, I², and τ². Small-study effects were assessed with Egger’s test and trim-and-fill, recognizing the limited power of these methods with five studies.

**Results:** The pooled **Risk Ratio (RR)** was **0.6179842911219003** (95% CI **0.468 to 0.817**; p = **0.000713121416039625**; k = 5; N = 757). The supplied analysis classified favorability as **“neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted)”**; this designation is retained rather than independently inferring direction from the numerical RR. Heterogeneity was moderate to substantial (I² = **61.29%**; τ² = **0.056378**; Q = **10.188**, p = **0.0374**). Egger’s intercept was **−0.2083** (p = **0.5896**), and trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀ = **0**); the supplied publication-bias classification was low risk.

**Conclusions:** The native-scale synthesis produced a statistically significant pooled RR below 1, but the supplied direction field did not assign the result to either arm. Consequently, the magnitude and precision of the pooled association can be reported confidently, whereas the clinical direction should be verified against the underlying event coding before drawing a treatment-benefit conclusion. Heterogeneity and the small number of studies further limit certainty.

## Introduction

Otitis media with effusion (OME) is characterized by middle-ear fluid without the acute inflammatory manifestations of acute otitis media. Persistent effusion may be associated with conductive hearing impairment and concern about communication or developmental consequences, although its natural history often includes spontaneous resolution. Intranasal corticosteroids have been proposed as a non-surgical treatment, particularly when nasal inflammation or adenoidal disease may contribute to Eustachian-tube dysfunction.

This review addressed the following question: among children with OME, what is the effect of an intranasal corticosteroid—with any co-treatment administered equally in both eligible arms—versus no intranasal steroid treatment (either no treatment or the same co-treatment alone), excluding placebo nasal-spray controls, on persistence of middle-ear effusion at the first post-baseline assessment occurring no later than 6 weeks after treatment initiation?

The prespecified outcome was dichotomous persistence of OME. Eligible definitions included effusion still present; a type-B tympanogram; type-C2 or another non-type-A tympanogram when explicitly defined by investigators as failure; not cured; no improvement; or persistent effusion. Resolution included a type-A tympanogram or complete clearance. Persistence could be derived as the randomized or analyzed total minus the number resolved when only resolution or improvement was reported. Child-level observations were preferred; if only ear-level results were available, the intended approach was to aggregate events and totals over both ears rather than select one ear.

- **k = 5 studies**

The confidence interval excluded the null RR of 1, and the model-based test was statistically significant.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was statistically significant and moderate to substantial:

- **Cochran’s Q = 10.188**
- **Q-test p = 0.0374**
- **I² = 61.29%**
- **τ² = 0.056378**

The Q test rejects exact homogeneity at the 0.05 level, while I² indicates that a meaningful proportion of observed variability was attributable to between-study differences rather than sampling error alone. The REML random-effects model was therefore appropriate for allowing underlying effects to vary across studies. Nevertheless, with only five studies, estimates of I² and τ² are imprecise, and no prediction interval was supplied.

Potential sources of heterogeneity could include corticosteroid molecule and dose, adherence, age, unilateral versus bilateral OME, baseline duration or severity, co-treatment, outcome definition, child-level versus ear-level analysis, and exact timing of the assessment. The supplied study characteristics were insufficient to evaluate these explanations.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **−0.2083** with **p = 0.5896**. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0** missing studies and reported an adjusted standardized value of **−0.5526**. The supplied overall publication-bias classification was **low risk**.

These findings provide no statistical evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or missing studies. They do not establish that reporting bias is absent: with only five studies, Egger’s test has low power, funnel-plot methods are difficult to interpret, and trim-and-fill depends on assumptions that may be violated when genuine heterogeneity exists. The publication-bias findings should therefore be regarded as reassuring but weak.

## Discussion

This synthesis included five studies and 757 participants evaluating short-term persistence of OME after initiation of an intranasal corticosteroid compared with no intranasal steroid. The REML random-effects analysis yielded a pooled RR of **0.6179842911219003** (95% CI **0.468 to 0.817**), with a statistically significant p value of **0.000713121416039625**. The numerical estimate was accompanied by statistically significant heterogeneity (**Q = 10.188**, p = **0.0374**) and **I² = 61.29%**.

The principal interpretive issue is direction. The outcome specification treats persistence as the event and states that an RR below 1 favors intranasal corticosteroids. In contrast, the supplied meta-analysis output explicitly classifies favorability as neither arm or as dependent on event coding. Because the reporting instructions require use of the supplied `favors` field rather than independent inference from the numerical estimate, the present report preserves the non-directional designation. This conflict should be resolved by checking, for every study, whether events represent persistence or resolution and whether treatment and control numerators and denominators were entered in the intended order.

The study-level Hedges’ g displays should not be substituted for the pooled native-scale result. Their negative signs under a convention in which positive values favor intranasal steroids add to the need for coding verification, particularly because the native pooled RR is below 1. A transparent audit should reproduce the 2 × 2 table for each trial from source reports, document any derivation of persistence from resolution, identify child- versus ear-level data, and confirm that both ears—not a selectively chosen ear—were included where necessary.

The observed heterogeneity suggests that a single common effect would not adequately describe all settings. Without study-level clinical and methodological details, it is impossible to determine whether heterogeneity reflects genuine differences in treatment response, varying definitions of persistence, differences in exact follow-up time, or analytic choices. Likewise, the absence of detected publication bias is not decisive given the small evidence base.

From a clinical perspective, this analysis concerns only anatomical persistence of effusion during the first 6 weeks. It does not directly establish effects on hearing, symptoms, language development, quality of life, recurrence, longer-term clearance, or harms. Those outcomes were outside the specified review question and should not be inferred from the pooled RR.

## Data Quality and Validation Notes

No formal `validation_flags` field was supplied. Nevertheless, the provided results contain issues requiring explicit validation:

1. **Direction inconsistency:** the prespecified outcome is persistence and the pooled RR is below 1, but the supplied `favors` field assigns the result to neither arm or indicates uncertain event coding.
2. **Metric inconsistency at different reporting levels:** individual effects were supplied as Hedges’ g, whereas the meta-analysis used native RR pooling. These metrics must not be conflated.
3. **Unavailable arm-level data:** event counts and denominators by randomized arm were not provided, preventing independent reconstruction of study RRs and confirmation of event coding.
4. **Unavailable study metadata:** exact follow-up times, outcome definitions, intervention regimens, co-treatments, and unit of analysis were not supplied.
5. **Unavailable review-process data:** database searches, screening counts, exclusion reasons, reviewer procedures, and trial-level risk-of-bias assessments were absent.

These are not evidence that the numerical analysis is incorrect, but they materially constrain reproducibility and directional interpretation.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations.

First, only aggregate pipeline results were available. The lack of arm-level events and totals means that the pooled RR, its standard error, and each study’s contribution could not be independently verified. Second, no bibliographic or design information was provided for the five studies, precluding assessment of clinical comparability, randomization quality, missing outcome data, selective reporting, and other trial-level biases. Third, the direction field conflicts with the expected interpretation of a persistence RR; the treatment implication must remain unresolved pending a coding audit.

Fourth, heterogeneity was meaningful (**I² = 61.29%**) but could not be investigated through credible subgroup analyses or meta-regression. Fifth, publication-bias procedures have very low power with only five studies and may be distorted by heterogeneity. Sixth, no prediction interval was provided, so the likely range of effects in a new setting cannot be quantified. Seventh, the review is restricted to the first assessment within 6 weeks and does not address durability or patient-centered outcomes. Finally, incomplete reporting of search and screening procedures prevents full appraisal of review comprehensiveness and strict replication of the evidence-identification process.

## Conclusion

Across five studies involving **757 participants**, the REML random-effects model yielded a pooled **Risk Ratio (RR) of 0.6179842911219003** (95% CI **0.468 to 0.817**; p = **0.000713121416039625**) for short-term persistence of OME. Heterogeneity was moderate to substantial (**I² = 61.29%**, **τ² = 0.056378**), and statistical tests did not identify publication bias, although their power was limited.

The supplied analysis classified the result as favoring neither arm or as directionally dependent on event coding. Therefore, despite the statistically significant pooled estimate, a definitive statement that intranasal corticosteroids reduce or increase persistence should not be made until the underlying event and arm coding are verified. Future reporting should provide trial-level 2 × 2 data, exact assessment times, intervention details, risk-of-bias judgments, and complete PRISMA search and selection information.
