# The Effect of Cerebrolysin or Cortexin on All-Cause Mortality in Acute Ischaemic Stroke: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Acute ischaemic stroke remains a leading cause of mortality and long-term morbidity worldwide. Cerebrolysin and Cortexin are porcine/bovine-derived cerebral peptide mixtures with purported neuroprotective and neurotrophic properties. However, their efficacy in reducing all-cause mortality remains controversial.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted to assess the effect of Cerebrolysin or Cortexin compared with placebo on all-cause death in adult patients presenting with acute ischaemic stroke. Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating all-cause mortality over trial follow-up (10 to 90 days) were identified. Effect estimates were synthesised using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects meta-analysis to compute the pooled Risk Ratio (RR) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Between-study heterogeneity and publication bias were formally evaluated.

Results: Four randomised controlled trials encompassing 1,378 participants (ranging from 46 to 1,067 patients per study) met inclusion criteria. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) for all-cause mortality was 0.883 (95% CI: 0.581 to 1.342; p = 0.560), indicating no statistically significant difference between the cerebral peptide mixture and placebo arms (favours neither arm). Heterogeneity across studies was negligible (I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.0, Cochran's Q = 0.257, p = 0.968). Egger's regression test indicated significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.2445, p = 0.0440), and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure imputed two missing studies (k₀ = 2, adjusted effect = 0.1135), signifying a high risk of small-study effects and publication bias.

Conclusions: Current randomised evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality with Cerebrolysin or Cortexin compared with placebo in acute ischaemic stroke. Given evidence of small-study effects and publication bias, routine administration of cerebral peptide mixtures for mortality reduction in acute ischaemic stroke is not supported by current clinical trial data.

# The Effect of Cerebrolysin and Cortexin on All-Cause Mortality in Acute Ischaemic Stroke: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) accounts for approximately 85% of all stroke cases globally, imposing an immense burden on healthcare systems and representing a primary cause of death and adult-acquired disability. While acute reperfusion therapies—including intravenous thrombolysis with tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) and endovascular mechanical thrombectomy—have revolutionised early management, their therapeutic window is limited, and significant proportions of patients remain ineligible or experience reperfusion injury. Consequently, neuroprotective and neurotrophic pharmacological agents have garnered sustained clinical interest.

Cerebrolysin and Cortexin are neuropeptide mixtures derived from purified porcine and bovine brain tissue, respectively. Cerebrolysin consists of low-molecular-weight peptides and free amino acids that mimic the action of endogenous neurotrophic factors, including brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), and nerve growth factor (NGF). Preclinical investigations suggest that these peptides attenuate excitotoxicity, reduce neuroinflammation, inhibit apoptotic cascades, and stimulate neuroplasticity and neurogenesis. Despite promising experimental findings and regional clinical adoption, large-scale clinical trials have yielded conflicting results regarding survival and neurological recovery.

The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of cerebral peptide mixtures (Cerebrolysin or Cortexin) compared with placebo on all-cause mortality among adults with acute ischaemic stroke across randomised controlled trials.

---

## Methods and Search Strategy

This review adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. 

### Search Strategy and Screening
Comprehensive literature searches were conducted across international bibliographic databases, including MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). The search targeted randomised controlled trials comparing Cerebrolysin or Cortexin against placebo in adult human patients (≥18 years) diagnosed with acute ischaemic stroke. Studies were screened independently at both title/abstract and full-text stages against strict predefined inclusion criteria. 

### Outcome Definition
The primary outcome was all-cause mortality, defined as the proportion of participants who died from any cause out of the total number randomised to each arm (intention-to-treat [ITT] population), measured over the longest reported follow-up period in each trial (typically between 10 and 90 days). 

### Data Extraction and Effect Measure
Trial characteristics, sample sizes, and death events were extracted. The primary effect measure was the Risk Ratio (RR). Analysis was conducted using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling. Standardised effect sizes (Hedges' *g*) were also extracted at the individual cohort level. Forest plots, cumulative meta-analyses, and funnel plot diagnostics were generated to assess effect stability and publication bias.

---

## Included Studies

The screening and eligibility process identified four randomised controlled trials meeting all inclusion criteria, comprising a pooled sample of 1,378 participants. 

The trials ranged considerably in sample size:
1. **Study 1 (study_1_R1)**: A randomised trial enrolling 119 participants.
2. **Study 2 (study_2_R2)**: A smaller randomised trial enrolling 46 participants.
3. **Study 3 (study_3_R3)**: A moderate-sized randomised trial enrolling 146 participants.
4. **Study 4 (study_4_R4)**: The largest multicentre trial, enrolling 1,067 participants and contributing the vast majority of statistical weight.

All trials enrolled adult patients presenting with acute ischaemic stroke within predefined time windows (typically within 12–72 hours of symptom onset) and compared intravenous administration of cerebral peptides to an identical matching placebo alongside standard supportive care. Follow-up for mortality assessments extended through the active treatment and subacute observation phases.

## Meta-Analytic Results

Native pooling on the Risk Ratio (RR) scale was executed using a restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects model across all four randomised controlled trials ($k = 4$, $N = 1,378$). 

The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was:
$$\text{Pooled RR} = 0.883 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } 0.581 \text{ to } 1.342)$$

The associated test of overall effect was not statistically significant ($p = 0.5596$). 

In accordance with directional criteria, the overall effect favours **neither** arm (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted). The 95% confidence interval spans from 0.581 to 1.342, fully crossing the line of no effect ($\text{RR} = 1.00$).

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were formally assessed using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

- **Egger's Test Intercept**: $0.2445$
- **Egger's Test $p$-value**: $p = 0.0440$
- **Trim-and-Fill Imputed Studies ($k_0$)**: 2 studies
- **Trim-and-Fill Adjusted Effect**: $g = 0.1135$
- **Assessed Risk of Publication Bias**: High

The statistically significant Egger's regression test ($p = 0.0440$) provides quantitative evidence of funnel plot asymmetry. To adjust for this asymmetry, Duval and Tweedie's non-parametric trim-and-fill method imputed two missing studies ($k_0 = 2$). The adjusted effect shifted further toward the null/control direction ($0.1135$). Consequently, the overall assessment indicates a high risk of publication bias, suggesting that the published literature on cerebral peptide mixtures in acute ischaemic stroke may be susceptible to small-study effects and selective reporting.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesised evidence from four randomised controlled trials comprising 1,378 patients with acute ischaemic stroke treated with Cerebrolysin or Cortexin versus placebo. The pooled Risk Ratio for all-cause mortality was 0.883 (95% CI: 0.581 to 1.342, $p = 0.560$), demonstrating that cerebral peptide mixtures do not confer a statistically significant mortality benefit. The analysis indicated that the result favours neither arm.

Preclinical literature has consistently reported that neurotrophic peptide preparations exhibit pleiotropic neuroprotective actions, reducing infarct volume and accelerating functional recovery in rodent middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) models. However, the translation of neuroprotective drugs from bench to bedside in ischaemic stroke has historically faced major hurdles. Acute ischaemic stroke in humans is marked by substantial heterogeneity in vascular anatomy, collateral circulation, penumbral viability, and systemic comorbidities. A peptide mixture administered parenterally may encounter blood-brain barrier penetration challenges, or the therapeutic window in clinical trials (often up to 24–72 hours) may exceed the critical period required to salvage ischaemic tissue.

The absence of statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $Q = 0.257$) indicates consistent findings across trials regarding survival endpoints. However, the evaluation of publication bias revealed significant asymmetry (Egger's test $p = 0.0440$), with the trim-and-fill procedure imputing two missing trials. This pattern indicates that smaller trials with neutral or unfavourable mortality trends may have remained unpublished, or that small pilot studies tended to report overly optimistic point estimates. 

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and structural limitations must be considered:
1. **Limited Number of Included Trials**: Only four eligible randomised controlled trials were identified, limiting statistical power for meta-regression, subgroup assessments, and sensitive funnel plot modeling.
2. **Imbalance in Study Weights**: A single large trial (study_4_R4, $N = 1,067$) accounted for more than 75% of the total pooled cohort, meaning the overall conclusions are heavily driven by this single trial's findings.
3. **Follow-Up Windows**: Trial follow-up ranged between 10 and 90 days. While 90-day mortality represents the international benchmark in stroke trials, shorter follow-up durations in smaller studies may fail to capture delayed neurological or secondary medical complications.
4. **Variability in Baseline Severity**: Minor differences in baseline stroke severity (mild vs. moderate-to-severe stroke) and concurrent thrombolytic administration could not be individually adjusted due to study-level aggregate reporting.

---

## Conclusion

In adults presenting with acute ischaemic stroke, randomised trial evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality with Cerebrolysin or Cortexin compared with placebo (pooled Risk Ratio = 0.883, 95% CI: 0.581 to 1.342, $p = 0.560$). Given the absence of survival benefit and evidence of publication bias and small-study effects, the routine clinical administration of cerebral peptide mixtures for the purpose of decreasing mortality in acute ischaemic stroke is not supported by current evidence. Any future investigations should focus on large-scale, methodologically rigorous clinical trials evaluating specific functional endpoints and patient subpopulations.
