# Medium-Dose Inhaled Corticosteroid Plus Long-Acting β2-Agonist Versus the Same Dose of Inhaled Corticosteroid Alone for Uncontrolled Asthma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

### Background
For adolescents and adults whose asthma remains uncontrolled with medium-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), adding a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) in a single inhaler may reduce exacerbations. This review evaluated the participant-level risk of experiencing at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation during randomised treatment.

### Methods
Eligible comparisons evaluated medium-dose ICS/LABA in one inhaler against the same medium-dose ICS alone. The intended outcome was the number of participants with at least one exacerbation requiring systemic corticosteroids, an emergency visit, or hospitalisation during the full randomised treatment period. Risk ratios (RRs) were pooled with a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) model. The supplied pipeline contained 12 studies and seven effect sizes suitable for pooling.

### Results
Seven comparisons involving 4,677 participants contributed to the meta-analysis. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 0.668324467485099 (95% CI 0.522 to 0.856; p=0.00144565386954686). Heterogeneity was low to moderate (I²=30.81%; τ²=0.028803; Q=7.596, p=0.2692). The supplied direction field classified the result as “neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).” This classification creates an important interpretive inconsistency because the prespecified event was an adverse outcome and the protocol stated that RR below 1 represents benefit with ICS/LABA. Egger’s test did not identify statistically significant asymmetry (intercept −0.7493; p=0.3395), although trim-and-fill estimated one potentially missing study; publication-bias risk was therefore rated moderate.

### Conclusions
The pooled estimate was below 1 and statistically significant, but the supplied favourability classification did not assign the result to either treatment arm because of uncertainty in event coding. Accordingly, the numerical result is consistent with a lower event risk under the prespecified adverse-event definition, but the clinical direction should be confirmed against original arm-level event counts before a definitive treatment-favouring conclusion is adopted. Incomplete search, screening, study-characteristic, risk-of-bias, and arm-level data further limit certainty.

## Search/Screening

The review question was framed around adolescents and adults with asthma remaining uncontrolled on medium-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS). The eligible intervention was medium-dose ICS plus a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) delivered in a single inhaler. The comparator was the same medium-dose ICS at the same dose without LABA. The prespecified outcome was the proportion of randomised or analysed participants experiencing at least one moderate or severe asthma exacerbation during the complete randomised treatment period. Moderate or severe exacerbations were defined as those requiring systemic corticosteroids, an emergency visit, or hospitalisation; when only severe exacerbations were reported, or several definitions were available, the definition closest to moderate-or-severe exacerbation was intended to be used.

The supplied pipeline summary identified 12 studies and seven effect sizes. Thus, seven comparisons were quantitatively synthesised, while five identified studies did not contribute a pooled effect. ## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) pooled seven comparisons and 4,677 participants. The **pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 0.668324467485099 (95% CI 0.522 to 0.856; p=0.00144565386954686)**.

The supplied `favors` field classified this finding as **“neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).”** This supplied classification is retained rather than independently inferring favourability from the magnitude of the RR. It is nevertheless inconsistent with the prespecified outcome coding, under which the counted event was at least one moderate or severe exacerbation and RR below 1 was defined as favouring ICS/LABA. Resolution requires verification of the original arm-level counts and confirmation that events, rather than event-free participants, were entered in every comparison.

## Heterogeneity

Observed heterogeneity was low to moderate:

- I²=30.81%
- τ²=0.028803 on the reported analysis scale
- Cochran’s Q=7.596 with 6 degrees of freedom
- Q-test p=0.2692

The Q test did not reject homogeneity at the conventional 0.05 threshold. Its power is limited with only seven comparisons, however, and the non-zero τ² and I² indicate some between-study variability. Potential sources include differences in treatment duration, exacerbation definitions, asthma severity, age distribution, inhaler formulation, adherence, and trial conduct, but these possibilities could not be tested with the available study characteristics.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of −0.7493 with p=0.3395. This result was not statistically significant and did not provide strong evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. With only seven comparisons, however, asymmetry testing has limited power and a non-significant result does not establish the absence of reporting bias.

Trim-and-fill estimated one potentially missing study (`k0=1`). The supplied adjusted value was −0.1315, but it was labelled `trim_fill_g` rather than RR. It therefore cannot be treated as an adjusted pooled RR or compared directly with the native pooled RR of 0.668324467485099. On the basis of the non-significant Egger test, the trim-and-fill indication of one potentially missing study, and the small evidence base, the supplied publication-bias assessment was **moderate risk**.

No funnel-plot path was included among the supplied figures. Accordingly, visual symmetry could not be reviewed from the available outputs.

## Discussion

This synthesis addressed whether adding LABA to the same medium dose of ICS in one inhaler changes the participant-level risk of at least one moderate or severe exacerbation among adolescents and adults whose asthma remains uncontrolled on medium-dose ICS. Seven comparisons involving 4,677 participants produced a pooled RR of 0.668324467485099, with a 95% CI excluding 1 and p=0.00144565386954686. Statistical heterogeneity was limited (I²=30.81%), and the Q test was not statistically significant.

The magnitude of the pooled ratio is potentially clinically important. Nevertheless, treatment direction cannot be stated without qualification because the supplied result metadata classified the effect as favouring neither arm, despite the protocol defining an RR below 1 for an adverse event as favouring ICS/LABA. This discordance may reflect uncertain event coding, a mismatch between reported odds ratios and requested RRs, or a data-processing inconsistency. The original participant event counts and denominators are needed to resolve it.

The outcome was appropriately conceived as a participant-level dichotomous endpoint rather than an exacerbation rate. This distinction matters because recurrent event rates per person-year and risks of experiencing at least one event answer different questions. However, the absence of arm-level counts prevents confirmation that all studies consistently contributed participant risks rather than rates, odds, or transformed summary statistics.

Moderate publication-bias risk also warrants caution. Egger’s test was not significant, but only seven comparisons were available, and trim-and-fill suggested one potentially missing study. Trim-and-fill is itself assumption-dependent, and its supplied adjusted statistic was not on a confirmed RR scale. It should therefore be treated as a sensitivity signal rather than a corrected primary estimate.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

Although no separate `validation_flags` object was supplied, several explicit data-quality issues are evident in the provided results:

1. The pipeline listed 12 studies but only seven pooled effect sizes; exclusion or non-contribution reasons for five studies were not reported.
2. The source papers reportedly presented odds ratios, whereas the requested and final pooled measure was RR. The participant counts needed to verify native RR calculation were unavailable.
3. Study-level values were stored in a field labelled `hedges_g`, while the meta-analysis was labelled as native RR pooling. These scales cannot be reconciled from the supplied data.
4. The pooled result’s `favors` field classified the finding as favouring neither arm, in conflict with the prespecified adverse-event coding in which RR below 1 represents fewer exacerbations with ICS/LABA.
5. The trim-and-fill adjusted statistic was labelled as `trim_fill_g`, not RR, and is not directly interpretable on the requested native scale.
6. Search methods, screening counts, study characteristics, arm-level outcome data, trial-level risk-of-bias judgments, and certainty-of-evidence ratings were not available.

These issues do not change the exact reported pooled statistic, but they materially restrict reproducibility and confidence in its clinical interpretation.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations. First, the search and selection process was incompletely documented, preventing confirmation that the evidence base was comprehensive and PRISMA-compliant. Second, five of the 12 identified studies did not contribute to the synthesis for unspecified reasons, creating a possible selection concern. Third, original arm-level event counts and denominators were unavailable, so intention-to-treat status, event coding, continuity corrections, and the RR calculations could not be verified.

Fourth, there was an unresolved mismatch between odds ratios reportedly presented in source papers and the requested RR synthesis. Fifth, study-level effects were stored under a standardized-effect label that does not match the native ratio measure, and the favourability classification conflicted with the prespecified event direction. Sixth, no trial characteristics or risk-of-bias assessments were supplied, precluding examination of clinical heterogeneity, methodological quality, and applicability. Seventh, publication-bias analyses were underpowered because only seven comparisons were available. Finally, no GRADE assessment was possible; the certainty of evidence should therefore not be assumed to be high solely because the pooled confidence interval excluded the null.

## Conclusion

Across seven comparisons and 4,677 participants, the REML random-effects meta-analysis yielded a pooled **Risk Ratio (RR) of 0.668324467485099 (95% CI 0.522 to 0.856; p=0.00144565386954686)**, with low-to-moderate heterogeneity (I²=30.81%; τ²=0.028803; Q=7.596, p=0.2692). The supplied direction metadata classified the result as favouring neither arm. Because this conflicts with the prespecified adverse-event definition and the stated interpretation of RR below 1, original arm-level data should be checked before concluding definitively that medium-dose ICS/LABA reduces exacerbations compared with the same dose of ICS alone. Publication-bias risk was rated moderate, and incomplete reporting of search methods, study characteristics, risk of bias, and effect derivation further limits confidence.
