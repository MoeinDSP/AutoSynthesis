# Antifibrinolytic drugs for prevention of haematoma expansion after acute spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage: a systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis

## Abstract

## Abstract

**Background:** Early intracerebral haematoma expansion is associated with poor outcome after spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH). Antifibrinolytic drugs may limit expansion by stabilising clot formation.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of antifibrinolytic treatment, compared with placebo or open no-drug control, on dichotomous haematoma expansion at approximately 24 hours in adults with acute spontaneous ICH.

**Methods:** Eligible comparisons involved tranexamic acid, aminocaproic acid, or a similar antifibrinolytic versus placebo or no drug. The prespecified outcome was haematoma expansion on the primary repeat brain scan at or around 24 hours, according to each trial’s definition. The requested effect measure was the risk ratio (RR), pooled with a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) model. Five effect estimates from six studies in the supplied evidence set contributed to the quantitative synthesis; the pooled analysis included 2,683 participants. Publication bias was assessed using Egger’s regression and trim-and-fill.

**Results:** The pooled RR was **0.8824566891353104** (95% CI **0.780 to 0.999**; p = **0.0474049441221533**; k = **5**; n = **2,683**). Heterogeneity was not detected (I² = **0.0%**; τ² = **0.0**; Q = **0.642**, p = **0.9583**). Although the numerical RR was below 1, the supplied directional classification designated the result as favouring **neither arm**. Egger’s intercept was **−0.1333** (p = **0.7184**), and trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀ = **0**), indicating no detected small-study asymmetry, albeit with low power because only five estimates were available.

**Conclusions:** The synthesis produced a borderline pooled association with an RR close to 1 and no detected between-study heterogeneity. Under the supplied result metadata, the finding favoured neither antifibrinolytic therapy nor control. Interpretation is limited by the small number of studies, unavailable study-level event counts and bibliographic details, inability to verify intention-to-treat denominators and outcome definitions from the supplied data, and an effect-measure conflict noting that source reports explicitly presented odds ratios whereas the requested synthesis used RRs.

# Introduction

Spontaneous, non-traumatic intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH) is a severe form of stroke with high early mortality and disability. Enlargement of the initial haematoma during the first hours after onset is a potentially modifiable process associated with neurological deterioration and adverse functional outcome. Antifibrinolytic agents, including tranexamic acid and aminocaproic acid, inhibit fibrin degradation and may stabilise the haemostatic plug at sites of active bleeding. Their principal hypothesised early benefit in ICH is therefore a reduction in haematoma expansion.

This review addressed the following question: among adults with acute spontaneous ICH, what is the effect of an antifibrinolytic drug, compared with placebo or open no-drug control, on the risk of intracerebral haematoma expansion on repeat brain imaging at or around 24 hours after the baseline scan?

The prespecified dichotomous outcome was expansion on the primary repeat scan, using each trial’s reported definition—commonly at least 33% relative growth, approximately 6 mL absolute growth, or a composite of relative and absolute criteria. The intended extraction rule was to use the number with expansion and the intention-to-treat randomised denominator in each eligible arm, rather than percentages restricted to participants with available follow-up imaging. Later imaging, 7-day or 90-day outcomes, and reports limited to mean volume change were not eligible substitutes.

- **p = 0.7184**

This test did not detect funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0** missing studies. These findings should be interpreted cautiously. With only five estimates, Egger’s test has low power, and trim-and-fill can fail to identify selection processes. A non-significant asymmetry test does not prove that publication bias or selective outcome reporting is absent.

# Discussion

## Principal findings

Across five comparisons involving 2,683 participants, the REML random-effects analysis yielded a pooled RR of **0.8824566891353104** (95% CI **0.780 to 0.999**; p = **0.0474049441221533**) for haematoma expansion at approximately 24 hours. Statistical heterogeneity was not detected (I² = **0.0%**, τ² = **0.0**), and formal analyses did not identify small-study asymmetry.

The estimate was close to the null and its upper confidence limit was 0.999. The supplied result metadata classified the result as favouring neither arm; that classification is retained here. Even if one focuses on the numerical ratio, the borderline precision means that small changes in event classification, denominator choice, continuity handling, or effect-measure reconstruction could affect conventional statistical significance.

## Clinical interpretation

Limiting early haematoma expansion is biologically plausible and potentially clinically meaningful, but an imaging effect does not necessarily translate into improved survival or functional outcome. This review evaluated only the specified approximately 24-hour imaging endpoint. It did not assess death, disability, thromboembolic complications, seizures, or other adverse events and therefore cannot establish the overall clinical benefit–harm balance of antifibrinolytic treatment.

Variation in expansion definitions may also affect absolute event frequencies and relative estimates. Although combining trial-specific definitions can be reasonable when they capture the same underlying construct, unavailable study-level definitions prevented examination of whether thresholds based on relative growth, absolute growth, or composite criteria produced consistent findings.

## Certainty and robustness

The lack of detected heterogeneity supports statistical consistency but does not by itself establish high certainty. Confidence is reduced by the small number of contributing studies, dominance of the evidence base by one large comparison (n = **2,325** of **2,683** participants), missing trial-level extraction details, and the recorded conflict between requested RR pooling and odds ratios explicitly reported in source paper(s). No sensitivity analyses using odds ratios, fixed-effect models, alternative τ² estimators, leave-one-out exclusion, or different denominator assumptions were supplied.

# Limitations

1. **Incomplete search documentation:** No database strategy, search dates, registry search, language policy, duplicate-screening method, or complete PRISMA flow was provided.
2. **Missing bibliographic information:** Study authors, titles, years, and publication details were unavailable, preventing conventional study identification and citation.
3. **Unavailable 2×2 data:** Events and randomised participants by arm were not provided, so study-level RRs, intention-to-treat denominators, zero-event handling, and percentage-to-count conversions could not be verified.
4. **Unclear non-contribution of one study:** Six studies were identified, but only five estimates were pooled; the reason the sixth did not contribute is unknown.
5. **Outcome-definition uncertainty:** The exact trial-specific definitions and timing of repeat imaging were unavailable.
6. **Effect-measure conflict:** The requested synthesis used RRs, while a validation field states that source paper(s) explicitly reported odds ratios. No alternative odds-ratio analysis was supplied.
7. **No risk-of-bias assessment:** Randomisation, allocation concealment, blinding, missing imaging, selective reporting, and other bias domains could not be evaluated.
8. **Limited power for heterogeneity and reporting-bias tests:** With k = 5, estimates of heterogeneity are uncertain and Egger’s test is underpowered.
9. **Concentration of information:** One comparison contributed 2,325 participants, approximately 86.7% of the total analysed sample, so the pooled estimate is likely strongly influenced by that study.
10. **Surrogate endpoint:** Haematoma expansion is an imaging outcome; this analysis does not determine whether treatment improves patient-important functional or survival outcomes.

# Conclusion

In the supplied evidence set, five comparisons involving 2,683 participants yielded a random-effects pooled **Risk Ratio of 0.8824566891353104** (95% CI **0.780 to 0.999**; p = **0.0474049441221533**) for haematoma expansion at approximately 24 hours. No statistical heterogeneity was detected (I² = **0.0%**; τ² = **0.0**), and no small-study asymmetry was identified, although both assessments were limited by the small number of studies. The supplied directional metadata classified the pooled finding as favouring **neither arm**. Given the borderline estimate, absent arm-level event data, incomplete study documentation, and the RR-versus-OR validation conflict, the result should be interpreted cautiously and confirmed through transparent extraction of trial-level intention-to-treat event counts and sensitivity analyses on both ratio scales.
