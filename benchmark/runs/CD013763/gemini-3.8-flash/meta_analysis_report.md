# Efficacy of Ultrasound-Guided Erector Spinae Plane Block Compared with No Block on 24-Hour Postoperative Opioid Consumption in Adult Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Postoperative pain control remains a crucial determinant of surgical recovery, patient satisfaction, and complication rates. While systemic opioids serve as the conventional mainstay for postoperative analgesia, their dose-dependent adverse effects—including respiratory depression, postoperative nausea and vomiting, sedation, and potential for chronic opioid dependence—have accelerated the adoption of regional anaesthetic techniques. Ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (ESPB) is an interfascial plane block targeting spinal nerves that has gained rapid traction across thoracic, abdominal, and spinal surgical procedures. 

Objectives: To evaluate the analgesic efficacy of ultrasound-guided ESPB compared with no block (general anaesthesia and standard postoperative analgesia only) on cumulative 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption in adult surgical patients.

Methods: A systematic literature search was conducted across international databases to identify randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating single-shot or continuous ultrasound-guided ESPB versus no block in adult patients undergoing surgery. The primary outcome was cumulative postoperative opioid consumption at 24 hours, measured in oral morphine equivalents (OME) or equivalent systemic metric. Data extraction and critical appraisal adhered to PRISMA recommendations. Random-effects meta-analysis was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to calculate the pooled Mean Difference (MD) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Between-study heterogeneity was quantified via the Cochran Q test, I² statistic, and tau-squared (τ²). Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed through Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie’s trim-and-fill method.

Results: Seventeen independent trials comprising 1,006 adult participants (ESPB: n = 503; control: n = 503) met inclusion criteria and provided quantitative effect size data. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) indicated an overall reduction favoring ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (ESPB) of -57.06 mg [95% CI: -116.40 to 2.28 mg], though the omnibus test fell just short of traditional statistical significance (p = 0.0595). Substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed across the included trials (Q = 837.38, df = 16, p < 0.0001; I² = 99.99%; τ² = 15485.12). Egger's regression test demonstrated significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 2.0046, p < 0.0001), signalling high risk of publication bias or small-study effects, whereas the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating that asymmetry may predominantly reflect severe between-study clinical and methodological diversity.

Conclusions: Ultrasound-guided ESPB demonstrates a substantial clinical trend toward reducing cumulative 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption compared to no block; however, extreme heterogeneity across surgical types, baseline surgical trauma, and varied opioid unit scales prevented the pooled mean difference from achieving statistical significance at the conventional alpha level. Standardisation of surgical indications, local anaesthetic regimens, and unified opioid consumption reporting metrics is required to confirm the precise magnitude of the opioid-sparing benefit.

# Efficacy of Ultrasound-Guided Erector Spinae Plane Block on Cumulative 24-Hour Postoperative Opioid Consumption in Adults Undergoing Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

In modern surgical and anaesthetic practice, multimodal analgesia has emerged as the paradigm of choice to mitigate surgical stress, facilitate early postoperative mobilization, and prevent chronic postsurgical pain syndromes. Opioids remain a central component of acute postsurgical pain protocols, but their administration is inextricably linked to clinically significant adverse events, including respiratory depression, sedation, postoperative ileus, urinary retention, and postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). Furthermore, excessive acute postoperative opioid exposure has been implicated in persistent postsurgical opioid use and escalating healthcare costs. Consequently, regional anaesthetic techniques that provide somatic and visceral neural blockade while reducing systemic opioid dependence are an area of intense clinical investigation.

First described by Forero and colleagues in 2016 for the management of thoracic neuropathic pain, the ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (ESPB) is an interfascial plane injection placed deep to the erector spinae muscle complex and superficial to the transverse processes of the vertebrae. Local anaesthetic administered in this plane disperses cranio-caudally over multiple dermatomes, penetrating anteriorly across the intertransverse connective tissue into the paravertebral and epidural spaces to block both dorsal and ventral rami of spinal nerves as well as sympathetic fibers. Because the injection site is distant from major vascular structures, pleura, and neuraxial canal, ESPB offers a potentially advantageous safety profile compared to thoracic epidural analgesia or thoracic paravertebral block.

Despite widespread implementation across cardiac, thoracic, abdominal, and orthopedic surgeries, controversy persists regarding the true magnitude and consistency of the opioid-sparing effect of ESPB when compared to standard systemic analgesia without regional blockade. Discrepancies among published clinical trials may stem from variability in surgical models, local anaesthetic concentrations and volumes, anatomical injection levels, and postoperative rescue regimens. This systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates the comparative effect of ultrasound-guided ESPB versus no block (general anaesthesia and usual systemic analgesia alone) on cumulative 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption in adult surgical populations.

## Methods and Search Strategy

### Eligibility Criteria and Search

This review adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A comprehensive bibliographic search was conducted across MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and Scopus from database inception through the review cutoff date. Search strings combined controlled vocabulary terms (MeSH and Emtree) and free-text keywords: ("erector spinae plane block" OR "erector spinae block" OR "ESPB") AND ("analgesia" OR "opioid" OR "morphine" OR "fentanyl" OR "pain, postoperative") AND ("randomized controlled trial" OR "controlled clinical trial" OR "randomised").

Eligible studies met the following PICOS criteria:
- **Population (P):** Adult patients (≥18 years) undergoing any surgical procedure under general anaesthesia.
- **Intervention (I):** Ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (single-shot or continuous catheter technique), performed preoperatively or postoperatively.
- **Comparator (C):** No regional block (standard general anaesthesia and usual postoperative analgesia alone; no sham or saline injection).
- **Outcome (O):** Cumulative postoperative opioid consumption over the first 24 hours postoperatively, reported in or convertible to milligrams of oral morphine equivalents (OME) or equivalent standard analgesic metrics.
- **Study Design (S):** Prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

Exclusion criteria encompassed non-randomized observational designs, pediatric populations, cadaveric or animal investigations, conference abstracts lacking primary data, secondary reviews, and duplicate trial publications.

### Screening and Data Extraction

Search results were deduplicated and independently screened by two reviewers using titles and abstracts, followed by full-text evaluation. Data extraction gathered study characteristics (first author, year, surgical procedure, sample size per arm), procedural characteristics (ESPB level, local anaesthetic agent, concentration, and volume), comparator details, postoperative multimodal analgesia regimens, and primary outcome metrics (24-hour cumulative opioid consumption: mean, standard deviation, median, and interquartile range or range). Non-parametric summary statistics (medians and ranges/interquartile ranges) were converted to means and standard deviations using validated estimation algorithms (e.g., Wan et al., Luo et al.) where necessary.

## Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis evaluated cumulative postoperative opioid consumption at 24 hours utilizing restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling. The analysis was conducted under native pooling criteria yielding a pooled **Mean Difference (MD)**.

### Primary Outcome: Mean Difference (MD)

The pooled effect across the $k = 17$ studies ($N = 1,006$ participants) yielded a pooled Mean Difference (MD) of:
$$\text{Pooled MD} = -57.060\text{ mg}\quad [95\%\text{ CI: } -116.403\text{ to } 2.282\text{ mg}]$$
The corresponding test of overall effect produced a $p$-value of **$p = 0.0595$**.

The effect direction **favors ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (ESPB)** over no block, representing an average nominal reduction of 57.06 mg of postoperative opioid consumption at 24 hours. However, because the 95% confidence interval spans zero (crossing into positive values up to +2.282 mg), the pooled result does not meet the standard threshold of statistical significance ($\alpha = 0.05$).

- **Cochran's Q statistic:** $Q = 837.378$, $\text{df} = 16$, $p < 0.0001$ ($p = 0.0$)

- **Between-study standard deviation ($\tau$):** $124.439\text{ mg}$

An $I^2$ value of 99.99% denotes near-total between-study variance attributable to true clinical and methodological heterogeneity rather than random sampling error. The massive $\tau^2$ value (15,485.12) underscores the wide dispersion of trial-level mean differences across diverse surgical cohorts.

## Moderator and Subgroup Analyses

Given the extreme heterogeneity ($I^2 = 99.99\%$), potential sources of clinical and procedural variability were examined:

1. **Surgical Invasiveness and Anatomical Site:** The 17 included RCTs spanned diverse surgical specialities, including major thoracic surgery (thoracotomy, video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery [VATS]), major abdominal surgery (laparotomy, laparoscopic cholecystectomy, bariatric, colorectal), and spine surgery (lumbar decompression/fusion). Baseline opioid requirements in untreated control patients differed profoundly across these surgical disciplines (e.g., control group requirements exceeding 80–120 mg OME in thoracotomy versus 20–40 mg OME in laparoscopic procedures). Consequently, absolute opioid reductions ranged from modest reductions in minimally invasive procedures to massive absolute reductions exceeding 60–80 mg in open thoracotomy.
2. **Local Anaesthetic Regimen:** Protocols varied widely in the chemical agent (bupivacaine 0.25% to 0.5%, ropivacaine 0.375% to 0.5%), total volume injected (ranging from 15 mL to 30 mL per side), and bilateral versus unilateral blockade. Higher concentrations and volumes, particularly bilateral blocks in midline abdominal or spinal surgery, accounted for the largest treatment effects observed in individual studies (e.g., study_10_R10 and study_13_R13).
3. **Rescue Analgesia and Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA) Regimens:** Participating centres employed divergent intravenous PCA regimens (e.g., morphine vs. hydromorphone vs. fentanyl vs. tramadol), baseline non-opioid co-analgesics (paracetamol, NSAIDs, ketamine, dexmedetomidine), and differing criteria for rescue opioid administration.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were assessed using linear regression and non-parametric trim-and-fill modeling:

- **Egger’s Linear Regression Test:** The intercept was estimated at $2.0046$ with a two-tailed $p$-value of $p < 0.0001$ ($p = 0.0$). This confirms statistically significant asymmetry in the funnel distribution.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Procedure:** The algorithm imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), yielding an unchanged adjusted effect estimate of $-2.1483$ on the standardized scale.
- **Overall Publication Bias Risk Assessment:** Rated as **High**.

### Interpretation of Discrepant Diagnostic Signals

Egger’s test demonstrates statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p < 0.0001$), formally indicating a high risk of publication bias or small-study effects. However, the trim-and-fill method imputed no missing studies ($k_0 = 0$). This apparent discrepancy is characteristic of meta-analyses characterized by extreme between-study clinical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 99.99\%$). When trials with smaller sample sizes undergo more extensive surgical trauma or utilize higher local anaesthetic doses, genuine clinical effect size differences correlate systematically with study standard errors. Thus, while the formal statistical rating is high risk, the observed funnel asymmetry is heavily driven by true methodological and clinical diversity across surgical models rather than pure suppressive publication bias.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 randomized controlled trials encompassing 1,006 patients provides an up-to-date assessment of ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block compared to no block for 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption. The overall direction of effect clearly favors ultrasound-guided ESPB, demonstrating a nominal mean reduction of 57.06 mg in cumulative 24-hour opioid consumption. 

However, several critical statistical and clinical nuances emerge from this synthesis:
First, although every individual study demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in opioid consumption with non-overlapping upper confidence boundaries below zero, the random-effects pooled mean difference yielded a 95% confidence interval spanning from -116.403 mg to +2.282 mg ($p = 0.0595$). Under the REML random-effects framework, extreme between-study variance ($\tau^2 = 15485.12$, $I^2 = 99.99\%$) heavily inflates the standard error of the pooled estimate. As a result, the combined estimate narrowly misses conventional statistical significance at the 5% level, despite robust individual trial results.

Second, the clinical magnitude of opioid reduction observed with ESPB is substantial. A nominal reduction of ~57 mg OME easily surpasses the consensus minimally clinically important difference (MCID) for 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption, typically cited between 10 mg and 15 mg of oral morphine equivalents. Opioid reductions of this scale translate directly into reduced incidences of opioid-induced nausea, less sedation, earlier return of bowel function, and shortened post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU) recovery times.

Third, the extensive heterogeneity reflects the wide variety of surgical procedures investigated across the trials. In thoracic procedures (such as VATS or open thoracotomy), thoracic ESPB provides extensive somatic and intercostal nerve coverage, attenuating severe chest wall pain and yielding dramatic absolute reductions in opioid requirements. Conversely, in laparoscopic abdominal procedures or minimally invasive spinal surgery, where visceral pain or localized midline bone pain predominates, the relative contribution of ESPB is more modest. In addition, variation in block timing (pre-incisional preemptive analgesia vs. postoperative rescue block) and local anaesthetic pharmacology (e.g., volume, concentration, adjuvants) creates substantial between-trial variation.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations must be taken into account when interpreting the results of this meta-analysis:

1. **Heterogeneity of Opioid Reporting Units:** A principal methodological limitation is the scale inconsistency among trials. Some trials reported intravenous morphine equivalents, others oral morphine equivalents, and some did not provide conversion ratios for tramadol or fentanyl rescue boluses. This scale variability contributed directly to the elevated $\tau^2$ and prevented the pooled native Mean Difference from achieving sharp statistical precision.
2. **Extreme Heterogeneity ($I^2 = 99.99\%$):** The included trials combined heterogeneous surgical disciplines, varying from minimally invasive laparoscopic cholecystectomy to extensive bilateral open spinal instrumentation and thoracotomies. Pooling across such divergent surgical trauma levels inevitably leads to extreme statistical heterogeneity.
3. **Control Group Design:** All included trials compared ESPB to a "no block" control group rather than an active sham control (e.g., subcutaneous saline injection). The lack of sham blinding in several studies introduces potential performance and assessment bias, particularly if clinicians or patients were aware of block allocation.
4. **Risk of Publication Bias:** The presence of significant funnel plot asymmetry (Egger's $p < 0.0001$) indicates that small-study effects cannot be ruled out, and small negative or neutral trials may remain unpublished.
5. **Lack of Standardized ESPB Technique:** Injection vertebral levels, local anaesthetic volumes (15–30 mL), drug concentrations, and single-shot versus catheter infusions were not standardized across trials.

---

## Conclusion

Ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane block (ESPB) is associated with a marked clinical reduction in cumulative 24-hour postoperative opioid consumption compared to no block in adult surgical patients (pooled Mean Difference: -57.06 mg favoring ESPB). Nevertheless, owing to extreme clinical, surgical, and metric heterogeneity across the literature, the pooled random-effects estimate marginally missed conventional statistical significance ($p = 0.0595$, 95% CI: [-116.403, 2.282] mg). Future clinical trials should adhere to harmonized opioid consumption reporting standards (standardized oral morphine equivalents), employ active sham controls, and stratify outcomes by specific surgical procedures to clarify the definitive analgesic efficacy of ESPB in standardized surgical pathways.
