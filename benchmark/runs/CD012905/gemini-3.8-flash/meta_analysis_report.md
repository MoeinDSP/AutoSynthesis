# Efficacy of Fenoldopam Compared with Control on the Prevention of Acute Kidney Injury: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Acute kidney injury (AKI) remains a major source of morbidity, mortality, and healthcare expenditure across surgical, critical care, and medical populations. Fenoldopam mesylate, a selective dopamine-1 receptor agonist, enhances renal blood flow and natriuresis without systemic alpha- or beta-adrenergic stimulation. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of fenoldopam compared with control (placebo or saline) in preventing AKI across clinical populations.

Methods: A systematic literature search identified randomized and observational comparative studies evaluating fenoldopam versus placebo or saline control. Outcomes were partitioned into analytical cohorts based on underlying primary effect scales. Pooled estimates were synthesized using random-effects models (restricted maximum likelihood [REML]). The primary analytical metric was the pooled Risk Ratio (RR). Heterogeneity was quantified via Cochran's Q and Higgins' I². Publication bias was evaluated using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: From 8 identified studies, 7 comparative effect sizes across 1,104 patients were analyzed. The primary pooled meta-analysis cohort comprised 6 studies (n = 821) evaluated on the Risk Ratio scale. Fenoldopam significantly reduced the incidence of AKI compared with control (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.6133, 95% CI [0.485, 0.775], p = 4.17 × 10⁻⁵). Between-study heterogeneity was negligible (I² = 0.0%, Cochran's Q = 3.709, p = 0.5921; tau² = 0.0). A second analytical subgroup consisting of a single study (n = 283) reporting an independent RR scale metric could not be statistically pooled due to insufficient studies (k = 1, single effect = 0.0574). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.461, p = 0.4489), and trim-and-fill analysis imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: Administration of fenoldopam significantly decreases the risk of acute kidney injury compared to placebo or saline control, conferring an approximate 39% relative risk reduction with remarkably low statistical heterogeneity across included trials.

# Efficacy of Fenoldopam Versus Control for the Prevention of Acute Kidney Injury: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a critical clinical syndrome characterized by a rapid decline in renal excretory function, accumulation of metabolic waste products, and dysregulation of extracellular volume and electrolytes. AKI occurs frequently in intensive care units, following major cardiovascular or vascular surgery, and subsequent to radiocontrast media administration. The development of AKI is independently associated with prolonged hospitalization, increased progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD), requirement for renal replacement therapy (RRT), and heightened short- and long-term mortality.

Pathophysiologically, renal hypoperfusion and ischemia-reperfusion injury trigger cortical and medullary vasoconstriction, microvascular endothelial injury, and tubular epithelial cell death. Fenoldopam mesylate is a selective, high-affinity dopamine-1 (DA-1) receptor agonist that induces systemic and renal vasodilation without stimulating alpha-adrenergic, beta-adrenergic, or dopamine-2 (DA-2) receptors. By selectively dilating renal interlobular and afferent arterioles, fenoldopam increases renal plasma flow, enhances glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and promotes natriuresis and diuresis while maintaining or reducing renal vascular resistance.

Despite these favorable physiological mechanisms, clinical trials investigating fenoldopam for renal protection have yielded heterogeneous conclusions, ranging from substantial reductions in AKI incidence to neutral effects accompanied by systemic hypotension. This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to synthesize the available evidence and quantify the effect of fenoldopam compared with control (placebo or saline) on the development of AKI across patient populations.

## Search Strategy and Screening

A systematic literature search was conducted across MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). Search strategies utilized controlled vocabulary (e.g., Medical Subject Headings) and free-text terms including "fenoldopam", "dopamine-1 agonist", "acute kidney injury", "acute renal failure", "renal protection", and "placebo".

### Eligibility Criteria
- **Population:** Adult and pediatric patient populations at risk of acute kidney injury (e.g., cardiovascular surgery, critical illness, contrast-induced nephropathy).
- **Intervention:** Continuous infusion or targeted administration of fenoldopam mesylate.
- **Comparator:** Placebo infusion or normal saline control.
- **Outcome:** Incidence of acute kidney injury as defined by standardized consensus criteria (e.g., RIFLE, AKIN, KDIGO) or study-specific serum creatinine elevation thresholds.
- **Study Design:** Prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and prospective comparative studies.

Articles were screened sequentially by title, abstract, and full text. A total of 8 unique studies met initial screening criteria, yielding 7 quantitative effect sizes for formal analytical consideration.

Potential reporting bias and funnel plot asymmetry were rigorously assessed across the evidence base.
- **Egger's Linear Regression Test:** Intercept = -0.461, $t = -0.83$, $p = 0.4489$.

- **Qualitative Risk Evaluation:** Low risk of publication bias.

Because Egger's test revealed no statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.4489$) and the trim-and-fill algorithm imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), there is no evidence that selective non-reporting or suppression of negative/small trials distorted the meta-analytic findings.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis establishes that fenoldopam infusion significantly reduces the risk of acute kidney injury compared to placebo or saline control (pooled Risk Ratio = 0.6133, 95% CI [0.485, 0.775], $p < 0.0001$). Importantly, the pooled evidence demonstrated remarkable consistency, exhibiting an $I^2$ value of 0.0% and a non-significant test of heterogeneity ($Q = 3.709, p = 0.5921$).

The biological plausibility of these findings is robust. In high-risk states—such as cardiopulmonary bypass, aortic clamping, or intravenous iodinated radiocontrast administration—endothelin-mediated and catecholamine-induced renal vasoconstriction drastically impairs medullary oxygen delivery. Fenoldopam, by selectively binding to vascular DA-1 receptors, stimulates adenylate cyclase, increases intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP), and prompts relaxation of renal arterial smooth muscle cells. This targeted hemodynamic action augments medullary blood flow, attenuates tubular ischemic necrosis, and preserves filtration fractions without eliciting adverse beta-adrenergic tachycardia or alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction.

The clinical implication of an approximate 39% relative risk reduction is substantial. AKI in hospitalized patients is strongly tied to prolonged mechanical ventilation, increased need for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and escalating mortality. Interventions that preserve renal perfusion during periods of physiologic stress provide pivotal organ-protective value.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

In accordance with systematic review and meta-analytic validation protocols, the following data handling and validation points are documented plainly:
1. **Metric and Scale Harmonization:** A primary effect measure conflict was identified during data ingestion. The systematic protocol requested Risk Ratio (RR) as the primary pooling metric, whereas several underlying primary trial publications originally reported odds ratios (OR). Per explicit protocol directives, the user request for Risk Ratio was maintained as authoritative, and native pooling was performed on the RR scale.
2. **Subgroup Stratification:** Study `study_6_R6` ($n = 283$) was partitioned into a distinct analytical stratum (`acute kidney injury::measure=RR`) due to differing native metric declarations. Because this subgroup contained only $k = 1$ trial, it was flagged with `insufficient_data = true` and was not synthesized into a pooled statistic.
3. **Extraction Consistency:** Of the 8 studies retrieved in the initial systematic pipeline, 7 independent effect sizes were extracted and analyzed. No duplicate cohort reports or overlapping registry publications were included, preventing double-counting of participant data.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Variation in AKI Definitions:** Although between-study statistical heterogeneity was absent ($I^2 = 0.0\%$), definitions of AKI across older and newer trials varied from traditional absolute serum creatinine increases (e.g., $\ge 0.5$ mg/dL) to standardized consensus classifications (e.g., RIFLE, AKIN).
2. **Hemodynamic Side Effects:** While fenoldopam is a renal vasodilator, high doses can induce systemic arterial vasodilation and hypotension. Systemic hypotension, if uncorrected, could paradoxically exacerbate renal hypoperfusion. Individual patient-level hemodynamic data and vasopressor requirements could not be pooled in this study-level analysis.
3. **Long-Term Renal Endpoints:** The analyzed trials predominantly reported short-term in-hospital AKI incidence. Data on long-term chronic kidney disease progression, dialysis dependence at 90 days, and overall long-term survival remain limited across primary reports.

---

## Conclusion

Fenoldopam mesylate significantly reduces the incidence of acute kidney injury compared to placebo or saline control across clinical populations (pooled Risk Ratio = 0.6133, 95% CI [0.485, 0.775], $p = 4.17 \times 10^{-5}$). The treatment effect is supported by negligible statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$) and no indication of publication bias. Fenoldopam represents an effective renal-protective pharmacological strategy in patients at elevated risk for acute kidney injury.
