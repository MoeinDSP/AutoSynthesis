# Exercise Therapy for Functional Ability in Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Patellofemoral pain syndrome (PFPS) is one of the most prevalent lower-limb musculoskeletal complaints affecting physically active adolescents and adults. While exercise therapy is widely recommended as a conservative intervention, evidence regarding its short-term efficacy on functional ability across randomized controlled trials warrants rigorous quantitative synthesis.

**Objectives:** To determine the effect of exercise therapy, compared with control interventions (no treatment, placebo, waiting list, or health education, including exercise plus a co-intervention versus that co-intervention alone), on short-term self-reported functional ability (up to 3 months) in patients with PFPS.

**Methods:** A systematic literature search identified randomized controlled trials comparing exercise therapy to control conditions in individuals diagnosed with PFPS. Functional ability outcomes measured using validated instruments (e.g., Anterior Knee Pain Scale/Kujala score) at short-term follow-up (≤3 months) were extracted. Random-effects meta-analysis was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Between-study heterogeneity was evaluated via Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed using Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

**Results:** Five randomized comparisons comprising 370 participants met inclusion criteria for quantitative synthesis. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) for short-term functional ability was 5.70 points (95% CI: [-0.867, 12.267], p = 0.0889), indicating a positive trend favoring exercise therapy that did not reach statistical significance at the conventional 5% threshold. Substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed (I² = 81.89%, tau² = 45.65, Q = 19.789, p = 0.0005). Egger's test revealed a marginal trend toward funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -1.4494, p = 0.097), though the trim-and-fill procedure imputed no missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating moderate publication bias risk.

**Conclusion:** Exercise therapy demonstrates a trend toward improved short-term functional ability in patients with patellofemoral pain syndrome, though the pooled mean difference does not achieve statistical significance and exhibits marked heterogeneity across trials. Clinicians should interpret these findings in light of trial-level variations in exercise prescription, adherence, and baseline severity.

# The Effect of Exercise Therapy on Short-Term Functional Ability in Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Patellofemoral pain syndrome (PFPS)—frequently referred to as anterior knee pain, runner's knee, or chondromalacia patellae—is a highly prevalent musculoskeletal disorder characterized by diffuse retro- or peripatellar pain provoked by activities that load the patellofemoral joint, such as squatting, stair ambulation, running, and prolonged sitting with flexed knees. PFPS accounts for approximately 25% to 40% of all knee problems managed in sports medicine and orthopaedic clinics, disproportionately affecting adolescents and physically active young adults, particularly females.

The pathophysiology of PFPS is multifactorial, involving altered patellar tracking, quadriceps weakness (specifically vastus medialis obliquus insufficiency or dyssynchrony), hip abductor and external rotator weakness, hamstring and iliotibial band tightness, and suboptimal lower-limb biomechanics. Consequently, active exercise therapy—incorporating targeted knee-targeted strengthening, hip-targeted exercises, or combined trunk-pelvis-knee interventions—is widely recommended as the cornerstone of non-operative management.

Despite broad clinical consensus, empirical trial results vary substantially in magnitude. Several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) report dramatic improvements in self-reported function, whereas others observe modest or non-significant differences when compared against control conditions (e.g., wait-and-see, educational brochures, or placebo interventions). Furthermore, evaluating functional recovery specifically in the short term (up to 3 months post-randomization) is of paramount clinical importance, as this window dictates early rehabilitation compliance, return to physical activity, and long-term functional trajectories.

The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to quantify the effect of exercise therapy compared with control conditions on self-reported functional ability in the short term (up to 3 months) in patients with patellofemoral pain syndrome.

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Statistically significant and substantial between-study heterogeneity was present among the included trials:
- **Cochran's $Q$:** 19.789 ($df = 4$, $p = 0.0005$)
- **Inconsistency index ($I^2$):** 81.89%
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$):** 45.6548
- **Between-study standard deviation ($\tau$):** 6.7568

## Moderator and Subgroup Considerations

Potential sources of the observed heterogeneity were inspected across study characteristics. Key clinical and methodological factors likely contributing to variance include:
1. **Exercise Protocol Composition:** Trials incorporating proximal hip-muscle strengthening (abductors and external rotators) alongside local quadriceps re-education (e.g., `study_3_R3`, `study_5_R5`) yielded markedly larger functional improvements ($g > 1.0$) compared with trials utilizing isolated knee-extension exercises or non-supervised home programs (`study_1_R1`, `study_6_R6`).
2. **Control Condition Structure:** Control arms ranged from waitlist or minimal intervention to active education or co-intervention delivery. In `study_1_R1`, the control regimen may have conferred modest non-specific therapeutic benefits, narrowing the treatment-control contrast.
3. **Sample Size and Precision:** Smaller trials (`study_3_R3`, $n = 43$; `study_5_R5`, $n = 60$) reported much larger effect sizes than larger trials (`study_2_R2`, $n = 122$; `study_6_R6`, $n = 81$), highlighting potential small-study effects.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential publication bias were evaluated through statistical modeling:
- **Egger's Linear Regression Test:** Intercept = -1.4494, $p = 0.0970$
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Method:** Number of imputed missing studies ($k_0$) = 0; adjusted pooled estimate = 0.4442 (standardized metric).

Egger's test revealed a marginal trend toward funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.0970$). However, because the non-parametric trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), the risk of publication bias is characterized as **moderate**. The apparent asymmetry appears driven primarily by true clinical heterogeneity between small trials evaluating intensive hip-and-knee interventions and larger trials evaluating pragmatic or lower-intensity exercise protocols, rather than overt suppression of small negative studies.

## Discussion

This meta-analysis synthesized evidence from 5 randomized comparisons comprising 370 participants to determine the short-term functional efficacy of exercise therapy in patellofemoral pain syndrome. The pooled Mean Difference of 5.70 points (95% CI: [-0.867, 12.267]) demonstrated an overall positive functional trend for exercise therapy, but the difference did not achieve statistical significance ($p = 0.0889$).

On the widely utilized Kujala Anterior Knee Pain Scale, the established minimal clinically important difference (MCID) ranges between 8 and 10 points. While the point estimate of 5.70 points indicates meaningful functional gain for some patients, the confidence interval spans from -0.87 (negligible harm or slight control advantage) to +12.27 (exceeding the clinical threshold). This wide interval reflects the substantial between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 81.89\%$, $\tau^2 = 45.65$).

Clinically, trials that demonstrated the most pronounced functional improvements (`study_3_R3` and `study_5_R5`) utilized comprehensive multi-joint rehabilitation protocols targeting both the hip abductors/external rotators and the quadriceps. Biomechanical evidence confirms that hip muscle weakness permits excessive femoral internal rotation and adduction during weight-bearing, amplifying patellofemoral joint stress. Consequently, active interventions that restore proximal dynamic control appear more effective than isolated knee exercises.

Conversely, the lack of statistical significance in the pooled model underscores that exercise therapy cannot be viewed as a uniform intervention. Variations in exercise dosing, supervision levels, participant baseline severity, and compliance play critical roles in determining short-term recovery.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Limited Number of Included Trials:** Quantitative synthesis was restricted to 5 comparisons ($N = 370$), which limits statistical power for subgroup analyses and reduces the sensitivity of tests for funnel plot asymmetry.
2. **Substantial Heterogeneity:** An $I^2$ of 81.89% denotes substantial heterogeneity, driven by divergent exercise prescriptions, intervention durations, and control modalities.
3. **Follow-up Window:** This meta-analysis focused strictly on the short term (≤3 months). Long-term functional retention, recurrence rates, and structural joint adaptation cannot be inferred from these data.
4. **Risk of Bias within Trials:** Blinding of participants and treating therapists is inherently unfeasible in exercise therapy trials, which introduces potential performance and expectancy biases in self-reported functional questionnaires.

---

## Conclusion

Exercise therapy produces a positive trend toward improved short-term functional ability in individuals with patellofemoral pain syndrome, with a pooled Mean Difference of 5.70 points (95% CI: [-0.867, 12.267]). However, this effect did not reach conventional statistical significance ($p = 0.0889$) and was accompanied by substantial between-trial heterogeneity ($I^2 = 81.89\%$). Rehabilitation specialists should consider individualized, multi-joint exercise programs incorporating both hip and quadriceps strengthening to maximize early functional recovery in PFPS.
