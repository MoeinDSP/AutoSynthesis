# Fibrin-Based Versus Active Non-Fibrin Haemostasis During Liver Resection and All-Cause Perioperative Mortality: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis

## Abstract

## Abstract

**Background:** Fibrin-based haemostatic products are used to control bleeding from the hepatic transection surface, but their effect on perioperative survival relative to active non-fibrin haemostatic approaches is uncertain.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of fibrin-based haemostatic agents, compared with active non-fibrin agents or techniques, on all-cause perioperative mortality among adults undergoing liver resection.

**Methods:** The supplied review dataset contained 10 studies, of which 7 contributed an effect size to the mortality meta-analysis. These 7 studies included 824 participants. The prespecified effect measure was the risk ratio (RR), calculated as mortality risk in the fibrin group divided by mortality risk in the non-fibrin group. A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was used. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed with Cochran’s Q, I², and τ². Small-study effects were evaluated using Egger’s test and trim-and-fill. Search dates, databases, complete screening counts, trial-level death counts, risk-of-bias assessments, and mortality definitions were not supplied.

**Results:** The pooled RR was 1.1552804540664858 (95% CI 0.506 to 2.635; p = 0.7315307145747422; k = 7; n = 824). The supplied analysis classified the result as favoring neither group. Heterogeneity was modest: I² = 25.34%, τ² = 0.310467, and Q = 8.014 (p = 0.237). Egger’s intercept was 0.6272 (p = 0.657), and trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀ = 0). These diagnostics did not identify small-study effects, although their reliability is limited with only seven studies.

**Conclusions:** The available evidence does not demonstrate a difference in all-cause perioperative mortality between fibrin-based and active non-fibrin haemostatic approaches during liver resection. The confidence interval is wide and remains compatible with potentially important effects in either direction. Interpretation is materially constrained by absent trial-level event counts, incomplete review-process information, lack of risk-of-bias assessments, and inconsistent labeling of the supplied study-level effect fields.

## Introduction

Liver resection carries a risk of bleeding from the hepatic transection surface. Surgeons may use topical or adjunctive haemostatic products in addition to operative haemostatic techniques. Fibrin-based products—including fibrin sealants, fibrin glue, fibrin patches, and fibrin-coated collagen—promote clot formation by supplying components of the coagulation cascade. Alternative active approaches include oxidised cellulose, non-fibrin collagen sponges, argon beam coagulation, electrocautery, and standard surgical haemostasis incorporating an active technique.

Although technical outcomes such as time to haemostasis and blood loss are frequently evaluated, all-cause perioperative mortality is the most clinically consequential safety outcome. This review therefore addressed the following question: among adults undergoing hepatic resection, what is the effect of a fibrin-based haemostatic agent, compared with an active non-fibrin haemostatic agent or technique, on all-cause perioperative mortality?

The intended outcome was death from any cause during the index hospital admission or within approximately 30–90 days after resection, according to each trial’s definition. Treatment-related mortality alone, late mortality at one year or beyond, and non-mortality outcomes were outside the scope of this synthesis.

## Search/Screening

### Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies compared a fibrin-based haemostatic agent used during liver resection with an active non-fibrin haemostatic agent or technique. The target population comprised adults undergoing hepatic resection. The eligible outcome was all-cause perioperative mortality, expressed as the number of participants who died divided by the number randomised in each group. Intention-to-treat denominators were preferred; treated or safety-population denominators were to be used only when randomised denominators were unavailable and were to be flagged. Explicitly reported zero-event arms were eligible. Reports that did not provide perioperative mortality were to be excluded from this outcome.

For eligible multi-arm trials, the planned approach was to retain the fibrin arm and combine all eligible non-fibrin comparator arms by summing deaths and participants. Blood loss, time to haemostasis, transfusion, bile leak, reoperation, infection, and all other non-mortality outcomes were not included.

## Effect Sizes

The prespecified and pooled effect measure was the **Risk Ratio (RR)**, defined as:

\[
RR = \frac{\text{perioperative mortality risk with fibrin-based haemostasis}}{\text{perioperative mortality risk with active non-fibrin haemostasis}}.
\]

Under this definition, RR < 1 favors the fibrin-based agent and RR > 1 favors the non-fibrin comparator. An RR of 1 is the null value. Native RR pooling was reported as having been used.

The supplied study-level records contain values under a field named `hedges_g`, including negative values. Those entries cannot represent native risk ratios because an RR cannot be negative. Consistent with the specified effect-measure labeling, they are not presented here as study-level RRs and are not relabeled as a different confirmed statistic. Arm-level deaths and denominators were unavailable, so valid trial-specific RRs, continuity-correction choices for zero-event studies, and the handling of double-zero trials cannot be independently reconstructed or verified.

## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects model estimated by restricted maximum likelihood produced a pooled **Risk Ratio (RR)** of **1.1552804540664858** (95% CI **0.506 to 2.635**; p = **0.7315307145747422**), based on **7 studies and 824 participants**. The supplied analysis classified the result as favoring **neither group**.

The confidence interval crosses the null value of 1 and is broad. Accordingly, the analysis does not demonstrate a statistically detectable difference in perioperative mortality between fibrin-based and active non-fibrin haemostatic approaches. The interval remains compatible with appreciably lower or appreciably higher mortality with fibrin-based treatment. Because event totals were not provided, the result cannot be translated into an absolute risk difference or number needed to treat or harm.

The point estimate must not be interpreted in isolation. In particular, failure to reject the null hypothesis is not evidence that the two strategies are equivalent. No equivalence or non-inferiority margin was specified, and the imprecision is substantial.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was estimated as follows:

- **I² = 25.34%**
- **τ² = 0.310467**
- **Cochran’s Q = 8.014**, with **p = 0.237**

These statistics indicate modest observed inconsistency, and the Q test did not reject homogeneity. Nevertheless, Q has limited power when only seven studies are available, while I² is itself imprecisely estimated in a small meta-analysis. The absence of statistically significant heterogeneity should therefore not be interpreted as proof that the underlying effects are identical.

Potential clinical sources of heterogeneity could include the type and formulation of the fibrin product, comparator technique, extent and indication for resection, baseline operative risk, co-interventions, surgeon experience, and the mortality ascertainment window. None could be examined using the supplied study characteristics.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **0.6272** with **p = 0.657**. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0** missing studies. The supplied trim-and-fill output also contained a value of **0.1196** in a field labeled `trim_fill_g`; because this label is not the confirmed RR scale and no corresponding RR confidence interval was supplied, that value is not interpreted as an adjusted pooled risk ratio.

The supplied analysis categorized publication-bias risk as low, and the diagnostics did not identify funnel-plot asymmetry or imputed missing studies. This finding should be interpreted cautiously: with only seven studies, Egger’s test has low power, funnel-based methods are unstable, and trim-and-fill depends on assumptions that may not hold. Moreover, selective non-reporting of mortality cannot be excluded because three of the ten studies in the pipeline did not contribute to the meta-analysis and their reasons for non-contribution were not given.

## Discussion

### Principal findings

Across seven studies with 824 participants, the REML random-effects analysis found no statistically detectable difference in all-cause perioperative mortality between fibrin-based and active non-fibrin haemostatic strategies. The pooled RR was 1.1552804540664858, with a 95% CI from 0.506 to 2.635. The supplied directional classification was that the estimate favored neither group. Observed heterogeneity was modest (I² = 25.34%), but the estimate was imprecise.

### Clinical interpretation

Mortality after liver resection is influenced by many perioperative factors, and haemostatic products may primarily target technical endpoints rather than survival. The present synthesis does not establish a mortality advantage for either strategy. It also does not establish equivalence or safety parity, because the confidence interval encompasses potentially important effects in both directions.

Clinical choice should therefore not be based on a claimed mortality benefit from these results alone. Decisions may reasonably consider product-specific adverse effects, haemostatic performance, operative context, surgeon familiarity, availability, and cost, but those outcomes were outside the scope of this review and were neither extracted nor pooled.

### Statistical interpretation

The random-effects model appropriately allows the true intervention effect to vary across studies. However, estimating between-study variance from seven studies is uncertain. In addition, mortality following hepatic resection may be uncommon, making RR estimates sensitive to sparse events, zero-event handling, and continuity corrections. Because trial-level deaths and arm totals were not supplied, these aspects could not be audited.

The non-significant Egger test and k₀ = 0 from trim-and-fill do not provide strong assurance against reporting bias. These procedures have limited informativeness in small meta-analyses and cannot detect all forms of selective outcome reporting.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

Although no formal `validation_flags` object was supplied, several issues are apparent in the provided results and require explicit reporting:

1. The pipeline listed 10 studies but only 7 effect sizes. Reasons for the three studies’ non-contribution were not provided.
2. Trial-level mortality counts, arm totals, and mortality time windows were absent. The intended use of intention-to-treat denominators and the treatment of zero-event arms therefore could not be verified.
3. The pooled analysis was explicitly labeled and natively computed as a Risk Ratio, but the study-level records were stored under a `hedges_g` field and included negative values, which are impossible for RRs. Those values were not treated as study-specific mortality RRs in this report.
4. The trim-and-fill result included a value in a `trim_fill_g` field rather than a confirmed RR field. It was therefore not interpreted as an adjusted RR.
5. No information was available to identify duplicate reports or overlapping trial populations.
6. No risk-of-bias assessment, certainty-of-evidence assessment, or source bibliography was supplied.
7. The generated figures should be validated against original arm-level mortality data before dissemination, particularly where their underlying study-level scale is unclear.

## Limitations

This report is limited by the information available in the supplied context. First, the search strategy and screening history were absent, preventing evaluation of search completeness and full PRISMA flow reporting. Second, study-level bibliographic and clinical details were unavailable, limiting assessment of applicability and clinical heterogeneity. Third, the absence of arm-level death counts prevents independent recalculation of RRs and verification of sparse-data methods, multi-arm combination, and denominator selection.

Fourth, no study-level risk-of-bias judgments were provided. The internal validity of the evidence is therefore unknown. Fifth, the study-level effect fields were inconsistent with the reported pooled RR scale. Although the aggregate result was reported as a native RR analysis, this inconsistency reduces auditability and requires resolution before publication. Sixth, only seven studies were pooled, limiting the reliability of heterogeneity and publication-bias diagnostics. Seventh, no certainty-of-evidence framework such as GRADE was supplied; a formal certainty rating cannot be assigned from the available data.

Finally, perioperative mortality definitions may have ranged from in-hospital mortality to approximately 30–90-day mortality. Without study-specific definitions, possible time-window heterogeneity cannot be quantified.

## Conclusion

In seven studies involving 824 participants undergoing liver resection, fibrin-based haemostatic agents did not show a statistically detectable effect on all-cause perioperative mortality compared with active non-fibrin haemostatic agents or techniques. The pooled Risk Ratio was 1.1552804540664858 (95% CI 0.506 to 2.635; p = 0.7315307145747422), and the supplied analysis classified the result as favoring neither group. Heterogeneity was modest (I² = 25.34%), and statistical diagnostics did not identify small-study effects, although they were underpowered.

The evidence is insufficient to conclude mortality equivalence or to exclude clinically important benefit or harm. Original trial reports and arm-level event data should be retrieved to verify the analysis, reconcile the effect-scale inconsistencies, assess risk of bias, and establish the certainty of the evidence.
