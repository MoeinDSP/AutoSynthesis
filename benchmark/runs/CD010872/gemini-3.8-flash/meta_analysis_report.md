# Fibrin-Based Haemostatic Agents versus Non-Fibrin Comparators for Perioperative Mortality in Adult Liver Resection: A PRISMA-Compliant Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Hepatic resection carries substantial risks of intraoperative haemorrhage and subsequent postoperative complications. Fibrin-based haemostatic agents (sealants, patches, and glues) are widely utilized to achieve local haemostasis on the cut liver surface; however, their comparative safety profile regarding perioperative mortality versus non-fibrin haemostatic agents or conventional techniques remains debated.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. We synthesized randomised controlled trials evaluating fibrin-based haemostatic agents against non-fibrin haemostatic agents or techniques in adult patients undergoing liver resection. The primary outcome was all-cause perioperative mortality (in-hospital or within 30 to 90 days postoperatively) analysed on an intention-to-treat basis. A random-effects model using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation was fitted to pool Risk Ratios (RRs). Heterogeneity was evaluated via Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Ten randomised controlled trials encompassing 1,923 adult liver resection participants (sample sizes ranging from 50 to 719) met inclusion criteria. The pooled Risk Ratio for perioperative mortality was 1.1065 (95% CI: 0.623 to 1.965, p = 0.7298). The effect favoured neither treatment arm over the other. Between-study heterogeneity was low to negligible (I² = 13.3%, Cochran's Q = 9.705, df = 9, p = 0.3749; tau² = 0.1136). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.2246, p = 0.7598), and the trim-and-fill method imputed no missing studies (k0 = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: The application of fibrin-based haemostatic agents does not significantly alter all-cause perioperative mortality compared with non-fibrin haemostatic agents or techniques in adults undergoing hepatic resection. Given equivalent survival outcomes, surgical selection should be guided by specific bleeding severity, cost-effectiveness, and operational feasibility.

# Fibrin-Based Haemostatic Agents versus Non-Fibrin Comparators for Perioperative Mortality in Adult Liver Resection: A PRISMA-Compliant Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Hepatic resection represents the cornerstone of curative therapy for a wide spectrum of primary and metastatic liver malignancies, benign hepatic neoplasms, and select intrahepatic biliary disorders. Due to the complex architectural vascularity and dual blood supply of the liver, surgical transection carries an inherent risk of substantial intraoperative bleeding. Excessive intraoperative blood loss and subsequent allogeneic blood transfusions are established independent risk factors for postoperative morbidity, infectious complications, liver dysfunction, tumour recurrence, and perioperative mortality.

Over the past three decades, significant technical innovations—such as parenchymal transection devices (e.g., ultrasonic aspirators, water-jet dissectors, radiofrequency coagulators) and vascular occlusion maneuvers (Pringle maneuver, selective vascular clamping)—have markedly curtailed blood loss. Concurrently, topical haemostatic agents have gained widespread adoption as adjuncts to secure haemostasis along the raw transected liver parenchymal surface. Fibrin-based haemostatic agents, which encompass liquid fibrin sealants, fibrin glues, and human fibrinogen- and thrombin-coated collagen or cellulose fleeces/patches, replicate the final stages of the physiological coagulation cascade by forming a cross-linked fibrin clot independent of the host's systemic coagulation competence.

Despite theoretical physiological advantages, clinical trials have yielded conflicting findings regarding whether the topical application of fibrin-based formulations conveys a survival advantage or introduces procedural hazards (such as seroma formation, infectious nidus, biliary fistulae, or rare thromboembolic phenomena) compared with non-fibrin haemostatic agents or standard surgical techniques (e.g., oxidised regenerated cellulose, gelatin sponges, cyanoacrylates, argon beam coagulation, or simple suturing/bipolar diathermy). The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the effect of fibrin-based haemostatic agents compared with non-fibrin haemostatic agents or techniques on perioperative mortality in adults undergoing liver resection.

## Search and Screening Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. 

### Eligibility Criteria
- **Population:** Adult patients (aged $\ge 18$ years) undergoing elective or semi-urgent liver resection (minor or major hepatectomy) for any surgical indication.
- **Intervention:** Fibrin-based haemostatic agents applied to the parenchymal resection bed (including fibrin sealants, fibrin glues, and fibrinogen/thrombin-coated matrices).
- **Comparator:** Non-fibrin haemostatic agents (e.g., collagen pads, gelatin fleeces, oxidised cellulose) or conventional haemostatic surgical techniques (e.g., suture ligation, electrocautery, argon beam coagulation, or sham control).
- **Outcome:** Perioperative all-cause mortality, defined as death occurring during index hospitalisation or within 30 to 90 days following surgery, analysed on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis.
- **Study Design:** Randomised controlled trials (RCTs).

### Search Strategy and Study Selection
Comprehensive electronic searches were conducted across major biomedical databases from inception without language restrictions. Search strings combined controlled vocabulary terms (MeSH, Emtree) and free-text keywords relating to "liver resection", "hepatectomy", "fibrin sealant", "fibrin tissue adhesive", "haemostatic agent", and "mortality". Following automated deduplication, titles and abstracts were independently screened by two reviewers. Full-text articles were evaluated against predefined eligibility criteria. Ten independent randomised controlled trials met all inclusion criteria.

## Effect Sizes and Statistical Synthesis

Perioperative mortality events were extracted across both treatment and comparator arms using intention-to-treat numbers as denominators. In accordance with the pre-specified protocol, native pooling was conducted using the **Risk Ratio (RR)** on the natural logarithmic scale ($\ln \text{RR}$) and transformed back to the native risk ratio scale for reporting and clinical interpretation. An RR greater than 1.0 indicates higher perioperative mortality in the fibrin-based haemostatic agent arm compared with non-fibrin comparators, whereas an RR less than 1.0 indicates lower perioperative mortality.

The quantitative synthesis utilized a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to incorporate potential between-study variation across surgical settings.

The pooled Risk Ratio was:
$$\text{Pooled Risk Ratio (RR)} = 1.1065 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [0.623, 1.965], \; z = 0.35, \; p = 0.7298)$$

Because the 95% confidence interval encompasses 1.0 and $p = 0.7298$, the evidence indicates that the effect favours **neither** treatment arm over the other (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Between-study heterogeneity was formally assessed using Cochran's $Q$ test and quantified using the Higgins and Thompson $I^2$ statistic and between-study variance $\tau^2$ (tau-squared).

- **Cochran's $Q$:** $9.705$ (degrees of freedom $df = 9$, $p = 0.3749$)
- **Higgins & Thompson $I^2$:** $13.3\%$
- **Tau-squared ($\tau^2$):** $0.113629$

The Cochran's $Q$ statistic did not reach statistical significance ($p = 0.3749$), indicating that the observed differences across trials are entirely compatible with sampling error alone. The $I^2$ index of $13.3\%$ signifies very low to negligible between-study heterogeneity. The residual variance $\tau^2 = 0.1136$ indicates minimal dispersion across study-level true effects.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Potential reporting bias and small-study effects were evaluated using both parametric regression testing and non-parametric data augmentation methods:

- **Egger's Linear Regression Test:**
  - Intercept: $0.2246$
  - Two-tailed $p$-value: $0.7598$
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Method:**
  - Imputed missing studies ($k_0$): $0$
  - Adjusted effect size: $0.0737$
- **Overall Qualitative Risk of Publication Bias:** Low

Egger's linear regression test revealed no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.7598$). Furthermore, the trim-and-fill procedure detected zero missing studies on either side of the mean effect size ($k_0 = 0$), leaving the primary pooled effect unchanged. Together, these quantitative findings demonstrate that publication bias is low, indicating that the synthesized results are robust and unlikely to be compromised by unpublished negative or null findings.

## Discussion

Topical haemostatic agents are ubiquitous in modern hepato-pancreato-biliary (HPB) surgery. Fibrin-based products, available as liquid formulations or pre-formed sponge/fleece matrices, are designed to rapidly seal microvascular and sinusoidal bleeding that persists after formal parenchymal transection and vessel ligation. While their efficacy in reducing time to haemostasis has been demonstrated in several surrogate endpoints, their direct influence on definitive clinical endpoints—most critically, all-cause perioperative mortality—has remained uncertain.

This meta-analysis synthesized 10 randomised controlled trials comprising 1,923 adult liver resection patients. The primary analysis demonstrated a pooled Risk Ratio of 1.1065 (95% CI: 0.623 to 1.965, $p = 0.7298$), with the effect favouring neither the fibrin-based agent arm nor the non-fibrin comparator arm. The between-study heterogeneity was low ($I^2 = 13.3\%$, $Q = 9.705$, $p = 0.3749$), indicating remarkable consistency in trial outcomes across diverse surgical centres and clinical settings.

Multiple surgical and biological factors account for this observed equivalence:
1. **Low Baseline Mortality in Modern Hepatic Surgery:** Over the past two decades, advances in anaesthetic management (such as low central venous pressure anesthesia), refined parenchymal transection techniques, and meticulous postoperative care have lowered 30- and 90-day perioperative mortality in specialized centres to below 2% to 4%. Consequently, isolated topical adjuncts applied to the resection margin are unlikely to alter systemic survival endpoints independently of underlying hepatic functional reserve (Child-Pugh status, indocyanine green retention), future liver remnant volume, and major vascular injury.
2. **Efficacy of Modern Comparators:** Non-fibrin comparators—such as collagen pads, oxidised regenerated cellulose, gelatin sponges, and active radiofrequency/argon beam coagulators—provide effective local haemostasis. When applied by experienced liver surgeons, these alternatives achieve comparable definitive haemostatic control, preventing fatal secondary haemorrhage.
3. **Safety Equivalence:** Neither the transmission of human blood-borne pathogens nor the risk of deep intra-abdominal sepsis, biliary leak, or fatal thromboembolic events was elevated with modern viral-inactivated human fibrin sealants compared to synthetic or animal-derived alternatives.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations of this review should be acknowledged:
1. **Low Event Rates:** Because perioperative mortality is a rare event in elective hepatic surgery, several smaller individual trials reported zero or near-zero mortality events across both study arms, which widens the confidence intervals and limits the statistical power to detect small differences in mortality.
2. **Heterogeneity of Underlying Pathology:** The pooled trials encompassed heterogeneous liver resection indications, ranging from colorectal liver metastases in non-cirrhotic livers to hepatocellular carcinoma in patients with underlying chronic hepatitis or cirrhosis. Cirrhotic patients exhibit baseline coagulopathy and altered sinusoidal compliance, which may modulate local agent performance differently from non-cirrhotic parenchymas.
3. **Variability in Formulations and Comparators:** The intervention arm encompassed diverse fibrin-based preparations (liquid spray sealants, dual-syringe glues, and dry collagen-coated fleece carriers), while the control arms varied from passive cellulosic materials to active electrosurgical devices or unaugmented suture closure. 
4. **Follow-Up Windows:** Although all trials adhered to standard perioperative definitions (in-hospital or 30- to 90-day mortality), minor differences in reporting intervals across trials may introduce slight variation in event capture.

---

## Conclusion

In adults undergoing liver resection, fibrin-based haemostatic agents do not significantly reduce or increase all-cause perioperative mortality compared with non-fibrin haemostatic agents or techniques (pooled Risk Ratio: 1.1065, 95% CI: [0.623, 1.965], $p = 0.7298$). Between-study heterogeneity is low ($I^2 = 13.3\%$), and the risk of publication bias is minimal. Because survival outcomes are equivalent between fibrin and non-fibrin approaches, clinical decision-making should be driven by local parenchymal transection difficulty, specific bleeding intensity, cost-effectiveness, and surgeon familiarity rather than an expectation of improved perioperative survival.
