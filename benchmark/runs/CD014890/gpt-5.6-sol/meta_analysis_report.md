# Vocabulary Interventions and Immediate Distal Expressive L2 Vocabulary in Children Aged Six Years or Younger: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

## Abstract

## Abstract

**Background:** Vocabulary interventions may support second-language (L2) development in young children, but improvement on taught words does not necessarily generalize to distal expressive vocabulary.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of shared book reading, dialogic reading, explicit word teaching, dual-language instruction, or related vocabulary interventions on distal expressive L2 vocabulary at the first assessment immediately after intervention among children aged up to and including six years.

**Methods:** Eligible comparisons evaluated a vocabulary intervention against standard care, usual classroom instruction, or no intervention without another vocabulary intervention. The outcome was a standardized or general expressive L2 vocabulary measure requiring production, naming, labeling, or definition of words not specifically taught. Proximal taught-word tests, receptive outcomes, first-language outcomes, delayed follow-ups, mixed receptive–expressive scores, and non-vocabulary outcomes were excluded. Post-test effects were synthesized with a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) model using the Standardized Mean Difference (SMD). Six effect sizes from seven studies were available for quantitative synthesis, representing 848 children.

**Results:** The pooled SMD was 0.06627870198604194 (95% CI −0.069 to 0.202; p = 0.33798725249757), favoring the vocabulary-intervention arm as specified in the analysis, but the confidence interval included no effect. Heterogeneity was estimated as I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.0, and Q = 3.24 (p = 0.6631; k = 6). Individual SMDs ranged from −0.228060 to 0.407832, and every individual 95% confidence interval included zero. Egger’s intercept was 0.0433 (p = 0.8997). Trim-and-fill estimated one potentially missing study and produced an adjusted SMD of 0.0734. Publication-bias risk was classified as moderate.

**Conclusions:** The available evidence does not show a statistically detectable immediate benefit of vocabulary interventions on distal expressive L2 vocabulary. The pooled estimate was small and imprecise enough to remain compatible with effects ranging from a small disadvantage to a small benefit. The limited number of studies, incomplete reporting of review methods and study characteristics, and low power of publication-bias analyses warrant cautious interpretation.

## Search/Screening

The review question was defined using the following eligibility framework:

- **Population:** Children aged up to and including six years who were learning an L2.
- **Intervention:** Vocabulary-focused intervention, including shared book reading, dialogic reading, explicit word teaching, dual-language instruction, or a related approach.
- **Comparator:** Standard care, usual classroom instruction, or no intervention, provided that the comparator did not receive another vocabulary intervention.
- **Outcome:** Distal expressive vocabulary in the L2 at the first assessment immediately after intervention. Eligible measures required children to produce, name, label, or define words not specifically taught during the intervention.
- **Excluded outcomes:** Taught-word or otherwise proximal measures, receptive vocabulary, first-language outcomes, delayed follow-ups, mixed receptive–expressive total scores, and non-vocabulary outcomes.
- **Effect-size data:** Post-test mean, standard deviation, and number of children in each arm were the intended inputs. When several eligible outcomes were available, selection prioritized the standardized and broadest distal expressive measure, with one measure selected per trial.

The supplied results indicate that seven studies entered the evidence set and that six study-level effect sizes were available for meta-analysis. No database names, search dates, complete search strategies, deduplication counts, title/abstract screening counts, full-text exclusion counts, or reasons for individual full-text exclusions were provided. Consequently, a reproducible electronic search strategy and a numerical PRISMA flow diagram cannot be reconstructed from the supplied information. One of the seven studies did not contribute an analyzable effect size; the reason was not reported.

## Effect Sizes

The prespecified synthesis statistic was the **Standardized Mean Difference (SMD)**. Positive effects were defined as favoring the vocabulary intervention for distal expressive L2 vocabulary, while negative effects favored the comparator. The meta-analysis record indicates that native pooling was used and that the measure was selected by explicit user request.

The six individual SMDs ranged from −0.228060 to 0.407832. Four estimates were positive and two were negative, but all six 95% confidence intervals crossed zero. The smallest study, study_2_R2 (n = 20), had the least precise estimate: SMD = −0.228060 (95% CI −1.112 to 0.656). The largest positive estimate was from study_5_R5: SMD = 0.407832 (95% CI −0.091 to 0.907; n = 63). The three largest samples produced estimates close to zero or small in magnitude: study_1_R1, −0.039356; study_4_R4, 0.038085; and study_6_R6, 0.138955.

## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) synthesized six comparisons and 848 children. The pooled **Standardized Mean Difference (SMD)** was **0.06627870198604194** (95% CI **−0.069 to 0.202**; p = **0.33798725249757**). In accordance with the specified direction field, this estimate favors the vocabulary-intervention arm. Nevertheless, the effect was not statistically significant, and its 95% confidence interval included zero.

The point estimate corresponds to a very small standardized difference. Its confidence interval remains compatible with a small effect in either direction, from a modest disadvantage to a small benefit. Thus, the analysis does not establish that vocabulary interventions improve immediate distal expressive vocabulary in the L2, even though the pooled point estimate is on the intervention-favoring side of the null.

## Heterogeneity

Estimated statistical heterogeneity was absent:

- **Cochran’s Q:** 3.24 with 5 degrees of freedom
- **Q-test p value:** 0.6631
- **I²:** 0.0%
- **τ²:** 0.0

These values indicate that the observed dispersion was no greater than expected from sampling error under the fitted model. The heterogeneity summary is shown at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/heterogeneity_summary.png`.

An I² of 0.0% should not be interpreted as proof that the studies had identical true effects. With only six effect sizes, both the Q test and between-study variance estimation have limited power. Moreover, potentially important clinical and methodological differences could not be examined because intervention, population, setting, and measurement characteristics were not supplied.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **0.0433** with **p = 0.8997**, providing no statistical evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill nevertheless estimated **one potentially missing study** and returned an adjusted SMD of **0.0734**. The supplied assessment classified publication-bias risk as **moderate**.

The trim-and-fill estimate was close to the primary pooled SMD of 0.06627870198604194, suggesting little change in the point estimate under that procedure. However, neither method can exclude selective publication or selective outcome reporting. With k = 6, asymmetry tests have low power, while trim-and-fill can be unstable and depends on assumptions that may not hold. The absence of a statistically significant Egger test should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that reporting bias is absent.

## Discussion

This meta-analysis addressed a deliberately distal outcome: children’s ability to express L2 words that were not specifically taught during the intervention. This distinction is important because gains on directly trained words may demonstrate learning of intervention content without showing broader generalization to expressive vocabulary.

Across six analyzable comparisons and 848 children, vocabulary interventions produced a pooled SMD of 0.06627870198604194. The estimate favored intervention according to the prespecified direction, but the 95% CI of −0.069 to 0.202 crossed zero and the associated p value was 0.33798725249757. The available evidence therefore does not demonstrate an immediate intervention effect on broad or standardized distal expressive L2 vocabulary.

The small pooled estimate may indicate that intervention-related learning did not generalize substantially beyond directly taught vocabulary by the first immediate post-test. Other explanations remain possible. Distal standardized tests may be less sensitive than proximal measures to short-duration intervention effects; intervention dosage may have been insufficient to affect broad vocabulary; or effects may have varied across program design, child characteristics, and language contexts. The supplied study information is too limited to evaluate these explanations.

Although estimated heterogeneity was zero, this should not be taken to imply universal equivalence among vocabulary interventions. Statistical homogeneity can arise when study estimates are imprecise or when the number of studies is small. The individual effects were all compatible with no difference, but their confidence intervals varied substantially in width. In particular, the two smallest samples produced comparatively imprecise estimates.

The findings pertain only to the first immediate post-intervention assessment and only to distal expressive vocabulary in the L2. They do not address receptive vocabulary, performance on specifically taught words, first-language outcomes, mixed language scores, non-vocabulary language outcomes, or maintenance at delayed follow-up. Consequently, the result should not be generalized to the overall effectiveness of these interventions across all language domains.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

No formal `validation_flags` field was included in the supplied context. Nevertheless, several data constraints are evident from the analysis record:

1. Seven studies were represented, but only six effect sizes were available; the reason the seventh study did not contribute to pooling was not supplied.
2. Search, screening, study-characteristic, and risk-of-bias data were absent, preventing reconstruction of a complete PRISMA flow and limiting assessment of internal and external validity.
3. Bibliographic identifiers for the primary studies were unavailable, so study labels could not be mapped to full citations.
4. The analysis metadata records that SMD was retained as the authoritative requested effect measure despite a discrepancy with terminology reportedly used in the underlying papers. Results in this report are therefore consistently labeled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD), as specified by the meta-analysis record.
5. The moderator underlying the generated bubble chart was not identified, so the chart cannot support a reported moderator conclusion.

## Limitations

First, only six effect sizes were pooled. This limits precision, reduces power to detect heterogeneity and funnel-plot asymmetry, and precludes reliable examination of moderators. Second, the pooled confidence interval permits both a small adverse effect and a small beneficial effect; absence of statistical significance is not proof of equivalence.

Third, the supplied information did not include risk-of-bias assessments. Potential concerns involving randomization, allocation concealment, baseline imbalance, missing data, outcome measurement, contamination, clustering, and selective reporting could not be evaluated. If any studies used cluster allocation but were analyzed without accounting for clustering, their precision could be overstated; the available data do not indicate whether such adjustment was needed or performed.

Fourth, study characteristics and outcome instrument names were unavailable. It was therefore impossible to assess clinical comparability, verify independently that every selected measure was distal and expressive, or determine how broadly the findings apply across languages, educational systems, ages, and intervention models.

Fifth, the use of one selected outcome per study reduces double-counting but may introduce outcome-selection sensitivity when several eligible measures exist. No sensitivity analysis using alternative eligible outcomes was reported. Sixth, publication-bias methods were applied to only six effects and should be considered exploratory. Finally, no protocol or registration information was supplied, and deviations from prespecified review methods cannot be assessed.

## Conclusion

Among children aged six years or younger learning an L2, vocabulary interventions did not produce a statistically detectable improvement in distal expressive L2 vocabulary at the first immediate post-intervention assessment. The REML pooled SMD was 0.06627870198604194 (95% CI −0.069 to 0.202; p = 0.33798725249757), favoring intervention according to the specified direction but representing a very small and uncertain effect. Estimated heterogeneity was I² = 0.0% and τ² = 0.0, although the small evidence base limits confidence in those estimates. Publication-bias risk was classified as moderate.

Future trials should be adequately powered, distinguish distal expressive outcomes from taught-word and receptive outcomes, report cluster-adjusted estimates where appropriate, and include both immediate and longer-term assessments. Future syntheses would benefit from complete study-level characteristics, prespecified outcome-selection rules, formal risk-of-bias assessment, and moderator analyses based on intervention intensity, instructional approach, child age, and language context.
