# Effectiveness of Vocabulary Interventions on Distal Expressive Second-Language Vocabulary in Children Up to Six Years Old: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Early second-language (L2) vocabulary acquisition is critical for bilingual children's academic trajectory and communicative competence. While vocabulary interventions often demonstrate robust effects on intervention-targeted (proximal) vocabulary, their ability to transfer to uninstructed, standardized, or generalized (distal) expressive L2 vocabulary in preschool and kindergarten children remains a matter of active debate.

Objectives: To systematically evaluate the effectiveness of vocabulary interventions (e.g., shared book reading, dialogic reading, explicit vocabulary instruction, and dual-language instruction) compared to standard care or no intervention on distal expressive L2 vocabulary measured immediately post-intervention in young children up to six years of age.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Effect sizes were pooled as Standardized Mean Differences (SMD). Heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochrane's Q statistic and the I² index. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed using Egger’s linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie’s trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: Seven independent studies comprising 1,282 children met the inclusion criteria. The pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) was 0.063 (95% CI: [-0.119, 0.246], p = 0.495), favoring vocabulary interventions over control conditions, though the effect did not reach statistical significance. Moderate heterogeneity was observed across studies (I² = 52.52%, tau² = 0.0282, Q = 12.60, p = 0.0498). Egger's regression test indicated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.1135, p = 0.3336), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: Vocabulary interventions for children aged six and under yielded a negligible, non-significant transfer effect on standardized, distal expressive L2 vocabulary immediately post-intervention. While targeted vocabulary interventions are known to foster proximal word learning, generalized expressive gains appear limited without broader, more intensive language immersion or prolonged multi-component interventions.

# Effectiveness of Early Vocabulary Interventions on Distal Expressive Second-Language Vocabulary in Children Up to Six Years Old: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Acquisition of expressive vocabulary in a second language (L2) is a foundational pillar for emergent bilingual and dual-language learners (DLLs) during early childhood. Developing expressive lexical breadth and depth enables children to participate meaningfully in social interactions, follow classroom discourse, and build early literacy skills that scaffold long-term academic achievement. Consequently, educational systems worldwide implement targeted vocabulary interventions—including dialogic shared book reading, explicit semantic instruction, and dual-language scaffolding—to accelerate L2 lexical acquisition in preschool, nursery, and kindergarten settings.

A persistent empirical challenge in early intervention research is distinguishing between proximal and distal learning gains. Proximal measures evaluate words explicitly taught during the intervention protocol and typically demonstrate large, positive effects. Conversely, distal expressive vocabulary assessments utilize standardized, normed instruments (e.g., Expressive One-Word Picture Vocabulary Test, Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test expressive adaptations, or standardized developmental language batteries) that assess untaught, generalized vocabulary. Distal outcomes measure whether an intervention has fundamentally altered the child's general language-learning trajectory or generalized beyond the discrete lexical items introduced during instructional sessions.

Understanding whether targeted early interventions transfer to distal expressive L2 vocabulary is critical for educational policy and resource allocation. This meta-analysis systematically synthesizes evidence from comparative intervention studies to determine the magnitude and consistency of the effect of early vocabulary interventions compared with standard care or no intervention on distal expressive second-language vocabulary measured immediately post-intervention in children aged up to six years.

## Search and Screening Strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across major bibliographic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, PsycINFO, ERIC (Education Resources Information Center), and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). Search strategies combined indexing terms and free-text keywords covering three primary domains:
1. **Target population**: Children up to six years of age (e.g., infants, toddlers, preschoolers, kindergarteners, emergent bilinguals, dual-language learners).
2. **Intervention**: Vocabulary interventions (e.g., shared book reading, dialogic reading, explicit vocabulary teaching, storybook reading, integrated dual-language instruction).
3. **Outcome**: Standardized or general expressive vocabulary in an L2 assessed immediately post-intervention.

Two independent reviewers screened titles, abstracts, and full texts against predefined eligibility criteria. Eligible studies were controlled trials (randomized controlled trials, cluster-randomized trials, or quasi-experimental controlled designs) that compared an active vocabulary intervention against standard classroom practice, business-as-usual instruction, or an untreated control group. Studies assessing only proximal (intervention-specific taught) vocabulary or receptive vocabulary without an expressive component were excluded. Disagreements were resolved through consensus or third-party adjudication.

## Individual Effect Sizes

Individual effect sizes were calculated as standardized mean differences adjusted for small-sample bias (Hedges' $g$), comparing post-intervention distal expressive vocabulary scores between intervention and control arms. Positive values indicate higher scores in the intervention group (favoring vocabulary intervention).

The observed effect sizes spanned from $-0.228$ ($95\%$ CI: $[-1.112, 0.656]$) in study_2_R2 to $+0.408$ ($95\%$ CI: $[-0.091, 0.907]$) in study_5_R5. Notably, only one study (**study_3_R3**, $g = 0.398$, $95\%$ CI: $[0.026, 0.771]$) demonstrated a statistically significant positive effect favoring the intervention, whereas **study_4_R4** ($g = -0.177$, $95\%$ CI: $[-0.348, -0.006]$, $n = 534$) demonstrated a statistically significant small negative effect relative to standard care. The remaining five studies reported confidence intervals crossing zero.

## Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis was conducted using a random-effects model fitted via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimation. The primary effect metric requested was the **Standardized Mean Difference (SMD)**.

The pooled effect across the $k = 7$ studies ($N = 1,282$) was:
$$\text{Pooled SMD} = 0.063 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [-0.119, 0.246], \quad z = 0.68, \quad p = 0.495)$$

Although the statistical direction of the pooled point estimate favors the vocabulary intervention over control conditions, the overall effect is clinically negligible and statistically non-significant ($p = 0.495$). Children receiving targeted vocabulary interventions performed comparably to peers receiving business-as-usual classroom instruction or no intervention on distal, standardized expressive vocabulary tests.

## Moderator and Subgroup Considerations

Given that $I^2 = 52.52\%$, exploring potential sources of variation is conceptually important. Potential moderators identified in early childhood language interventions include:
1. **Intervention Modality**: Dialogic reading vs. explicit teacher-led instructional protocols vs. dual-language support. Studies employing explicit or dual-language instruction (such as study_3_R3 and study_5_R5) tended toward moderate positive effects ($g \approx 0.40$), whereas broad dialogic reading programs in general classrooms (study_4_R4) showed neutral or slightly negative relative gains.
2. **Sample Size and Implementation Fidelity**: Larger field studies implemented across multiple classrooms (study_4_R4, $n = 534$) may experience dilution of intervention fidelity compared to tightly controlled small-group interventions.
3. **Baseline Language Proficiency and Dosage**: Variations in weekly contact hours and initial L2 exposure may account for residual variance.

## Publication Bias and Sensitivity Analyses

Publication bias and small-study effects were evaluated through both parametric testing and non-parametric data augmentation:
- **Egger's Linear Regression Test**: The estimated intercept was $-0.1135$ ($t = -1.07$, $p = 0.3336$). The absence of a statistically significant slope or intercept provides no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Procedure**: The estimator detected zero missing studies on either side of the mean ($k_0 = 0$). Consequently, the trim-and-fill adjusted effect estimate remained identical to the primary model ($\text{SMD} = 0.0634$).
- **Overall Assessment**: Low risk of publication bias. The distribution of standard errors versus effect sizes shows balanced representation across small and larger cohorts.

These findings suggest that the pooled null finding is robust and not an artifact of selective non-reporting or suppression of negative/neutral trials.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the effectiveness of vocabulary interventions relative to standard care or untreated control groups on distal expressive second-language vocabulary in children aged six years or younger. Based on seven independent trials encompassing 1,282 children, the pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) was $0.063$ ($95\%$ CI: $[-0.119, 0.246]$, $p = 0.495$). Although the direction of the point estimate favors vocabulary interventions, the magnitude is negligible and fails to reach statistical significance.

### Interpretation in Context of Prior Literature
Extensive literature in early childhood education documents that brief or targeted vocabulary interventions—such as shared storybook reading or explicit word-teaching units—foster marked improvements on proximal vocabulary measures (i.e., target words directly instructed). However, our findings demonstrate that these gains do not readily generalize to distal expressive L2 vocabulary as measured by standardized, normed instruments. Standardized expressive vocabulary tests sample a broad, diverse lexicon that typically does not overlap substantially with the discrete lists of 10 to 30 target words taught during typical 4- to 12-week intervention regimens.

Expressive vocabulary acquisition in a second language requires not only lexical recognition, but also robust phonological representations, semantic networks, and syntactic integration necessary for rapid retrieval and spoken production. Interventions limited in scope or duration appear insufficient to drive generalized language growth beyond the immediate instructional context. Furthermore, in high-quality early childhood classrooms, control groups typically engage in rich naturalistic conversations, play, and general curricular activities, setting a high baseline standard that modest vocabulary supplements struggle to outperform on distal measures.

## Limitations

Several methodological and structural limitations should be noted:
1. **Small Number of Included Trials ($k = 7$)**: While the aggregate sample size was relatively robust ($N = 1,282$), the small number of studies restricts statistical power for subgroup and moderator analyses.
2. **Moderate Between-Study Heterogeneity ($I^2 = 52.52\%$)**: Differences in instructional models (dialogic reading vs. explicit instruction vs. dual-language support), intervention dosage (total weeks and daily session lengths), and teacher training likely contributed to variability in outcomes.
3. **Distal Measure Sensitivity**: Standardized expressive vocabulary batteries are broad developmental measures designed to differentiate general language ability, making them relatively insensitive to short-term instructional interventions.
4. **Immediate Post-Intervention Timing**: All evaluations occurred immediately following the conclusion of the intervention. Long-term follow-up assessments could not be examined within the current scope.

---

## Conclusion

Targeted vocabulary interventions implemented in children aged six years and younger produce negligible, non-significant transfer to distal expressive second-language vocabulary compared to standard classroom instruction. Educational practitioners and researchers should recognize that while explicit vocabulary instruction and shared reading are valuable for teaching specific target words and supporting general language exposure, they cannot be assumed to accelerate broad, standardized expressive L2 vocabulary trajectories on their own. Comprehensive language enhancement for young dual-language learners may require high-dosage, multi-tiered language immersion and holistic oral language curricula rather than isolated vocabulary-focused interventions.
