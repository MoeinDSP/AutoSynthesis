# Metformin Plus Megestrol Acetate Versus Megestrol Acetate Alone for Complete Histological Regression of Endometrial Hyperplasia: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis of Randomised Trials

## Abstract

## Abstract

**Background:** Megestrol acetate is used as conservative therapy for endometrial hyperplasia. Metformin has been proposed as an adjunct that may improve complete histological regression.

**Objective:** To determine whether randomised controlled trials show that metformin plus megestrol acetate increases complete histological regression at the end of approximately 3–6 months of treatment compared with megestrol acetate alone.

**Methods:** Eligible comparisons were randomised combination-therapy and megestrol-alone arms in women with endometrial hyperplasia, with or without atypia. Complete regression was defined as return to proliferative, secretory, atrophic, or otherwise normal endometrium; persistence, progression, and partial response were non-events. Four studies contributed four effect sizes and 258 participants. Odds ratios were pooled using a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood model. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed with Cochran’s Q, I², and τ². Small-study effects were assessed using Egger’s test and trim-and-fill.

**Results:** The pooled Odds Ratio (OR) was 2.7624625897383948 (95% CI 1.392–5.484; p = 0.0036792212160975802; k = 4; N = 258). Heterogeneity was not detected (Q = 3.851, p = 0.278; I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0). The supplied favourability field classified the result as “neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).” Consequently, although the event was specified as complete regression and the numerical OR exceeded 1, arm-level favourability is not independently reassigned in this report. Egger’s intercept was −0.2761 (p = 0.2276), and trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀ = 0); publication-bias risk was classified as low.

**Conclusions:** The pooled analysis identified a statistically significant association between treatment allocation and complete histological regression, with an OR of 2.7624625897383948. However, the supplied direction classifier did not confirm that this estimate favoured either arm. Interpretation is further limited by only four trials, unavailable study-level event counts and trial characteristics, and insufficient information for risk-of-bias assessment or subgroup analysis.

## Introduction

Endometrial hyperplasia is characterised by abnormal proliferation of the endometrial glands and encompasses disease with and without atypia. Progestin therapy, including megestrol acetate, is used for conservative management in selected women. Metformin may plausibly augment progestin treatment through metabolic and antiproliferative mechanisms, but its clinical benefit should be established from randomised comparisons using histological outcomes.

This review addressed the following question: among women with endometrial hyperplasia, with or without atypia, do randomised controlled trials show that metformin plus megestrol acetate increases complete histological regression at the end of treatment compared with megestrol acetate alone?

## Search and Screening

### Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies were randomised controlled trials enrolling women with endometrial hyperplasia, with or without atypia, and directly comparing:

- **Treatment:** metformin plus megestrol acetate; and
- **Control:** megestrol acetate alone.

The outcome was complete histological regression at the end of the treatment course, approximately 3–6 months after treatment initiation. Complete regression was defined as return to proliferative, secretory, atrophic, or otherwise normal endometrium. Persistence, progression, and partial response were treated as non-events. Only the eligible combination and megestrol-alone arms were to be analysed. Where atypical and non-atypical hyperplasia were reported separately within a trial, the prespecified approach was to sum events and denominators across strata. Reported percentages were to be converted to integer event counts using arm denominators, with randomised arm sizes used when analysed denominators were unavailable.

### Search and selection information

The supplied results contained four included studies and four effect sizes. No database names, search dates, complete search strings, deduplication counts, numbers screened, full-text exclusions, or reasons for exclusion were provided. A reproducible search strategy and numerical PRISMA flow diagram therefore cannot be reconstructed from the available material. No additional records were inferred or invented.

## Included Studies

Four randomised studies, labelled `study_1_R1` through `study_4_R4`, contributed a total of 258 participants. Their total sample sizes were 102, 56, 16, and 84, respectively. Each study contributed one comparison to the synthesis.

The supplied dataset did not include study authors, publication years, countries, care settings, participant ages, body mass index, diagnostic criteria, proportions with atypia, drug doses, exact treatment durations, attrition, arm-specific sample sizes, or arm-specific numbers with complete regression. It was therefore not possible to present a conventional detailed characteristics table, independently verify event-count reconstruction, or determine whether all participants were analysed according to randomised allocation.

No study-level risk-of-bias assessments were supplied. Accordingly, randomisation procedures, allocation concealment, masking, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, and other potential biases could not be judged.

## Meta-Analytic Result

A random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood included all four studies and 258 participants. The pooled **Odds Ratio (OR)** was **2.7624625897383948** (95% CI **1.392–5.484**; p = **0.0036792212160975802**).

The confidence interval excluded the null value of 1, and the model-based test was statistically significant. Nevertheless, the supplied `favors` field classified this estimate as **“neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).”** In accordance with the prespecified reporting instruction, this report uses that supplied classification rather than independently inferring which arm was favoured from the numerical OR. Thus, the analysis demonstrates a statistically significant difference in the odds of the coded outcome, but the accompanying direction metadata does not confirm arm-level favourability.

This direction-label limitation is important because the clinical event was described as complete histological regression and the research protocol stated that an OR above 1 would ordinarily represent more complete regression with metformin plus megestrol acetate. The discrepancy between that stated coding convention and the supplied favourability field should be resolved against the original arm-level event data before making a definitive treatment-direction claim.

## Heterogeneity

Statistical heterogeneity was not detected:

- Cochran’s Q = **3.851** with p = **0.278**;
- I² = **0.0%**; and
- τ² = **0.0**.

These results indicate that the model estimated no between-study variance. However, with only four studies, Q has low power to identify genuine heterogeneity, while I² and τ² are imprecisely estimated. The apparent consistency should therefore not be interpreted as proof that the treatment effect is identical across populations or protocols. ## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **−0.2761** with p = **0.2276**. This was not statistically significant and therefore did not provide evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0** missing studies. The supplied publication-bias classification was **low**.

A trim-and-fill value labelled `trim_fill_g` was also supplied as **0.5259**. Because this value is on a g-labelled scale rather than the confirmed native OR scale, it is not interpreted as an adjusted odds ratio and should not be compared numerically with the pooled OR of 2.7624625897383948. As no studies were imputed, the trim-and-fill procedure did not indicate a need to alter the set of included studies.

These analyses are weakly powered when only four studies are available. A non-significant Egger test and k₀ = 0 do not exclude publication bias, selective outcome reporting, or other small-study effects.

## Discussion

### Principal findings

This synthesis incorporated four randomised comparisons with 258 participants. The random-effects model yielded an Odds Ratio (OR) of 2.7624625897383948 (95% CI 1.392–5.484; p = 0.0036792212160975802). Estimated statistical heterogeneity was absent (I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0), and the available diagnostics did not identify funnel-plot asymmetry or missing studies.

The result is statistically compatible with a substantial difference between regimens in the odds of the coded outcome. However, the supplied direction classifier identified neither arm as favoured. This conflicts with the stated event definition and expected coding convention, under which an OR above 1 would normally indicate more complete regression in the combination arm. Without study-level event tables, this report cannot audit whether the OR was oriented as treatment versus control, whether an inverse ratio was used, or whether the event was inadvertently coded as non-regression. Consequently, the numerical association should not be converted into a definitive claim that combination therapy improves regression until coding is verified.

### Clinical interpretation

Complete histological regression is clinically meaningful because it indicates normalisation of the endometrium rather than merely partial improvement. If the event and arm coding are confirmed as prespecified, an OR greater than 1 would be consistent with greater odds of complete regression under combination therapy. Odds ratios, however, do not directly convey absolute benefit. The baseline regression risk in the megestrol-alone arms was not provided, so absolute risk differences and numbers needed to treat cannot be calculated.

The analysis combined women with and without atypia where trials reported these groups separately. This matches the prespecified question but may conceal clinically important effect modification. Atypical hyperplasia carries different malignant potential and may respond differently from non-atypical disease. Separate estimates would be desirable if adequate trial-level data become available.

### Certainty and applicability

The randomised design of the included studies is a strength, as is use of complete histological regression rather than a surrogate symptom outcome. Nonetheless, certainty cannot be formally graded from the supplied information. Risk of bias was not assessed, confidence in the exact outcome coding is limited, and the confidence interval remains compatible with effects ranging from an OR of 1.392 to 5.484. Applicability also cannot be assessed without clinical details concerning participants, pathology review, dosing, duration, and follow-up.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

Several validation constraints require explicit attention:

1. The pooled analysis was confirmed as native binary pooling using the Odds Ratio (OR), but the supplied study-level displays were Hedges’ g values. These are different effect scales and should not be conflated.
2. Arm-specific complete-regression events and denominators were not supplied. Event reconstruction, stratum summation, attrition handling, and OR orientation could not be independently verified.
3. The supplied favourability classification was “neither,” despite the prespecified statement that an OR above 1 would indicate more regression with combination therapy. This unresolved inconsistency prevents a definitive directional treatment claim.
4. The publication-bias output included a g-labelled trim-and-fill value of 0.5259, which is not a confirmed adjusted OR and was therefore not interpreted on the native outcome scale.
5. Search records, screening counts, excluded reports, duplicate-publication checks, and study citations were unavailable. PRISMA flow and protection against double-counting could not be independently validated.
6. No outcomes required separate meta-analysis groups in the supplied data; one end-of-treatment complete-regression synthesis was reported.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations:

- Only four trials and 258 participants were included, limiting precision, heterogeneity assessment, publication-bias diagnostics, and subgroup analysis.
- Study-level event counts and arm denominators were unavailable, precluding independent calculation of odds ratios, absolute effects, and numbers needed to treat.
- Trial identities and bibliographic details were not provided, preventing assessment of overlapping cohorts, duplicate reports, retractions, or study provenance.
- Search methods and screening results were not supplied, so completeness of evidence retrieval cannot be evaluated.
- No risk-of-bias information was available.
- Clinical and methodological characteristics—including atypia distribution, pathology criteria, doses, treatment duration, adherence, attrition, and intention-to-treat handling—were unavailable.
- The study-level standardised effects and pooled native binary effect were reported on different scales.
- The supplied favourability field conflicts with the expected interpretation based on the stated event coding; this is the most consequential validation issue.
- Egger’s test and trim-and-fill have very limited reliability with four studies.
- End-of-treatment regression does not establish durability, recurrence risk, fertility outcomes, progression to carcinoma, adverse effects, or long-term safety.

## Conclusion

Across four randomised studies with 258 participants, the random-effects pooled Odds Ratio (OR) for complete histological regression was 2.7624625897383948 (95% CI 1.392–5.484; p = 0.0036792212160975802), with Q = 3.851 (p = 0.278), I² = 0.0%, and τ² = 0.0. Publication-bias diagnostics were not statistically significant, although they were underpowered.

The pooled result establishes a statistically significant difference in the odds of the coded outcome, but the supplied metadata classified the result as favouring neither arm. The evidence therefore cannot be stated definitively to show that metformin plus megestrol acetate increases complete regression until treatment/control orientation and event coding are checked against the original arm-level data. Future updates should provide complete event tables, trial characteristics, risk-of-bias assessments, and separate results by atypia status where feasible.
