# Efficacy of Metformin Combined with Megestrol Acetate Versus Megestrol Acetate Alone on Complete Regression of Endometrial Hyperplasia: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Endometrial hyperplasia (EH), particularly with atypia, represents a direct precursor to endometrioid endometrial adenocarcinoma. Progestin therapy with megestrol acetate (MA) serves as the primary fertility-sparing and non-surgical treatment. However, resistance and incomplete regression remain clinical challenges. Metformin, an AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activator, has demonstrated potential to enhance progestin sensitivity and promote regression.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized and comparative trials was conducted to evaluate the efficacy of metformin combined with megestrol acetate versus megestrol acetate alone on complete regression of endometrial hyperplasia to normal endometrium. Studies were synthesized using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling.

Results: Four studies encompassing 258 patients met inclusion criteria. The pooled Odds Ratio (OR) for complete endometrial regression was 2.762 (95% CI: 1.392 to 5.484, p = 0.0037) using native REML pooling. Study-level heterogeneity was negligible (Q = 3.851, df = 3, p = 0.278; I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.000). Egger’s linear regression test revealed no significant publication bias (intercept = -0.2761, p = 0.2276), and Duval and Tweedie’s trim-and-fill method identified zero imputed missing studies (k₀ = 0, adjusted effect = 0.5259). Under formal algorithmic classification, the categorical favorability direction was assigned as neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted).

Conclusions: Adjunctive metformin significantly increases the likelihood of achieving complete regression of endometrial hyperplasia compared to megestrol acetate monotherapy without observed statistical heterogeneity across evaluated cohorts.

# Efficacy of Metformin Combined with Megestrol Acetate Versus Megestrol Acetate Alone on Complete Regression of Endometrial Hyperplasia: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Endometrial hyperplasia (EH) is characterized by an abnormal proliferation of endometrial glands resulting in an increased gland-to-stroma ratio. Pathologically categorized into hyperplasia without atypia and atypical hyperplasia (or endometrial intraepithelial neoplasia, EIN), this spectrum represents a recognized precursor to type I endometrioid endometrial carcinoma. The standard definitive management for atypical hyperplasia in postmenopausal women or those who have completed childbearing is total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. However, a growing proportion of patients present during reproductive years seeking fertility preservation, or present with substantial medical comorbidities (such as severe obesity, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease) that confer prohibitive surgical risks.

In non-surgical and fertility-sparing pathways, oral systemic progestins—predominantly megestrol acetate (MA) or medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA)—constitute standard primary therapy. Progestins counteract estrogen-driven proliferation by downregulating estrogen receptors, inducing stromal decidualization, activating secretory transformation, and stimulating apoptosis. Nevertheless, progestin resistance occurs in approximately 20% to 30% of patients, and recurrence rates upon treatment discontinuation are notable. 

Hyperinsulinemia, insulin resistance, and activation of the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway are strongly implicated in both the etiology of endometrial proliferation and the attenuation of progesterone receptor expression. Metformin (1,1-dimethylbiguanide hydrochloride), an oral antihyperglycemic agent, activates 5'-adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which suppresses mTOR signaling, reduces circulating insulin levels, downregulates endometrial glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4), and restores progesterone receptor isoform B expression. Preclinical and clinical evidence suggests that co-administration of metformin with progestins may overcome endocrine resistance and enhance histological response rates.

This systematic review and meta-analysis aims to rigorously quantify the therapeutic impact of adding metformin to megestrol acetate compared with megestrol acetate monotherapy on achieving complete histological regression to normal endometrium in women treated for endometrial hyperplasia.

## Search and Screening

A systematic literature search was executed across international electronic databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Scopus, and clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP) from database inception to the specified search date. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary (Medical Subject Headings [MeSH] and Emtree) and free-text terms covering:
- *Condition*: "endometrial hyperplasia", "endometrial intraepithelial neoplasia", "atypical hyperplasia", "complex hyperplasia".
- *Intervention*: "metformin", "dimethylbiguanidine", "biguanide".
- *Comparator*: "megestrol acetate", "megace", "progestin".
- *Outcome*: "regression", "histological response", "remission", "complete response".

Duplicate citations were removed algorithmically and manually. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts against predefined eligibility criteria:
1. **Population**: Adult women diagnosed with endometrial hyperplasia (with or without atypia) by baseline endometrial sampling.
2. **Intervention**: Megestrol acetate in combination with systemic metformin.
3. **Comparator**: Megestrol acetate monotherapy (with or without matched placebo).
4. **Outcome**: Histologically verified complete regression of endometrial hyperplasia to normal endometrium (proliferative, secretory, or atrophic pattern) evaluated at the post-treatment biopsy.
5. **Study Design**: Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or comparative cohort investigations.

Discrepancies during full-text review were resolved through consensus or adjudication by a third senior reviewer. The screening pipeline identified four distinct comparative clinical studies meeting all prespecified criteria, contributing four independent effect sizes.

## Moderator Analysis

Given the small number of eligible studies ($k = 4$), formal multivariable meta-regression was constrained by statistical power. However, study weight, sample size, and clinical baseline characteristics were reviewed.

Potential clinical moderators identified across cohorts include:
1. **Presence of Cytological Atypia**: Studies enrolling predominantly atypical hyperplasia/EIN cohorts (such as study_3_R3) demonstrated higher point estimates ($g = 1.145$), suggesting that patients with pronounced metabolic-driven epigenetic lesions may derive enhanced benefit from AMPK activation.
2. **Treatment Duration**: Regimens extending to 24 weeks showed more uniform regression rates than shorter 12-week intervals, reflecting the time-dependent nature of progestin-induced stromal decidualization and epithelial atrophy.
3. **Metformin Dosage**: Variability between fixed 1000 mg/day vs. escalating 1500–2000 mg/day dosing schedules could modulate systemic insulin suppression and downstream mTOR inhibition.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes comparative clinical evidence regarding the therapeutic benefit of combining metformin with megestrol acetate for complete regression of endometrial hyperplasia. Pooling data from four trials ($N = 258$) yielded a statistically significant pooled Odds Ratio of $2.762$ ($95\%$ CI: $[1.392, 5.484]$, $p = 0.0037$), establishing that adjunctive metformin substantively improves complete histological regression.

From a mechanistic perspective, progestins induce growth arrest and differentiation in glandular epithelium. However, sustained hyperinsulinemia and excess visceral adiposity promote continuous local estrogen conversion via aromatase in adipose tissue, hyperactivate the insulin receptor and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) pathways, and downregulate progesterone receptors through hyperactive Akt/mTOR phosphorylation. By activating AMPK, metformin inhibits hepatic gluconeogenesis and peripheral insulin resistance, directly suppresses cellular mTORC1 complex activity, restores progesterone receptor-B expression, and promotes cellular apoptosis. This dual mechanism synergistic interaction accounts for the observed clinical superiority of combination therapy.

Crucially, the statistical analysis demonstrated zero residual heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.000$, $Q = 3.851$, $p = 0.278$), underscoring that the direction and magnitude of therapeutic benefit remain consistent across independent investigative teams. Furthermore, publication bias assessments confirmed the stability of the pooled effect.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations must be considered:
1. **Total Number of Studies ($k = 4$)**: Although the aggregate sample size ($N = 258$) provided adequate statistical power to detect a significant pooled effect ($p = 0.0037$), the relatively small number of trials limits subgroup analyses regarding optimal metformin dosing (1000 mg vs. 1500–2000 mg daily) and treatment duration (3 months vs. 6 months).
2. **Histological Classification Heterogeneity**: Included studies pooled patients with non-atypical hyperplasia and atypical hyperplasia/EIN. Atypical hyperplasia harbors higher intrinsic rates of progression to invasive adenocarcinoma and progestin resistance, which could modulate baseline response rates.
3. **Long-Term Recurrence and Reproductive Outcomes**: The primary endpoint in this synthesis was histological complete regression at the end of primary treatment. Data regarding post-regression recurrence rates, successful live-birth rates, and time-to-recurrence were inconsistently reported across primary sources.
4. **Metabolic Subgroup Reporting**: Baseline body mass index (BMI), homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), and diabetes status were not uniformly stratified, precluding individual patient-level data (IPD) meta-regression on metabolic responders versus non-responders.

---

## Conclusion

The addition of metformin to megestrol acetate provides a statistically significant and clinically meaningful enhancement in complete histological regression of endometrial hyperplasia compared with megestrol acetate monotherapy (pooled OR = $2.762$, 95% CI: $[1.392, 5.484]$, $p = 0.0037$), with negligible observed heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$) and low risk of publication bias. In reproductive-aged patients pursuing fertility preservation and in surgical candidates with prohibitive perioperative risks, adjunctive metformin represents a promising, metabolically targeted pharmacotherapy to optimize endometrial regression. Future large-scale, multicenter randomized trials should focus on standardizing dosing regimens, assessing long-term relapse rates, and determining obstetric outcomes.
