# The Efficacy of Combined Dietary and Physical Activity Interventions on Adolescent Body Mass Index: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Short-Term Outcomes

## Abstract

Background: Adolescent obesity is a pressing global public health priority associated with immediate psychological morbidity and heightened long-term cardiometabolic risk. Combined lifestyle interventions targeting nutrition and physical activity represent first-line non-pharmacological strategies; however, trial outcomes vary according to intervention design and measurement metrics.

Objectives: To quantitatively synthesize the short-term efficacy of combined dietary and physical activity interventions compared to control conditions on body mass index (BMI, BMI z-score, or percentile) among adolescents aged 12 to 18 years.

Methods: A systematic literature search was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. Eligible randomized controlled trials comparing concurrent dietary and physical activity interventions against control arms (usual care, waitlist, or no intervention) in adolescent cohorts were synthesized. Because included trials reported anthropometric outcomes across heterogeneous metrics (raw BMI kg/m², BMI z-scores, and BMI percentiles), standardized mean differences (Hedges' g) were calculated via random-effects meta-analysis using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Between-study heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed using Egger's regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Seven distinct effect sizes from randomized comparisons encompassing 1,697 adolescent participants were included. The pooled standardized effect was Hedges' g = -0.109 (95% CI: -0.208 to -0.011, p = 0.0290), indicating a statistically significant reduction in BMI that favors the dietary and physical activity intervention over control. Between-study heterogeneity was negligible (Q = 6.074, df = 6, p = 0.415, I² = 0.01%, tau² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶). Egger's linear regression test indicated borderline funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.3281, p = 0.0554), though the trim-and-fill algorithm imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), yielding an unchanged adjusted effect (g = -0.1094).

Conclusions: Combined dietary and physical activity interventions produce a modest but statistically significant short-term reduction in adolescent adiposity relative to control conditions. Given borderline publication bias and reliance on standardized fallback pooling across differing anthropometric scales, findings should be interpreted with measured caution. Future trials should standardize reporting metrics and evaluate the longitudinal persistence of these anthropometric improvements.

# The Efficacy of Combined Dietary and Physical Activity Interventions on Adolescent Body Mass Index: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Short-Term Outcomes

## 1. Introduction

Adolescence represents a critical developmental period marked by rapid somatic growth, neurodevelopmental remodeling, and the consolidation of lifelong behavioral patterns. Over the past four decades, the global prevalence of overweight and obesity among children and adolescents aged 5–19 years has surged from 4% in 1975 to over 18% in recent epidemiological assessments. Adolescent obesity is directly linked to an increased risk of premature cardiometabolic dysfunction—including dyslipidemia, systemic hypertension, insulin resistance, and endothelial dysfunction—as well as adverse psychosocial sequelae, such as depression, social stigmatization, and diminished quality of life. Furthermore, adolescent adiposity exhibits strong tracking into adulthood, substantially heightening lifetime risks of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and overall mortality.

Non-pharmacological multicomponent lifestyle interventions focusing concurrently on dietary modification and physical activity promotion are established as first-line therapeutic recommendations by major pediatric and public health organizations. The theoretical rationale for combining these modalities rests on energy balance physiology: caloric intake reduction combined with increased total energy expenditure fosters an energy deficit while preserving lean muscle mass and improving metabolic flexibility. However, empirical clinical trials evaluating multicomponent lifestyle interventions in adolescent populations have yielded heterogeneous results. Discrepancies may arise from variability in intervention intensity, delivery settings (school-based, primary care, family-based, or digital), compliance challenges unique to teenage autonomy, and heterogeneity in primary anthropometric outcome definitions.

In pediatric literature, body mass index (BMI) is variously evaluated as raw metric BMI ($\text{kg/m}^2$), age- and sex-adjusted BMI z-scores (standard deviation scores, SDS), or BMI percentiles based on international reference growth charts (e.g., CDC or WHO). This review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates the evidence regarding the efficacy of dietary and physical activity interventions compared to inactive, waitlist, or usual care control conditions on short-term BMI outcomes among adolescents aged 12 to 18 years.

## 2. Search, Screening, and Identification

The systematic review and meta-analytic synthesis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) standards. 

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across core bibliographic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and PsycINFO, spanning inception through the review cut-off date. Search strings incorporated combinations of controlled vocabulary terms (MeSH and Emtree) and free-text keywords covering three conceptual domains:
1. **Population:** Adolescents, youth, teens, teenagers, secondary school students (aged 12–18 years).
2. **Intervention:** Multicomponent interventions integrating both nutritional/dietary interventions (e.g., caloric guidance, macronutrient distribution, nutrition education) and physical activity interventions (e.g., structured exercise, physical education, sedentary behavior displacement).
3. **Control & Design:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing the intervention against usual care, attention control, waitlist control, or no intervention.

The screening pipeline processed a total of 11 candidate study records. Following deduplication, title/abstract screening, and full-text eligibility appraisal against strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, 7 independent effect sizes were extracted and met all quantitative criteria for meta-analytic synthesis. The remaining records were either non-randomized designs, lacked appropriate control arms, evaluated monotherapies (dietary only or physical activity only), or reported follow-up durations outside the defined short-term follow-up window.

## 3. Included Studies and Characteristics

The meta-analytic sample encompasses $k = 7$ randomized comparisons involving an aggregate of 1,697 adolescent participants. Sample sizes across individual comparisons ranged from 33 to 559 participants, capturing both small exploratory pilot trials and adequately powered multicenter investigations.

All interventions shared the core feature of dual-component lifestyle modification, combining structured nutrition education or dietary behavior modification with physical activity programming or active lifestyle counseling. Comparators across all studies comprised passive controls, waitlist cohorts, or routine standard care/health curriculum as usual. The primary endpoint was anthropometric adiposity assessed at short-term follow-up (defined as immediate post-intervention or short-term post-intervention maintenance up to 6 months post-baseline).

## 4. Effect Size Computation & Scale Reconciliation

Individual effect sizes were calculated as standardized mean differences (Hedges' $g$) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The direction of effect is defined such that a negative raw statistical value represents a reduction in BMI, BMI z-score, or BMI percentile in the intervention group relative to the control group. In accordance with clinical terminology where lower adiposity is beneficial, this negative value favors the dietary and physical activity intervention.

A critical methodological consideration in synthesizing pediatric anthropometry is the divergence in outcome scaling:
- Several included trials explicitly reported mean differences (MD) on the raw BMI scale ($\text{kg/m}^2$).
- Other trials reported standardized age- and sex-adjusted BMI z-scores (SDS) or percentiles.

Because raw mean difference is strictly scale-dependent, unstandardized mean differences cannot be pooled when underlying metrics lack identical measurement units. Consequently, native-scale pooling could not be confirmed, and standardized-effect pooling using Hedges' $g$ was implemented as the standardized fallback methodology across all studies.

## 5. Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis of the $k = 7$ comparisons was conducted using a random-effects model parameterized via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimation. 

The pooled standardized mean difference across all 1,697 participants was:
$$\text{Pooled Hedges' } g = -0.1094 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [-0.208, -0.011]; \; z = -2.18, \; p = 0.0290)$$

Because this meta-analysis utilizes standardized fallback pooling (native pooling was not used due to unaligned outcome units across studies), this estimate represents a standardized metric difference. The result demonstrates a statistically significant reduction in BMI favoring the **Dietary and physical activity intervention** over the control conditions.

## 6. Heterogeneity Analysis

Between-study statistical heterogeneity was formally evaluated using Cochran's $Q$ test and Higgins' and Thompson's $I^2$ index.

- **Cochran's $Q$ Statistic:** $Q = 6.074$, degrees of freedom $df = 6$, $p = 0.415$
- **Between-Study Variance ($\tau^2$):** $\tau^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}$
- **Inconsistency Index ($I^2$):** $I^2 = 0.01\%$

The statistical test for heterogeneity did not reach significance ($p = 0.415$), and the estimated $I^2$ value of $0.01\%$ indicates an absence of observed excess variability between trials. Nearly 100% of the variance observed across individual study estimates can be accounted for by within-study sampling error.

## 8. Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were evaluated through Egger's linear regression test of funnel plot asymmetry and Duval and Tweedie's non-parametric trim-and-fill method.

- **Egger's Test Intercept:** $-0.3281$ ($t = -2.48$, $p = 0.0554$)
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill:** Imputed studies $k_0 = 0$
- **Trim-and-Fill Adjusted Effect:** Hedges' $g = -0.1094$ (unchanged)
- **Overall Publication Bias Risk Assessment:** Moderate

Egger’s test reveals borderline funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.0554$), suggesting potential small-study effects. The non-parametric trim-and-fill method did not impute any theoretically missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), leaving the point estimate identical at $g = -0.1094$. Consequently, the overall risk of publication bias is classified as moderate. While the pooled effect estimate appears structurally stable against standard imputation techniques, subtle selective reporting or small-trial attrition bias cannot be definitively excluded. These meta-analytic findings should therefore be interpreted with measured caution.

## 10. Discussion

This meta-analysis provides a rigorous synthesis of 7 randomized controlled trials evaluating the short-term efficacy of combined dietary and physical activity interventions compared to inactive or usual care controls in adolescents aged 12 to 18 years. The primary pooled estimate demonstrates a statistically significant effect (Hedges' $g = -0.109$, $95\%\text{ CI: } [-0.208, -0.011], p = 0.0290$), confirming that concurrent lifestyle interventions produce a greater reduction in BMI than control conditions.

Although the effect size magnitude is modest by conventional Cohen benchmarks ($|g| < 0.20$), its clinical significance warrants contextualization within pediatric obesity management. Unlike adults, growing adolescents naturally experience increases in height and lean body mass, which can attenuate reductions in raw BMI. In pediatric populations, modest standardized reductions in adiposity indices often correlate with meaningful improvements in surrogate markers of cardiometabolic health, such as reduced fasting plasma insulin, homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), systolic blood pressure, and visceral adiposity.

A notable strength of this synthesis is the minimal between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.01\%, \tau^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}, p = 0.415$). The high degree of consistency across the largest trials—such as `study_2_R2` ($g = -0.200$) and `study_3_R3` ($g = -0.153$)—reinforces the robustness of the intervention effect. The two trials reporting point estimates in the opposite direction were small pilot interventions ($n = 50$ and $n = 33$) with wide confidence intervals encompassing the null.

Nonetheless, clinical implementation requires careful consideration of adolescent developmental psychology. Dual interventions that combine nutrition education with exercise programming require sustained behavioral adherence. The short-term follow-up window captured in this analysis demonstrates initial feasibility and efficacy; however, whether these modest benefits persist into late adolescence and adulthood remains an open empirical question.

---

## 11. Limitations

Several methodological and structural limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Standardized Fallback Metric:** Because included trials reported anthropometric outcomes using distinct units (raw BMI, BMI z-score, and percentile), unstandardized mean difference pooling was not feasible. While Hedges' $g$ provides a standardized estimate, it does not directly convey physical units (such as $\text{kg/m}^2$), which can complicate direct clinical interpretation.
2. **Follow-Up Duration:** This synthesis was restricted to short-term follow-up endpoints. Long-term post-intervention maintenance (e.g., $\ge 12$ to 24 months) could not be evaluated within this cohort, leaving open whether weight regain occurs after intervention contact ceases.
3. **Borderline Funnel Asymmetry:** Egger's test indicated borderline asymmetry ($p = 0.0554$), indicating possible small-study bias. Even though trim-and-fill analysis imputed no missing studies, residual publication bias or selective reporting cannot be excluded.
4. **Behavioral Fidelity and Adherence:** Individual trials varied in intervention intensity, setting (school vs. clinical), and parental involvement. Because participant-level adherence data were inconsistently reported, dose-response relationships could not be quantified.

---

## 12. Conclusion

In conclusion, combined dietary and physical activity interventions achieve a statistically significant, modest short-term reduction in body mass index among adolescents aged 12 to 18 years compared with control conditions (Hedges' $g = -0.109$, $95\%\text{ CI: } [-0.208, -0.011]$). Between-study heterogeneity was negligible ($I^2 = 0.01\%$). However, given borderline publication bias signals and necessary reliance on standardized fallback pooling across heterogeneous anthropometric metrics, findings should be interpreted with measured caution. Future randomized trials should standardize reporting on uniform pediatric metrics (such as BMI z-score or percentage of the 95th percentile) and incorporate extended longitudinal follow-ups to evaluate long-term cardiometabolic benefits.
