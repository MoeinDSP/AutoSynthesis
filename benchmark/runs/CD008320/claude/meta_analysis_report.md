# Kappa-Opioid Receptor Agonists versus Placebo for Uremic (CKD-Associated) Pruritus: A PRISMA-Compliant Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Parallel-Group Trials Using a 0–10 cm Visual Analogue Scale

## Abstract

**Background.** Chronic kidney disease-associated (uremic) pruritus affects a large proportion of patients receiving maintenance haemodialysis and is associated with impaired sleep, depression, and increased mortality. An imbalance between mu- and kappa-opioid receptor signalling has been proposed as a mechanism, motivating the development of selective kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) agonists such as difelikefalin, nalbuphine and nalfurafine.

**Objective.** To quantify the effect of KOR agonists, pooled as a single drug class, versus placebo on pruritus severity measured on a 0–10 cm visual analogue scale (VAS) in patients with uremic pruritus, restricted to parallel-group designs.

**Methods.** A systematic search of the bibliographic corpus assembled for this review identified randomised and controlled studies of KOR agonists in uremic pruritus. Five studies were retained after full-text screening; two parallel-group studies reported VAS itch data in a form permitting quantitative synthesis (means with standard deviations or convertible statistics) for both the KOR agonist and the placebo arm. The pre-specified effect measure was the Mean Difference (MD) on the VAS scale, pooled in a random-effects model using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation of between-study variance. Heterogeneity was quantified with Cochran's Q, I² and τ². Small-study effects were to be examined by funnel plot asymmetry and regression-based tests where the number of studies permitted.

**Results.** Two studies contributing 294 randomised participants were pooled. The pooled Mean Difference was −7.753 (95% CI −13.118 to −2.387; p = 0.0046), a statistically significant result that, per the direction field carried with the analysis, favours the kappa-opioid receptor agonist class over placebo. Native-scale (mean difference) pooling was used; the effect measure was confirmed and the result is therefore reported on its native scale rather than as a standardised fallback. Between-study heterogeneity was nil (I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.0000; Q = 0.457, df = 1, p = 0.4991). The two contributing study-level standardised effects were −0.303 (95% CI −0.556 to −0.050; n = 243) and −0.466 (95% CI −1.022 to 0.091; n = 51), i.e. small-to-moderate in magnitude and consistent in direction. The magnitude of the pooled MD (−7.753 points) exceeds what is arithmetically interpretable on a 0–10 cm scale and most plausibly reflects a mixed-units issue (VAS reported in millimetres on a 0–100 mm scale in at least one contributing study) rather than a true 7.75-cm reduction; the direction and statistical significance of the estimate are therefore more trustworthy than its absolute size. With k = 2, moderator analyses and formal publication-bias testing were not possible.

**Conclusions.** Evidence from two parallel-group trials indicates that kappa-opioid receptor agonists reduce uremic pruritus severity on the VAS more than placebo, with no detectable between-study heterogeneity. Certainty in the magnitude of benefit is low: the synthesis rests on only two studies, the pooled point estimate shows a scale/units inconsistency that could not be resolved from the extracted data, and small-study effects could not be assessed. The study-level standardised effects suggest the true benefit is small to moderate rather than extremely large.

# Introduction

Chronic kidney disease-associated pruritus (CKD-aP), historically termed uremic pruritus, is reported by 40–70% of patients receiving maintenance haemodialysis, with roughly one in five describing itch as severe or extremely bothersome. Beyond the symptom itself, CKD-aP is independently associated with sleep disturbance, depressive symptoms, impaired health-related quality of life, non-adherence to dialysis prescriptions, and excess mortality. Conventional symptomatic treatments — emollients, antihistamines, gabapentinoids, ultraviolet B phototherapy — are supported by evidence of variable quality, are frequently poorly tolerated in dialysis populations, and leave a substantial proportion of patients with inadequate relief.

One mechanistic account of CKD-aP centres on dysregulated endogenous opioid signalling: relative overactivity of mu-opioid receptor (MOR) pathways, which are pruritogenic, combined with reduced kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) tone, which is antipruritic. This hypothesis predicts that selective KOR agonism should attenuate itch. Three agents have been evaluated clinically in this population: difelikefalin, a peripherally restricted selective KOR agonist now licensed in several jurisdictions for moderate-to-severe CKD-aP in haemodialysis patients; nalbuphine, a mixed KOR agonist/MOR antagonist; and nalfurafine, a centrally acting KOR agonist approved in Japan. Although individual trials have reported benefit, they differ in agent, route, dose, duration, and — critically for synthesis — in the instrument and scaling used to record itch intensity, with numerical rating scales, the 5-D itch scale, Skindex-10, and visual analogue scales (VAS) all in use.

The present systematic review and meta-analysis addresses a deliberately narrow and clinically interpretable question: in patients with uremic pruritus, what is the effect of KOR agonists — pooled as a single drug class — compared with placebo on pruritus severity measured on a 0–10 cm VAS, restricted to parallel-group studies? Confining the synthesis to a single instrument and a single design class maximises interpretability of the pooled estimate at the cost of reducing the number of eligible studies, a trade-off whose consequences are discussed in detail below.

# Methods

## Protocol and reporting

This review is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) statement. The analysis plan — population, intervention class, comparator, outcome instrument, effect measure, and design restriction — was specified before data synthesis. The effect measure (Mean Difference on the VAS) was fixed by explicit a priori request rather than selected post hoc from the data.

## Eligibility criteria

- **Population:** adults with chronic kidney disease-associated (uremic) pruritus, including patients on maintenance haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis.
- **Intervention:** any kappa-opioid receptor agonist (difelikefalin, nalbuphine, nalfurafine, or another kappa agonist), analysed as one pooled drug class irrespective of agent, dose, or route.
- **Comparator:** placebo.
- **Outcome:** pruritus severity recorded on a 0–10 cm VAS (0 = no itch, 10 = worst imaginable itch), reported either as endpoint scores or as change from baseline, with means and standard deviations (or statistics convertible to them) available for both arms.
- **Design:** parallel-group studies. Cross-over designs were not eligible for the primary synthesis because carry-over and period effects cannot be addressed consistently without paired data.
- **Exclusions:** narrative and systematic reviews, editorials, duplicate reports of the same trial population, and studies reporting itch only on instruments other than a 0–10 VAS.

## Information sources and search

Records were drawn from the bibliographic corpus assembled for this review, covering trials of opioid-receptor-targeted therapy for uremic pruritus. Titles and abstracts were screened against the eligibility criteria, and potentially eligible records advanced to full-text assessment. Reference lists of included reports and of prior reviews identified during screening were inspected for additional candidate studies.

## Data extraction

From each eligible study the following were extracted: agent, dose and schedule, route, treatment duration, dialysis modality, sample size per arm, and VAS itch means with dispersion measures per arm at the pre-specified timepoint. Where dispersion was reported as a standard error, interquartile range, or confidence interval, standard conversions to the standard deviation were applied.

## Effect measures and synthesis

The primary effect measure was the **Mean Difference (MD)** between the KOR agonist and placebo arms on the VAS scale. Pooling was performed on the native MD scale (native pooling confirmed), using a random-effects model with between-study variance estimated by restricted maximum likelihood (REML) and Wald-type 95% confidence intervals. Study-level standardised effects (Hedges' *g*, bias-corrected) were additionally computed to permit comparison of relative magnitude across studies with differing scaling; these are reported descriptively and were not the basis of the pooled estimate.

Heterogeneity was assessed with Cochran's Q, the I² statistic, and the REML estimate of τ². Pre-specified moderator analyses (by agent, dose, route and treatment duration) were to be undertaken by random-effects meta-regression if at least ten studies contributed; subgroup analyses required at least four studies per subgroup. Publication bias was to be assessed by contour-enhanced funnel plot and Egger's regression if at least ten studies contributed. Sensitivity to the influence of any single study was to be examined by leave-one-out and cumulative analysis.

**Direction convention.** The analysis carries an explicit direction field identifying the favoured arm. Raw pooled values retain the treatment-minus-control sign convention, under which a lower VAS score is the clinically better outcome; the favoured arm is therefore reported from the direction field rather than inferred from the sign of the point estimate.

# Search and Screening

Screening of the assembled corpus against the eligibility criteria yielded **five studies** retained after full-text assessment as relevant to kappa-opioid receptor agonism in uremic pruritus. Of these, **two studies** reported pruritus severity on a 0–10 cm VAS in a parallel-group design with arm-level means and dispersion statistics sufficient for quantitative pooling, and these two constitute the meta-analytic dataset (k = 2; **294 participants** in total). The remaining three studies were retained in the qualitative review but could not enter the quantitative synthesis, for reasons that in this literature are typically one or more of the following: (i) itch severity reported exclusively on a numerical rating scale, 5-D itch scale, or other non-VAS instrument; (ii) a cross-over design, excluded by the pre-specified design restriction; or (iii) results presented only as responder proportions, medians without convertible dispersion, or graphically, without extractable means and standard deviations.

The effective screening yield — two of five topic-relevant studies contributing data, i.e. 40% — is the single most consequential feature of this review and is revisited in the Limitations section. No PRISMA flow diagram figure was generated by the analysis pipeline; the counts above (five studies assessed, two pooled, 294 participants analysed) constitute the flow information available.

# Included Studies

Two parallel-group studies contributed to the synthesis, identified in the analysis dataset as **study_1_R1** and **study_2_R2**.

| Study ID | Design | Analysed N | Study-level Hedges' *g* | 95% CI | Relative contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| study_1_R1 | Parallel-group, placebo-controlled | 243 | −0.303 | −0.556 to −0.050 | Dominant (82.7% of pooled sample) |
| study_2_R2 | Parallel-group, placebo-controlled | 51 | −0.466 | −1.022 to 0.091 | Minor (17.3% of pooled sample) |
| **Total** | — | **294** | — | — | — |

Both studies compared a kappa-opioid receptor agonist against placebo in patients with uremic pruritus and reported VAS itch severity. study_1_R1 is substantially the larger of the two, contributing 243 of the 294 analysed participants, and consequently dominates the inverse-variance weighting of the pooled estimate. study_2_R2, with 51 participants, is small enough that its confidence interval crosses the line of no effect.

Because the review protocol pooled all kappa agonists as a single drug class, the included set is heterogeneous at the level of pharmacology — peripherally restricted versus centrally penetrant agents, and selective KOR agonism versus mixed KOR-agonist/MOR-antagonist activity, are not distinguished in the pooled estimate. This is a deliberate design choice consistent with the stated research question, but it means the pooled value should be read as a class-level signal rather than as an estimate applicable to any single agent.

# Effect Sizes

Study-level standardised effects were computed to allow magnitude comparison across studies:

- **study_1_R1:** Hedges' *g* = **−0.303277** (95% CI −0.556 to −0.050), n = 243. The interval excludes zero, indicating a statistically significant reduction in itch severity relative to placebo, of small magnitude by conventional benchmarks.
- **study_2_R2:** Hedges' *g* = **−0.465645** (95% CI −1.022 to 0.091), n = 51. The point estimate is somewhat larger — small-to-moderate — but the interval spans zero, so this study alone is not statistically conclusive.

Both standardised estimates point in the same direction, and their confidence intervals overlap substantially. Taken together they imply a true class effect in the small-to-moderate range (roughly *g* ≈ −0.3 to −0.5), which for a 0–10 cm VAS with typical between-patient standard deviations of 2–3 cm corresponds to an absolute reduction on the order of 0.6–1.5 cm relative to placebo.

# Meta-Analytic Result

A single meta-analytic group was defined (one outcome construct, one timepoint); no additional outcome/timepoint groups required separate reporting.

**Pooled result (random-effects, REML; k = 2; N = 294):**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Effect measure | **Mean Difference (MD)** |
| Pooled Mean Difference | **−7.753** |
| 95% confidence interval | **−13.118 to −2.387** |
| p-value | **0.0046** |
| Favoured arm (per direction field) | **Kappa-opioid receptor agonist class** |
| Studies (k) | 2 |
| Participants (N) | 294 |
| Between-study variance estimator | REML |
| Native-scale pooling | **Confirmed (native MD pooling used)** |

The pooled effect is statistically significant at conventional thresholds (p = 0.0046), and the 95% confidence interval excludes the null throughout its range. Per the direction field accompanying the analysis, the result **favours the kappa-opioid receptor agonist class over placebo**, i.e. patients receiving a KOR agonist reported lower VAS itch severity than those receiving placebo.

The effect measure was pooled on its native scale — the Mean Difference — and native pooling was confirmed by the analysis. The pooled value is therefore a genuine native-scale mean difference and **not** a standardised fallback; it should be named a *pooled Mean Difference* and must not be described as Hedges' *g*, notwithstanding any contrary phrasing in auto-generated narrative summaries.

**An important caveat on the magnitude.** A mean difference of −7.753 points cannot be literally interpreted on a 0–10 cm VAS: a 7.75-cm placebo-adjusted reduction would exceed the plausible dynamic range of the instrument for the baseline severities typically enrolled in these trials, and it is flatly inconsistent with the study-level standardised effects of −0.303 and −0.466 reported above, which imply a benefit roughly an order of magnitude smaller. The most parsimonious explanation is a **units inconsistency** in the extracted data: VAS itch is commonly recorded in millimetres on a 0–100 mm line, and a −7.75 mm placebo-adjusted difference (≈ −0.78 cm) is entirely consistent with the observed standardised effects. A secondary possibility is underestimation of within-study standard deviations during conversion from reported dispersion statistics, which would inflate precision and, in combination with mixed scaling, distort the pooled location. Readers should therefore treat the **direction and the statistical significance** of this result as the reliable findings, and the **absolute magnitude as uncertain and probably overstated by a factor consistent with a cm/mm conversion**. The standardised study-level effects are the better guide to the clinical size of the benefit.

The forest plot for the pooled analysis is available at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/forest_plot.png`. A cumulative meta-analysis, ordered by study contribution, is provided at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/cumulative_plot.png`; with k = 2 this reduces to the dominant study followed by the pooled estimate and demonstrates that the overall finding is driven predominantly by study_1_R1. A formal leave-one-out sensitivity analysis is not informative at k = 2, since omitting either study leaves a single trial rather than a meta-analysis; it is worth stating explicitly that removing study_1_R1 would leave only study_2_R2, whose own confidence interval includes the null.

# Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was **nil** on every available index:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| I² | **0.0%** |
| τ² | **0.0000** |
| Cochran's Q | **0.457** (df = 1) |
| Q p-value | **0.4991** |

The Q test provides no evidence against the null hypothesis of homogeneity (p = 0.4991), and the REML estimate of between-study variance is exactly zero, so the random-effects model collapses numerically to a fixed-effect (common-effect) model. The two study-level standardised effects (−0.303 and −0.466) are indeed close and identically directed, consistent with this result.

This apparent consistency must be interpreted with considerable caution. With k = 2 studies there is a single degree of freedom for the Q test, and both Q and I² are known to be extremely imprecise — I² in particular has very wide uncertainty intervals and is frequently estimated at exactly 0% in small meta-analyses even when substantial true heterogeneity exists. A τ² of zero at k = 2 is better understood as *no heterogeneity detectable* than as *no heterogeneity present*. Given that the two studies may well differ in agent, dose, route, treatment duration and dialysis population, the absence of statistical heterogeneity should not be taken as evidence of clinical or pharmacological interchangeability within the KOR agonist class.

A graphical summary of the heterogeneity diagnostics is provided at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/heterogeneity_summary.png`.

# Moderator Analyses

No moderator analyses were performed. The pre-specified plan required a minimum of ten contributing studies for random-effects meta-regression and four studies per subgroup for categorical subgroup analysis; with k = 2 neither threshold is approached. Consequently, the clinically important questions of whether effects differ by **agent** (difelikefalin vs nalbuphine vs nalfurafine), by **route** (intravenous vs oral), by **dose**, by **treatment duration**, or by **baseline itch severity** cannot be addressed by this synthesis. The estimated τ² of zero provides no residual variance for moderators to explain in any case.

A bubble chart was generated at `outputs/bJG9avlBat4bRDhUYzkgRQ/bubble_chart.png`. With two data points it depicts study size against effect magnitude but supports no meta-regression inference whatsoever, and any apparent trend in it is an artefact of plotting two points; it should not be interpreted as evidence of a dose-, size- or covariate-effect relationship.

# Publication Bias

Formal assessment of small-study effects and publication bias was **not possible**. Funnel plot asymmetry and regression-based tests (Egger's test, Begg's rank correlation) require at least ten studies to achieve even minimally adequate statistical power; with k = 2, these procedures are uninterpretable and were not performed. No funnel plot was generated.

Several considerations nonetheless suggest that the possibility of small-study and reporting bias should not be dismissed:

1. **Design-and-instrument restriction as a selection filter.** Only two of five topic-relevant studies reported VAS itch data in a poolable parallel-group format. If the choice to report VAS (as opposed to NRS or responder proportions) was influenced by which instrument produced the more favourable result, the available estimate would be biased in the direction of benefit. Selective outcome reporting is a recognised problem in the pruritus literature, where multiple itch instruments are routinely administered within a single trial.
2. **Sponsorship.** Trials of KOR agonists in CKD-aP are predominantly industry-sponsored registration or dose-finding studies, a context in which positive results are more likely to be published promptly and in full.
3. **Dominance of a single study.** With 82.7% of participants from one trial, the pooled result is effectively one large trial plus a supportive small trial, a configuration that offers no protection against the biases of that single trial.

In the absence of any testable asymmetry, the honest position is that publication bias **cannot be excluded and cannot be quantified** in this dataset.

# Discussion

This meta-analysis of two parallel-group studies comprising 294 randomised participants found that kappa-opioid receptor agonists, pooled as a drug class, produced a statistically significant reduction in uremic pruritus severity on the visual analogue scale relative to placebo (pooled Mean Difference −7.753, 95% CI −13.118 to −2.387; p = 0.0046), favouring the KOR agonist arm. Both contributing studies pointed in the same direction, and no between-study heterogeneity was detectable (I² = 0.0%; Q = 0.457, p = 0.4991).

The **direction** of this finding is biologically coherent and externally consistent. It aligns with the mu/kappa imbalance hypothesis of uremic itch, with the regulatory approval of difelikefalin for moderate-to-severe CKD-aP in haemodialysis patients on the basis of phase 3 evidence, and with the licensure of nalfurafine for refractory pruritus in Japan. It is also consistent in direction with prior syntheses of opioid-modulating therapy in this population.

The **magnitude**, however, requires careful qualification, and this is the central interpretive issue of the report. A placebo-adjusted mean difference of −7.753 points is not credible on a 0–10 cm VAS, and it is internally inconsistent with the study-level standardised effects computed from the same data (*g* = −0.303 and −0.466). The two are reconcilable if at least one contributing study recorded VAS in millimetres on a 0–100 mm scale — in which case the pooled estimate is best read as approximately **−0.78 cm (−7.75 mm)** on a 0–10 cm equivalent scale — or if within-study standard deviations were underestimated during conversion. On either reading, the clinically meaningful interpretation is a **small-to-moderate benefit**: roughly a 0.6–1.5 cm placebo-adjusted improvement on a 10-cm VAS, in the vicinity of commonly cited minimal clinically important differences for itch instruments (typically 1.5–2.0 points on a 0–10 scale, or a 2–4 point improvement on the Worst Itch NRS). That is a real but modest effect, likely to be worthwhile for a symptom with few effective alternatives, while falling short of complete symptom control for most patients. Readers should explicitly not interpret the point estimate as indicating near-abolition of itch.

Three further features temper enthusiasm. First, the evidence base is a single dominant trial with one small supportive trial; the smaller trial's confidence interval includes no effect, so statistical significance of the pooled result depends materially on the larger study. Second, pooling difelikefalin, nalbuphine and nalfurafine as one class conflates agents with materially different pharmacology — peripherally restricted selective KOR agonism versus centrally acting agonism versus mixed KOR-agonist/MOR-antagonist activity — and different adverse-effect profiles (somnolence, dizziness, paraesthesia, nausea). A class-level estimate cannot guide the choice of agent. Third, this review addressed efficacy on a single itch-intensity instrument only; it did not evaluate sleep outcomes, quality-of-life measures (e.g. Skindex-10, 5-D itch), responder rates, durability beyond the assessed timepoint, or harms. A complete clinical appraisal of KOR agonists in CKD-aP must weigh the modest itch benefit against tolerability and cost, neither of which is informed by these data.

# Data Quality and Validation Notes

No automated validation flags were returned by the analysis pipeline for this dataset. The following data-quality observations arise from inspection of the results themselves and are stated plainly rather than inferred from flags:

- **Study attrition into the synthesis.** Five studies were retained as topic-relevant after full-text screening, but only two supplied parallel-group VAS means with dispersion sufficient for pooling. Three relevant studies therefore contribute nothing to the quantitative estimate. The reasons (non-VAS instruments, cross-over designs, or non-extractable summary statistics) were not individually recorded in the analysis output and could not be itemised per study.
- **Scale/units inconsistency in the pooled estimate.** The pooled Mean Difference (−7.753) is arithmetically incompatible with a 0–10 cm VAS and with the study-level standardised effects derived from the same inputs (−0.303, −0.466). This strongly suggests that VAS values were entered in mixed units (centimetres in one study, millimetres in the other) or that within-study standard deviations were underestimated. This discrepancy was **not** resolved within the analysis and constitutes a material limitation on the interpretability of the absolute effect size. The direction, p-value and heterogeneity statistics are unaffected in sign but the location and width of the confidence interval are scale-dependent.
- **Effect measure provenance.** The Mean Difference was fixed by explicit user specification rather than chosen from the data, native-scale pooling was confirmed, and no effect-measure conflict was recorded. The pooled value is consequently a native MD and not a standardised fallback.
- **Degenerate dispersion statistics.** τ² = 0.0000 and I² = 0.0% at k = 2 are boundary estimates with a single degree of freedom and should be treated as uninformative rather than as positive evidence of homogeneity.
- **Analyses not performed.** Moderator meta-regression, subgroup analysis, funnel-plot inspection, Egger's regression and leave-one-out sensitivity analysis were all precluded by k = 2. The bubble chart and cumulative plot were produced but are descriptive only.

# Limitations

1. **Very small number of studies (k = 2).** This is the overriding limitation. Two studies provide one degree of freedom for heterogeneity assessment, no capacity for moderator or bias analysis, and no robustness to the idiosyncrasies of a single trial. Random-effects pooling at k = 2 produces notoriously unstable τ² estimates; here τ² was estimated at exactly zero, collapsing the model to a common-effect analysis and probably understating the true uncertainty of the pooled estimate.
2. **Uninterpretable absolute effect magnitude.** As detailed above, the pooled MD of −7.753 cannot be taken at face value on a 0–10 cm VAS. The confidence interval (−13.118 to −2.387) inherits this problem and should not be quoted as a clinical range on the 0–10 scale.
3. **Dominance of one trial.** 82.7% of analysed participants derive from study_1_R1; the pooled estimate is close to that study's result and the analysis provides little independent corroboration.
4. **Class-level pooling across pharmacologically distinct agents.** Difelikefalin, nalbuphine and nalfurafine differ in receptor selectivity, central nervous system penetration, route and dosing; the pooled estimate cannot be attributed to any one of them.
5. **Single-instrument, single-design restriction.** Confining eligibility to 0–10 cm VAS outcomes in parallel-group studies excluded relevant evidence recorded on NRS, 5-D itch and Skindex instruments and in cross-over trials, reducing precision and raising the possibility of outcome-reporting selection.
6. **No assessment of risk of bias within studies.** Domain-level risk-of-bias appraisal (e.g. RoB 2) was not available in the analysis dataset, so allocation concealment, blinding integrity, missing outcome data and selective reporting could not be factored into the synthesis or into certainty rating.
7. **Publication and small-study bias not assessable.** No funnel plot or regression test could be computed; bias cannot be excluded.
8. **No harms, no durability, no patient-reported global outcomes.** The synthesis is restricted to short-term itch intensity; benefit–harm balance and persistence of effect are outside its scope.
9. **Heterogeneity in timepoint and clinical setting not characterised.** The analysis defined a single outcome group, but the precise assessment timepoints, dialysis modalities and baseline severities of the two trials were not recorded in a form that permitted verification of their comparability.

Applying GRADE-style reasoning, certainty in the estimate of effect would be rated **low**: the evidence derives from randomised comparisons (starting high) but is downgraded for serious imprecision (k = 2, one dominant trial, wide interval) and for indirectness/inconsistency of measurement scale (unresolved units discrepancy, class-level pooling), with publication bias unassessable.

---

# Conclusion

In patients with uremic (CKD-associated) pruritus, pooling two parallel-group studies (N = 294) yielded a statistically significant pooled **Mean Difference** of **−7.753** (95% CI −13.118 to −2.387; p = 0.0046) on the visual analogue scale, **favouring kappa-opioid receptor agonists over placebo**, with no detectable between-study heterogeneity (I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.0000; Q = 0.457, p = 0.4991). Native-scale mean-difference pooling was confirmed, so this is a native-scale result rather than a standardised fallback.

The *direction* of benefit is consistent across both contributing studies and coherent with the pharmacological rationale and with regulatory experience for this drug class. The *magnitude*, however, should not be read literally: a −7.753-point difference is impossible on a 0–10 cm VAS and is incompatible with the study-level standardised effects of −0.303 and −0.466 computed from the same data, which together indicate a **small-to-moderate** benefit of roughly 0.6–1.5 cm on a 10-cm scale once a probable centimetre/millimetre scaling inconsistency is accounted for.

Clinically, these data support kappa-opioid receptor agonism as an active antipruritic strategy in CKD-aP while indicating that the average benefit is modest and that many patients will retain residual itch. For research, the priorities are clear: harmonised itch instruments and reporting units across trials, head-to-head or agent-stratified comparisons rather than class-level pooling, individual-participant-data synthesis to permit moderator analysis at the patient level, and consistent reporting of harms and durability alongside efficacy. Until a substantially larger body of comparable parallel-group VAS evidence exists, the present estimate should be cited for its direction, not its size.
