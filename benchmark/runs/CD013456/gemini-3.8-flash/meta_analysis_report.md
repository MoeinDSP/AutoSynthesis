# Efficacy of Psychosocial Interventions for PTSD Symptom Severity Following Rape or Sexual Assault in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Sexual assault and rape are severe traumatic events with a high incidence of subsequent post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Psychosocial interventions are widely recommended, yet estimates of their immediate post-treatment efficacy relative to inactive comparators vary across trials.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood [REML]) was conducted evaluating the immediate post-treatment effect of psychosocial interventions versus inactive controls (wait-list, usual care, or assessment only) on continuous PTSD symptom severity in adult survivors of rape or sexual assault. 

Results: Ten effect sizes across 15 screened records encompassing 595 participants were pooled. Psychosocial interventions produced a statistically significant reduction in PTSD symptom severity compared to inactive controls (pooled Standardized Mean Difference [SMD] = -1.421, 95% CI [-2.680, -0.162], p = 0.027), favoring the psychosocial intervention. Substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed (I² = 97.79%, τ² = 3.867, Cochran's Q = 121.236, p < 0.001). Egger's test revealed significant asymmetry (intercept = 1.217, p < 0.001), indicating a high risk of publication bias and small-study effects, although Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0, adjusted SMD = -1.421).

Conclusions: Psychosocial interventions significantly alleviate PTSD symptom severity immediately following treatment in adult survivors of sexual assault. However, extreme statistical heterogeneity and pronounced small-study asymmetry warrant cautious interpretation and emphasize the need for rigorous, adequately powered randomized trials.

# Efficacy of Psychosocial Interventions for PTSD Symptom Severity Following Rape or Sexual Assault in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Rape and sexual assault represent pervasive, acutely traumatizing violations associated with profound psychological morbidity. Among adult survivors, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)—characterized by intrusive re-experiencing, persistent avoidance, negative alterations in cognition and mood, and marked trauma-related hyperarousal—is one of the most debilitating and prevalent psychiatric sequelae. If unaddressed, PTSD can follow a chronic trajectory, leading to enduring occupational dysfunction, impaired interpersonal relationships, elevated somatic distress, and substantial suicide risk.

Psychosocial interventions, including trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (TF-CBT), cognitive processing therapy (CPT), prolonged exposure (PE), and eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR), are frontline clinical modalities designed to facilitate cognitive restructuring, emotional processing, and habituation to trauma-related cues. Although clinical practice guidelines broadly advocate trauma-informed psychological care, empirical trials have yielded heterogeneous findings when evaluating immediate post-treatment symptom relief compared against inactive control conditions, such as wait-lists, treatment as usual, or repeated assessments.

The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to determine the quantitative effect of psychosocial interventions compared with inactive controls on PTSD symptom severity immediately after treatment completion in adult survivors of rape or sexual assault.

---

## 2. Search and Screening Methods

A comprehensive systematic literature search was executed following PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines across electronic biomedical and psychological databases, including MEDLINE/PubMed, PsycINFO, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL).

Eligible studies met the following criteria:
1. **Population:** Adults (aged $\ge 18$ years) who experienced rape, sexual assault, or intimate partner sexual violence.
2. **Intervention:** Structured psychosocial interventions (individual or group psychotherapy, behavioral interventions, or cognitive processing modalities).
3. **Comparator:** Inactive control conditions, including wait-list (WL), usual care/standard care (UC), or assessment-only controls.
4. **Outcome:** Validated continuous measures of PTSD symptom severity administered immediately upon completion of the intervention phase (e.g., Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale [CAPS], Posttraumatic Diagnostic Scale [PDS], PTSD Checklist [PCL], PTSD Symptom Scale-Self Report [PSS-SR], or Impact of Event Scale-Revised [IES-R]).
5. **Study Design:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or controlled prospective interventional evaluations.

The systematic screening pipeline identified 15 potentially relevant study reports. Following rigorous full-text appraisal, 10 distinct effect size estimates representing 595 total participants provided sufficient post-intervention continuous data for quantitative meta-analytic synthesis.

## 4. Individual Study Effect Sizes

Individual effect estimates displayed substantial clinical and statistical diversity across trials:
- Seven cohorts demonstrated negative point estimates indicative of reduced PTSD symptom severity in the psychosocial intervention arm (`study_1_R1`, `study_2_R2`, `study_4_R4`, `study_5_R5`, `study_6_R6`, `study_7_R7`, and `study_14_R14`).
- Among these, large to very large reductions were documented in moderate-sample studies (e.g., `study_5_R5`: estimate = -1.923, 95% CI [-2.689, -1.156]; `study_7_R7`: estimate = -2.792, 95% CI [-3.798, -1.787]), culminating in an extreme outlier in `study_14_R14` (estimate = -8.770, 95% CI [-11.798, -5.742], $n = 18$).
- Conversely, two studies found modest symptom differences favoring inactive control conditions (`study_3_R3`: estimate = +0.392, 95% CI [0.026, 0.758]; `study_13_R13`: estimate = +0.520, 95% CI [-0.074, 1.114]), while `study_11_R11` observed a virtually null effect (estimate = +0.051, 95% CI [-0.555, 0.657]).

## 5. Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis was executed using a random-effects model fitted via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimation to compute the pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD). A negative value denotes a reduction in PTSD symptom scores, indicating an advantage for the active psychosocial treatment arm.

The pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) was:
$$\text{Pooled SMD} = -1.421, \quad 95\%\text{ CI } [-2.680, -0.162], \quad p = 0.027$$

Because the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval is below zero (-0.162) and the two-tailed test achieves statistical significance ($p = 0.0269$), the synthesis formally favors the **Psychosocial intervention**. Across the 10 trials ($N = 595$), adult sexual assault survivors who received structured psychosocial interventions experienced significantly lower PTSD symptom severity immediately after treatment compared to inactive controls.

## 6. Heterogeneity Analysis

Between-study variance was pronounced and statistically significant:
- **Cochran's $Q$:** $121.236$ ($df = 9$, $p < 0.001$)
- **Tau-squared ($\tau^2$):** $3.867$
- **Inconsistency index ($I^2$):** $97.79\%$

An $I^2$ value of $97.79\%$ indicates that almost all observed variation in effect sizes is attributable to genuine differences across trial populations, protocols, or execution rather than sampling error. The between-study variance parameter ($\tau^2 = 3.867$) further confirms substantial dispersion across study-level true effects. ## 7. Moderators and Sources of Heterogeneity

The observed heterogeneity is likely driven by several critical clinical and methodological factors:
1. **Influence of Small Sample Size Outliers:** The most influential contributor to statistical dispersion is `study_14_R14` ($n = 18$), which yielded an extreme SMD of -8.770 (95% CI [-11.798, -5.742]). In ultra-small trauma intervention trials, restricted sample variance or non-normality can inflate standard deviations or denominator estimates, generating disproportionate standardized metrics.
2. **Intervention Modality and Intensity:** Included psychosocial interventions varied in theoretical orientation (e.g., exposure-focused protocols, cognitive processing, supportive counseling, or psychoeducational crisis intervention) and dosage (ranging from single-session brief crisis protocols to multi-week comprehensive psychotherapies).
3. **Assessment Modality and Timing:** The continuous instruments used across studies encompass both clinician-administered interviews (e.g., CAPS) and self-report symptom inventories (e.g., PCL, PDS). Differences in scale sensitivity, trauma recency (acute vs. chronic post-assault presentations), and survivor attrition rates between active and control arms introduce non-trivial variability.
4. **Control Arm Nature:** Variations among wait-list controls, treatment-as-usual (which may permit access to supportive primary care or emergency services), and assessment-only groups may account for studies where controls performed comparatively better (`study_3_R3`, `study_13_R13`).

## 8. Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were appraised using linear regression tests of funnel plot asymmetry and non-parametric trim-and-fill analysis.

- **Egger's Regression Test:** Intercept = $1.217$, $p < 0.001$.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill:** Number of imputed missing studies $k_0 = 0$; adjusted pooled SMD = $-1.421$ (95% CI [-2.680, -0.162]).
- **Risk Assessment:** High risk of publication bias and small-study effects.

Egger's test indicates statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p < 0.001$), pointing to a high risk of publication bias or small-study effects. Although the trim-and-fill method did not impute any missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), this likely reflects methodological limitations in detecting complex asymmetry—particularly in the presence of extreme statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 97.79\%$) and outlying small studies—rather than a true absence of bias. Consequently, the overall risk of publication bias remains high, and the pooled findings must be interpreted with circumspection.

---

## 9. Discussion

This meta-analysis demonstrates that psychosocial interventions yield a statistically significant reduction in continuous PTSD symptom severity immediately following treatment in adult survivors of rape or sexual assault (pooled Standardized Mean Difference [SMD] = -1.421, 95% CI [-2.680, -0.162], $p = 0.027$), favoring the psychosocial intervention arm over inactive controls.

From a clinical perspective, standard guidelines prioritize trauma-focused cognitive and behavioral modalities for acute and chronic trauma survivors. Our pooled estimate confirms that structured psychotherapies outperform passive monitoring or wait-list conditions. However, the magnitude of the pooled point estimate (-1.421) is unusually large for clinical psychological trials, driven in part by small trials reporting extreme treatment effects. When small studies with large effect estimates dominate the lower end of the sample distribution, standard meta-analytic weights can amplify the pooled effect size.

Furthermore, the presence of positive point estimates in three trials (`study_3_R3`, `study_11_R11`, `study_13_R13`) underscores that psychosocial interventions are not uniformly superior across all settings. In early post-assault interventions, premature intensive trauma processing or poorly targeted psychoeducation can occasionally disrupt natural recovery trajectories or induce transient symptom exacerbation compared to supportive monitoring.

---

## 10. Limitations

Several methodological limitations qualify the findings of this review:
1. **Extreme Statistical Heterogeneity:** With an $I^2$ of $97.79\%$ and $\tau^2 = 3.867$, summarizing these trials under a single pooled metric carries inherent imprecision regarding the expected effect for any individual patient or specific protocol.
2. **Small-Study Outliers:** Study `study_14_R14` ($n = 18$, SMD = -8.770) represents an extreme statistical outlier that heavily inflates overall between-study variance and influences model stability.
3. **High Risk of Publication Bias:** The highly significant Egger's test ($p < 0.001$) suggests that smaller trials with non-significant or unfavorable results may have remained unpublished, or that small-study effects have distorted the symmetry of the literature base.
4. **Immediate Post-Treatment Scope:** This evaluation was restricted to outcomes assessed immediately upon treatment cessation. Consequently, long-term symptom trajectories, relapse rates, and delayed treatment gains could not be characterized.
5. **Heterogeneity of Control Groups:** Combining wait-list, usual care, and assessment-only controls into a composite inactive control group may obscure differences in control group engagement and baseline symptom improvement.

---

## 11. Conclusion

Psychosocial interventions significantly reduce PTSD symptom severity immediately after treatment in adults who have experienced rape or sexual assault, favoring psychosocial intervention over inactive controls (pooled Standardized Mean Difference [SMD] = -1.421, 95% CI [-2.680, -0.162], $p = 0.027$). However, the presence of marked between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 97.79\%$) and pronounced small-study asymmetry (Egger's test $p < 0.001$) indicates that the true population treatment effect may be more modest. Future research must prioritize large-scale, pre-registered randomized controlled trials with active comparators and standardized outcome reporting to establish the comparative durability and safety of specific psychotherapy protocols for sexual assault survivors.
