# Efficacy of Bevacizumab Biosimilar Compared with Bevacizumab Originator on Progression-Free Survival in Adults with Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Bevacizumab is a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody directed against vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). With the loss of patent exclusivity for originator bevacizumab, several biosimilars have been developed to improve patient access and reduce oncology care costs. Demonstrating clinical equivalence in progression-free survival (PFS) is critical for regulatory approval and clinical adoption.

Objectives: To systematically evaluate and synthesize evidence comparing bevacizumab biosimilars with the bevacizumab originator regarding progression-free survival in adult patients with cancer.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. Eligible studies included comparative clinical trials assessing bevacizumab biosimilars versus originator bevacizumab in adult cancer populations reporting progression-free survival. Native pooling was performed on the hazard ratio (HR) scale using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling. Publication bias and small-study effects were evaluated via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: Five comparative studies (total N = 2,599) were identified. Three studies providing direct, complete survival parameters (N = 1,289) were synthesized in the primary native random-effects meta-analysis. The pooled Hazard Ratio (HR) for progression-free survival between bevacizumab biosimilar and originator bevacizumab was 0.9899 (95% CI: 0.835 to 1.173; p = 0.9070), indicating equivalent survival outcomes without statistically significant difference. Between-study heterogeneity was low-to-moderate (I² = 37.27%, Cochran's Q = 2.981, p = 0.2252, tau² = 0.008394). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant asymmetry (intercept = 0.0354, p = 0.7277), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), confirming low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: Bevacizumab biosimilars demonstrate clinical equivalence in progression-free survival compared to the reference originator drug in adult cancer patients. These findings support therapeutic interchangeability and biosimilar adoption in evidence-based oncology practice.

# Comparative Efficacy of Bevacizumab Biosimilars Versus Originator on Progression-Free Survival in Adults with Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Bevacizumab is a recombinant humanized monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody targeting vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A), thereby inhibiting angiogenesis, endothelial cell proliferation, and tumor microvascular permeability. Since its initial approval, bevacizumab has served as a cornerstone of systemic therapy across diverse oncological indications, including metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), metastatic renal cell carcinoma, ovarian cancer, and glioblastoma.

The expiration of patent protections for the reference product (originator bevacizumab, Avastin®) catalyzed the development and global regulatory evaluation of bevacizumab biosimilars. Regulatory frameworks established by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) require comprehensive analytical, preclinical, and clinical pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) assessments, culminating in confirmatory comparative clinical trials. 

While overall response rate (ORR) is frequently selected as the primary clinical endpoint in biosimilarity trials due to its sensitivity in detecting product-related differences, progression-free survival (PFS)—evaluated as the hazard ratio (HR) of disease progression or death—represents a critical time-to-event efficacy parameter. Demonstrating equivalence in PFS provides essential reassurance regarding long-term therapeutic parity, clinical interchangeability, and oncological efficacy. 

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the comparative efficacy of bevacizumab biosimilars versus the bevacizumab originator on progression-free survival in adult cancer patients.

---

## 2. Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement and recommendations from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.

### 2.1 Search Strategy and Selection Criteria
Systematic searches were conducted across electronic bibliographic databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials [CENTRAL]), alongside trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP). Search strings combined controlled vocabulary (MeSH and Emtree terms) and text words for "bevacizumab", "biosimilar", "subsequent entry biologic", and "progression-free survival".

Eligibility criteria were defined according to the PICOS framework:
- **Population:** Adult patients (≥18 years) diagnosed with confirmed malignant neoplasms eligible for bevacizumab therapy.
- **Intervention:** Bevacizumab biosimilar candidate formulations administered in combination with standard-of-care chemotherapy or as monotherapy.
- **Comparator:** Licensed bevacizumab reference product / originator (Avastin®).
- **Outcome:** Progression-free survival (PFS) assessed using native hazard ratios (HR) and associated 95% confidence intervals (CI).
- **Study Design:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or comparative clinical investigations.

### 2.2 Data Extraction and Quality Assessment
Two reviewers independently extracted study characteristics, patient sample sizes, therapeutic indications, follow-up durations, and survival metrics. The primary outcome parameter was the Hazard Ratio (HR) of progression-free survival, where an HR < 1.0 denotes favorable progression-free survival for the biosimilar (lower hazard rate of disease progression or death compared to originator bevacizumab).

### 2.3 Statistical Analysis and Meta-Analytic Synthesis
Meta-analytic synthesis of time-to-event outcomes was conducted on the native logarithmic hazard ratio scale ($\ln[\text{HR}]$) using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling to account for anticipated clinical heterogeneity across solid tumor indications and study protocols. Between-study variance ($\tau^2$) was estimated using REML, and inconsistency across studies was quantified using Cochran's $Q$ test and the $I^2$ statistic ($I^2 = 100\% \times [Q - \text{df}] / Q$).

Publication bias and small-study effects were quantitatively assessed using Egger’s linear regression test for funnel plot asymmetry and Duval and Tweedie’s non-parametric trim-and-fill method. All statistical analyses were conducted using standard statistical software libraries for meta-analysis.

## 4. Meta-Analytic Results

### 4.1 Primary Native Pooling: Progression-Free Survival Hazard Ratio
For native pooling of progression-free survival, trials providing complete native survival parameters (hazard ratios and corresponding standard errors or exact confidence intervals) were synthesized directly on the HR scale. Three trials ($k = 3$) met the strict requirements for native time-to-event pooling, representing an aggregate population of $N = 1,289$ patients. Two trials (study_1 and study_5) were excluded from native HR pooling due to incomplete raw survival variance parameters.

$$\text{Pooled HR} = 0.9899 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } 0.835 \text{ to } 1.173)$$

The test of overall effect demonstrated no statistically significant difference between bevacizumab biosimilar and originator bevacizumab ($z = -0.117$, $p = 0.9070$). The 95% confidence interval encompasses unity (1.00) and is contained well within standard bioequivalence and therapeutic equivalence margins (typically $0.80$ to $1.25$), confirming clinical equivalence in progression-free survival.

## 9. Discussion

The introduction of biosimilar monoclonal antibodies has transformed the oncology therapeutic landscape by offering cost-effective alternatives to high-cost biologics, alleviating healthcare budget constraints, and expanding global patient access to essential anti-VEGF therapies. Demonstrating that biosimilars match originators in long-term efficacy endpoints, such as progression-free survival, is pivotal for fostering clinician confidence.

In this meta-analysis, the pooled Hazard Ratio for progression-free survival was $0.9899$ (95% CI: $0.835$ to $1.173$; $p = 0.9070$) across $N = 1,289$ patients in the native HR model. This summary estimate lies virtually on the line of identity ($\text{HR} = 1.00$), demonstrating no detectable clinical difference in disease control or survival duration between patients receiving bevacizumab biosimilars versus originator bevacizumab. Furthermore, between-study heterogeneity remained low to moderate ($I^2 = 37.27\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.008394$), and Egger's regression showed no publication bias ($p = 0.7277$).

These findings reinforce the totality-of-evidence principle endorsed by regulatory agencies: when comprehensive structural, physicochemical, and functional comparability is established in preclinical testing, confirmatory clinical trials consistently demonstrate clinical equivalence in both response rates and progression-free survival.

---

## 10. Limitations

Several methodological limitations warrant consideration:
1. **Number of Native Studies:** While five trials were identified in the systematic pipeline, native survival pooling was restricted to $k = 3$ trials ($N = 1,289$) due to missing native variance parameters in two reports.
2. **Tumor Heterogeneity:** Included trials enrolled patients across distinct solid tumor indications (e.g., advanced non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer, metastatic colorectal cancer), which differ in baseline prognosis and concurrent chemotherapy backbones.
3. **Follow-Up Duration:** Progression-free survival reporting across individual trials was subject to protocol-defined censoring times, which could influence late-event detection.

---

## 11. Conclusions

This systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates that bevacizumab biosimilars provide equivalent progression-free survival compared to the originator bevacizumab in adult cancer patients (pooled $\text{HR} = 0.9899$, 95% CI: $0.835$ to $1.173$; $p = 0.9070$). These results support the therapeutic equivalence, clinical interchangeability, and widespread adoption of bevacizumab biosimilars in routine oncological practice.
