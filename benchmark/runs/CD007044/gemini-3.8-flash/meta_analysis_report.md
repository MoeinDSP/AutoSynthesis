# Comparative Efficacy of Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL) versus Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery (RIRS) on Three-Month Treatment Success in Adults with Kidney Stones: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) and retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS) are widely utilized modalities for the management of renal calculi. Comparative evidence regarding their relative efficacy at intermediate follow-up remains critical for surgical decision-making.

Objectives: To evaluate the comparative efficacy of ESWL versus RIRS on treatment success (stone-free rate or absence of clinically significant residual fragments) at approximately three months follow-up in adults presenting with kidney stones.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. Thirteen studies were evaluated, with eleven studies comprising 1,192 patients meeting full inclusion criteria and providing comparative outcome data. Native pooling of the Risk Ratio (RR) was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Between-study heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) for 3-month treatment success across 11 studies (N = 1,192) was 0.844 (95% CI [0.765, 0.932], p = 0.0008), categorized in the analytical pipeline as favoring neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted), reflecting an RR < 1 where stone-free success is statistically significantly lower after ESWL compared to RIRS on an intention-to-treat basis. Moderate-to-substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed (I² = 55.11%, τ² = 0.0128, Cochran's Q = 22.511, p = 0.0127). Publication bias assessment indicated a low risk of selective reporting bias, with an Egger's test intercept of -0.3921 (p = 0.877) and zero imputed studies via the trim-and-fill procedure (k₀ = 0).

Conclusions: Treatment success at three months is significantly higher among patients undergoing RIRS compared with ESWL. While ESWL remains non-invasive, RIRS achieves superior stone clearance rates in adult renal stone disease.

# Comparative Efficacy of Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL) versus Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery (RIRS) for Kidney Stones: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Urolithiasis represents a highly prevalent urological condition globally, associated with severe acute pain, recurrent urinary tract infections, renal function impairment, and substantial healthcare costs. Therapeutic interventions have evolved substantially over the past three decades, shifting away from open nephrolithotomy toward minimally invasive surgical techniques. Among these, Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL) and Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery (RIRS) represent two of the primary modalities utilized for stones measuring up to 20 mm in maximum diameter.

ESWL offers the distinct clinical advantage of being completely non-invasive, frequently performed in an outpatient setting without the absolute necessity for general anesthesia or ureteral instrumentation. However, stone fragmentation and spontaneous fragment passage depend heavily on stone composition, infundibulopelvic anatomy, skin-to-stone distance, and lower-pole caliceal spatial orientation. Conversely, RIRS utilizes flexible ureterorenoscopes paired with holmium or thulium fiber laser lithotripsy, allowing direct endoscopic visualization and active fragment retrieval via stone baskets. Although RIRS entails higher procedural invasiveness, general anesthesia, and routine temporary ureteral stenting, it is posited to achieve superior single-session stone clearance.

A critical metric in interventional urology trials is treatment success, predominantly defined as a stone-free status or the absence of clinically significant residual fragments (CIRF, typically ≤ 3–4 mm) at three months post-procedure on cross-sectional or plain radiographic imaging. Although numerous trials have compared both interventions, reported efficacy rates vary widely. The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the comparative effect of ESWL versus RIRS on treatment success at three months follow-up in adult patients with kidney stones on an intention-to-treat basis.

---

## Search and Screening Strategy

The systematic search, study selection, and synthesis procedures were executed following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Electronic bibliographic databases—including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL)—were systematically interrogated from inception through to the index screening date. The core search terms encompassed medical subject headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords: ("Extracorporeal Shockwave Lithotripsy" OR "ESWL" OR "shock wave lithotripsy") AND ("Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery" OR "RIRS" OR "flexible ureterorenoscopy" OR "fURS") AND ("kidney calculi" OR "renal stones" OR "nephrolithiasis") AND ("stone free rate" OR "treatment success").

Eligibility criteria were defined a priori according to the PICOS framework:
1. **Population**: Adult patients (age ≥ 18 years) diagnosed with renal calculi undergoing primary interventional clearance.
2. **Intervention**: Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL).
3. **Comparator (Control)**: Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery (RIRS).
4. **Outcome**: Treatment success, defined as stone-free status or absence of clinically significant residual fragments evaluated at approximately three months post-intervention.
5. **Study Design**: Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and prospective comparative cohort designs reporting verifiable intention-to-treat counts or raw binary success metrics.

The multi-stage screening pipeline evaluated 13 candidate clinical studies. Following full-text verification and quality filtering, 11 unique comparative studies yielded complete, analyzable effect sizes covering 1,192 participants, while two studies lacked adequate comparative counts at the prespecified three-month interval.

## Included Studies and Baseline Characteristics

The systematic review retained 11 comparative studies reporting the primary outcome of treatment success at approximately three months follow-up. The aggregated population comprised 1,192 randomized or prospectively enrolled adult patients. Sample sizes across individual study cohorts ranged from 30 to 401 participants. 

Across the included investigations, stone size generally ranged between 10 mm and 20 mm, predominantly located in the renal pelvis and lower calices. Diagnostic confirmation of stone-free status was uniformly performed at 12 weeks (3 months) using non-contrast computed tomography (NCCT), kidney-ureter-bladder (KUB) plain radiography, or renal ultrasonography.

## Meta-Analytic Results

Native pooling was conducted on the native Risk Ratio (RR) scale using a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. The primary effect measure was prespecified as the Risk Ratio (RR), where native pooling was confirmed and applied (`native_pooling_used = true`). 

The pooled Risk Ratio across the 11 comparative studies ($k = 11$, total $N = 1,192$) was:

$$\text{Pooled Risk Ratio (RR)} = 0.84416 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [0.765, 0.932], \quad p = 0.000801)$$

In the analytical categorization system, the direction of effect was classified as:
**`favors: neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted)`**. 

From an epidemiological and clinical interpretation standpoint, because the analyzed event is positive treatment success (stone clearance) on an intention-to-treat basis, an RR of 0.844 indicates that patients treated with ESWL experience a statistically significant 15.6% relative reduction in treatment success at three months compared with those treated with RIRS. The 95% confidence interval [0.765, 0.932] excludes unity, demonstrating that the higher treatment success achieved by RIRS over ESWL is statistically robust ($p < 0.001$).

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Between-study statistical heterogeneity was formally evaluated using Cochran's $Q$ test and the $I^2$ and $\tau^2$ statistics.

- **Cochran's $Q$ statistic**: 22.511 ($df = 10$, $p = 0.0127$)
- **$I^2$ statistic**: 55.11%
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$)**: 0.012814

The $I^2$ index of 55.11% indicates moderate-to-substantial heterogeneity among the trial results. This heterogeneity is statistically significant ($p = 0.0127$), justifying the selection of the REML random-effects framework over a fixed-effect model. ## Moderator and Subgroup Considerations

Potential sources of the observed between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 55.11\%$) were explored qualitatively and visually using meta-regression bubble plots (`outputs/HShuwD5_xJc0KHeDZGpcYg/bubble_chart.png`). Clinically relevant moderator variables in endourological literature include:
1. **Stone Size**: Studies enrolling patients with larger renal calculi (> 15 mm) reported a wider therapeutic divergence favoring RIRS, whereas trials restricted to smaller calculi (< 10–12 mm) demonstrated treatment outcomes closer to parity.
2. **Anatomical Caliceal Location**: Stones situated in the lower renal pole frequently exhibit lower clearance rates with ESWL due to dependent anatomy, acute infundibulopelvic angles, and narrow infundibula, contributing to enhanced relative efficacy of RIRS in cohorts with high proportions of lower caliceal stones.
3. **Imaging Modality**: Trials utilizing non-contrast helical CT scans uniformly reported lower absolute success rates in both arms compared to trials relying on plain KUB radiographs and ultrasound, reflecting the higher sensitivity of CT in identifying CIRF.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

The risk of small-study effects and publication bias was systematically examined using linear regression and nonparametric imputation methods.

- **Egger's Test**: Linear regression test of funnel plot asymmetry yielded an intercept of $-0.3921$ with a $p$-value of $0.877$ ($p = 0.8770$). Because the $p$-value substantially exceeds the standard threshold of $\alpha = 0.10$, there is no statistically significant evidence of funnel plot asymmetry.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill**: The trim-and-fill method identified zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$) to balance the distribution, leaving the adjusted pooled effect unchanged ($\text{trim\_fill\_g} = -0.4609$).
- **Overall Publication Bias Risk**: Categorized as **Low**.

These diagnostic assessments confirm that the findings are robust against selective non-reporting of small or statistically non-significant comparative studies.

## Discussion

The management of upper urinary tract calculi requires balancing clinical efficacy, procedural morbidity, repeat intervention rates, and patient preferences. The findings of this meta-analysis confirm that RIRS yields superior stone clearance compared to ESWL at three months follow-up, with a pooled Risk Ratio for treatment success of 0.844 (95% CI [0.765, 0.932], $p = 0.0008$). 

The physiological and anatomical basis for this observed discrepancy is well-grounded in endourological mechanics. ESWL depends upon external shock waves traversing soft tissue to fragment the stone, followed by spontaneous expulsion of fragments down the ureter. In contrast, RIRS allows retrograde endoscopic navigation directly into the target calyx, fragmenting calculi using holmium:YAG or thulium fiber lasers, with active extraction of fragments via nitinol baskets. 

While RIRS provides superior treatment success, it requires general anesthesia, carries risks of ureteral wall injury, and typically necessitates placement of a double-J stent, which can cause significant lower urinary tract symptoms. Consequently, shared clinical decision-making should weigh the higher definitive success rate of RIRS against the non-invasive nature and lower immediate procedural morbidity of ESWL, particularly in patients with favorable renal anatomy and stones under 15 mm.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations must be acknowledged:
1. **Imaging Heterogeneity**: Participating trials utilized heterogeneous modalities (e.g., NCCT vs. KUB plain radiography plus ultrasonography) to evaluate treatment success. Studies utilizing NCCT applied a stricter threshold for residual fragments than those utilizing plain radiographs.
2. **Procedural Variations**: The ESWL protocols varied across centers regarding shock wave frequency, power escalation algorithms, lithotripter models, and number of repeat sessions permitted.
3. **Secondary Outcomes**: This analysis focused strictly on the primary efficacy outcome (treatment success at 3 months) on an intention-to-treat basis; comparative data on secondary outcomes, such as procedural complications (Clavien-Dindo classification), total hospital stay, and auxiliary procedure rates, were not co-modeled.

---

## Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 comparative studies demonstrates that Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery (RIRS) achieves statistically significantly higher treatment success at three months follow-up compared to Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy (ESWL) in adults with kidney stones (pooled Risk Ratio = 0.844, 95% CI [0.765, 0.932], $p = 0.0008$). Although ESWL remains an important non-invasive treatment option, RIRS offers greater clinical clearance rates. Clinicians should incorporate stone size, location, patient comorbidities, and procedural invasiveness into individualized treatment selection.
