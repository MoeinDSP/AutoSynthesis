# Rituximab Versus Tacrolimus for Preventing Relapse by 12 Months in Children With Steroid-Sensitive Nephrotic Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Random-Effects Meta-analysis

## Abstract

### Background
Relapse prevention is a central goal in the management of childhood steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome. This review evaluated rituximab compared with tacrolimus for the binary occurrence of nephrotic syndrome relapse by 12 months after randomization.

### Methods
The supplied evidence set identified three studies, of which two contributed effect sizes to the quantitative synthesis. Trial-level relapse counts, arm-specific randomized totals, bibliographic details, relapse definitions, and exact trial-specific assessment windows were not included in the supplied results and therefore could not be independently verified or reproduced. The requested effect measure was the risk ratio (RR). A random-effects model using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) pooled two studies comprising 161 participants. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q, I², and τ².

### Results
The pooled risk ratio was **0.5208093504866264** (95% CI **0.153 to 1.772**; p = **0.2964809639680268**; k = **2**; total N = **161**). The supplied analysis classified the result as favoring **neither arm**. The confidence interval included the null value of RR = 1, and the result was not statistically significant. Heterogeneity was substantial: Q = **5.154** (p = **0.0232**), I² = **80.6%**, and τ² = **0.630171**. With only two contributing studies, moderator analysis and meaningful assessment of small-study effects or publication bias were not possible.

### Conclusions
The available synthesis does not establish a statistically significant difference between rituximab and tacrolimus in relapse risk by 12 months. The point estimate was imprecise, the confidence interval encompassed potentially important effects in either direction, and between-study heterogeneity was substantial. Conclusions are further constrained by unavailable trial-level event data, relapse definitions, exact assessment windows, and study identifiers.

## Introduction

Steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome in children commonly follows a relapsing course. Although most affected children initially respond to corticosteroids, repeated relapses can result in substantial cumulative corticosteroid exposure and treatment-related toxicity. Steroid-sparing therapies are therefore used to sustain remission and reduce relapse risk. Rituximab, a B-cell-depleting monoclonal antibody, and tacrolimus, a calcineurin inhibitor, are two such treatment strategies, but they differ in administration, monitoring requirements, adverse-effect profiles, and duration of immunosuppression.

This systematic review and meta-analysis addressed the following question: **Among children with steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome, what is the effect of rituximab compared with tacrolimus on the risk of relapse by 12 months after randomization?** The prespecified outcome was the binary occurrence of nephrotic syndrome relapse, using each trial’s reported events and total number randomized in each arm. The requested effect measure was the risk ratio (RR), for which RR < 1 was prespecified as favoring rituximab and RR > 1 as favoring tacrolimus. Nevertheless, the supplied meta-analysis output itself classified the pooled result as favoring **neither arm**; that supplied classification is retained throughout this report.

## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

A random-effects model fitted using **restricted maximum likelihood (REML)** included **2 studies** and **161 participants**. The **pooled risk ratio** was:

> **RR = 0.5208093504866264 (95% CI 0.153 to 1.772; p = 0.2964809639680268).**

The confidence interval crossed the null value of RR = 1, and the pooled result was not statistically significant. In accordance with the supplied `favors` field, this finding was classified as favoring **neither arm**. The estimate is imprecise: its confidence interval includes both a potentially substantial reduction and a potentially important increase in relapse risk.

Because trial-level event counts and arm-specific denominators were not supplied, absolute risks, risk differences, numbers needed to treat, and the clinical implications at specific baseline risks could not be calculated reliably.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was substantial:

- **Cochran’s Q = 5.154**;
- **Q-test p = 0.0232**;
- **I² = 80.6%**; and
- **τ² = 0.630171**.

The Q test indicated statistically detectable inconsistency, while I² suggested that a large proportion of the observed dispersion was associated with between-study heterogeneity rather than sampling error. The REML estimate of τ² also indicated material between-study variance on the model’s analysis scale.

These statistics require caution because they are based on only two studies. With such a small number of studies, Q has limited operating characteristics, I² is uncertain, and τ² can be unstable. Even so, the observed discrepancy between study effects and the reported I² of 80.6% make a single common clinical effect implausible without further investigation.

Potential explanations could include differences in participant relapse history, steroid dependence or frequent-relapse status, prior immunosuppression, rituximab regimen, tacrolimus exposure, concomitant treatment, relapse definition, and follow-up timing. None of these explanations could be tested with the supplied data. ## Discussion

### Principal findings

In the supplied evidence set, the REML random-effects synthesis of two studies found a pooled RR of **0.5208093504866264**, with a wide 95% CI from **0.153 to 1.772** and p = **0.2964809639680268**. The supplied analysis classified the result as favoring neither rituximab nor tacrolimus. Thus, the evidence does not establish a statistically significant difference in relapse risk by 12 months after randomization.

The lack of statistical significance should not be equated with equivalence. The confidence interval is broad and compatible with materially different clinical conclusions. Precision is constrained by the small evidence base, and the substantial heterogeneity (I² = **80.6%**) further limits confidence in applying the pooled estimate to a specific clinical population.

### Clinical interpretation

Treatment decisions between rituximab and tacrolimus cannot be based on the pooled relapse estimate alone. Relevant considerations would normally include treatment burden, infusion-related risks, infection risk, immunization status, duration of B-cell depletion, nephrotoxicity, metabolic adverse effects, therapeutic drug monitoring, adherence, cost, access, and family preferences. These outcomes and considerations were outside the supplied quantitative dataset.

The inability to inspect relapse definitions is especially important. In nephrotic syndrome trials, differences in urine protein thresholds, required duration, confirmation procedures, response to recurrent corticosteroids, and adjudication can alter event classification. Similarly, nominally comparable “12-month” outcomes may differ if one trial measures events through day 365 and another reports status at a scheduled visit or uses a broader visit window.

### Certainty and applicability

A formal GRADE assessment could not be completed because study-level risk-of-bias information, directness details, and evidence concerning selective reporting were not provided. Nonetheless, confidence in the estimate is limited by imprecision, substantial inconsistency, the very small number of contributing studies, and incomplete reporting of the underlying event data and outcome definitions.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations:

1. **Incomplete search documentation:** Databases, dates, search strategies, and complete screening counts were unavailable, precluding full reproducibility and a complete PRISMA flow diagram.
2. **Only two pooled studies:** Statistical power and precision were limited, and publication-bias assessment was not feasible.
3. **Substantial heterogeneity:** I² was 80.6%, and its source could not be explored.
4. **Unavailable trial-level binary data:** Relapse events and randomized denominators by arm were not supplied, preventing independent calculation of study RRs, verification of intention-to-treat denominators, or calculation of absolute effects.
5. **Outcome-definition uncertainty:** Trial-specific relapse criteria and exact assessment windows were unavailable.
6. **Unexplained noncontribution:** The pipeline listed three studies, but only two contributed effect sizes; no reason was supplied for the third study’s omission from pooling.
7. **Study-level metric inconsistency:** The displayed study-level statistics were labeled Hedges’ g, while the pooled native measure was RR; this could not be reconciled from the available information.
8. **No risk-of-bias assessment:** Domain-level judgments for randomization, deviations from intended interventions, missing data, outcome measurement, and selective reporting were not provided.
9. **No formal certainty rating:** A defensible GRADE rating could not be assigned from the supplied information.
10. **Limited clinical scope:** Adverse events, corticosteroid exposure, kidney function, treatment discontinuation, quality of life, and economic outcomes were not synthesized.

## Conclusion

Based on two studies with 161 participants, the REML random-effects **pooled risk ratio was 0.5208093504866264 (95% CI 0.153 to 1.772; p = 0.2964809639680268)** for relapse by 12 months after randomization. The supplied analysis classified this result as favoring **neither arm**, and it did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference between rituximab and tacrolimus. Substantial heterogeneity (I² = **80.6%**, τ² = **0.630171**) and a wide confidence interval make the estimate uncertain. Better-reported comparative trials—and access to arm-level event data, consistent relapse definitions, and exact assessment windows—are required before firm conclusions can be drawn.
