# Prophylactic Implantable Gentamicin-Collagen Sponge and Superficial Sternal Surgical Site Infection in Adult Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Objective: To evaluate the clinical efficacy of an implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge placed in the sternotomy wound, compared to no topical antimicrobial therapy (routine care or placebo), for the prevention of superficial sternal surgical site infections (SSIs) in adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Methods: A systematic literature search was conducted following PRISMA guidelines across biomedical databases to identify comparative clinical trials evaluating implantable gentamicin-collagen sponges in adult cardiac surgery. The primary outcome was the incidence of superficial sternal SSI occurring within 30 to 90 days postoperatively. Random-effects meta-analysis via restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was performed for studies reporting risk ratios (RR). Heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were evaluated via Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: Five clinical studies comprising 5,382 patients met inclusion criteria. Studies were stratified by reported effect metric. In the primary pooled analysis of four studies reporting Risk Ratio (RR) metrics (k = 4, N = 4,662), placement of an implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge was associated with a trend toward reduction in superficial SSI risk (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.604, 95% CI [0.344, 1.061], p = 0.079), though this did not reach nominal statistical significance. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed across these studies (I² = 71.59%, τ² = 0.224, Q = 12.837, p = 0.005). One additional study (k = 1, N = 720) reported an odds ratio metric and could not be mathematically pooled due to insufficient subgroup data; it demonstrated an individual standardized effect of g = -0.254 (95% CI [-0.401, -0.108]). Publication bias assessment revealed a borderline trend toward asymmetry (Egger's intercept = -1.101, p = 0.093), with the trim-and-fill method imputing no missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a moderate risk of reporting bias.

Conclusions: Prophylactic application of an implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge demonstrates a favorable directional trend toward reducing superficial sternal wound infections following adult cardiac surgery, but statistical significance was not achieved in pooled analysis and substantial inter-study heterogeneity exists. Routine adoption cannot be unequivocally supported without risk-stratified patient selection.

# Efficacy of Implantable Gentamicin-Collagen Sponge Prophylaxis on Superficial Sternal Surgical Site Infections in Adult Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Surgical site infections (SSIs) following median sternotomy represent one of the most critical complications in adult cardiac surgery. While deep sternal wound infections (DSWIs) and mediastinitis carry devastating morbidity and mortality, superficial sternal wound infections (superficial SSIs) are far more prevalent, occurring in 2% to 8% of cardiac surgical cohorts. Superficial SSIs compromise wound healing, prolong hospital stays, necessitate prolonged antimicrobial therapy or secondary surgical debridement, and carry an established risk of secondary progression into the deeper mediastinal space.

Systemic intravenous antimicrobial prophylaxis is standard of care, yet achieving adequate bactericidal concentrations in sternal cortical bone, avascular retrosternal fat, and subcutaneous tissues remains challenging, particularly in patients with compromised microvasculature secondary to diabetes mellitus, peripheral vascular disease, or prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass. Consequently, topical adjuvant strategies have garnered intense clinical interest. 

The implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge (such as Collatamp G or Septocoll) delivers ultra-high local concentrations of gentamicin directly into the sternotomy wound bed while maintaining low, non-toxic serum levels. Concurrently, the bovine- or equine-derived type I collagen matrix provides local hemostasis and serves as a biodegradable scaffold. However, clinical trial findings have yielded conflicting results, ranging from dramatic reductions in wound infection rates to paradoxically neutral or negative findings in large multi-center randomized trials. This systematic review and meta-analysis was undertaken to rigorously quantify the effect of implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge placement versus no topical antimicrobial agent on the incidence of superficial sternal SSI within 30 to 90 days postoperatively in adult cardiac surgery patients.

---

## Search and Screening Strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across international biomedical databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and Web of Science from inception through the study cut-off date. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary (MeSH terms and Emtree headings) and free-text keywords:
- ("gentamicin-collagen sponge" OR "gentamicin sponge" OR "gentamicin fleece" OR "gentamicin-containing collagen" OR "Collatamp")
- AND ("cardiac surgery" OR "sternotomy" OR "coronary artery bypass" OR "valve surgery")
- AND ("surgical site infection" OR "sternal wound infection" OR "superficial infection" OR "wound complications").

Eligibility criteria were defined *a priori* using the PICOS framework:
1. **Population (P)**: Adult patients (≥ 18 years) undergoing median sternotomy for elective or urgent cardiac surgical procedures.
2. **Intervention (I)**: Implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge applied directly to the sternal halves or presternal space before wound closure.
3. **Comparator (C)**: Routine care or surgical closure without topical antimicrobial application (placebo or standard care control).
4. **Outcome (O)**: Incidence of superficial sternal surgical site infection documented between 30 and 90 days postoperatively according to standard diagnostic criteria (e.g., Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC] criteria).
5. **Study Design (S)**: Randomized controlled trials and controlled comparative cohort studies.

Records were deduplicated and independently screened by two reviewers at the title/abstract and full-text stages. Discrepancies were resolved via consensus.

Four studies ($k = 4$, $N = 4,662$ patients; Studies 1, 2, 3, and 5) reported outcomes using the Risk Ratio metric. Native pooling was performed on the log risk ratio scale using a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation.

- **Pooled Risk Ratio (RR)**: **0.604**
- **95% Confidence Interval**: **[0.344, 1.061]**
- **Test of Overall Effect**: $z = -1.755$, **$p = 0.0793$**

Although the point estimate demonstrates a 39.6% relative risk reduction in superficial sternal wound infections favoring the implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge group, the wide 95% confidence interval crosses unity ($1.00$), rendering this finding non-significant at the conventional $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold.

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Statistically significant and substantial between-study heterogeneity was detected within the primary Risk Ratio pool ($k = 4$):
- **Cochran's $Q$ Statistic**: $12.837$ ($df = 3$, $p = 0.005$)
- **Inconsistency Index ($I^2$)**: **71.59%**
- **Between-Study Variance ($\tau^2$)**: $0.2242$

An $I^2$ value of 71.59% indicates that more than two-thirds of the total variance across studies reflects true differences in underlying intervention efficacy rather than random sampling error. Sternal sponge placement protocols (e.g., retrosternal, presternal, or bi-layered interposition), vehicle sponge hydration techniques (e.g., dry sponge insertion versus saline pre-soaking), and baseline host risk factors (e.g., bilateral internal mammary artery harvesting, insulin-dependent diabetes) across the included trials contribute substantially to this dispersion.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

During data extraction, synthesis, and validation, the following methodological factors were documented:
1. **Separation of Outcome Metrics**: The included trials could not be aggregated into an unstratified single pool because Study 4 ($N = 720$) explicitly reported its primary endpoint using an Odds Ratio (OR), whereas the clinical protocol requested Risk Ratio (RR) pooling. To preserve mathematical integrity, Study 4 was segregated into its own grouping ($k = 1$), which had insufficient data to support statistical pooling and is therefore presented as an unpooled single-study result.
2. **Native Pooling Verification**: The primary analysis ($k = 4, N = 4,662$) utilized verified native pooling on the Risk Ratio (RR) scale via REML estimation without standardized proxy substitutions.
3. **Study Overlap and Independence**: No patient cohort overlap was identified across the five included studies; each study contributed unique surgical cohorts without double-counting.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes the current clinical trial evidence examining the efficacy of prophylactic implantable gentamicin-collagen sponges for the prevention of superficial sternal SSIs in adult cardiac surgery. In the primary pooled analysis of four studies ($N = 4,662$), the point estimate indicated a 39.6% relative risk reduction (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.604), but this did not achieve nominal statistical significance (95% CI [0.344, 1.061], $p = 0.079$). 

The observed clinical heterogeneity is prominent ($I^2 = 71.59\%$). The mechanism of action of the gentamicin-collagen sponge relies on local antimicrobial elution exceeding minimal inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for common wound pathogens, primarily *Staphylococcus aureus* and coagulase-negative staphylococci. However, surgical technique plays a profound role in clinical efficacy:
- **Hydration Technique**: Soaking the sponge prior to placement elutes a substantial fraction of the water-soluble gentamicin into the surgical basin before wound closure, markedly reducing in situ bactericidal capacity.
- **Anatomical Placement**: Inserting the sponge between the sternal halves without securing adequate presternal coverage may protect deep retrosternal compartments while leaving the superficial subcutaneous plane under-treated.
- **Foreign Body Effect**: In dry, poorly vascularized subcutaneous tissue, collagen fibers that fail to resorb promptly can act as a foreign nidus or stimulate seroma formation, mimicking or predisposing to superficial wound breakdown.

Consequently, while the intervention demonstrates biological plausibility and clinical promise, the pooled evidence demonstrates that routine, unselected application does not confer a statistically dependable reduction in superficial SSI across all surgical contexts.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations must be taken into account when interpreting these findings:
1. **Substantial Heterogeneity**: Sizable between-study variance ($\tau^2 = 0.224, I^2 = 71.59\%$) reflects differing surgical techniques, antibiotic soaking protocols, patient comorbidities, and definitions of superficial wound breakdown.
2. **Metric Heterogeneity**: One trial ($N = 720$) could not be statistically combined with the primary pool due to reporting differences (Odds Ratio vs. Risk Ratio), leaving four trials in the primary pooled synthesis.
3. **Superficial vs. Deep Infection Definitions**: Distinguishing between superficial sternal wound breakdown, sterile stitch abscesses, seroma formation, and true microbial cellulitis varies between blinded and non-blinded wound assessors.
4. **Antibiotic Resistance Profiles**: None of the included trials systematically accounted for local institutional rates of gentamicin-resistant staphylococcal isolates.

---

## Conclusion

Current evidence indicates that prophylactic placement of an implantable gentamicin-collagen sponge in adult cardiac surgery is associated with a favorable trend toward reduced risk of superficial sternal surgical site infections (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.604, 95% CI [0.344, 1.061]), but this association does not achieve statistical significance ($p = 0.079$) and is accompanied by substantial heterogeneity ($I^2 = 71.59\%$). Routine, universal adoption cannot be recommended based on aggregate data alone. Targeted application may be best reserved for high-risk surgical subsets (e.g., bilateral internal mammary artery grafting or poorly controlled diabetes) within dedicated institutional protocols.
