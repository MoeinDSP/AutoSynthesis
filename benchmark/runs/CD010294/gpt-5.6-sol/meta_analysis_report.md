# Serious Adverse Events With Vasopressin Type 2 Receptor Antagonists in Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Vasopressin type 2 receptor (V2R) antagonists, including tolvaptan, are used to slow disease progression in autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD), but their effect on serious adverse events (SAEs) requires quantitative assessment.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of V2R antagonist therapy, compared with placebo or standard therapy without a V2R antagonist, on the risk of experiencing at least one SAE during trial follow-up.

**Methods:** The supplied evidence set contained four studies, three of which contributed quantitative effect estimates. The meta-analysis used randomized/intention-to-treat denominators where available and pooled risk ratios using a random-effects restricted maximum likelihood (REML) model. The three analyzed studies included 1,634 participants. Heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochran’s Q, I², and τ². Small-study effects were assessed using Egger’s test and trim-and-fill, recognizing the severe limitations of these methods with only three studies.

**Results:** The pooled risk ratio was **0.8774781819334048** (95% CI **0.246 to 3.131**; p = **0.8404117980008647**; k = **3**; total n = **1,634**). According to the supplied direction classification, this result favored **neither arm**. The confidence interval was very wide and included both potentially substantial benefit and substantial harm. Statistical heterogeneity was moderate (I² = **55.47%**; τ² = **0.737095**), although Cochran’s Q was not statistically significant (Q = **4.457**, p = **0.1077**). Egger’s intercept was **0.7211** (p = **0.328**), and trim-and-fill imputed no studies (k₀ = **0**). The supplied assessment classified publication-bias risk as low, but the diagnostic evidence was underpowered.

**Conclusions:** The available randomized evidence does not establish that V2R antagonists either reduce or increase the occurrence of SAEs in people with ADPKD. The estimate is highly imprecise, heterogeneity is nontrivial, and only three studies contributed to pooling. Missing arm-level event counts, trial-specific SAE definitions, follow-up periods, and bibliographic details prevent full verification and limit clinical interpretation.

## Introduction

Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is a progressive inherited disorder characterized by renal cyst development, kidney enlargement, and declining kidney function. Vasopressin type 2 receptor (V2R) antagonism can reduce vasopressin-mediated cyclic adenosine monophosphate signaling and slow aspects of disease progression. Tolvaptan is the best-established therapy in this class, but its adverse-effect profile—including aquaretic effects and clinically important hepatic toxicity—makes careful safety evaluation essential.

This review addressed the following question: among people with ADPKD, what is the effect of V2R antagonist therapy, compared with placebo or standard therapy without a V2R antagonist, on the occurrence of serious adverse events (SAEs)? The prespecified outcome was the number of participants experiencing at least one SAE during each trial’s follow-up. The requested effect measure was the risk ratio (RR), using randomized/intention-to-treat denominators where available. An RR below 1 would favor V2R antagonist therapy, whereas an RR above 1 would favor control; however, interpretation of the pooled estimate below follows the explicit direction classification supplied with the analysis.

## Meta-Analytic Result

Using a random-effects restricted maximum likelihood model, the **pooled risk ratio** was **0.8774781819334048** with a 95% CI of **0.246 to 3.131**. The test of the pooled effect gave p = **0.8404117980008647**. The analysis included **3 studies** and **1,634 participants**.

The supplied `favors` classification designated the result as favoring **neither arm**—essentially a tie, or a ratio result for which favorability depends on the event coding. Consistent with that explicit classification, the synthesis provides no evidence of a difference in SAE risk between V2R antagonist therapy and control.

The uncertainty is clinically important. The confidence interval ranges from an RR of **0.246** to **3.131**, encompassing effects compatible with a large reduction as well as a large increase in SAE risk. Thus, absence of statistical significance should not be interpreted as evidence of safety equivalence or noninferiority. An absolute risk difference and number needed to treat or harm could not be calculated because control-group event risks and arm-level counts were unavailable.

## Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was **moderate**, with I² = **55.47%**. The estimated between-study variance was τ² = **0.737095**. Cochran’s Q was **4.457**, with p = **0.1077** (for three studies, the implied degrees of freedom are 2).

The nonsignificant Q test does not establish homogeneity, especially because Q has low power when only three studies are available. The magnitude of I² and τ² indicates meaningful between-study dispersion, while their precision could not be assessed because confidence intervals for heterogeneity statistics were not supplied.

Potential clinical and methodological sources of heterogeneity include differences in V2R antagonist dose, treatment duration, disease stage, baseline kidney function, participant age, control condition, SAE definitions, reporting windows, and safety surveillance intensity. None of these characteristics was supplied at study level, so these explanations could not be tested.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression yielded an intercept of **0.7211** with p = **0.328**, providing no statistically significant evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill estimated **no missing studies** (k₀ = **0**). The supplied trim-and-fill output also contained a value of **−0.1914** in a field using a standardized-effect naming convention; because its native RR interpretation was not confirmed, it should not be treated as an adjusted pooled RR.

The supplied overall publication-bias classification was **low risk**. Nevertheless, this conclusion must be interpreted very cautiously. Egger’s test and trim-and-fill are unreliable and severely underpowered with only three studies, and the absence of detected asymmetry is not evidence that selective publication or selective outcome reporting did not occur. No trial-registry comparison or outcome-reporting assessment was available.

## Discussion

This random-effects meta-analysis of three studies and 1,634 participants found a pooled RR of **0.8774781819334048** for experiencing at least one SAE with V2R antagonist therapy versus placebo or standard therapy. The 95% CI (**0.246 to 3.131**) was broad, and the pooled effect was not statistically significant (p = **0.8404117980008647**). Following the supplied direction classification, the result favored neither arm.

The principal clinical conclusion is uncertainty rather than equivalence. SAEs are comparatively infrequent outcomes, and reliable estimation often requires large samples and adequate follow-up. Even though one included study was substantially larger than the others, the random-effects model incorporated between-study heterogeneity, and the resulting interval remained very imprecise. Both clinically important benefit and clinically important harm remain compatible with the data.

Moderate heterogeneity (I² = **55.47%**) further limits the interpretation of a single pooled effect. In safety analyses, heterogeneity may arise from variation in follow-up duration and surveillance intensity as well as from genuine differences in toxicity. A trial with longer observation provides more opportunity for a participant to experience at least one SAE. Pooling trial-level risks without accounting for person-time may therefore combine different exposure windows. This concern cannot be evaluated because the trial-specific ascertainment periods were not supplied.

The outcome also aggregates potentially diverse events. A composite of “one or more SAEs” may include events related to ADPKD, background morbidity, study procedures, or treatment toxicity. It does not distinguish hepatic injury, cardiovascular events, severe aquaretic complications, hospitalization, death, or unrelated medical events. Consequently, a neutral pooled all-cause SAE result does not exclude an excess of a specific serious toxicity, nor does it establish a favorable overall benefit–harm balance.

The randomized-denominator approach is appropriate for preserving trial allocation and avoiding attrition-related bias when participants with at least one event are counted once. However, arm-level event counts and denominators were not supplied, so continuity corrections, zero-event handling, and adherence to the intention-to-treat principle could not be independently checked. The discrepancy between the transformed-looking study-level computational values and the confirmed native RR pooling also prevents a full audit of the calculations.

## Data Quality and Validation Notes

No formal `validation_flags` object was included in the supplied context. Nonetheless, several material data-quality constraints were evident:

1. The pipeline identified **four studies**, but only **three effect sizes** were available; the fourth study’s exclusion reason was not reported.
2. Arm-level SAE counts and randomized denominators were absent, preventing independent reconstruction of study-specific RRs and verification of intention-to-treat denominators.
3. Study-level computational values included negative numbers and therefore cannot be native RRs. Their transformation was not documented, although the aggregate metadata states that native RR pooling was used.
4. SAE definitions and ascertainment periods were unavailable for every study.
5. Trial citations and bibliographic identifiers were not supplied, preventing verification against full reports or trial registries.
6. No duplicate-publication assessment or explanation of how multiple reports from the same trial were handled was provided.
7. The trim-and-fill value of **−0.1914** was not confirmed on the native RR scale and should not be interpreted as such.

These issues do not justify substituting a different pooled statistic, but they materially reduce reproducibility and confidence in the evidence synthesis.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations:

- Only three studies contributed to the pooled result, limiting precision, heterogeneity assessment, and all publication-bias diagnostics.
- The 95% CI was extremely wide and included substantial benefit and substantial harm.
- Four studies were identified, but one lacked a quantitative effect estimate and could not be incorporated.
- Arm-level event counts and denominators were unavailable, preventing independent recalculation and assessment of zero-event methods.
- Trial-specific SAE definitions, coding methods, adjudication procedures, and ascertainment periods were not supplied.
- Follow-up duration could not be examined, despite its direct relevance to the cumulative risk of experiencing at least one SAE.
- Study design, participant, intervention, comparator, and disease-severity characteristics were unavailable, precluding clinical subgroup analysis.
- No study-level risk-of-bias assessment was provided.
- No certainty-of-evidence assessment, such as GRADE, could be completed from the supplied information.
- The anonymous study labels prevented linkage to publications and trial registrations.
- Publication-bias methods are not dependable with k = **3**, notwithstanding the supplied low-risk classification.
- The study-level computational scale was not fully documented and could not be reconciled directly with the native RR scale from the available data.

## Conclusion

Among people with ADPKD, the available evidence does not demonstrate that V2R antagonist therapy either reduces or increases the risk of experiencing at least one SAE compared with placebo or standard therapy. The REML random-effects **pooled risk ratio was 0.8774781819334048** (95% CI **0.246 to 3.131**; p = **0.8404117980008647**; k = **3**; n = **1,634**), and the supplied direction classification favored neither arm. Heterogeneity was moderate (I² = **55.47%**), and the estimate was too imprecise to exclude clinically consequential benefit or harm.

Future syntheses should report arm-level SAE counts, randomized denominators, zero-event handling, exact SAE definitions, and uniform ascertainment periods. Access to full trial reports and registries is also needed to evaluate selective reporting, avoid double-counting, examine specific SAE categories, and establish the certainty of the evidence.
