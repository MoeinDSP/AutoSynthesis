# SGLT2 Inhibitors Versus Placebo and All-Cause Mortality in Adults With Chronic Kidney Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors may affect survival in adults with chronic kidney disease (CKD), irrespective of type 2 diabetes status or CKD stage.

**Objective:** To estimate the effect of an SGLT2 inhibitor versus placebo on time to death from any cause among adults with CKD.

**Methods:** The supplied evidence set contained six studies and three extractable effect sizes. Eligible results were hazard ratios (HRs) for all-cause mortality from the whole randomized population at the longest reported follow-up; death counts without an HR were not eligible for reconstruction. Three studies, comprising 15,314 participants, contributed to a random-effects meta-analysis using restricted maximum likelihood (REML). Publication bias was assessed using Egger’s test and trim-and-fill.

**Results:** The pooled Hazard Ratio (HR) was 0.8049674973963845 (95% CI 0.708 to 0.915; p = 0.0008827094935916275; k = 3; n = 15,314). The supplied direction field classified this result as “neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted),” so the result is not relabeled as favoring either arm here. Statistical heterogeneity was absent (Q = 2.0, p = 0.3678; I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0). Egger’s intercept was 0.2748 (p = 0.3168), and trim-and-fill estimated no missing studies (k₀ = 0); publication-bias risk was classified as low.

**Conclusions:** Across three placebo-controlled study results, SGLT2 inhibition was associated with a statistically significant pooled HR below 1 for all-cause mortality, with no detected between-study heterogeneity. Interpretation is constrained by incomplete study-level bibliographic and methodological information, the inclusion of only three effects from six studies in the supplied pipeline, and an inconsistency between the mortality endpoint and the supplied neutral favorability classification.

## Search/Screening

The review question was defined by the following eligibility framework:

- **Population:** Adults with chronic kidney disease (CKD), with or without type 2 diabetes and across CKD stages.
- **Intervention:** An SGLT2 inhibitor, including canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, bexagliflozin, or another agent in the class.
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcome:** Time to death from any cause.
- **Eligible effect estimate:** The adjusted or primary reported hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval for all-cause mortality, based on the whole randomized population and the longest available follow-up.

Results providing death counts without an HR were to be excluded rather than converted into reconstructed HRs.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced:

- **Intercept = 0.2748**
- **p = 0.3168**

The test was not statistically significant and therefore provided no evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. Trim-and-fill estimated **k₀ = 0** missing studies. The supplied trim-and-fill value was **−0.1267**, but it was labeled `trim_fill_g`; it is therefore not reported as an HR or interpreted on the native hazard-ratio scale. Overall publication-bias risk was classified as **low** in the supplied analysis.

These findings require substantial caution because publication-bias diagnostics are poorly powered and potentially unstable with only three studies. A nonsignificant Egger’s test does not establish the absence of selective publication or selective outcome reporting.

## Discussion

This meta-analysis synthesized three placebo-controlled study results involving 15,314 adults with CKD. The REML model yielded a pooled HR of 0.8049674973963845 (95% CI 0.708 to 0.915; p = 0.0008827094935916275) for all-cause mortality. The estimate was statistically distinguishable from the null, and no statistical heterogeneity was detected.

The consistency statistics are reassuring but should be interpreted in the context of the small evidence base. An I² of 0.0% may reflect genuinely similar estimates, low power to detect heterogeneity, or both. Clinical variation could still exist across SGLT2 inhibitor molecules, diabetes status, CKD severity, background therapy, and follow-up duration, none of which could be examined with the information supplied.

The analysis appropriately used a time-to-event measure rather than substituting crude death counts or related cardiovascular and kidney outcomes. This is important because HRs incorporate event timing and censoring, whereas risk ratios reconstructed from final death totals would not preserve the randomized trials’ time-to-event analyses. Native-scale HR pooling was explicitly confirmed for the summary model.

The supplied direction classification warrants attention. Although the endpoint was specified as death from any cause and the pooled HR was below 1, the data field used to designate favorability stated “neither.” This report preserves that classification rather than independently overriding it. Before publication or clinical interpretation, the event coding and favorability metadata should be checked against the original trial reports.

The absence of evidence for funnel asymmetry and the trim-and-fill estimate of zero missing studies are compatible with low publication-bias risk. However, with k = 3, these methods cannot reliably exclude reporting bias. Searches of trial registries, regulatory documents, protocols, and statistical analysis plans would be necessary to evaluate whether all eligible mortality HRs were reported.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

No formal `validation_flags` object was supplied. Nonetheless, several data-quality issues are evident from the provided fields:

1. The pipeline identified six studies but included only three effect sizes; study-specific exclusion reasons were not supplied.
2. Individual effects were presented as standardized estimates labeled `hedges_g`, while the pooled result was explicitly reported as a native Hazard Ratio (HR). The standardized values must not be mistaken for study-level HRs.
3. The pooled result’s `favors` field classified the result as favoring neither arm, despite the endpoint being specified as all-cause death. Event coding and direction metadata should be verified.
4. The trim-and-fill estimate was supplied on a `g` scale and cannot be treated as a native HR.
5. Bibliographic details, trial characteristics, original HRs, follow-up durations, extraction locations, and risk-of-bias judgments were unavailable, preventing source-level validation and duplicate-publication assessment.

## Limitations

This report has several important limitations:

- Only three studies contributed to the pooled analysis, limiting precision in heterogeneity and publication-bias assessments.
- Three of the six studies in the pipeline did not contribute effects, but reasons for their exclusion were not supplied.
- The search strategy, search dates, databases, registry searches, language restrictions, screening process, and reviewer agreement were unavailable.
- Study names and citations were unavailable, precluding verification against original publications.
- Participant characteristics, CKD stages, diabetes status, interventions, follow-up durations, and trial-specific risks of bias were not reported.
- Native study-level HRs and their 95% CIs were not supplied; instead, the study-level table contained standardized values.
- No sensitivity analyses were reported using alternative estimators, fixed-effect models, Hartung–Knapp adjustment, leave-one-out analysis, or influence diagnostics.
- No certainty-of-evidence assessment, such as GRADE, could be completed.
- No subgroup or meta-regression analysis was possible.
- Publication-bias procedures are not dependable with only three studies.
- The supplied favorability classification requires validation before the pooled estimate is given a directional clinical interpretation.

## Conclusion

In three placebo-controlled study results comprising 15,314 participants with CKD, REML random-effects synthesis produced a pooled **Hazard Ratio (HR) of 0.8049674973963845 (95% CI 0.708 to 0.915; p = 0.0008827094935916275)** for all-cause mortality. No between-study heterogeneity was detected (I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.0; Q = 2.0, p = 0.3678). Publication-bias diagnostics did not identify asymmetry, although they were underpowered. The supplied direction field classified the pooled result as favoring neither arm; this classification and the mismatch between standardized study-level values and the native pooled HR should be checked against the primary trial data before clinical or guideline application.
