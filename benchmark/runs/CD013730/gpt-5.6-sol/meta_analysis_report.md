# Early Low-Dose Systemic Dexamethasone for Prevention of Death or Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia in Preterm Infants: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Early systemic dexamethasone may reduce pulmonary inflammation in preterm infants but must be evaluated against clinically important benefits and harms. This review assessed early low-dose systemic dexamethasone, initiated before postnatal day 7 with a cumulative course dose below 2 mg/kg, versus placebo or no treatment for the composite of death before 36 weeks’ postmenstrual age (PMA) or bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) at 36 weeks’ PMA.

**Methods:** Eligible participants were preterm infants born before 37 weeks and at risk of BPD. The prespecified binary outcome combined death before assessment with BPD—defined as oxygen requirement, oxygen dependence, or chronic lung disease—at 36 weeks’ PMA. Seven comparisons involving 1,312 randomized infants contributed to a native-scale risk-ratio meta-analysis. Because a fixed-effect Mantel–Haenszel estimate was not available in the supplied results, the reported synthesis used a random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) model. Risk ratios (RRs) below 1 were prespecified as beneficial, although the supplied result metadata classified the pooled result as favoring neither arm.

**Results:** The pooled RR was 0.9344082345801781 (95% CI 0.830–1.053; p=0.26402674931583414; k=7; n=1,312). The confidence interval included the null and the result was classified as favoring neither early low-dose dexamethasone nor placebo/no treatment. Estimated heterogeneity was negligible: Q=8.776 (6 df), p=0.1866; I²=0.0%; τ²=0.000001. Egger’s intercept was 0.0921 (p=0.5204), providing no statistical evidence of small-study effects, but trim-and-fill estimated one potentially missing study; publication-bias risk was therefore rated moderate.

**Conclusions:** Available pooled evidence did not establish that early systemic low-dose dexamethasone reduces or increases the composite risk of death or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA. The estimate is compatible with effects ranging from a potentially meaningful reduction to a small increase in risk. Interpretation is limited by unavailable trial-level event counts, study characteristics, risk-of-bias judgments, and search details.

## Introduction

Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) remains a major complication of preterm birth. Because pulmonary inflammation contributes to its pathogenesis, systemic corticosteroids can facilitate respiratory improvement and reduce ongoing oxygen or ventilatory dependence. Dexamethasone use in the first postnatal week nevertheless requires careful evaluation because any respiratory benefit must be weighed against mortality and known concerns about systemic corticosteroid exposure in a vulnerable population.

This systematic review and meta-analysis addressed the following question: among preterm infants born before 37 weeks and at risk of BPD, what is the effect of early systemic low-dose dexamethasone—started before postnatal day 7 and administered at a cumulative course dose below 2 mg/kg—compared with placebo or no treatment on the composite outcome of death before 36 weeks’ postmenstrual age (PMA) or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA?

The outcome was restricted to the composite endpoint specified in the review question. BPD was defined as oxygen requirement, oxygen dependence, or chronic lung disease at 36 weeks’ PMA. BPD alone, death alone, BPD at 28 days, and developmental or other clinical outcomes were not acceptable substitutes.

## Search/Screening

The supplied analytical dataset contained 11 study entries, of which seven contributed eligible effect estimates to the quantitative synthesis. The resulting meta-analysis included 1,312 randomized infants. Four study entries did not contribute an effect estimate; the supplied information did not identify their exclusion reasons or clarify whether they were excluded reports, ineligible studies, duplicate reports, or studies with insufficient outcome data.

No database names, search dates, search strings, language restrictions, registry searches, citation searches, deduplication procedures, or reviewer-screening methods were provided. Consequently, the reproducibility and completeness of the evidence search cannot be independently evaluated, and a numerical PRISMA flow diagram cannot be reconstructed beyond the supplied counts of 11 study entries and seven synthesized comparisons.

Eligibility required all of the following:

- preterm birth before 37 completed weeks;
- risk of BPD;
- systemic dexamethasone initiated before postnatal day 7;
- cumulative dexamethasone course dose below 2 mg/kg;
- placebo or no-treatment comparator; and
- reporting sufficient data for the composite of death before 36 weeks’ PMA or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA.

The intended extraction rule was to use intention-to-treat denominators. Where death and BPD among survivors were reported separately, the required numerator was the sum of the mutually exclusive counts of deaths before assessment and surviving infants with BPD, with the number randomized as the denominator.

## Effect Sizes

The prespecified and confirmed native effect measure was the **Risk Ratio (RR)**. An RR of 1 denotes equal composite risk in the two groups; values below 1 were prespecified as representing lower risk with dexamethasone. The supplied meta-analysis metadata nevertheless classified the pooled result as **“neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted)”**. In accordance with that classification, the pooled result is reported as favoring neither group rather than assigning benefit from the numerical direction alone.

The source dataset also contained study-level fields labeled `hedges_g`. Those auxiliary standardized values should not be interpreted as trial-level risk ratios and are not used here as the clinical effect measure. Native pooling was explicitly confirmed for the aggregate RR analysis. However, arm-level event counts and native trial-level RRs were not supplied, preventing independent recalculation of the individual study estimates or verification of their weights.

## Meta-Analytic Result(s)

Seven comparisons involving 1,312 infants were pooled. The supplied synthesis used restricted maximum likelihood (REML), indicating a random-effects analysis. A fixed-effect Mantel–Haenszel result was not available in the supplied output; accordingly, no fixed-effect estimate was reconstructed or substituted.

The **pooled risk ratio** was:

- **RR 0.9344082345801781**
- **95% CI 0.830–1.053**
- **p=0.26402674931583414**
- **k=7; n=1,312**

The confidence interval crossed 1 and the hypothesis test was not statistically significant. The supplied favorability field classified the result as favoring neither early low-dose systemic dexamethasone nor placebo/no treatment. Thus, the analysis did not establish a reduction or increase in the composite risk of death or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA.

In clinical terms, the confidence limits remain compatible with an approximate relative reduction of 17.0% at the lower limit and an approximate relative increase of 5.3% at the upper limit. These are relative-scale interpretations only; absolute risk differences and numbers needed to treat or harm cannot be calculated without a representative control-group event risk.

## Heterogeneity

Statistical heterogeneity was low to negligible:

- Cochran’s **Q=8.776** with **6 degrees of freedom**;
- Q-test **p=0.1866**;
- **I²=0.0%**; and
- **τ²=0.000001**.

The Q test did not reject homogeneity, I² attributed none of the observed variation to detectable between-study heterogeneity, and the estimated between-study variance was effectively zero. These findings indicate that the included estimates were statistically compatible with a common underlying effect. They do not, however, establish clinical equivalence among the trials, particularly because details of population risk, respiratory management, dosing schedules, and outcome ascertainment were unavailable. With only seven comparisons, both heterogeneity statistics and tests have limited ability to identify modest between-study differences.

## Publication Bias

Egger’s regression produced an intercept of **0.0921** with **p=0.5204**. This was not statistically significant and therefore provided no statistical evidence of funnel-plot asymmetry or small-study effects. The trim-and-fill procedure estimated **one potentially missing study** (`k0=1`) and returned an auxiliary standardized adjusted value of **−0.0238**. Because that value was not supplied as a native RR, it must not be interpreted as an adjusted pooled risk ratio or directly compared with the pooled RR of 0.9344082345801781.

The supplied publication-bias assessment rated risk as **moderate**. This rating is appropriate given the combination of a nonsignificant Egger test and one imputed study under trim-and-fill. Moreover, with only seven comparisons, Egger’s test has low power and trim-and-fill depends on assumptions that may not hold. Publication bias and selective nonreporting therefore cannot be excluded.

## Discussion

### Principal findings

This synthesis of seven comparisons and 1,312 randomized infants found no statistically significant effect of early low-dose systemic dexamethasone on death or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA. The pooled risk ratio was 0.9344082345801781 (95% CI 0.830–1.053), and the supplied result classification favored neither arm. Statistical heterogeneity was negligible, but this consistency should not be overinterpreted because only seven comparisons were available and clinical characteristics were not reported.

The composite outcome is clinically important because it accounts for the competing event of death. An analysis limited to BPD among survivors could be misleading if treatment altered survival. The specified approach—combining deaths before assessment with surviving infants who had BPD and retaining randomized denominators—is therefore preferable when the mutually exclusive components are available.

### Clinical interpretation

The pooled result does not prove equivalence or absence of effect. Its confidence interval permits both a potentially important relative reduction and a small relative increase in the composite outcome. Furthermore, the absence of absolute event rates prevents translation into risk differences, numbers needed to treat, or population-specific expected benefits.

The intervention definition was deliberately narrow: systemic dexamethasone had to begin before day 7 and the cumulative course dose had to remain below 2 mg/kg. The findings should not be extrapolated to later initiation, higher cumulative doses, inhaled corticosteroids, hydrocortisone, or infants outside the included BPD-risk populations.

### Certainty and applicability

A formal GRADE assessment could not be completed from the supplied data. Precision is limited because the confidence interval includes both potentially beneficial and slightly harmful effects. Risk of bias is indeterminate because trial-level judgments were unavailable. Indirectness cannot be fully assessed because participant and intervention details were not reported. Statistical inconsistency was not evident, while publication-bias risk was judged moderate.

Accordingly, the evidence supports uncertainty rather than a definitive clinical benefit. Decisions about early dexamethasone should also consider adverse outcomes and longer-term neurodevelopment, neither of which was evaluated in this outcome-specific synthesis.

## Limitations

1. **Incomplete search documentation:** Databases, search strategies, dates, restrictions, grey-literature searches, and screening methods were unavailable, preventing assessment of search reproducibility and completeness.
2. **Unexplained study attrition:** Eleven study entries were identified, but only seven contributed effects; reasons for the four noncontributing entries were not provided.
3. **No trial citations or characteristics:** The absence of bibliographic and clinical details prevented assessment of setting, eligibility, dosing comparability, baseline risk, and applicability.
4. **No arm-level event counts:** Composite events and randomized denominators by arm were unavailable. Individual RRs, intention-to-treat handling, and the required summation of mutually exclusive death and BPD counts could not be independently verified.
5. **Unavailable fixed-effect Mantel–Haenszel result:** The requested fixed-effect model was not supplied. The report therefore relies on the available REML random-effects estimate.
6. **No risk-of-bias assessment:** Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, missing outcomes, selective reporting, and other bias domains could not be evaluated.
7. **Limited power for heterogeneity and small-study effects:** Seven comparisons provide limited sensitivity for detecting heterogeneity, funnel asymmetry, or publication bias.
8. **No moderator analyses:** Potentially important differences in maturity, respiratory severity, timing, and dose could not be examined.
9. **Composite-outcome constraints:** Although clinically appropriate, the composite does not reveal whether any effect arose from mortality, BPD among survivors, or both.
10. **No safety or developmental synthesis:** This analysis cannot determine the overall benefit–harm balance of early dexamethasone.

## Conclusion

In seven comparisons involving 1,312 preterm infants, early systemic low-dose dexamethasone initiated before postnatal day 7 was not associated with a statistically significant change in the composite risk of death or BPD at 36 weeks’ PMA compared with placebo or no treatment. The pooled RR was 0.9344082345801781 (95% CI 0.830–1.053; p=0.26402674931583414), with negligible estimated heterogeneity (I²=0.0%; τ²=0.000001). The supplied metadata classified the result as favoring neither arm. Publication-bias risk was moderate despite a nonsignificant Egger test. Better-reported trial-level data, transparent searches, native event counts, formal risk-of-bias assessment, and evaluation of adverse and long-term developmental outcomes are needed before firm treatment conclusions can be drawn.
