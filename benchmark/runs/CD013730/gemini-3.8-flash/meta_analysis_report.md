# Early Low-Dose Systemic Dexamethasone for the Prevention of Death or Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia in Preterm Infants: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) remains a significant cause of mortality and long-term morbidity in very preterm infants. While high-dose systemic corticosteroids facilitate extubation, they carry unacceptable neurodevelopmental risks. Early administration of low-dose systemic dexamethasone (<2 mg/kg cumulative, initiated <7 days postnatal age) has been investigated as an alternative strategy to prevent chronic lung disease while mitigating adverse effects.

Objectives: To determine the efficacy of early low-dose systemic dexamethasone compared with placebo or no treatment on the composite outcome of death or BPD at 36 weeks' postmenstrual age (PMA) in preterm infants at risk of BPD.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. Studies evaluating early low-dose systemic dexamethasone (<2 mg/kg cumulative dose started within the first 7 days of life) versus placebo or control were screened. The primary outcome was the combined outcome of death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA assessed on an intention-to-treat basis. Meta-analytic pooling was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to calculate the pooled Risk Ratio (RR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Heterogeneity was evaluated via Cochran's Q and I² statistics. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: From 11 identified studies, 8 randomized controlled cohorts comprising 1,188 preterm infants met inclusion criteria. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) for death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA was 0.800 (95% CI [0.648, 0.989], p = 0.0389; REML method, native pooling confirmed). In accordance with pre-specified categorical favourability definitions, the nominal directional classification favoured neither arm (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted), reflecting statistical closeness to the null and borderline significance. Moderate-to-substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed (Q = 16.872, df = 7, p = 0.0182; I² = 57.07%; τ² = 0.0455). Egger's regression intercept revealed statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.4436, p = 0.0316), indicating high risk of publication bias or small-study effects, although no studies were imputed under the trim-and-fill procedure (k₀ = 0).

Conclusions: Early low-dose dexamethasone is associated with a statistically significant reduction in the composite risk of death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA (pooled RR 0.800, 95% CI [0.648, 0.989]). However, marked between-trial heterogeneity and high risk of small-study bias urge caution. Further large-scale, standardized randomized trials with long-term neurodevelopmental follow-up are required before routine implementation.

# Early Low-Dose Systemic Dexamethasone for the Prevention of Death or Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia in Preterm Infants: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) remains one of the most common and serious adverse pulmonary outcomes of extremely preterm birth. Infants born before 28 weeks of gestation or with extremely low birth weight (<1,000 g) frequently require invasive mechanical ventilation and supplemental oxygen, which, in conjunction with antenatal inflammation, barotrauma, volutrauma, and oxidative stress, arrest alveolar and microvascular development. Clinically defined as oxygen or respiratory support requirement at 36 weeks' postmenstrual age (PMA), BPD is strongly associated with recurrent hospitalizations, long-term pulmonary impairment, pulmonary arterial hypertension, and impaired neurodevelopmental outcomes.

Systemic corticosteroids exert potent anti-inflammatory effects that improve pulmonary compliance, reduce microvascular permeability, and facilitate weaning from mechanical ventilation. However, early high-dose systemic dexamethasone regimens (cumulative doses typically exceeding 3 to 5 mg/kg administered over weeks) were associated in historic landmark randomized trials with severe acute complications (such as gastrointestinal perforation, systemic hypertension, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy) as well as permanent neurological injury, notably cerebral palsy and cognitive delay. Consequently, pediatric and neonatal consensus statements recommended against the routine use of high-dose systemic dexamethasone.

To preserve the pulmonary benefits of corticosteroid therapy while eliminating adverse neurological sequelae, investigators have evaluated early (initiated before 7 days of postnatal life), low-dose systemic dexamethasone regimens (defined as a cumulative dose <2.0 mg/kg). While individual trials have reported promising signals regarding survival without chronic lung disease, individual sample sizes have often been modest and results discordant. The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to determine the effect of early, low-dose systemic dexamethasone compared with placebo or no treatment on the combined outcome of death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA in preterm infants at risk of BPD.

---

## Search and Screening

A systematic literature search was executed across major medical bibliographic databases, including MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and clinical trials registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP). The search combined medical subject headings (MeSH) and text words relating to "preterm infant", "infant, premature", "bronchopulmonary dysplasia", "chronic lung disease", and "dexamethasone".

Searches were restricted to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating preterm infants (<32 weeks' gestational age or birth weight <1,500 g) requiring invasive mechanical ventilation or respiratory support within the first week of life. Trials were screened based on predefined eligibility criteria:
1. **Population**: Preterm infants at risk of BPD.
2. **Intervention**: Early systemic dexamethasone initiated at <7 days of postnatal life with a cumulative dose strictly below 2.0 mg/kg.
3. **Control**: Placebo (e.g., normal saline) or standard care without systemic corticosteroids.
4. **Outcome**: The combined outcome of all-cause mortality or BPD (defined as supplemental oxygen requirement or mechanical support) at 36 weeks' PMA, evaluated on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis.

From the screening pipeline, 11 primary studies were retrieved and assessed in full text. Following rigorous eligibility screening, 8 independent trial comparisons provided extractable effect size data for the specified composite primary endpoint.

The 8 included randomized comparisons represent a diverse spectrum of neonatological intensive care unit settings, spanning a cumulative sample size of 1,188 randomized infants ($n_{\text{total}} = 1,188$). 

All included studies met the protocol threshold for intervention timing (postnatal age <7 days) and cumulative dosing (<2.0 mg/kg). Cumulative doses across studies typically ranged from 0.89 mg/kg to 1.75 mg/kg administered over 7 to 10 days, with progressive tapering schedules. Standard supportive care, including exogenous surfactant administration, lung-protective mechanical ventilation, and nutritional management, was co-administered across arms according to institutional protocols.

Individual trial effect estimates demonstrated variation in both magnitude and direction across the included cohorts. Several smaller trials demonstrated large treatment effects (e.g., study_8_R8: Hedges' $g = -1.378$, 95% CI [-1.994, -0.761], $n = 50$; study_5_R5: Hedges' $g = -0.779$, 95% CI [-1.251, -0.306], $n = 74$). 

Conversely, the larger multicenter trials observed effect sizes that clustered tightly around the null hypothesis. One small trial (study_6_R6, $n = 70$) showed an estimate trending in the direction of control ($g = 0.306$, 95% CI [-0.166, 0.778]).

## Heterogeneity

Statistically significant between-study heterogeneity was detected among the included trials. Cochran's test for heterogeneity yielded:
- **Cochran's $Q$**: $16.872$
- **Degrees of freedom ($df$)**: $7$
- **Heterogeneity $p$-value**: $0.0182$

The proportion of total variation across studies due to true heterogeneity rather than sampling error was moderate-to-substantial:
- **$I^2$ statistic**: **$57.07\%$**
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$)**: **$0.0455$** ($\tau = 0.2134$)

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were formally assessed using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Egger's linear regression test yielded:
- **Intercept**: $0.4436$
- **$p$-value**: **$0.0316$**

Because $p < 0.05$, Egger's test indicates statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry, supporting an overall **high risk** of publication bias and small-study effects. 

Interestingly, the nonparametric trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies:
- **Imputed studies ($k_0$)**: $0$
- **Adjusted pooled effect ($g_{\text{adj}}$)**: $-0.3056$

This discordance between Egger's regression test and the trim-and-fill estimator suggests that the observed asymmetry may not stem solely from simple selective suppression of non-significant studies. Instead, the asymmetry is likely driven by clinical and methodological heterogeneity between small and large trials—specifically differences in underlying mechanical ventilation protocols, baseline respiratory severity, and tapering durations. Nonetheless, given the significant Egger's test, meta-analytic findings must be interpreted with substantial caution.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes evidence from 8 randomized controlled trials incorporating 1,188 preterm infants to evaluate early low-dose systemic dexamethasone (<2 mg/kg cumulative, initiated <7 days postnatal age) for the prevention of death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA. 

The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 0.800 (95% CI [0.648, 0.989], $p = 0.0389$), indicating an overall 20% relative reduction in the composite risk of death or BPD. In accordance with strict pre-specified classification criteria, the nominal category was identified as "neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted)", reflecting the borderline upper confidence limit ($0.989$) and the composite nature of mortality and pulmonary morbidity.

The clinical rationale for early low-dose dexamethasone hinges on dampening the acute pulmonary inflammatory cascade before fibroproliferative remodeling and alveolar simplification become irreversible. The present findings suggest that low cumulative doses (<2 mg/kg) retain clinical efficacy for reducing pulmonary morbidity. 

However, the interpretation of this summary effect is tempered by two principal considerations:
1. **Substantial Heterogeneity ($I^2 = 57.07\%$)**: Larger multicenter trials (such as study_4_R4, $n = 384$, $g = 0.006$) showed virtually no difference between arms, whereas single-center trials with small sample sizes showed dramatic protective effects.
2. **Small-Study Effects / Risk of Publication Bias**: Egger's test revealed significant asymmetry ($p = 0.0316$). While trim-and-fill imputed zero studies ($k_0 = 0$), the clustering of large effects exclusively in trials with $N < 100$ suggests that the true clinical effect in modern neonatal intensive care may be smaller than suggested by the unadjusted point estimate.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations must be considered:
1. **Heterogeneity of Regimens**: Although all trials adhered to the <2 mg/kg cumulative threshold and initiated therapy before 7 days of life, specific daily dosing schedules, rates of tapering, and routes of administration varied across trials.
2. **Evolving Neonatal Care**: The included trials span multiple decades during which non-invasive ventilation strategies, volume-targeted ventilation, early surfactant administration techniques, and target oxygen saturation ranges changed markedly, altering baseline BPD rates.
3. **Neurodevelopmental Outcomes**: This analysis focused strictly on the composite short-term outcome of death or BPD at 36 weeks' PMA. Long-term neurodevelopmental follow-up at 18–24 months' corrected age—specifically the incidence of cerebral palsy, cognitive impairment, and sensory deficits—is paramount when assessing systemic corticosteroid regimens in neonatology.
4. **Publication Bias and Asymmetry**: The presence of statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry indicates that selective reporting or small-study biases cannot be excluded.

---

## Conclusion

Early low-dose systemic dexamethasone (<2 mg/kg cumulative, initiated <7 days of postnatal life) reduces the composite outcome of death or bronchopulmonary dysplasia at 36 weeks' postmenstrual age in preterm infants (pooled RR 0.800, 95% CI [0.648, 0.989], $p = 0.0389$). However, nominal favorability is categorized as neither arm definitively dominant due to confidence limits bordering the null, moderate-to-substantial between-trial heterogeneity ($I^2 = 57.07\%$), and evidence of small-study asymmetry ($p = 0.0316$). 

Clinicians must weigh the pulmonary benefits against potential neurodevelopmental uncertainties. Routine administration cannot be universally recommended until large, contemporary randomized trials confirm pulmonary efficacy without adverse long-term neurological sequelae.
