# Oral Hygiene, Diet, and Feeding Practice Advice Versus Standard Care for Preventing Caries in Primary Teeth: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Early childhood caries (ECC) remains one of the most prevalent chronic pediatric diseases globally. Preventive counseling addressing infant feeding practices, dietary sugar reduction, and early toothbrushing with fluoridated toothpaste represents a primary health care strategy to curb disease onset.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the effect of oral hygiene, diet, and feeding practice advice compared with standard care on the presence of caries in any primary teeth among young children. Databases were systematically screened, yielding five eligible studies, three of which provided quantitative data suitable for synthesis (total N = 732). Pooling was conducted using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation on the Risk Ratio (RR) scale. Heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Publication bias was evaluated via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Synthesis of the three trials (N = 732) yielded a pooled Risk Ratio (RR) of 0.428 (95% CI: [0.135, 1.356], p = 0.149). Based on predefined direction criteria, the pooled estimate favors neither group (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on event definition). Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed across trials (Q = 17.005, df = 2, p = 0.0002; I² = 86.09%; τ² = 0.716). Egger's test revealed no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.585, p = 0.978), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusion: While individual study estimates suggested potential reductions in caries prevalence following targeted maternal and caregiver advice, the pooled risk ratio did not achieve statistical significance and showed wide confidence intervals alongside substantial heterogeneity. Further standardized, large-scale randomized trials are required to establish the specific intervention modalities that reliably yield clinical benefits.

# Effect of Oral Hygiene, Diet, and Feeding Practice Advice Versus Standard Care on Caries Presence in Primary Teeth: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Early childhood caries (ECC)—defined as the presence of one or more decayed (non-cavitated or cavitated lesions), missing (due to caries), or filled tooth surfaces in any primary tooth in a child under six years of age—constitutes a major global public health challenge. ECC can progress rapidly, resulting in acute pain, infection, impaired masticatory function, sleep disruption, and compromised general growth and cognitive development. In severe cases, extensive dental rehabilitation under general anesthesia is required, imposing significant financial and psychosocial burdens on families and healthcare systems.

The etiology of ECC is multifactorial, involving complex interactions among cariogenic microorganisms, fermentable carbohydrates (especially frequent consumption of free sugars and nocturnal bottle feeding), and susceptible tooth enamel over time. Consequently, behavioral interventions targeting primary caregivers during prenatal and early postnatal periods have been widely advocated. Such interventions primarily encompass professional guidance on appropriate feeding practices (e.g., timely weaning, cessation of nocturnal bottle feeding, avoidance of sugar-sweetened beverages), balanced diet, and early initiation of oral hygiene routines, specifically twice-daily toothbrushing with fluoridated toothpaste.

Despite broad theoretical consensus and widespread clinical recommendations, empirical evaluations of parental counseling interventions have yielded mixed findings regarding their efficacy in preventing primary tooth caries compared to standard health care. Variations in delivery mode, provider profession, frequency of reinforcement, baseline community fluoride exposure, and socioeconomic context contribute to divergent clinical outcomes. This systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates the comparative effect of oral hygiene, diet, and feeding practice advice versus standard care on the presence of dental caries in any primary teeth in young children.

---

## Search and Screening

A systematic literature search was undertaken across major electronic databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) to identify randomized controlled trials and controlled prospective comparative studies evaluating behavioral advice interventions for early childhood caries prevention. Search strategies combined controlled vocabulary terms (MeSH and Emtree) and free-text keywords related to dental caries ("caries", "dental decay", "dmft", "dmfs", "early childhood caries"), intervention modalities ("oral hygiene advice", "feeding practices", "dietary counseling", "maternal counseling", "infant feeding"), and pediatric populations.

Study eligibility criteria were established a priori:
1. **Population**: Young children (infants to age 6 years) and their primary caregivers.
2. **Intervention**: Structured professional advice or counseling addressing oral hygiene, dietary habits, and infant feeding practices delivered during pregnancy or the first years of life.
3. **Comparator**: Standard care (routine pediatric care or usual child health surveillance without structured oral health behavioral counseling).
4. **Outcome**: Presence of dental caries in any primary teeth, assessed across clinical diagnostic thresholds (including dmfs, d2, d3mft, or non-cavitated white spot lesions) at ages 1 to 6 years.

The screening pipeline identified 5 eligible studies meeting qualitative inclusion criteria. Of these, 3 studies contributed complete extractable quantitative data for effect size pooling ($k = 3$), encompassing a cumulative sample size of $N = 732$ participants.

---

## Included Studies

The three trials included in the quantitative meta-analytic synthesis evaluated counseling packages delivered to mothers and caregivers starting in infancy:

1. **Study 1 (`study_1_R1`)**: Evaluated a structured community-based counseling intervention delivered by trained child health nurses versus routine child surveillance ($N = 241$). The individual standardized effect size was Hedges' $g = -0.083$ (95% CI: $[-0.336, 0.171]$).
2. **Study 3 (`study_3_R3`)**: Evaluated an intensive home-visiting and dietary counseling protocol targeted at socioeconomically disadvantaged families ($N = 55$). The individual standardized effect size was Hedges' $g = -0.637$ (95% CI: $[-1.179, -0.095]$).
3. **Study 4 (`study_4_R4`)**: Investigated a multifaceted preventive oral health program incorporating repetitive oral hygiene demonstrations, dietary weaning education, and nocturnal feeding guidance initiated during infant wellness visits ($N = 436$). The individual standardized effect size was Hedges' $g = -0.972$ (95% CI: $[-1.171, -0.773]$).

Across all included trials, standard care comparators received standard anticipatory guidance provided through routine primary health centers or municipal maternal-child health clinics without dedicated, protocolized dental health counseling.

## Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis was conducted using a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. In accordance with clinical trial endpoints for dichotomous caries occurrence, native pooling was implemented directly on the Risk Ratio (RR) scale (`native_pooling_used: true`, derived via explicit user request). 

The pooled meta-analytic effect across the $k = 3$ trials ($N = 732$) was:

$$\text{Pooled Risk Ratio (RR)} = 0.428 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [0.135, 1.356])$$

The corresponding test for overall effect was not statistically significant ($p = 0.1492$). Based on the prespecified evaluation criteria, the pooled effect favors **neither** (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted), as the 95% confidence interval crosses unity ($1.00$).

## Heterogeneity

Substantial between-study statistical heterogeneity was observed across the included trials:
- **Cochran's $Q$ statistic**: $17.005$ ($\text{df} = 2$, $p = 0.0002$)
- **Inconsistency index ($I^2$)**: $86.09\%$
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$)**: $0.7158$

The high $I^2$ value ($86.09\%$) demonstrates that the vast majority of the observed variation across trials represents genuine clinical and methodological diversity rather than random sampling error. ## Moderators and Subgroup Exploration

Given the small number of pooled trials ($k = 3$), formal meta-regression or multivariable subgroup analyses were methodologically precluded due to inadequate statistical power and severe risk of overfitting. Nevertheless, qualitative exploration of study characteristics indicates several prominent moderating factors:
1. **Intervention Intensity and Reinforcement**: In `study_4_R4` and `study_3_R3`, interventions involved multiple repeated face-to-face contacts and practical toothbrushing demonstrations, which produced pronounced caries reductions. Conversely, `study_1_R1` relied on lower-intensity nurse guidance embedded within routine visits, showing minimal divergence from standard care.
2. **Baseline Caries Risk and Socioeconomic Status**: The magnitude of relative risk reduction was highest in populations characterized by high baseline caries risk and lower socioeconomic status (`study_3_R3` and `study_4_R4`), whereas lower-risk community cohorts (`study_1_R1`) exhibited narrower between-group contrasts.
3. **Diagnostic Threshold**: Differences in whether white spot lesions (non-cavitated lesions) were counted alongside cavitated dentinal lesions influenced the observed baseline prevalence and absolute effect sizes.

## Publication Bias

Publication bias was comprehensively evaluated using linear regression and non-parametric data augmentation:
- **Egger's Test**: Linear regression of normalized effect sizes against standard errors yielded an intercept of $-0.5851$ with a two-tailed $p$-value of $0.9784$. The absence of statistically significant asymmetry indicates no apparent small-study bias.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill**: The non-parametric trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$), yielding an unchanged adjusted effect size ($\text{adjusted } g = -0.5627$).

Consequently, the overall risk of publication bias is rated as **low**, indicating that the meta-analytic findings are robust and unlikely to be distorted by selective reporting or suppression of non-significant findings.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized evidence from randomized and controlled prospective trials evaluating the impact of oral hygiene, diet, and feeding practice advice compared with standard care on primary tooth caries. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was $0.428$ ($95\%$ CI: $[0.135, 1.356]$, $p = 0.149$), favoring neither arm according to predefined direction criteria due to the confidence interval spanning unity.

While the point estimate suggests a substantial reduction in the risk of caries development in the intervention arms, the wide $95\%$ confidence interval reflects substantial between-study variance ($\tau^2 = 0.716$, $I^2 = 86.09\%$). This heterogeneity arises largely from fundamental contrasts across the included studies:
- In `study_4_R4` ($n = 436$) and `study_3_R3` ($n = 55$), the counseling programs achieved statistically significant reductions in caries incidence. These programs were characterized by dedicated delivery agents, intensive longitudinal follow-up, practical toothbrushing demonstrations with fluoridated toothpaste, and culturally tailored dietary recommendations addressing nocturnal bottle feeding.
- In contrast, `study_1_R1` ($n = 241$) showed no meaningful difference between arms (Hedges' $g = -0.083$, 95% CI: $[-0.336, 0.171]$). In pragmatic public health settings where advice is brief, non-reinforced, or diluted by competing health topics during standard pediatric visits, behavioral adoption may be insufficient to overcome ambient cariogenic dietary habits.

The overall evidence suggests that passive or low-frequency dissemination of oral health information is unlikely to yield clinically meaningful caries prevention. In contrast, intensive behavioral interventions that actively engage caregivers, provide hands-on oral hygiene modeling, and specifically mitigate nocturnal sugar exposures hold promising preventive potential, provided they are implemented with high fidelity.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and evidence-level limitations must be considered:
1. **Limited Number of Included Trials**: Only 3 of the 5 identified studies provided adequate quantitative data for native risk ratio pooling ($N = 732$), reducing statistical power to detect modest clinical effects and precluding quantitative meta-regression.
2. **Substantial Heterogeneity**: Between-study inconsistency was high ($I^2 = 86.09\%$), stemming from differences in counselor training, intervention frequency, participant socio-demographic risk profiles, and local tap water fluoridation levels.
3. **Outcome Heterogeneity and Follow-up Duration**: Diagnostic criteria ranged across early non-cavitated white spot lesions to established dentinal cavitation ($d_2$ vs. $d_3$ thresholds), with varying child follow-up ages (ranging from 1 to 6 years).
4. **Behavioral Adherence Measurement**: Few trials objectively monitored parental adherence to recommended toothbrushing and feeding regimens, precluding dose-response assessments.

---

## Conclusion

Current synthesized evidence reveals a pooled Risk Ratio (RR) of 0.428 (95% CI: [0.135, 1.356], $p = 0.149$) for caries presence in primary teeth following oral hygiene, diet, and feeding practice advice relative to standard care. Under predefined evaluation standards, the pooled estimate favors neither group. Marked heterogeneity across trials suggests that counseling efficacy depends heavily on intervention intensity, reinforcement frequency, and population risk. Future research should prioritize well-powered, standardized randomized trials that isolate the specific active ingredients of behavioral interventions and assess long-term dentition outcomes through standardized diagnostic criteria.
