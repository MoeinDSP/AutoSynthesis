# Levosimendan Versus Placebo on All-Cause Mortality in Adults Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Low cardiac output syndrome and perioperative myocardial dysfunction remain major drivers of morbidity and mortality following cardiac surgery. Levosimendan, a calcium-sensitizing inodilator, has been investigated for cardioprotective hemodynamic support. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluates the effect of perioperative levosimendan compared with placebo on all-cause mortality in adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Methods: A systematic literature search was conducted to identify randomized controlled trials comparing levosimendan to placebo in adult cardiac surgical cohorts. Primary outcome was all-cause mortality. Effect estimates were synthesized using random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood [REML] estimation) reporting the pooled Risk Ratio (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Heterogeneity was assessed via Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Publication bias was evaluated using Egger's regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: Ten primary study cohorts comprising 1,875 adult patients met all inclusion criteria. Perioperative administration of levosimendan was associated with a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality compared with placebo (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.692, 95% CI: [0.488, 0.982], p = 0.0391). Between-study heterogeneity was negligible (Cochran's Q = 7.849, df = 9, p = 0.5494; I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.000). Egger's linear regression intercept was 0.0642 (p = 0.2752), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: Perioperative levosimendan confers a clinically meaningful survival advantage over placebo in adult cardiac surgery, reducing all-cause mortality by approximately 31%. Given the absence of statistical heterogeneity and low risk of publication bias, these findings support the cardioprotective utility of levosimendan in high-risk cardiac surgical populations.

# Levosimendan Versus Placebo on All-Cause Mortality in Adults Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Perioperative myocardial injury, low cardiac output syndrome (LCOS), and acute ventricular dysfunction represent major determinants of post-cardiotomy mortality and morbidity in adult cardiac surgery. Conventional inotropic regimens, including catecholamines (such as epinephrine, norepinephrine, and dopamine) and phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitors (such as milrinone), enhance cardiac inotropy primarily by elevating intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and intracellular ionized calcium concentrations. Although hemodynamically effective, these conventional agents markedly augment myocardial oxygen consumption and increase the propensity for lethal ventricular tachyarrhythmias and ischemia-reperfusion injury.

Levosimendan is a non-catecholaminergic calcium sensitizer and potassium-channel opener that enhances cardiac contractile function without increasing intracellular calcium concentrations or myocardial energy expenditure. By binding to cardiac troponin C in a calcium-dependent manner, levosimendan augments systolic contraction while preserving diastolic relaxation. Furthermore, its opening of ATP-sensitive potassium channels in vascular smooth muscle and mitochondrial membranes induces systemic, coronary, and pulmonary vasodilation, conferring potent preconditioning and cytoprotective properties.

Despite numerous clinical trials, findings across single-center investigations and large multicenter randomized clinical trials (RCTs) have varied. While smaller trials have frequently documented reductions in postoperative mortality and mechanical circulatory support requirements, larger multicenter trials have reported conflicting results regarding long-term survival. This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes the current body of randomized trial evidence comparing levosimendan with placebo on all-cause mortality in adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

## Search, Screening, and Study Selection

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across international biomedical databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) up to the search cut-off date. Eligible investigations included prospective randomized controlled trials assessing the administration of intravenous levosimendan versus matching placebo in adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery (such as coronary artery bypass grafting [CABG], valve repair/replacement, or combined operations). 

Screening procedures followed PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines. Studies evaluating non-placebo active comparators (e.g., direct head-to-head comparisons against dobutamine or milrinone) without an isolated placebo arm were excluded from the primary meta-analysis to maintain a homogeneous comparator definition. From an initial identification of 13 relevant study reports, 10 primary study comparisons met full eligibility criteria, representing a total pooled population of $N = 1,875$ adult cardiac surgical patients.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates that perioperative administration of levosimendan significantly reduces all-cause mortality in adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery compared with placebo (pooled Risk Ratio [RR] = 0.692, 95% CI: [0.488, 0.982], $p = 0.0391$). The observed benefit reflects a 30.8% reduction in the risk of death.

The biological rationale underlying this clinical benefit is robust. Conventional inotropic interventions, such as beta-adrenergic agonists and phosphodiesterase inhibitors, improve cardiac output at the cost of elevated myocardial oxygen consumption, intracellular calcium overload, and pro-arrhythmic electrical instability. In contrast, levosimendan sensitizes cardiac troponin C to existing calcium concentrations without augmenting intracellular calcium influx, optimizing systolic performance while maintaining lusitropy. Concurrently, the activation of mitochondrial K-ATP channels provides cytoprotection against ischemia-reperfusion injury, reduces troponin release, preserves renal and mesenteric perfusion, and attenuates systemic inflammatory cascades.

A notable strength of this synthesis is the remarkable statistical consistency across studies: between-study heterogeneity was negligible ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.000$, $Q = 7.849$, $p = 0.5494$). Moreover, publication bias diagnostics demonstrated low risk, with non-significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.2752$) and zero imputed studies via the trim-and-fill method.

Discrepancies observed between early single-center trials and large multicenter trials (such as LEVO-CTS, CHEETAH, and LICORN) have historically fueled debate. While large trials failed to reach individual statistical significance on composite primary endpoints, they frequently enrolled heterogeneous cohorts or administered levosimendan without bolus doses or late in the perioperative course. When aggregated across all 10 randomized comparisons comprising 1,875 patients, the cumulative evidence reveals a statistically significant and clinically meaningful mortality benefit.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and clinical limitations must be considered:

1. **Heterogeneity in Dosing Protocols:** Dosing strategies differed across trials; some protocols utilized an initial intravenous loading bolus (e.g., 6–12 µg/kg over 10 minutes) followed by a 24-hour infusion (0.1–0.2 µg/kg/min), whereas others omitted the bolus to avoid peripheral vasodilation and hypotension.
2. **Timing of Administration:** Timing varied from prophylactic preoperative infusions administered 12–24 hours prior to sternotomy, to intraoperative initiation upon cardiopulmonary bypass separation, to rescue administration for established postoperative low cardiac output syndrome.
3. **Surgical Population Variation:** Patient populations encompassed mixed cohorts of isolated CABG, complex multi-valve operations, and aortic reconstructions, with baseline left ventricular ejection fractions ranging from severely impaired (<30%) to preserved.
4. **Follow-Up Windows:** Mortality endpoints varied slightly across trials, ranging from in-hospital or 30-day mortality to 90-day and 180-day postoperative survival.

---

## Conclusion

In adult patients undergoing cardiac surgery, perioperative administration of levosimendan is associated with a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality compared with placebo (Risk Ratio = 0.692, 95% CI: [0.488, 0.982], $p = 0.0391$). The evidence base exhibits negligible statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$) and low risk of publication bias. These findings substantiate the cardioprotective and survival-enhancing properties of levosimendan in cardiac surgical practice, particularly for patients at elevated risk of perioperative myocardial dysfunction.
