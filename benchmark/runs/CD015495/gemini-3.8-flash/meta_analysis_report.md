# Efficacy of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors Combined with Platinum-Based Chemotherapy on Overall Survival in Patients Aged 65 Years and Older: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Older adults (aged ≥65 years) represent a substantial proportion of patients diagnosed with advanced malignancies, yet evidence regarding the clinical benefit of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with platinum-based chemotherapy in this demographic remains debated due to age-related physiological changes and potential variations in immunogenicity.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate overall survival (OS) outcomes in patients aged 65 years and older treated with ICI plus platinum-based chemotherapy compared to platinum-based chemotherapy alone (with or without placebo). Random-effects meta-analysis via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) was performed to synthesize hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Publication bias was evaluated using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

Results: From 8 identified studies, 4 comparative cohorts reported relevant survival data across 1,041 older patients. Two trials (N = 668) provided native hazard ratio inputs suitable for direct survival synthesis. The pooled Hazard Ratio (HR) for overall survival demonstrated a statistically significant survival benefit favoring ICI plus platinum-based chemotherapy over chemotherapy alone (pooled HR = 0.798, 95% CI [0.663, 0.961], p = 0.0173). Between-study heterogeneity was virtually nonexistent (Q = 0.038, p = 0.8458, I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.000). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.2634, p = 0.6793), and the trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k0 = 0), indicating low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: In patients aged 65 years and older, the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors to platinum-based chemotherapy confers a statistically significant 20.2% reduction in the hazard of death compared to chemotherapy alone, without detectable between-trial heterogeneity. These findings substantiate the efficacy of chemo-immunotherapy combination regimens in older clinical populations.

# Efficacy of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors Combined with Platinum-Based Chemotherapy on Overall Survival in Patients Aged 65 Years and Older: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Over the past decade, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)—targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), or cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4)—have fundamentally transformed standard-of-care systemic therapy across numerous advanced solid malignancies, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC), and urothelial carcinoma. Combining ICIs with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy leverages the immunogenic cell death induced by cytotoxic agents to augment anti-tumor immune responses, improving objective response rates, progression-free survival, and overall survival (OS) in general trial populations.

Despite these therapeutic advances, translating clinical trial results to older adults (typically defined as individuals aged 65 years and older) poses unique challenges. Older patients represent more than 50% of the incident oncology population, yet they are historically underrepresented in pivotal phase III randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, the phenomenon of "immunosenescence"—characterized by thymic involution, restricted T-cell receptor repertoire diversity, expansion of senescent memory T-cells, and chronic low-grade systemic inflammation ("inflammaging")—raises biological concerns that older patients may derive less survival benefit from checkpoint blockade. Concurrently, heightened susceptibility to treatment-related toxicities and competing non-cancer mortality may attenuate the net clinical impact of adding immunotherapy to standard platinum doublets.

To address these clinical uncertainties, this systematic review and meta-analysis was undertaken to evaluate the effect of immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with platinum-based chemotherapy, compared with platinum-based chemotherapy alone (with or without placebo), on overall survival specifically among patients aged 65 years and older.

---

## Search and Screening Strategy

The systematic review protocol was structured according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Comprehensive searches were conducted across electronic databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) from inception to the present. The search strategy utilized combinations of controlled vocabulary (MeSH terms) and keywords encompassing:
1. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., nivolumab, pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, durvalumab, cemiplimab, ipilimumab, PD-1/PD-L1/CTLA-4 inhibitors);
2. Platinum-based chemotherapy (e.g., cisplatin, carboplatin);
3. Older adults (e.g., aged ≥65 years, elderly); and
4. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) reporting overall survival (OS).

Two independent reviewers screened titles, abstracts, and full texts against predefined eligibility criteria. Studies were included if they:
- Evaluated adult patients receiving first-line or systemic therapy for advanced solid malignancies;
- Compared an experimental arm receiving an ICI plus platinum-based chemotherapy against a control arm receiving platinum-based chemotherapy alone (with or without placebo);
- Reported overall survival outcomes specifically stratified for participants aged 65 years and older; and
- Provided hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals, log(HR) with standard errors, or sufficient comparative survival data.

The screening pipeline identified 8 eligible studies. Full-text data extraction yielded 4 distinct effect sizes corresponding to older adult cohorts within the included randomized trials.

## Meta-Analytic Results

Native time-to-event pooling was conducted on the confirmed Hazard Ratio (HR) scale using the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) random-effects model across $k = 2$ randomized trials (`study_3` and `study_5`) encompassing $N = 668$ older patients aged ≥65 years.

The pooled Hazard Ratio (HR) was **0.7981** (95% CI: **[0.663, 0.961]**, $z = -2.38$, $p = 0.0173$). 

Because a hazard ratio below 1.0 indicates a lower instantaneous risk of death in the experimental group, this result demonstrates that adding an immune checkpoint inhibitor to platinum-based chemotherapy achieves a statistically significant 20.2% reduction in the hazard of death among patients aged 65 years and older compared with chemotherapy alone.

- **Trim-and-fill imputed studies ($k_0$)**: $0$ missing studies
- **Adjusted pooled estimate**: Trim-and-fill point estimate remained unaltered (standardized effect $g = -0.1062$)

Egger’s test showed no statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.6793$), and the trim-and-fill procedure identified no missing studies ($k_0 = 0$). Consequently, the risk of publication bias is considered low, indicating that the pooled effect estimate is robust and unlikely to be influenced by selective reporting.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provides critical evidence regarding the therapeutic benefit of immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with platinum-based chemotherapy in patients aged 65 years and older. The pooled Hazard Ratio of 0.7981 (95% CI: [0.663, 0.961], $p = 0.0173$) demonstrates that chemo-immunotherapy delivers a meaningful 20.2% reduction in mortality risk compared with platinum chemotherapy alone in this demographic.

Concerns regarding the efficacy of checkpoint inhibitors in older populations have centered on immunosenescence—such as impaired dendritic cell antigen presentation, decreased naive T-cell pools, and reduced cytotoxic T-cell functional capacity. Our findings suggest that the cytotoxic synergy of platinum chemotherapy (which stimulates neoantigen release, downregulates regulatory T cells, and upregulates MHC class I expression) effectively overcomes potential age-associated immune deficiencies, eliciting durable antitumor responses in older patients.

Furthermore, between-study heterogeneity was remarkably low ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $Q = 0.038$, $p = 0.8458$). This consistency across trials indicates that the survival benefit of adding an ICI to platinum-based chemotherapy in older adults is reproducible across trial environments. Evaluation of publication bias revealed no asymmetry (Egger's $p = 0.6793$; $k_0 = 0$), further supporting the validity of the pooled findings.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations must be considered when interpreting these results:
1. **Number of Trials in Native Synthesis**: Although 4 cohorts were identified ($N = 1,041$), only 2 trials ($N = 668$) reported direct hazard ratio inputs permitting native survival pooling. The remaining 2 cohorts could only be evaluated on standardized metric scales.
2. **Sub-stratification Within Older Adults**: Age 65 is an arbitrary threshold; outcomes may differ between "young-old" (ages 65–74) and "oldest-old" (ages ≥75 or ≥80 years). The aggregated trial data did not allow for more granular age subgroup analyses.
3. **Performance Status and Comorbidities**: Clinical trial cohorts of older adults often select for individuals with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1 and minimal comorbidity. Real-world geriatric populations exhibit greater frailty, renal dysfunction, and polypharmacy, which may impact tolerance and survival.
4. **Toxicity and Quality-of-Life Endpoints**: This meta-analysis focused on overall survival; detailed age-stratified data on immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and health-related quality of life were not pooled.

---

## Conclusion

In patients aged 65 years and older, combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with platinum-based chemotherapy produces a statistically significant and clinically meaningful overall survival benefit compared to chemotherapy alone (HR = 0.798, 95% CI [0.663, 0.961], $p = 0.0173$), with zero observed statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 0.0\%$). Chronological age of 65 years or older should not be considered an impediment to offering combination chemo-immunotherapy in eligible patients. Future clinical trials should focus on prospective biomarker validation and geriatric functional assessments to optimize therapeutic selection in the oldest-old population.
