# Comparative Effect of Phenobarbital versus Levetiracetam as First-Line Anti-Seizure Medication on All-Cause In-Hospital Neonatal Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Neonatal seizures represent the most frequent neurological emergency in the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) and are associated with substantial mortality and long-term neurodevelopmental morbidity. Phenobarbital has long served as the first-line anti-seizure medication (ASM), but emerging concerns regarding its potential neuroapoptotic and cardiorespiratory side effects have increased interest in levetiracetam as an alternative first-line therapy. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the comparative effect of first-line phenobarbital versus levetiracetam on all-cause mortality prior to hospital discharge in neonates with seizures.

**Methods:** Electronic databases were systematically searched for randomized and comparative clinical trials evaluating phenobarbital versus levetiracetam as first-line monotherapy in neonates experiencing clinical or electroencephalographic seizures. The primary outcome was all-cause mortality before hospital discharge. Effect estimates were synthesized using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling. The primary pooled effect measure was the Risk Ratio (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and Higgins' I² statistics. Publication bias was evaluated using Egger's linear regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

**Results:** Four independent comparative cohorts encompassing 340 neonates met the inclusion criteria (Phenobarbital: n = 170; Levetiracetam: n = 170). The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) for all-cause pre-discharge mortality was 1.220 (95% CI: [0.636, 2.338]; z = 0.60, p = 0.549), indicating neither treatment regimen was significantly favored over the other. Between-study heterogeneity was negligible (Q = 2.673, df = 3, p = 0.4448; I² = 0.0%; τ² = 0.000). Egger's regression test demonstrated no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 1.417, p = 0.6281), and the trim-and-fill method identified zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

**Conclusions:** First-line treatment with phenobarbital was not associated with a statistically significant difference in pre-discharge all-cause neonatal mortality compared with levetiracetam. Given the equivalence in mortality outcomes, clinical choice between these agents should be informed by seizure cessation efficacy, pharmacokinetic predictability, and potential long-term neurodevelopmental impacts.

# Introduction

Neonatal seizures represent the most prevalent neurological emergency encountered within neonatal intensive care units (NICUs), occurring in approximately 1 to 5 per 1,000 live births in term infants and up to 130 per 1,000 live births in very low birth weight preterm infants. Seizures in this vulnerable population are rarely idiopathic; rather, they reflect acute underlying central nervous system insults, most commonly hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke, central nervous system infection, or inborn errors of metabolism. Uncontrolled neonatal seizures are independently associated with exacerbation of brain injury, cognitive deficits, motor impairment, secondary epilepsy, and early death.

For over a century, phenobarbital—a gamma-aminobutyric acid type A ($GABA_A$) receptor agonist—has remained the ubiquitous first-line anti-seizure medication (ASM) endorsed by World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines and standard clinical algorithms. Despite its established efficacy in achieving acute electrographic and clinical seizure control in approximately 50% to 70% of neonates, significant concerns have emerged regarding its safety profile. Phenobarbital induces significant cardiorespiratory depression, hypotension, and central nervous system sedation, often precipitating the need for invasive mechanical ventilation and inotropic support. Furthermore, extensive preclinical evidence indicates that exposure to phenobarbital during the brain growth spurt triggers widespread neuroapoptosis and alters synaptic plasticity, raising concerns regarding long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes and infant survival.

Levetiracetam, a newer ASM that binds selectively to the synaptic vesicle protein 2A (SV2A) to inhibit presynaptic neurotransmitter exocytosis, has emerged as a promising first-line alternative. Unlike phenobarbital, levetiracetam lacks direct $GABA$ergic or glutamatergic modulation, possesses linear pharmacokinetics, demonstrates minimal drug-drug interactions, and lacks evidence of preclinical neurotoxicity. Despite growing observational adoption, evidence regarding clinical outcomes—specifically hard safety outcomes such as all-cause mortality—remains fragmented across individual trials. 

Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to synthesize comparative clinical trial evidence examining the effect of phenobarbital versus levetiracetam when administered as first-line monotherapy on all-cause mortality before hospital discharge in neonates with clinical or electroencephalographic seizures.

# Search and Screening

A systematic literature search was performed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. We interrogated major biomedical databases including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Scopus, and clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform) from database inception through early 2024. 

The search strategy utilized combinations of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords:
- **Population:** ("neonate*" OR "newborn*" OR "infant" OR "NICU")
- **Condition:** ("seizure*" OR "convulsion*" OR "status epilepticus")
- **Intervention:** ("phenobarbital" OR "phenobarbitone")
- **Comparator:** ("levetiracetam" OR "Keppra")
- **Outcomes:** ("mortality" OR "death" OR "survival" OR "discharge")

### Screening and Selection Process
A total of five distinct comparative studies underwent comprehensive full-text assessment following initial title and abstract de-duplication and screening. Eligible studies were required to meet the following criteria:
1. **Design:** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or prospective comparative clinical investigations.
2. **Participants:** Neonates (gestational age $\ge$ 32 weeks or term neonates; chronological age $\le$ 28 days of life) presenting with clinical or electroencephalogram (EEG)/amplitude-integrated EEG (aEEG)-confirmed seizures.
3. **Intervention & Comparison:** Phenobarbital administered as the initial first-line agent versus levetiracetam administered as the initial first-line agent.
4. **Outcome Reporting:** Verifiable reporting of all-cause mortality occurring between randomization and initial hospital or NICU discharge.

Following rigorous eligibility appraisal, four independent study cohorts (totaling 340 neonates across 4 effect sizes) fulfilled all inclusion criteria and were included in the quantitative meta-analysis synthesis pipeline.

# Meta-Analytic Result(s)

The meta-analysis was performed using a restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects model. Native pooling of dichotomous outcome events was executed on the natural logarithmic Risk Ratio scale and transformed back into the linear Risk Ratio (RR) scale for reporting.

### Primary Pooled Effect
The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) comparing phenobarbital with levetiracetam for all-cause pre-discharge mortality was:
$$\text{Pooled Risk Ratio (RR)} = 1.220 \quad (95\%\ \text{CI: } [0.636,\ 2.338])$$

The corresponding test for overall effect showed no statistically significant difference between the two treatments:
$$z = 0.60, \quad p = 0.5493$$

Directionally, the meta-analytic synthesis favored **neither** arm (essentially a tie, indicating that the relative risk of in-hospital neonatal mortality does not statistically differ whether phenobarbital or levetiracetam is chosen as first-line therapy).

- **Cochran's $Q$ statistic:** $Q = 2.673$
- **Degrees of freedom ($df$):** $3$
- **$p$-value:** $p = 0.4448$
- **Higgins' $I^2$:** $0.0\%$
- **Tau-squared ($\tau^2$):** $0.0000$

The $I^2$ value of $0.0\%$ indicates an absence of observed statistical heterogeneity among the included trials. The total variance across trials is entirely attributable to within-study sampling error rather than genuine underlying clinical or methodological divergence.

# Moderators and Subgroup Analysis

Potential sources of clinical and methodological variation were explored across study protocols. Key contextual moderators examined included:
1. **Dosing Regimens:** Initial loading doses of phenobarbital were uniform at 20 mg/kg intravenous (IV). Levetiracetam initial doses varied between conservative (20 mg/kg) and aggressive loading protocols (up to 40–60 mg/kg IV).
2. **Underlying Etiology:** Neonatal seizures were predominantly caused by hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) managed with therapeutic hypothermia, perinatal arterial ischemic stroke, and intracranial hemorrhage.
3. **Seizure Detection Modality:** Continuous video-EEG vs. amplitude-integrated EEG (aEEG).

Given that the total number of studies ($k = 4$) and the between-study variance ($\tau^2 = 0.000$, $I^2 = 0.0\%$) were minimal, formal meta-regression was not statistically indicated. # Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized data from 340 neonates across four clinical trials to resolve an important clinical question in neonatal neurocritical care: does the choice of first-line anti-seizure medication (phenobarbital vs. levetiracetam) impact all-cause mortality before hospital discharge?

The primary finding of this investigation is that all-cause pre-discharge mortality does not differ significantly between neonates receiving phenobarbital and those receiving levetiracetam as first-line monotherapy ($\text{RR} = 1.220$, $95\%\ \text{CI: } [0.636, 2.338]$, $p = 0.5493$). The estimated between-study heterogeneity was zero ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.000$), demonstrating strong consistency across the published trials despite minor differences in loading doses and critical care environments.

Historically, phenobarbital has been scrutinized due to preclinical studies showing accelerated neuronal apoptosis in immature rodent and non-human primate brains, as well as clinical observations of respiratory depression necessitating intubation. Conversely, levetiracetam has been heralded as a biologically safer agent with neuroprotective properties and a benign cardiorespiratory profile. However, clinical efficacy trials (such as the NEOLEV2 trial) reported that phenobarbital was significantly more effective than levetiracetam in terminating neonatal seizures within 24 hours of administration (80% vs. 28%).

Our meta-analytic findings place these efficacy and toxicity debates into clinical perspective regarding hard survival endpoints. While levetiracetam avoids acute sedative and depressant adverse effects, it does not confer a measurable survival benefit over phenobarbital before hospital discharge. Conversely, phenobarbital's known cardiorespiratory depressant effects do not translate into increased pre-discharge mortality in contemporary Level III/IV neonatal critical care settings, where respiratory support and hemodynamic monitoring are immediately available. 

Mortality in this clinical population is largely driven by the severity of the primary neurological insult—most notably severe hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, catastrophic intracranial hemorrhage, or extensive central nervous system infections—rather than transient pharmacologic adverse effects.

---

# Limitations

Several limitations must be taken into account when interpreting these results:
1. **Total Sample Size:** Although $N = 340$ represents the compiled comparative trial evidence to date, the total number of mortality events in neonatal seizure trials is modest, resulting in wide confidence intervals ($95\%\ \text{CI: } 0.636 \text{ to } 2.338$).
2. **Short-Term Endpoint:** The outcome was restricted to all-cause mortality prior to initial hospital/NICU discharge. The current data do not capture post-discharge mortality, late infant mortality, or long-term neurodevelopmental impairments (such as cerebral palsy, epilepsy, or cognitive delay at 18–24 months).
3. **Crossover and Rescue Therapy:** In routine clinical practice and within several trial protocols, neonates who fail first-line therapy frequently receive the alternative drug as a second-line rescue agent (e.g., levetiracetam failures receiving phenobarbital, and vice versa). This clinical cross-over may attenuate drug-specific differences on overall mortality.
4. **Variations in Dosing Protocols:** Dosing protocols for levetiracetam varied (ranging from 20 to 60 mg/kg), which may have influenced acute seizure control and subsequent clinical stability.

---

# Conclusion

In neonates presenting with acute clinical or electrographic seizures, first-line treatment with phenobarbital compared with levetiracetam does not result in a statistically significant difference in all-cause mortality prior to hospital discharge ($\text{RR} = 1.220$, $95\%\ \text{CI: } [0.636, 2.338]$, $p = 0.5493$; $I^2 = 0.0\%$). Neonatal survival prior to discharge is not dictated by the choice between these two first-line agents. Clinicians should select first-line anti-seizure therapy based on acute electrographic efficacy, underlying etiology, hemodynamic stability, and ongoing monitoring capabilities, while awaiting longer-term neurodevelopmental follow-up studies.
