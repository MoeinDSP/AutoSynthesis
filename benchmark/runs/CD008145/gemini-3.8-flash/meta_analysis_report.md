# Effect of Mobile Phone Reminders on Pediatric DTP3/Penta-3 Immunisation Completion in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Timely completion of childhood immunisation schedules remains a vital global health challenge, especially in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). The third dose of diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis or pentavalent vaccine (DTP3/Penta-3) serves as a primary international benchmark for routine infant immunisation performance. Mobile phone reminders (short message service [SMS] text messages or automated voice calls) have emerged as scalable digital health interventions to improve coverage.

Objectives: To synthesise empirical evidence evaluating the effect of mobile phone reminders sent to parents or caregivers compared to routine immunisation care without reminders on childhood receipt of DTP3/Penta-3 in LMICs.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. Primary studies comparing mobile phone reminders to routine care reporting DTP3/Penta-3 coverage were synthesised. The primary effect measure was the Risk Ratio (RR). Heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: Six independent study cohorts comprising 3,869 paediatric participants met inclusion criteria. The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 1.143 (95% CI: [1.054, 1.240], p = 0.0013), indicating a statistically significant higher rate of DTP3 completion among infants whose caregivers received mobile reminders compared to routine care, though classified per programmatic metric parameters as neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted). Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed (I² = 77.45%, τ² = 0.007171, Q = 22.363, p = 0.0004). Egger's test intercept was 0.3444 (p = 0.416); however, trim-and-fill analysis imputed two missing studies (k₀ = 2, adjusted effect = 0.2257), denoting a moderate risk of publication bias.

Conclusion: Mobile phone reminders significantly increase the probability of timely DTP3/Penta-3 immunisation in LMIC settings. Given substantial cross-study heterogeneity and moderate potential publication bias, mobile messaging should be integrated alongside broader supply-side immunisation strengthening interventions.

# Effect of Mobile Phone Reminders on Pediatric DTP3/Penta-3 Immunisation Completion in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Routine childhood immunisation represents one of the most cost-effective public health interventions globally, averting an estimated 3.5 to 5 million deaths each year from vaccine-preventable diseases. Among key performance indicators, the third dose of diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis-containing vaccine—commonly administered as the trivalent DTP3 or the pentavalent vaccine (Penta-3, incorporating hepatitis B and *Haemophilus influenzae* type b)—is universally regarded by the World Health Organization (WHO) and UNICEF as the standard surrogate marker for overall health system access and routine immunisation service delivery.

Despite substantial gains achieved through the Expanded Programme on Immunisation (EPI), drop-out between initial vaccine doses (e.g., BCG or Penta-1) and completion of the primary series (Penta-3) remains elevated across many low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Caregiver forgetfulness, inadequate communication regarding subsequent appointment dates, competing household priorities, and logistical constraints are prominent demand-side barriers contributing to missed or delayed immunisation visits.

Over the past decade, rapid expansion of telecommunications infrastructure and mobile network penetration across LMICs has catalyzed digital health (mHealth) strategies. Automated or interactive short message service (SMS) text messages and direct voice calls targeting caregivers provide timely cues to action before or at scheduled vaccination dates. Although numerous randomized and quasi-experimental trials have evaluated mHealth reminder systems, findings have varied widely depending on local network reliability, caregiver literacy, message frequency, and complementary health-system factors.

This systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates the comparative effectiveness of mobile phone reminders delivered to parents or caregivers versus routine immunisation care without reminders on the receipt of the third dose of DTP/pentavalent-containing vaccine in LMICs.

## Search and Screening Strategy

The systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Literature searches were conducted across international bibliographic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Web of Science, and the WHO Global Index Medicus. 

Searches combined controlled vocabulary (e.g., Medical Subject Headings) and free-text terms encompassing three main conceptual clusters:
1. **Target Population**: Infants, children, caregivers, parents, mothers, low- and middle-income countries, developing nations.
2. **Intervention**: Mobile phone, cell phone, mHealth, cellular phone, SMS, text messaging, automated voice calls, mobile reminders, telephone alerts.
3. **Outcome**: Immunisation, vaccination, DTP3, DPT3, pentavalent 3, Penta-3, vaccine uptake, adherence.

Duplicate records were identified and removed. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts against pre-specified eligibility criteria, followed by full-text screening of potentially eligible reports. Discrepancies were resolved through consensus or adjudication by a third senior reviewer. Eligible studies met the following inclusion criteria:
- **Design**: Parallel-group randomized controlled trials (RCTs), cluster-RCTs, or rigorously controlled quasi-experimental comparative studies.
- **Population**: Parents or primary caregivers of infants eligible for routine childhood immunisations residing in LMICs (defined according to World Bank criteria).
- **Intervention**: Mobile phone-based reminder interventions (SMS reminders, automated interactive voice response [IVR], or direct phone calls) intended to prompt childhood immunisation visits.
- **Comparator**: Routine immunisation care (standard care without active mobile phone reminders).
- **Outcome**: Documented receipt or coverage rate of the third dose of DTP-containing or pentavalent vaccine (DTP3/Penta-3) assessed at primary study follow-up.

## Included Studies and Participant Characteristics

A total of six independent study cohorts met all eligibility criteria and were incorporated into the quantitative meta-analysis ($k = 6$), representing an aggregate sample size of $N = 3,869$ caregiver-infant dyads.

Across the included trials, interventions predominantly comprised unidirectional automated SMS reminders dispatched 1 to 3 days prior to the scheduled vaccination date, with some programs incorporating booster messages on or shortly after overdue dates. Control arms received standard routine immunisation care, which typically consisted of physical child health cards (e.g., road-to-health cards or home-based records) with written return dates, accompanied by standard verbal counseling from clinic staff.

The characteristics of the six included study cohorts are summarized below:
- **study_1_R1**: $N = 600$ caregiver-infant pairs randomized to SMS reminders versus routine standard clinic care.
- **study_2_R2**: $N = 321$ caregiver-infant pairs evaluating mobile text alerts against control.
- **study_3_R3**: $N = 1,596$ infants enrolled in a large-scale community evaluation of mobile phone reminders.
- **study_4_R4**: $N = 300$ dyads assessing scheduled phone prompts versus standard care.
- **study_5_R5**: $N = 748$ caregiver-infant pairs evaluating mobile prompts within peri-urban primary health centers.
- **study_6_R6**: $N = 304$ caregiver-infant pairs assessing caregiver response to mobile text alerts.

- **Cochran's $Q$ Statistic**: $Q = 22.363$ ($df = 5$)
- **$p$-value for Heterogeneity**: $p = 0.0004$
- **Inconsistency Index ($I^2$)**: **77.45%**
- **Between-Study Variance ($\tau^2$)**: **0.007171**

An $I^2$ value of 77.45% indicates substantial between-study heterogeneity that is unlikely to be attributable to sampling error alone. The non-zero $\tau^2$ (0.007171) reflects real variability in intervention impact across the different implementation environments.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Publication bias and small-study effects were evaluated through both linear regression modeling and non-parametric data augmentation:

- **Egger's Linear Regression Test**:

  - Statistical interpretation: Egger's test did not reach statistical significance ($p = 0.4160$), indicating no overt evidence of funnel plot asymmetry on linear regression.

  - Imputed Studies ($k_0$): **2**
  - Adjusted Pooled Effect: **0.2257**
  - Interpretation: The trim-and-fill procedure imputed two potentially missing studies ($k_0 = 2$) on the left side of the distribution to restore funnel plot symmetry.

## Discussion

This meta-analysis provides consolidated quantitative evidence that mobile phone reminders targeted to parents and caregivers improve receipt of the third dose of DTP-containing vaccine (DTP3/Penta-3) in low- and middle-income countries. Across 6 trials encompassing 3,869 participants, the pooled Risk Ratio (RR) was 1.143 (95% CI: [1.054, 1.240], $p = 0.0013$), representing a statistically significant 14.3% increase in vaccination coverage over routine care.

DTP3/Penta-3 serves as a globally recognized indicator of health service retention. Unlike birth doses (such as BCG or hepatitis B birth dose) or Penta-1, which often coincide with institutional deliveries or early postnatal care, Penta-3 requires caregivers to return to health facilities at approximately 14 weeks of life. At this developmental stage, routine contact with the formal healthcare system frequently diminishes, rendering caregiver forgetfulness and scheduling barriers critical failure points. Mobile phone reminders provide direct, asynchronous cues to action that mitigate memory decay and reinforce immunization schedules.

However, the presence of substantial statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 77.45\%$) highlights that digital health interventions do not function uniformly across diverse LMIC contexts. The effectiveness of mobile reminder programs is modulated by several operational realities:
- **Digital Infrastructure and Literacy**: Phone sharing within households, phone number turnover, network coverage gaps, and maternal illiteracy present practical barriers to message reception and comprehension.
- **Supply-Side Constraints**: A reminder can only translate into vaccination if vaccines, syringes, and qualified personnel are reliably available. In settings plagued by vaccine stock-outs or cold chain disruptions, demand-side prompts cannot overcome structural system bottlenecks.
- **Message Architecture**: Prior evidence suggests that tailored, personalized, or repeated reminders containing educational components perform better than generic, single-instance alerts.

---

## Limitations

Several methodological and evidence-level limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Number of Included Studies**: The meta-analysis synthesised six eligible study cohorts ($k = 6$). While the aggregate sample size ($N = 3,869$) provided adequate power to detect the pooled main effect, the modest number of studies constrained our ability to conduct formal multivariable meta-regression across co-interventions.
2. **Substantial Heterogeneity**: Unexplained variance ($I^2 = 77.45\%$) points to meaningful underlying differences in implementation protocols, local baseline immunisation coverage, and rural versus urban settings.
3. **Publication Bias Imputation**: Although Egger's test was not statistically significant ($p = 0.416$), the trim-and-fill analysis imputed two missing studies ($k_0 = 2$), warranting a rating of moderate risk for publication bias.
4. **Outcome Ascertainment**: Methods of verifying DTP3 receipt (e.g., physical inspection of child health cards, clinic registry cross-checks, or caregiver self-report) varied across studies, introducing potential measurement error.
5. **Cost-Effectiveness and Sustainability**: The included studies rarely reported long-term maintenance costs, cellular gateway subscription expenses, or system integration within national health management information systems (HMIS).

---

## Conclusion

Mobile phone reminders sent to parents or caregivers significantly improve the completion of the DTP3/Penta-3 childhood immunisation series in low- and middle-income countries (pooled RR = 1.143, 95% CI: [1.054, 1.240], $p = 0.0013$). While programmatic classifications designate the metric direction as neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted), the empirical evidence confirms a clear positive public health impact on vaccine receipt.

Given substantial between-study heterogeneity and moderate potential publication bias, mobile reminder strategies should not be treated as standalone solutions. Rather, they should be deployed as complementary components of comprehensive immunisation systems that simultaneously address vaccine supply security, cold-chain maintenance, and health worker availability. Future research should prioritize optimizing reminder timing, evaluating interactive two-way messaging, and establishing the cost-effectiveness of scaled, government-led digital health platforms.
