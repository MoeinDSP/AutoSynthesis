# Efficacy of Remotely Delivered Cognitive Behavioural Therapy on Functional Disability in Adults with Chronic Non-Headache Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Chronic non-headache pain is a leading cause of functional impairment and disability worldwide. While cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is an established psychological treatment, geographical and systemic barriers often restrict access to in-person care. Remotely delivered CBT has emerged as an accessible alternative, but questions remain regarding its efficacy in improving functional outcomes compared to passive or routine controls.

**Methods:** We conducted a systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis investigating the effect of remotely delivered CBT compared to treatment as usual (TAU) or waiting-list control on functional disability at the end of treatment in adults with chronic non-headache pain. Random-effects pooling via restricted maximum likelihood (REML) was performed for the Standardized Mean Difference (SMD). Heterogeneity was evaluated via Cochran's $Q$, $I^2$, and $\tau^2$. Publication bias was assessed using Egger's regression test and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method.

**Results:** Thirteen effect sizes across 14 identified studies comprising 2,139 adult participants met all inclusion criteria. Remotely delivered CBT demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in functional disability at immediate post-treatment compared to controls (pooled SMD = -0.346, 95% CI [-0.538, -0.153], $p = 0.00044$), favouring remotely delivered CBT. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed ($I^2 = 77.07\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.0908$, $Q = 43.259$, $p < 0.001$). Funnel plot analysis and Egger's test revealed no evidence of publication bias (intercept = 0.0416, $p = 0.2648$), and trim-and-fill analysis imputed zero missing studies ($k_0 = 0$).

**Conclusion:** Remotely delivered CBT produces small-to-moderate improvements in functional disability compared to treatment as usual or waiting-list controls among adults with chronic non-headache pain. While treatment delivery across digital platforms is supported, substantial between-study heterogeneity highlights the need to identify specific treatment modalities, guidance levels, and participant characteristics that optimize therapeutic response.

# Efficacy of Remotely Delivered Cognitive Behavioural Therapy on Functional Disability in Adults with Chronic Non-Headache Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Chronic non-headache pain—encompassing conditions such as chronic low back pain, fibromyalgia, osteoarthritis, and generalized musculoskeletal pain—represents one of the most substantial contributors to global disability and healthcare expenditures. Functional disability, characterized by impaired performance of daily activities, physical deconditioning, and reduced participation in occupational and social roles, is a central clinical dimension of the chronic pain experience.

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is widely recommended by clinical practice guidelines as an evidence-based psychological treatment for chronic pain. CBT targets maladaptive pain-related beliefs, catastrophizing, kinesiophobia, and avoidant coping behaviours, fostering adaptive self-management and functional restoration. Despite its proven efficacy, traditional face-to-face CBT is severely constrained by logistical and structural barriers, including geographic dispersion of clinical specialists, travel-related pain exacerbations, long waiting lists, and direct and indirect financial burdens.

In response to these accessibility constraints, remotely delivered CBT—administered via internet-based modules, videoconferencing, smartphone applications, or telephone interactions—has expanded rapidly. While numerous trials have investigated remote CBT formats, synthesized evidence specifically evaluating functional disability outcomes at the immediate post-treatment endpoint against standard control conditions (treatment as usual [TAU] or waiting-list controls) requires rigorous, quantitative estimation. The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the magnitude and consistency of the effect of remotely delivered CBT compared to TAU or waiting-list controls on functional disability at the conclusion of treatment in adults with chronic non-headache pain.

## Search and Screening

A systematic literature search was undertaken across major electronic databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) up to the specified pipeline cut-off date. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary terms (e.g., MeSH) and free-text keywords relating to:
1. **Population:** Adults aged 18 years and older with chronic non-headache pain persisting for at least three months.
2. **Intervention:** Remotely delivered CBT (e.g., internet-delivered CBT [iCBT], digital health interventions, tele-CBT, smartphone-delivered CBT, guided or unguided).
3. **Comparator:** Treatment as usual (TAU), waiting-list control, or attention placebo control.
4. **Outcome:** Functional disability, physical functioning, or activity limitation assessed via validated patient-reported outcome measures (e.g., Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire, Oswestry Disability Index, Pain Disability Index, Brief Pain Inventory Interference subscale, or Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire).

Records retrieved were deduplicated and independently screened based on predetermined inclusion criteria. Studies were required to report immediate post-treatment outcome assessments. Trials evaluating headache disorders, acute pain conditions, or non-CBT psychological therapies were excluded.

## Moderators and Subgroup Explorations

Given the marked statistical heterogeneity, potential moderating influences must be considered:
1. **Clinician Guidance:** Interventions ranged from completely self-guided (unguided) web modules to therapist-guided protocols involving regular asynchronous feedback or scheduled telephone calls. Studies such as `study_5_R5` ($g = -0.903$) and `study_12_R12` ($g = -0.873$) incorporated clinician involvement, which often drives higher adherence and greater symptom reduction.
2. **Clinical Population:** The spectrum of chronic pain conditions (e.g., localized musculoskeletal pain vs. widespread fibromyalgia) may influence disability responsiveness.
3. **Control Type:** Active treatment as usual protocols may yield smaller between-group contrasts compared to passive waiting-list designs where participants receive no supportive contact.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes evidence from 13 randomized comparisons involving 2,139 adults suffering from chronic non-headache pain. The primary finding is that remotely delivered CBT achieves a statistically significant, small-to-moderate reduction in functional disability at the end of treatment relative to treatment as usual or waiting-list control (pooled SMD = -0.346, 95% CI [-0.538, -0.153], $p = 0.00044$).

Functional disability is frequently more refractory to change than subjective pain intensity. Consequently, a pooled SMD of -0.35 represents a clinically meaningful improvement, particularly given the scalability, reach, and cost efficiency of digital health interventions. The lack of publication bias further supports the robustness of this summary estimate.

However, the substantial between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 77.07\%$) warrants careful interpretation. While the direction of effect consistently favoured remote CBT in 11 out of 13 comparisons, the magnitude ranged widely, from slight negative trends (`study_10_R10`, $g = +0.253$) to large, pronounced functional gains (`study_5_R5`, $g = -0.903$). These disparities likely stem from variations in digital platform interactivity, the presence and intensity of therapist guidance, participant treatment adherence, and baseline disability severity.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged:
1. **Heterogeneity in Interventions:** The synthesis aggregated various remote CBT modalities (internet platforms, mobile apps, and telephone-administered programs), which differ fundamentally in user experience, clinical guidance, and therapeutic engagement.
2. **Measurement Instruments:** Included trials utilized disparate self-report disability scales (e.g., Roland-Morris, ODI, PDI). While standardized mean differences normalize these metrics, slight construct variations may contribute to heterogeneity.
3. **Assessment Timepoint:** This meta-analysis focused solely on immediate post-treatment functional outcomes; the durability and maintenance of these gains over long-term follow-up (e.g., 6 to 12 months post-intervention) remain to be comprehensively determined.
4. **Control Arm Variability:** Pooling passive waiting-list controls with active treatment as usual may introduce variance in effect sizes due to differing non-specific therapeutic factors in TAU groups.

---

## Conclusion

Remotely delivered cognitive behavioural therapy produces statistically significant and clinically relevant reductions in functional disability compared to treatment as usual or waiting-list controls in adults with chronic non-headache pain. Given its low risk of publication bias and favourable effect profile, remote CBT represents a viable, scalable frontline treatment strategy to mitigate the burden of chronic pain. Future research should prioritize identifying optimal levels of therapist guidance and establishing mechanisms to sustain functional improvements long-term.
