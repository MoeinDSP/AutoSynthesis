# The Effect of Testosterone Pretreatment on Live Birth and Ongoing Pregnancy in Assisted Reproductive Technology: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Poor ovarian response (POR) remains one of the most challenging presentations in assisted reproductive technology (ART), frequently leading to cycle cancellation and low pregnancy rates. Pretreatment with androgens, specifically testosterone, has been proposed to enhance ovarian sensitivity to gonadotropins and improve follicular recruitment.

Objectives: To systematically evaluate the effect of testosterone pretreatment compared with placebo or no treatment on live birth or ongoing pregnancy rates per randomised woman undergoing in vitro fertilisation (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted adhering to PRISMA guidelines. Randomised controlled trials evaluating testosterone pretreatment versus placebo or no treatment were identified. The primary outcome was live birth or ongoing pregnancy rate per randomised woman. Random-effects meta-analysis was performed using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to pool Odds Ratios (ORs). Heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Small-study effects and publication bias were assessed via Egger's linear regression and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure.

Results: From 8 identified records, 7 randomised comparisons comprising 508 randomised women (treatment n = 254, control n = 254 across trials) were included in the meta-analysis. The pooled Odds Ratio (OR) was 2.260 (95% CI [1.346, 3.794], p = 0.0020), with observed heterogeneity quantified at I² = 0.0% (Q = 3.804, p = 0.7031, τ² = 0.0). According to pre-specified directionality classification, the direction metric is classified as favors neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted). Egger's regression test indicated no significant funnel asymmetry (intercept = 1.0426, p = 0.6189), though the trim-and-fill method imputed one missing study (k₀ = 1, adjusted effect = 0.5627), yielding an overall assessment of moderate risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: In women undergoing IVF/ICSI, testosterone pretreatment is associated with a statistically significant increase in the Odds Ratio (OR) of 2.260 (95% CI [1.346, 3.794]) for live birth or ongoing pregnancy with zero observed statistical heterogeneity across trials. Nevertheless, overall sample sizes remain modest and the publication bias risk was judged to be moderate, warranting adequately powered multi-centre trials before routine clinical implementation.

# The Effect of Testosterone Pretreatment on Live Birth and Ongoing Pregnancy in Women Undergoing Assisted Reproductive Technology: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Poor ovarian response (POR) to controlled ovarian stimulation represents a major clinical hurdle in assisted reproductive technology (ART), occurring in approximately 9% to 24% of in vitro fertilisation (IVF) cycles. Women with POR, commonly defined by the Bologna criteria or the POSEIDON stratification, face substantially higher rates of cycle cancellation, fewer oocytes retrieved, lower fertilisation rates, and diminished cumulative live birth rates compared to normal responders.

In an effort to optimise ovarian follicular responsiveness, various adjuvant therapies have been investigated. Among these, androgen supplementation—specifically pre-treatment with transdermal or oral testosterone—has garnered substantial interest. Preclinical physiological evidence demonstrates that androgens play a pivotal role in early follicular development by upregulating follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) receptor expression on granulosa cells, promoting pre-antral and antral follicle growth, and inhibiting follicular atresia. 

Despite promising biological plausibility, clinical trial findings have yielded variable estimates of efficacy, often hampered by small sample sizes, varying pre-treatment protocols, and heterogeneous patient populations. The present systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate whether testosterone pretreatment improves the primary clinical reproductive endpoints of live birth or ongoing pregnancy per randomised woman compared with placebo or no treatment in women undergoing IVF or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

## Methods

### Search Strategy and Study Screening

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Electronic databases (including MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) were searched for randomised controlled trials (RCTs) investigating androgen administration prior to ovarian stimulation for IVF/ICSI. Bibliographies of relevant systematic reviews and clinical trial registries were hand-searched to identify additional eligible reports.

Screening was conducted across two sequential stages (title/abstract triage and full-text assessment) against predefined eligibility criteria:
- **Population:** Women undergoing ART (IVF or ICSI), primarily those diagnosed with poor ovarian response or diminished ovarian reserve.
- **Intervention:** Testosterone pretreatment (e.g., transdermal gel, patch, or oral formulation) administered prior to ovarian stimulation.
- **Comparator:** Placebo or no treatment.
- **Outcome:** Live birth or ongoing pregnancy rate per randomised woman (live birth prioritised; ongoing pregnancy accepted when live birth was not reported).
- **Study Design:** Randomised controlled trials.

From the screening pipeline, 8 records were initially tracked. Following quality screening and resolution of study eligibility, 7 study-level effect sizes were extracted for statistical synthesis.

## Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized data from 7 randomised controlled trials involving 508 women undergoing ART to determine the therapeutic efficacy of testosterone pretreatment compared to placebo or no intervention. The primary outcome was live birth or ongoing pregnancy per randomised woman.

The pooled estimate yielded an Odds Ratio (OR) of 2.260 (95% CI [1.346, 3.794], $p = 0.0020$). Statistical heterogeneity across the included comparisons was absent ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, Cochran's $Q = 3.804$, $p = 0.7031$). Pretreatment with androgens is hypothesized to prime ovarian granulosa and theca cells, increasing functional FSH and LH receptor density and promoting pre-antral follicle survival during early recruitment phases. The statistical finding of an elevated Odds Ratio is consistent with this physiological hypothesis.

Nonetheless, clinical interpretation requires caution. While Egger's regression test demonstrated no statistically significant asymmetry ($p = 0.6189$), the trim-and-fill procedure imputed one putative missing study ($k_0 = 1$), and the overall qualitative risk of publication bias was judged to be moderate. Given the modest cumulative sample size ($N = 508$), small-study effects and selective outcome reporting remain plausible concerns in reproductive medicine literature.

## Limitations

Several methodological limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Sample Size:** The total pooled population across 7 trials was 508 women, with individual study samples ranging from 49 to 110 participants. Modest sample sizes heighten vulnerability to random error and overestimation of treatment effects.
2. **Outcome Definition:** While live birth was prioritized, several trials reported ongoing pregnancy rates without long-term neonatal follow-up data.
3. **Protocol Heterogeneity:** Clinical protocols varied regarding testosterone formulation (transdermal gel versus patches), dosage (ranging from 10 to 20 mg daily), and duration of pretreatment (ranging from 3 to 8 weeks prior to stimulation).
4. **Publication Bias Risk:** The overall risk of publication bias was evaluated as moderate, reflecting inherent constraints of statistical funnel tests in small meta-analytic cohorts ($k < 10$).

---

## Conclusion

Testosterone pretreatment in women undergoing ART (IVF/ICSI) is associated with an increased Odds Ratio (OR) of 2.260 (95% CI [1.346, 3.794], $p = 0.0020$) for live birth or ongoing pregnancy per randomised woman, with no statistical heterogeneity observed among the included trials ($I^2 = 0.0\%$). However, given the moderate risk of publication bias and limited aggregate sample size ($N = 508$), routine clinical adoption should await confirmatory evidence from adequately powered, multi-centre randomised controlled trials standardizing dosage and treatment duration.
