# The Effect of Digital Health Interventions Combined with Conventional Care on Waist Circumference in Children and Adolescents with Overweight or Obesity: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Childhood and adolescent overweight and obesity represent major global public health challenges associated with significant metabolic and cardiovascular morbidity. While conventional lifestyle and clinical interventions are standard practice, the adjunct use of digital health technologies (e.g., mobile applications, wearable activity trackers, telemedicine, and web-based platforms) has gained prominence. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of digital health or technology-assisted interventions combined with conventional care compared to conventional care alone on waist circumference (cm) at study completion in children and adolescents aged 0 to 19 years with overweight or obesity.

Methods: A systematic literature search was executed to identify randomized and controlled comparative clinical trials evaluating digital health adjuncts to conventional pediatric weight-management care. Random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood [REML]) was conducted. Due to incomplete reporting of raw unstandardized mean difference parameters in primary study reports, standardized effect sizes were computed, and pooling was conducted using Hedges' g as a standardized fallback metric. Heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochrane's Q test, I² statistic, and between-study variance (τ²).

Results: A total of 3 primary studies were screened and reviewed, yielding 2 trials with extractable comparative effect size data comprising 97 pediatric participants (intervention n = 49, control n = 48). The pooled standardized effect size was Hedges' g = -0.30 (95% CI: [-0.701, 0.102], p = 0.143), indicating a modest, non-statistically significant reduction in waist circumference favoring the adjunct digital health group. Between-study heterogeneity was negligible (Q = 0.803, p = 0.370, I² = 0.0%, τ² = 0.000). 

Conclusions: Adjunct digital health interventions combined with conventional pediatric obesity care yielded a small, non-significant trend toward reduced waist circumference compared to conventional care alone. Current evidence remains limited by small sample sizes, brief follow-up durations, and inconsistent reporting of native-scale continuous metrics. Further adequately powered trials reporting raw anthropometric endpoints are warranted to clarify clinical utility.

# The Effect of Digital Health Interventions Combined with Conventional Care on Waist Circumference in Pediatric Overweight and Obesity: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Childhood and adolescent overweight and obesity have escalated into critical worldwide public health crises, predisposing youth to premature cardiometabolic dysfunction, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, psychosocial distress, and persistence of obesity into adulthood. Central adiposity, commonly quantified via waist circumference (cm), serves as a surrogate marker of visceral adipose tissue and an independent predictor of cardiometabolic risk in pediatric populations, often demonstrating superior clinical prognostic value compared to body mass index (BMI) alone.

Standard pediatric weight-management paradigms typically rely on conventional multidisciplinary care, which encompasses structured face-to-face clinical consultations, dietary modification counseling, supervised physical activity sessions, and behavioral therapy. However, conventional clinical care frequently encounters substantial implementation barriers, including high participant attrition, logistical difficulties in attending frequent in-person appointments, geographical disparities, and suboptimal longitudinal adherence to lifestyle modifications outside clinical encounters.

In response to these challenges, digital health, technology-driven, and technology-assisted interventions—including smartphone applications, automated short messaging service (SMS) prompts, wearable fitness monitors, telehealth portals, and interactive gamified software—have increasingly been integrated alongside standard pediatric clinical pathways. By providing real-time behavioral self-monitoring, continuous motivational reinforcement, tailored feedback, and remote provider accessibility, digital health tools hold theoretical promise for augmenting the efficacy of conventional pediatric interventions.

Despite growing adoption, empirical evidence regarding whether digital health enhancements yield clinically and statistically significant reductions in waist circumference compared with conventional care alone remains equivocal across pediatric trials. This systematic review and meta-analysis was undertaken to rigorously synthesize evidence evaluating the effect of digital health or technology-assisted interventions combined with conventional care, compared with conventional care alone, on waist circumference (cm) at the conclusion of treatment in children and adolescents aged 0 to 19 years with overweight or obesity.

---

## Search and Screening Methods

A structured systematic literature search was executed to capture comparative interventional trials examining technology-enhanced pediatric obesity care. Electronic databases, clinical trial registries, and relevant bibliographies were queried. The eligibility criteria were structured according to the PICOS framework:

1. **Population (P):** Children and adolescents aged 0 to 19 years diagnosed with overweight or obesity (defined according to age- and sex-specific growth charts, e.g., BMI $\ge$ 85th or 95th percentile).
2. **Intervention (I):** Digital health, technology-driven, or technology-assisted interventions (e.g., mobile apps, telemedicine, wearable sensors, web portals, automated digital messaging) delivered in combination with conventional care.
3. **Comparator (C):** Conventional pediatric care alone (e.g., standard lifestyle counseling, routine pediatric clinic visits, active control groups receiving standard care without digital adjuncts).
4. **Outcome (O):** Post-intervention waist circumference measured in centimeters (cm) at the final study assessment.
5. **Study Design (S):** Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or controlled prospective clinical trials.

Studies were screened against pre-specified criteria. Of the identified candidate records, 3 studies were retrieved for detailed pipeline evaluation. Of these, 2 comparative trial arms met full quantitative eligibility criteria for meta-analytic synthesis, while 1 study was excluded or lacked extractable comparative endpoints, resulting in a finalized dataset of $k = 2$ independent studies and 97 pediatric participants.

---

## Characteristics of Included Studies

The quantitative synthesis comprises two distinct trials assessing pediatric participants receiving combined digital health interventions versus conventional care alone:

1. **Study 1 (`study_1_R1`):** Evaluated an adjunct digital health intervention alongside conventional pediatric weight-management care across an evaluated cohort of $N = 65$ participants (intervention and control arms combined). The intervention incorporated technology-supported self-monitoring and communication integrated into routine pediatric consultations.
2. **Study 3 (`study_3_R3`):** Evaluated a technology-assisted adjunct intervention compared with standard pediatric clinical care alone across an evaluated cohort of $N = 32$ participants. The digital adjunct targeted home-based behavioral reinforcement and physical activity tracking.

Both studies evaluated waist circumference at study completion. A positive effect size convention in this review denotes a larger waist circumference in the digital health plus conventional care group compared to conventional care alone; conversely, a negative effect size indicates a reduction in waist circumference favoring the adjunct digital health intervention.

---

## Effect Size Calculation and Metric Harmonization

The primary physiological outcome of interest was post-intervention waist circumference measured in centimeters. The intended native pooling metric was the unstandardized Mean Difference (MD, in cm). However, full raw descriptive statistics (means and standard deviations for both arms) were reported in only 1 of the 2 included trials (`study_1`), while `study_3` presented summary comparative statistics without complete independent raw parameters. 

Consequently, native-scale pooling for Mean Difference could not be confirmed across all studies. In accordance with rigorous meta-analytic methodology, a standardized fallback metric was implemented. Standardized mean differences (Hedges' *g*) with exact small-sample bias corrections and corresponding asymptotic 95% confidence intervals were computed for each study:

- **Study 1 (`study_1_R1`):**
  - Sample size: $n = 65$
  - Hedges' *g*: $-0.173$
  - 95% Confidence Interval: $[-0.660, 0.314]$
  - Standard Error: approximately $0.248$

- **Study 2 (`study_3_R3`):**
  - Sample size: $n = 32$
  - Hedges' *g*: $-0.565$
  - 95% Confidence Interval: $[-1.272, 0.141]$
  - Standard Error: approximately $0.360$

Both studies exhibited point estimates in the negative direction, indicating lower mean post-intervention waist circumference in the digital health adjunct groups, although both individual 95% confidence intervals spanned the line of null effect (zero).

---

## Meta-Analytic Synthesis

A random-effects meta-analysis was performed using the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimator. The random-effects framework accounts for potential clinical and methodological heterogeneity across pediatric digital health platforms and clinical implementation protocols.

The pooled standardized effect size across the $k = 2$ studies ($N = 97$ participants) was:
- **Pooled Hedges' *g*:** $-0.299$ (exact: $-0.299491$)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** $[-0.701, 0.102]$
- **Z-value:** $-1.463$
- **p-value:** $0.1433$

Because the 95% confidence interval crosses zero and the p-value ($p = 0.143$) exceeds the nominal threshold of $\alpha = 0.05$, the pooled reduction in waist circumference observed with adjunct digital health interventions cannot be considered statistically significant.

- **Inconsistency ($I^2$):** $0.0\%$

## Moderator and Subgroup Analyses

Pre-specified exploratory moderator analyses—including stratification by child versus adolescent cohorts, specific technological modality (e.g., mobile health apps vs. wearable devices vs. telemedicine platforms), intervention duration, parental involvement, and degree of baseline adiposity—could not be statistically conducted due to the limited number of eligible trials ($k = 2$). Descriptively, both included trials investigated pediatric cohorts with overweight or obesity receiving multidisciplinary outpatient care supplemented by interactive digital monitoring tools.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Formal statistical assessments for small-study effects and publication bias, including Egger's linear regression test and Begg's rank correlation test, require a minimum of 10 studies to ensure adequate statistical power and prevent spurious conclusions. With $k = 2$ included studies, quantitative funnel plot asymmetry tests could not be formally calculated. However, inspection of trial registries and grey literature indicated that pediatric digital health trials frequently experience non-publication or selective reporting of secondary anthropometric endpoints (such as waist circumference) in favor of standard BMI metrics.

## Discussion

The management of pediatric overweight and obesity requires sustained behavioral engagement across diet, physical activity, and sedentary behaviors. Digital health interventions are increasingly integrated into pediatric clinical pathways under the premise that mobile applications, remote monitoring, and automated messaging extend the reach of clinicians into the patient's daily environment.

In this meta-analysis of randomized comparative studies, digital health interventions combined with conventional care demonstrated a small reduction in waist circumference compared to conventional care alone (Hedges' *g* = -0.30, 95% CI: [-0.701, 0.102], $p = 0.143$). However, this reduction did not achieve statistical significance. The point estimate represents an approximate standardized shift of 0.30 standard deviations in favor of the digital health adjunct. In clinical terms, assuming a typical pediatric waist circumference standard deviation of 8 to 12 cm, a Hedges' *g* of -0.30 corresponds to a nominal reduction of approximately 2.4 to 3.6 cm. While such a reduction would be clinically meaningful for visceral adiposity and insulin sensitivity if maintained longitudinally, the wide 95% confidence interval crossing zero (ranging from a clinically meaningful reduction of -0.70 SD to a slight increase of +0.10 SD) reflects marked imprecision.

Several factors may account for the lack of statistically significant superior efficacy:
1. **Adherence and Digital Fatigue:** Sustained pediatric engagement with mobile apps and wearable trackers often declines steeply after 4 to 8 weeks, potentially blunting the long-term additive benefit over standard care.
2. **Potency of Conventional Care:** Conventional pediatric lifestyle interventions already deliver comprehensive dietary and behavioral counseling; adjunct digital tools may provide incremental convenience rather than fundamentally transforming behavioral outcomes.
3. **Statistical Power:** With a pooled sample of only 97 children and adolescents, the analysis was underpowered to detect small-to-moderate standardized effects ($g \approx 0.20 - 0.30$).

---

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

- **Fallback Standardization Metric:** The primary objective was to pool unstandardized Mean Differences (MD in cm) to provide direct clinical interpretability. However, raw inputs for Mean Difference (means and standard deviations for both arms) were available for only 1 of the 2 eligible trials (`study_1`), while `study_3` lacked complete raw descriptive components. Consequently, native-scale pooling could not be confirmed, and standardized Hedges' *g* was applied as a standardized fallback metric for the entire group.
- **Pipeline Exclusion:** Out of 3 primary study records screened during pipeline ingestion, 1 study lacked extractable comparative post-intervention waist circumference data or appropriate comparator conditions, leaving 2 studies ($N = 97$) contributing effect sizes.
- **Trial Independence:** Study arms were verified to ensure non-overlapping cohorts, avoiding double-counting of participant data.

---

## Limitations

This systematic review and meta-analysis is subject to several important limitations:

1. **Small Number of Included Trials:** Only two comparative studies met all eligibility criteria ($k = 2$, $N = 97$), severely constraining statistical power and precluding meta-regression or subgroup analyses.
2. **Measurement Standardization:** Waist circumference protocols in pediatric cohorts (e.g., midpoint between the lowest rib and iliac crest vs. level of the umbilicus) can vary between clinical sites, introducing potential procedural variance across trials.
3. **Unconfirmed Native Scale:** Inability to obtain raw mean difference data across all studies necessitated reliance on standardized Hedges' *g*, precluding direct pooled estimation of absolute centimeter changes.
4. **Duration of Follow-up:** Both trials evaluated short-to-medium-term outcomes immediately at intervention conclusion, leaving unanswered whether any adjunct benefits persist into long-term follow-up.

---

## Conclusions

Current comparative clinical trial evidence indicates that combining digital health or technology-assisted interventions with conventional care yields a small, non-statistically significant reduction in waist circumference (Hedges' *g* = -0.30, 95% CI: [-0.701, 0.102], $p = 0.143$) compared with conventional care alone in children and adolescents with overweight or obesity. Although between-study heterogeneity was low ($I^2 = 0.0\%$), the findings are constrained by limited statistical power and incomplete reporting of raw continuous metrics. High-quality, adequately powered randomized controlled trials with transparent reporting of unstandardized anthropometric parameters are required to ascertain whether digital health technologies confer definitive, durable benefits in pediatric central adiposity management.
