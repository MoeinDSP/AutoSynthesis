# Effect of Antioxidant Therapy on Cardiovascular Death in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background:** Patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) experience accelerated atherogenesis and disproportionately high rates of cardiovascular mortality, driven in part by systemic oxidative stress and inflammation. Antioxidant pharmacotherapy has been proposed as a cardioprotective strategy, but empirical evidence across clinical trials remains inconclusive.

**Methods:** We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the efficacy of antioxidant therapy compared to placebo, usual care, or no treatment on cardiovascular death in adults with CKD (stages 3–5, dialysis-dependent, or kidney transplant recipients). Studies were pooled using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects models. Pre-specified groupings evaluated dichotomous risk ratios and time-to-event outcomes.

**Results:** Five effect sizes across seven screened study records met inclusion criteria. For the primary pooled analysis comprising four trials reporting dichotomous event data ($k = 4$, $N = 1,403$), antioxidant therapy demonstrated a pooled Risk Ratio (RR) of 0.958 (95% CI: [0.715, 1.284], $p = 0.774$). In accordance with predefined decision criteria, this result favored neither arm (tie or undetermined). Heterogeneity across these trials was negligible ($I^2 = 0.0\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.0$, Cochran's $Q = 1.868$, $p = 0.6003$). A fifth trial ($k = 1$, $N = 2,185$) reported time-to-event hazard data (standardized single effect size $g = 0.201$, 95% CI: [0.117, 0.285]) and was categorized as having insufficient data for separate multi-study pooling. Publication bias assessment revealed low risk of asymmetry (Egger's intercept = 0.0687, $p = 0.9003$; Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill imputed $k_0 = 0$ studies).

**Conclusions:** Current randomized evidence demonstrates that antioxidant supplementation does not significantly reduce cardiovascular death among adults with CKD stages 3–5, dialysis, or kidney transplantation. Routine clinical use of unselected antioxidants solely for cardiovascular mortality reduction is not supported by existing trial data.

# Effect of Antioxidant Therapy on Cardiovascular Death in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of morbidity and premature death in individuals across all stages of chronic kidney disease (CKD), including non-dialysis CKD stages 3–5, end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) managed with chronic maintenance dialysis, and functional kidney allograft recipients. In advanced CKD, cardiovascular mortality rates remain substantially higher than in age- and sex-matched general populations, even after adjusting for traditional Framingham risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidemia.

This excess risk has been attributed to "non-traditional" uremic cardiovascular risk factors, chief among which is chronic systemic oxidative stress. Oxidative stress in CKD arises from an imbalance between the excessive generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS)—driven by uremic toxins, activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, mitochondrial dysfunction, and bioincompatible dialysis membranes—and impaired endogenous antioxidant defense mechanisms, including depressed superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase, and catalase activity.

Therapeutic interventions designed to mitigate oxidative stress—ranging from vitamins (e.g., alpha-tocopherol/vitamin E, ascorbic acid), thiol donors (e.g., N-acetylcysteine), to synthetic nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) activators (e.g., bardoxolone methyl)—have generated intense clinical interest. While individual clinical trials have suggested potential benefits on intermediate surrogates or combined composite cardiovascular endpoints, findings regarding hard clinical outcomes, specifically cardiovascular death, remain conflicting and heterogeneous. 

The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to quantify the effect of antioxidant therapy, compared with placebo, usual care, or no treatment, on cardiovascular death in adults with CKD stages 3 to 5, dialysis, or kidney transplantation.

---

## 2. Methods and Search Strategy

### 2.1 Protocol and Eligibility Criteria
This review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. We included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) fulfilling the following criteria:
- **Population:** Adult patients ($\ge 18$ years of age) with established CKD stages 3 to 5 (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] $< 60$ mL/min/1.73 m²), dialysis-dependent CKD (hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis), or kidney transplant recipients.
- **Intervention:** Any systemic pharmacological antioxidant therapy (e.g., vitamin E, N-acetylcysteine, vitamin C, bardoxolone methyl, or antioxidant combinations) administered as monotherapy or adjunct therapy.
- **Comparator:** Matching placebo, standard medical care alone, or no intervention.
- **Outcome:** Cardiovascular death, defined as mortality attributable to myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, fatal arrhythmias, sudden cardiac arrest, or unspecified cardiac events occurring over the entire prospective follow-up period.

### 2.2 Information Sources and Search
Comprehensive searches were conducted across electronic databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials [CENTRAL]) from inception through study closeout. Search filters combined controlled vocabulary terms (MeSH, Emtree) and free-text keywords relating to chronic kidney failure, dialysis, kidney transplantation, antioxidant therapies, and cardiovascular mortality.

### 2.3 Screening and Data Extraction
Title and abstract screening, followed by full-text evaluation, was conducted to isolate randomized trials reporting cardiovascular mortality. Data extraction was conducted independently using standardized collection templates capturing trial demographics, sample size per treatment arm, CKD stage/modality, exact antioxidant formulation, dosing regimen, treatment duration, and reported mortality counts or survival metrics.

### 2.4 Statistical Synthesis and Modeling
Meta-analytic syntheses were performed on the native ratio scale using Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) random-effects estimation. Because trials differed in outcome reporting format (dichotomous incident mortality versus time-to-event hazard ratios), analyses were partitioned into distinct outcome groups based on effect metric compatibility. 

Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran's $Q$ statistic, Higgins and Thompson's $I^2$ index, and between-study variance $\tau^2$. Direction of effect was formally evaluated using pre-specified threshold rules, wherein a pooled point estimate crossing unity or showing statistical equivalence was classified as favoring neither arm. Publication bias was examined using Egger's linear regression test for funnel plot asymmetry and Duval and Tweedie's non-parametric trim-and-fill method.

## 11. Discussion

### 11.1 Principal Findings
This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized evidence from randomized controlled trials examining the impact of antioxidant therapies on cardiovascular mortality among adults across the clinical spectrum of CKD. The principal finding of this study is that antioxidant therapy does not significantly reduce the risk of cardiovascular death compared with placebo or standard control therapy (pooled Risk Ratio = 0.958, 95% CI: [0.715, 1.284], $p = 0.774$, favoring neither arm). Between-study heterogeneity for this pooled comparison was zero ($I^2 = 0.0\%$).

### 11.2 Biological and Clinical Context
In patients with CKD, oxidative stress is pervasive and correlates strongly with markers of endothelial injury, vascular calcification, and left ventricular hypertrophy. Despite sound pathophysiological rationale, translating exogenous antioxidant administration into tangible reductions in hard cardiovascular endpoints has proven difficult across clinical medicine. 

Several factors explain the neutral clinical efficacy observed:
1. **Complexity of Uremic Vasculopathy:** Cardiovascular death in end-stage kidney disease is predominantly non-atherosclerotic, driven by sudden cardiac arrest, heart failure, and diffuse medial arterial calcification (Mönckeberg's arteriosclerosis) rather than acute plaque rupture. Non-specific antioxidant supplementation does not reverse advanced vascular calcification or structural cardiomyopathy.
2. **Redox Homeostasis:** Physiological levels of ROS are essential for normal cellular signaling, host defense, and vascular tone. Non-targeted scavenging of ROS by pharmacological antioxidants may inadvertently perturb redox homeostasis ("antioxidative stress") or induce pro-oxidant states at higher therapeutic dosages.
3. **Targeting and Potency:** Conventional oral antioxidants (e.g., vitamin E) may not attain sufficient tissue concentrations within the mitochondrial matrix or vascular adventitia to counteract the intense local oxidative milieu of uremia.

Our results align with large-scale trials of antioxidants in general high-risk cardiovascular cohorts (such as HOPE and GISSI-Prevenzione), which similarly demonstrated that vitamin E and related supplements failed to decrease cardiovascular mortality.

## 12. Strengths and Limitations

### 12.1 Strengths
- **Rigorous Methodological Separation:** Avoided conflation of native dichotomous Risk Ratios with time-to-event Hazard Ratios, preventing mathematical artifacts.
- **Zero Statistical Heterogeneity:** The pooled dichotomous outcome demonstrated $I^2 = 0.0\%$, showing strong statistical concordance across included trials.
- **Publication Bias Verification:** Formal tests indicated minimal vulnerability to selective reporting or small-study distortion.

### 12.2 Limitations
- **Sample Size Constraints:** The pooled native RR analysis comprises 1,403 participants across four trials. While sufficient to rule out large therapeutic effects (e.g., relative risk reductions $> 30\%$), it lacks power to definitively exclude minor risk modulations.
- **Heterogeneous Antioxidant Formulations:** Included trials evaluated diverse chemical entities, ranging from vitamin E to N-acetylcysteine, which possess distinct pharmacokinetics and redox potentials.
- **Exclusion of the Largest Single Trial from Primary Pooling:** The large-scale bardoxolone trial (**study_4_R4**, $N = 2,185$) could not be pooled with dichotomous counts due to metric non-collapsibility, although its reported findings also showed no cardiovascular survival benefit.

---

## 13. Conclusions

Based on current randomized trial evidence, antioxidant pharmacotherapy does not reduce the risk of cardiovascular death in adults with CKD stages 3 to 5, dialysis dependence, or kidney allografts. Clinicians should not prescribe unselected antioxidant supplements for the express purpose of reducing cardiovascular mortality in this patient population. Future interventional research should focus on targeted, disease-specific molecular pathways of uremic vascular injury rather than broad-spectrum antioxidant supplementation.
