# Interleukin-1 Receptor Antagonists and All-Cause Mortality in Secondary Prevention of Established Cardiovascular Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Persistent vascular inflammation is recognized as a central contributor to atherothrombosis and recurrent adverse events in patients with established cardiovascular disease (CVD). Interleukin-1 (IL-1) signaling, primarily mediated by IL-1α and IL-1β, represents a primary inflammatory driver. Clinical trials evaluating interleukin-1 receptor antagonism (anakinra) and selective IL-1β inhibition (canakinumab) have yielded conflicting results regarding survival benefits.

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the effect of interleukin-1 receptor antagonists (anakinra or canakinumab) versus placebo or usual care on all-cause mortality for secondary prevention in adults with established CVD. Studies were synthesized using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) random-effects modeling. Due to heterogeneous reporting across trials where only one study reported native hazard ratios, standardized effect sizes (Hedges' g) were utilized as an unconfirmed standardized fallback metric. Positive values reflect lower mortality in the intervention group.

Results: Eight randomized controlled trials comprising 10,735 participants (sample sizes ranging from 34 to 10,061) met the inclusion criteria. The pooled standardized effect size was Hedges' g = -0.053 (95% CI: -0.432 to 0.326; z = -0.274, p = 0.784), indicating no statistically significant difference in all-cause mortality between interleukin-1 antagonist therapy and control. Extreme between-study heterogeneity was observed (I² = 91.48%, τ² = 0.246, Cochran's Q = 49.403, df = 7, p < 0.0001). Individual study point estimates ranged from -0.678 to +0.943, with the overall estimate heavily weighted toward null by the dominant trial (N = 10,061; g = -0.034, 95% CI: -0.076 to 0.007). Egger's regression test revealed no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = -0.211, p = 0.698), and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill procedure imputed zero missing studies (k₀ = 0).

Conclusions: Interleukin-1 targeted therapy does not significantly reduce or increase all-cause mortality in secondary cardiovascular prevention across pooled trials. The profound statistical heterogeneity reflects stark divergences between small Phase II acute coronary syndrome trials and large-scale long-term stable atherosclerosis trials. Further investigation is required to determine whether specific biomarker-defined subgroups derive survival benefit without offsetting risks of fatal infection.

# Interleukin-1 Receptor Antagonists and All-Cause Mortality in Secondary Prevention of Established Cardiovascular Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) remains the principal cause of global morbidity and premature mortality. Despite optimized guideline-directed medical therapy—including high-intensity statin regimens, proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitors, antiplatelet therapies, and neurohormonal blockade—a substantial proportion of patients experience recurrent ischemic events. This residual risk is increasingly recognized as having a major inflammatory component, often termed "residual inflammatory risk," characterized by elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels despite adequately controlled low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C).

The interleukin-1 (IL-1) cytokine pathway is a pivotal upstream mediator of vascular and systemic inflammation. The nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome processes pro-IL-1β into active IL-1β, which subsequently drives downstream signaling cascades, hepatic synthesis of interleukin-6 (IL-6), and CRP generation. Interventions targeting this pathway have focused primarily on two biologic agents:
1. **Anakinra**, a recombinant human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra) that blocks the biological activity of both IL-1α and IL-1β by competitively binding to the interleukin-1 type I receptor (IL-1R1).
2. **Canakinumab**, a high-affinity human monoclonal antibody selectively targeting IL-1β.

While the landmark Canakinumab Anti-inflammatory Thrombosis Outcome Study (CANTOS) demonstrated that IL-1β neutralization significantly reduced recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in stable post-myocardial infarction patients with elevated hs-CRP, all-cause mortality was not significantly reduced, partly due to a small, countervailing excess in fatal infections. Conversely, smaller trials evaluating anakinra in acute coronary syndromes (ACS), acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), and stable ischemic heart disease have demonstrated variable outcomes on inflammatory markers, cardiac remodeling, and clinical endpoints.

The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the pooled effect of interleukin-1 receptor antagonists and targeted inhibitors (anakinra and canakinumab) compared with placebo or usual care on all-cause mortality during follow-up in individuals with established cardiovascular disease.

---

## Search and Screening Strategy

The systematic review was conducted in adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. We executed comprehensive bibliographic searches across major electronic biomedical databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), supplemented by screening clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP).

### Eligibility Criteria
- **Population**: Adult patients (≥18 years) with established cardiovascular disease, including prior myocardial infarction, stable coronary artery disease, acute coronary syndrome, or peripheral arterial disease.
- **Intervention**: Interleukin-1 receptor antagonists or selective inhibitors (anakinra or canakinumab), regardless of dosing schedule or route of administration.
- **Comparator**: Placebo or standard usual care.
- **Outcome**: All-cause mortality during follow-up.
- **Study Design**: Parallel-group randomized controlled clinical trials (RCTs).

Across the screening pipeline, deduplicated records were reviewed at the title/abstract level, followed by full-text evaluation. Ultimately, 8 independent randomized controlled trials met all inclusion criteria for quantitative evidence synthesis.

## Effect Sizes and Individual Study Findings

Effect sizes were extracted and converted to a standardized comparative metric. In this analysis, **positive effect values favor the interleukin-1 antagonist group** (indicating lower all-cause mortality or a reduced risk of death compared to control), whereas negative effect values favor the control arm (indicating higher mortality in the intervention group).

Because survival data across 7 of the 8 studies did not provide complete time-to-event hazard ratio parameters (reporting event counts or standardized statistics), a standardized fallback metric (**Hedges' g**) was computed across all cohorts to ensure uniform mathematical aggregation.

The individual trial effect estimates and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals are displayed below:

- **Study 1 (R1)** ($N = 38$): Hedges' $g = -0.678$ (95% CI: $[-1.333, -0.024]$)
- **Study 2 (R2)** ($N = 52$): Hedges' $g = +0.360$ (95% CI: $[-0.215, 0.936]$)
- **Study 3 (R3)** ($N = 182$): Hedges' $g = -0.497$ (95% CI: $[-0.792, -0.202]$)
- **Study 4 (R4)** ($N = 189$): Hedges' $g = -0.603$ (95% CI: $[-0.895, -0.312]$)
- **Study 5 (R5)** ($N = 99$): Hedges' $g = +0.943$ (95% CI: $[0.511, 1.376]$)
- **Study 6 (R6)** ($N = 34$): Hedges' $g = +0.195$ (95% CI: $[-0.479, 0.869]$)
- **Study 7 (R7)** ($N = 10,061$): Hedges' $g = -0.034$ (95% CI: $[-0.076, 0.007]$)
- **Study 8 (R8)** ($N = 80$): Hedges' $g = -0.031$ (95% CI: $[-0.470, 0.407]$)

Individual point estimates exhibited substantial dispersion: Studies 1, 3, and 4 reported negative point estimates indicating excess mortality or adverse trends in the treatment group, whereas Study 5 demonstrated a statistically significant benefit favoring interleukin-1 blockade ($g = 0.943$, 95% CI: $[0.511, 1.376]$). ## Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis of all 8 studies was conducted using a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) variance estimation. 

The pooled summary effect size is:
$$\text{Pooled Hedges' } g = -0.053 \quad (95\% \text{ CI: } [-0.432, 0.326])$$
The test of the null hypothesis that interleukin-1 receptor antagonism has no effect on all-cause mortality yielded $z = -0.274$ ($p = 0.784$). Thus, there is no statistically significant effect of interleukin-1 receptor antagonists or selective inhibitors on all-cause mortality compared with placebo or usual care in patients with established cardiovascular disease.

The complete forest plot displaying study-level effect estimates, weightings, and the pooled summary diamond is provided in `outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/forest_plot.png`.

A cumulative meta-analysis was performed, sequencing studies sequentially, to assess temporal stability and evidence accumulation over time (`outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/cumulative_plot.png`). The cumulative trajectory indicates that early small trials fluctuated considerably, while the addition of the large-scale trial (Study 7) pulled the cumulative point estimate directly toward null, where it stabilized.

## Heterogeneity Analysis

Between-study heterogeneity was pronounced across the included trials:
- **Cochran's Q**: $49.403$ ($df = 7$, $p < 0.0001$)
- **$I^2$ Statistic**: $91.48\%$
- **Between-study variance ($\tau^2$)**: $0.2464$
- **$\tau$**: $0.4964$

An $I^2$ of $91.48\%$ indicates that over $91\%$ of the total observed variability in effect sizes is attributable to genuine between-study clinical and methodological heterogeneity rather than sampling error. The heterogeneity summary plot (`outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/heterogeneity_summary.png`) highlights this marked variance structure.

The primary driver of this extreme heterogeneity is the juxtaposition between:
1. Small phase II trials (e.g., Studies 3, 4, and 5) that yielded extreme effect sizes in opposing directions due to low event counts and small denominator numbers; and
2. The massive CANTOS trial (Study 7, $N = 10,061$), whose precision dominates fixed-effect assumptions and reveals a near-neutral survival effect ($g = -0.034$).

## Moderator Considerations and Study Discrepancies

To evaluate potential moderators of treatment effect, study-level characteristics were appraised. Sample size was a primary axis of divergence, visualized in the meta-analytic bubble chart (`outputs/rEBD27z_7qs5iDoQujyAfA/bubble_chart.png`).

Key clinical and methodological moderators underlying the observed differences include:

1. **Pharmacological Agent (Anakinra vs. Canakinumab)**:
   - *Anakinra* provides non-selective blockade of both IL-1α and IL-1β via IL-1R1 competitive antagonism. It has a short half-life (4–6 hours) requiring daily subcutaneous administration, utilized predominantly in acute settings (Studies 1–6, 8).
   - *Canakinumab* is a targeted monoclonal antibody neutralizing IL-1β without inhibiting IL-1α, with a long half-life (~26 days) administered quarterly, used in chronic stable ASCVD (Study 7). IL-1α is constitutively expressed on endothelial and epithelial cells and plays critical homeostatic roles in barrier defense and tissue repair. Non-selective inhibition vs. selective IL-1β suppression could plausibly elicit divergent clinical safety profiles.

2. **Acuity of Disease State**:
   - Several trials evaluated acute myocardial infarction where acute-phase inflammatory cascades are essential for infarct healing, scar formation, and myocardial stabilization. Over-suppression of early reparative inflammation during the immediate post-infarct window could theoretically lead to myocardial rupture or impaired remodeling, whereas chronic anti-inflammatory suppression in stable atherosclerosis reduces fibrous cap destabilization.

3. **Follow-Up Duration and Competing Risks**:
   - Short-term acute trials followed patients for days to months, capturing very few all-cause deaths, whereas CANTOS followed patients for a median of 3.7 years, revealing that a statistically significant reduction in cardiovascular death was offset by a low-incidence, statistically significant increase in fatal infection and sepsis ($0.31$ vs. $0.18$ per 100 person-years; $p = 0.02$).

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Small-study effects and potential reporting biases were evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively:

- **Egger's Linear Regression Test**: The estimated intercept was $\beta_0 = -0.2113$ ($p = 0.6978$). The regression test showed no statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Procedure**: The non-parametric trim-and-fill algorithm identified zero missing studies needing imputation ($k_0 = 0$). The adjusted pooled effect size remained identical to the primary analysis ($g = -0.0529$).

These diagnostics suggest a low risk of publication bias distorting the overall meta-analytic pooled estimate. However, because statistical tests for funnel plot asymmetry have limited power when $k < 10$ and are complicated by extreme heterogeneity, caution is warranted. The broad scatter of small studies reflects sampling variability and clinical heterogeneity rather than classical selective publication of positive findings.

## Data Quality & Validation Notes

In accordance with systematic review quality standards, the following data-level and methodological validation issues are documented:

1. **Unconfirmed Fallback Metric**: Across the 8 included trials, only 1 study (Study 7, CANTOS) reported complete time-to-event hazard ratio (HR) inputs for all-cause mortality. The remaining 7 trials reported sparse binary event counts or alternative summary formats. Because native pooling using hazard ratios could not be performed across the full dataset without excluding 7 of the 8 trials, the analysis defaulted to standardized mean difference pooling (**Hedges' g**). Consequently, **native pooling was not used**, and the pooled effect estimate of $g = -0.053$ is an unconfirmed standardized fallback metric rather than a confirmed native-scale time-to-event hazard ratio.
2. **Clinical Scale Interpretation**: Standardized mean differences applied to binary survival endpoints should be interpreted cautiously; native time-to-event metrics (hazard ratios) or risk ratios represent the optimal epidemiological scale for clinical decision-making.
3. **Weight Disproportion**: Although random-effects modeling using REML distributes study weights more evenly than a fixed-effect model, the sample size disparity between Study 7 ($N = 10,061$) and the smaller phase II trials ($N = 34$ to $189$) remains an inherent constraint of the current empirical literature.

## Discussion

The inflammatory hypothesis of atherothrombosis has garnered substantial scientific validation over the past two decades. Interleukin-1 is a key target within this paradigm. However, whether IL-1 inhibition translates into an overall survival benefit in secondary cardiovascular prevention remains unconfirmed by the present pooled evidence.

Our meta-analysis demonstrates that interleukin-1 receptor antagonists and targeted inhibitors do not confer a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality (Hedges' $g = -0.053$, 95% CI: $[-0.432, 0.326]$, $p = 0.784$). This overall null finding is consistent with the primary findings of CANTOS, where canakinumab at a dose of 150 mg subcutaneously every 3 months reduced the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint (nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, or cardiovascular death) by 15% (HR: 0.85, 95% CI: 0.74–0.98, $p = 0.021$), but failed to achieve a statistically significant decrease in all-cause mortality (HR: 0.94, 95% CI: 0.83–1.06, $p = 0.31$).

The fundamental mechanistic explanation for this neutrality lies in the dual biological consequences of systemic IL-1 suppression. On one hand, attenuation of IL-1 reduces vascular smooth muscle cell activation, endothelial adhesion molecule expression, macrophage pro-inflammatory polarization, and matrix metalloproteinase release, thereby stabilizing vulnerable coronary atheroma. On the other hand, IL-1 signaling is indispensable for innate host defense against bacterial and opportunistic pathogens. In CANTOS, canakinumab was associated with a small but statistically significant increase in lethal infections and sepsis. Consequently, the cardiovascular survival benefit was offset by excess infectious mortality, resulting in a neutral net effect on total survival.

In acute coronary syndromes, trials evaluating anakinra (such as VCU-ART, VCU-ART2, VCU-ART3, and MRC-ILA Heart Study) demonstrated reductions in systemic CRP and markers of ventricular remodeling; however, these trials were underpowered for survival, and early suppression of inflammatory signaling in acute ischemia carries distinct biological risks regarding scar maturation.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations must be acknowledged:

1. **Absence of Uniform Hazard Ratio Data**: As noted, 7 of the 8 trials lacked full time-to-event survival data, necessitating the use of standardized fallback effect sizes (Hedges' g). This precludes direct clinical risk estimation such as number needed to treat (NNT) or precise hazard reduction percentages.
2. **Extreme Statistical Heterogeneity**: The calculated $I^2$ of $91.48\%$ denotes profound variability across trials. This heterogeneity arises from differences in molecular agent (anakinra vs. canakinumab), patient baseline acuity (acute STEMI vs. stable chronic coronary disease), dosing regimens, and duration of follow-up.
3. **Imbalance in Study Weight**: The evidence base is overwhelmingly dominated by a single large trial (Study 7). The remaining 7 trials contributed fewer than 800 patients combined, making the overall evidence synthesis heavily dependent on CANTOS.
4. **Subgroup Heterogeneity by Inflammatory Response**: Prior post-hoc analyses of CANTOS revealed that individuals who achieved an on-treatment hs-CRP reduction $<2$ mg/L experienced a significant reduction in both cardiovascular and all-cause mortality (HR: 0.69, 95% CI: 0.58–0.82), whereas non-responders derived no benefit. Study-level aggregate data precluded examining patient-level on-treatment biomarker response subgroups.

---

## Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis indicates that interleukin-1 receptor antagonists (anakinra) and selective IL-1β inhibitors (canakinumab) do not produce a statistically significant reduction in all-cause mortality when utilized for secondary prevention in patients with established cardiovascular disease (pooled Hedges' $g = -0.053$, 95% CI: $[-0.432, 0.326]$, $p = 0.784$). While IL-1 targeted therapeutics significantly attenuate systemic inflammatory activity and reduce recurrent ischemic events, this benefit is neutralized with respect to overall survival, largely due to countervailing risks of severe infectious complications. Routine administration of interleukin-1 antagonists for secondary cardiovascular prevention cannot be recommended solely for the purpose of improving survival. Future research should prioritize identifying biomarker-guided patient subsets who achieve maximal atheroprotection while minimizing infectious susceptibility.
