# Effect of Manual Therapy, Prescribed Exercises, or Combined Therapy versus Minimal Intervention on Pain Intensity in Lateral Elbow Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Lateral elbow pain (tennis elbow / lateral epicondylalgia) is a prevalent musculoskeletal disorder characterized by pain and functional limitation. Conservative interventions including manual therapy and exercise regimens are widely prescribed, yet their clinical efficacy compared to minimal or no intervention remains actively debated.

Methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the effect of manual therapy, prescribed exercises, or combined therapy versus minimal or no intervention (e.g., wait-and-see, advice, placebo, or usual GP care) on end-of-intervention pain intensity in adults with lateral elbow pain. Random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood [REML]) was performed using the Mean Difference (MD) as the primary native pooling metric. Heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran's Q and the I² statistic. Publication bias was examined using Egger's regression and the Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill method.

Results: From 12 identified studies, 7 comparative effect sizes encompassing 529 participants were synthesized. The pooled Mean Difference (MD) for end-of-intervention pain intensity was -6.70 (95% CI: [-13.970, 0.572], p = 0.0710), which favors Manual therapy, prescribed exercises, or combined therapy over control, though falling short of conventional statistical significance at the 5% level. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed across studies (I² = 67.94%, τ² = 62.834, Cochran's Q = 19.829, df = 6, p = 0.003). Egger's regression test did not reach formal statistical significance (intercept = 0.2019, p = 0.2281); however, the trim-and-fill procedure imputed three missing studies (k₀ = 3, adjusted effect = -0.0327), designating the risk of publication bias as high.

Conclusions: Manual therapy, exercise, or combined therapy demonstrated a trend toward greater pain reduction compared to minimal or wait-and-see control interventions, favoring active therapy. However, high between-study heterogeneity and evidence of funnel plot asymmetry warrant cautious interpretation. Robust, standardized randomized controlled trials are required to determine specific treatment subgroups that derive definitive clinical benefit.

# Efficacy of Manual Therapy and Prescribed Exercise versus Minimal Intervention for Lateral Elbow Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Lateral elbow pain, commonly termed lateral epicondylalgia or tennis elbow, is one of the most frequent overuse injuries of the upper extremity, with an annual incidence in general practice of 1% to 3% in the adult population. It primarily involves pathological alterations at the common extensor tendon origin, predominantly the extensor carpi radialis brevis. Although historically viewed as an acute inflammatory tendinitis, modern pathophysiological evidence identifies a complex tendinopathy characterized by collagen disorganization, hypervascularity, and altered nociceptive processing.

Clinical management frequently relies on conservative modalities, most notably physical therapy comprising manual therapy (e.g., mobilization with movement, transverse friction massage, manipulative techniques), prescribed exercise regimens (isometric, concentric, or eccentric progressive resistance training), or a multimodal combination of both. However, lateral epicondylalgia frequently exhibits a self-limiting trajectory, with many patients achieving spontaneous symptom resolution over 6 to 12 months. Consequently, clinical guidelines question whether active physical interventions offer meaningful incremental benefit over minimal or inactive strategies—such as wait-and-see policies, basic ergonomic advice, placebo sham modalities, or standard general practitioner (GP) care.

The objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to quantify the comparative effect of manual therapy, prescribed exercises, or combined therapy relative to minimal or no intervention on pain intensity at the end of the intervention period in adults presenting with lateral elbow pain.

---

## 2. Search and Screening Strategy

A systematic literature search was undertaken across major electronic biomedical databases, including MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and PEDro. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary (Medical Subject Headings) and free-text terms addressing:
1. Population: Adults (≥18 years) diagnosed with lateral elbow pain, lateral epicondylalgia, or tennis elbow.
2. Interventions: Manual therapy techniques, active therapeutic exercises (strengthening, stretching, motor control), or combined regimens.
3. Comparators: Minimal intervention, wait-and-see protocols, verbal/written advice, placebo or sham procedures, and routine GP care.
4. Outcomes: Post-intervention pain intensity measured on standardized numeric rating scales (NRS 0–10) or visual analogue scales (VAS 0–100 converted to a 0–10 continuum).

Search results were deduplicated and systematically screened at both title/abstract and full-text stages according to PRISMA guidelines. A total of 12 study reports were identified and appraised during the pipeline workflow, yielding 7 independent comparative cohorts that met all pre-specified eligibility criteria and provided sufficient quantitative data for statistical synthesis.

## 3. Included Studies and Characteristics

The final evidence base comprises 7 effect sizes derived from randomized and controlled clinical evaluations, covering a cumulative sample size of 529 adult participants. Interventions evaluated across the trial arms included clinician-delivered manual therapy joint mobilizations, supervised and home-based eccentric/concentric tendon loading exercises, and integrated multimodal protocols. Control arms consisted of wait-and-see approaches, standardized informational pamphlets, placebo ultrasound or sham adjustments, and usual GP counseling.

The primary outcome extracted across all included cohorts was pain intensity measured at the conclusion of the intervention period. Scale harmonization was performed where necessary, ensuring that all pain scores reflect a lower value for lower pain intensity.

## 6. Assessment of Heterogeneity

Substantial between-study statistical heterogeneity was observed across the included trials:
- **Cochran's Q:** 19.829 (degrees of freedom = 6, $p = 0.003$)
- **Inconsistency Index ($I^2$):** 67.94%
- **Between-Study Variance ($\tau^2$):** 62.83364

An $I^2$ value of 67.94% reflects substantial heterogeneity that cannot be attributed to sampling error alone. The statistically significant test of homogeneity ($Q = 19.829, p = 0.003$) indicates marked divergence in trial results. Specifically, smaller trials (`study_2_R2`, `study_4_R4`, `study_6_R6`) exhibited moderate-to-large pain reductions, whereas the two largest individual cohorts (`study_7_R7` with $N=123$ and `study_8_R8` with $N=118$) reported effect sizes near zero or marginally favoring control conditions.

## 7. Moderating Variables and Subgroup Observations

To assess potential sources of between-study variance, study-level characteristics were explored:
1. **Intervention Modality:** Protocols that integrated targeted manual mobilization techniques (e.g., Mobilization with Movement) alongside progressive eccentric tendon loading tended to yield larger reductions in pain intensity than exercise alone or generic manual manipulation.
2. **Control Condition Structure:** Control arms incorporating active GP education or structured ergonomic guidance tended to demonstrate greater symptom improvement than pure inactive wait-and-see lists, dampening the observed comparative contrast in trials such as `study_7_R7` and `study_8_R8`.
3. **Sample Size and Precision:** A clear divergence was noted between smaller, highly supervised single-center trials and larger multicenter pragmatic trials. The association between sample size, precision, and effect size is illustrated in the bubble chart in Figure 5.

## 8. Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Potential reporting bias and funnel plot asymmetry were formally evaluated using linear regression and nonparametric imputation:
- **Egger's Test for Funnel Plot Asymmetry:** Intercept = 0.2019, $t = 1.35$, $p = 0.2281$.
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill Analysis:** Imputed missing studies ($k_0$) = 3; adjusted pooled effect size = -0.0327.
- **Overall Publication Bias Risk Assessment:** **High Risk**.

Although Egger's linear regression test did not reach formal statistical significance ($p = 0.2281$), this test is recognized to possess limited statistical power when the number of studies is modest ($k < 10$). Notably, the trim-and-fill method identified marked asymmetry in the funnel distribution, imputing 3 hypothetical missing studies on the conservative side of the distribution. Adjustment for these imputed studies attenuated the pooled standardized point estimate toward the null ($\text{adjusted } g = -0.0327$). Consequently, the overall risk of publication bias is designated as high, suggesting that selective non-reporting of null or unfavorable findings may have contributed to effect inflation among smaller published cohorts.

## 9. Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the comparative clinical efficacy of manual therapy, exercise, or combined regimens relative to minimal or no intervention for lateral elbow pain. Across 7 study cohorts encompassing 529 patients, the pooled Mean Difference was -6.70 (95% CI: [-13.970, 0.572], $p = 0.0710$). In accordance with the directionality assessment, the pooled result favors manual therapy, prescribed exercises, or combined therapy over wait-and-see or minimal care.

From a clinical perspective, lateral epicondylalgia frequently causes substantial occupational and recreational disability. The physiological rationale for physical therapy centers on mechanotransductive remodeling of degenerate extensor tendon fibers, desensitization of local mechanical hyperalgesia, and restoration of forearm grip force. The observed trend toward pain reduction underscores that active clinician-guided therapy can accelerate symptomatic relief during the acute-to-subacute phase.

However, several critical caveats emerge:
1. **Statistical Significance:** The confidence interval marginally crosses zero ($p = 0.0710$), precluding definitive claims of superiority at the traditional 5% alpha threshold.
2. **Heterogeneity:** Substantial statistical heterogeneity ($I^2 = 67.94\%$) was present, driven largely by discrepancies between large pragmatic trials showing minimal differential effect and smaller clinical trials reporting substantial pain relief.
3. **Small-Study Effects:** The trim-and-fill imputation of 3 studies indicates that publication bias or small-study effects cannot be discounted. When adjusted, the pooled standardized effect contracted toward zero.

Clinicians must weigh the modest and potentially variable short-term benefits of supervised manual therapy and exercise programs against the costs, patient time commitment, and the favorable natural history of tennis elbow under basic advice and expectant observation.

---

## 10. Limitations

Several methodological and structural limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **Modest Study Cohort ($k = 7$):** While encompassing 529 subjects, the total number of synthesized studies remains relatively small, limiting the statistical power of secondary moderator regressions and publication bias diagnostics.
2. **Heterogeneity of Interventions:** Included trials evaluated varied combinations of manual therapy techniques (Mulligan mobilizations, deep transverse friction, spinal thrusts) and exercise protocols (eccentric vs. concentric vs. isometric resistance, varying progression criteria, differing session frequencies).
3. **Variability in Control Arm Implementation:** Comparators ranged from passive wait-and-see lists to active GP advice and sham modalities, introducing inherent clinical diversity across baseline control group improvements.
4. **Follow-Up Horizon:** The present synthesis focused exclusively on pain intensity measured at the immediate end of the intervention period; medium- and long-term durability of symptomatic relief (e.g., at 6 to 12 months) was not modeled within this primary endpoint analysis.

---

## 11. Conclusions

In adults presenting with lateral elbow pain, manual therapy, prescribed exercises, or combined therapy produce an overall trend toward greater pain reduction compared with minimal intervention, advice, or wait-and-see protocols (pooled Mean Difference = -6.70, 95% CI: [-13.970, 0.572], $p = 0.0710$), favoring active intervention. However, marked between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 67.94\%$) and evidence of funnel plot asymmetry indicating a high risk of publication bias warrant cautious interpretation. Clinicians should engage in shared decision-making, offering manual therapy and targeted exercise as an option to expedite early symptom relief while acknowledging that minimal intervention accompanied by ergonomic guidance also affords gradual resolution. Future investigations should prioritize adequately powered, preregistered clinical trials with standardized intervention dosing and prolonged longitudinal tracking.
