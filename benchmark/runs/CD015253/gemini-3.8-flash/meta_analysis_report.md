# Effect of Autoinflation versus Watchful Waiting on Middle Ear Effusion Persistence in Children at up to 6 Weeks of Follow-Up: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

Background: Otitis media with effusion (OME), colloquially termed 'glue ear', is among the most frequent causes of conductive hearing impairment in pediatric populations. While watchful waiting is a standard initial approach, mechanical autoinflation has been proposed as a non-invasive, cost-effective intervention to promote Eustachian tube opening and middle ear fluid clearance. 

Methods: A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis (restricted maximum likelihood [REML] estimation) were conducted to assess the effect of autoinflation compared to watchful waiting or no treatment on the persistence of middle ear effusion at up to 6 weeks of follow-up (closest to 4 weeks). Outcomes included flat tympanograms (type B/C2) or persistent effusion identified on pneumatic otoscopy.

Results: Six randomized comparisons across 7 evaluated studies met the inclusion criteria (total N = 782 children). The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) for persistent effusion was 0.79 (95% CI [0.473, 1.304]; p = 0.3504), favoring neither intervention over control in a statistically significant manner. Substantial and statistically significant between-study heterogeneity was observed (I² = 95.43%, τ² = 0.3692, Cochran's Q = 40.26, p < 0.0001). Egger's linear regression test revealed no significant funnel plot asymmetry (intercept = 0.7482, p = 0.2796), and Duval and Tweedie's trim-and-fill method identified zero missing studies (k₀ = 0), indicating a low risk of publication bias.

Conclusions: While autoinflation shows a nominal trend toward reduced persistence of effusion at 4 to 6 weeks, the wide 95% confidence interval crosses unity, yielding no statistically significant superiority over watchful waiting or no treatment. Given marked heterogeneity in study design, compliance, and device mechanisms, clinicians should weigh potential short-term symptom relief against adherence challenges in young children.

# The Efficacy of Autoinflation versus Watchful Waiting on Middle Ear Effusion Persistence in Children: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Introduction

Otitis media with effusion (OME), commonly referred to as "glue ear," is defined by the presence of non-purulent fluid in the middle ear cleft behind an intact tympanic membrane. It is one of the most prevalent conditions seen in pediatric primary care and otolaryngology clinics, affecting up to 80% of children at least once before school age. Although OME is frequently self-limiting, persistent effusion can cause fluctuating conductive hearing loss, vestibular instability, delayed speech acquisition, and behavioral or academic difficulties.

Clinical practice guidelines generally recommend an initial period of watchful waiting lasting up to 3 months, given the high spontaneous resolution rate. However, surgical interventions—most notably the insertion of tympanostomy (ventilation) tubes with or without adenoidectomy—remain common when fluid persists, carrying surgical and anesthetic risks as well as health system costs.

Autoinflation is a non-invasive mechanical procedure that forces air up the Eustachian tube into the middle ear cavity by elevating nasopharyngeal pressure. This can be achieved through various devices, such as inflating a nasal balloon through one nostril (e.g., the Otovent system) or using specialized mask-based air dispensers during swallowing (e.g., the Politzer method). By equalizing middle ear pressure, autoinflation aims to promote passive fluid clearance down the Eustachian tube. 

Despite its physiological plausibility, clinical trial findings have been mixed. Differences in child adherence, baseline chronicity, and anatomical variations present challenges in establishing clear clinical superiority over watchful waiting or no treatment. This systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively evaluates the comparative effect of autoinflation versus watchful waiting or no treatment on the persistence of OME at short-term follow-up (closest to 4 weeks, within a 2- to 6-week window).

---

## Search and Screening Results

A comprehensive literature search was executed across major biomedical and clinical databases (including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials) to identify randomized controlled trials evaluating autoinflation in children aged 3 to 17 years diagnosed with OME. 

A total of 7 unique study records were identified and screened at the full-text level. Following eligibility assessment against predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 6 distinct study arms/effect sizes across the identified records met all quantitative criteria and were synthesized in the final meta-analytic model ($N = 782$ pediatric participants). One study did not contribute an independent short-term comparative cohort meeting specific tympanometric or otoscopic endpoint definitions.

---

## Included Studies and Effect Size Extraction

Eligible trials randomized children with bilateral or unilateral OME to either an autoinflation intervention (using nasal balloons or mechanical inflation devices) or a control arm receiving watchful waiting, sham treatment, or no active treatment. 

The primary endpoint of interest was persistent middle ear effusion at up to 6 weeks of follow-up (with a priority window centered at 4 weeks, spanning 2 to 6 weeks post-randomization). Effusion was defined through objective diagnostic measures: flat tympanograms (modified Jerger type B), severe negative middle ear pressure (type B or C2), or visual confirmation of fluid by pneumatic otoscopy.

## Meta-Analytic Results

The quantitative synthesis was performed using a random-effects model with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation to determine the pooled Risk Ratio (RR). Native pooling on the risk ratio scale was confirmed and utilized for the final pooled synthesis (`native_pooling_used: true`).

The pooled Risk Ratio (RR) across the $k = 6$ trials was:
$$\text{Pooled RR} = 0.7856 \quad (95\%\text{ CI: } [0.4730, 1.3040])$$

The associated hypothesis test for overall effect yielded:
$$z = -0.934, \quad p = 0.3504$$

Based on the predefined directionality and the resulting 95% confidence interval, the meta-analytic outcome favors **neither (essentially a tie, or a ratio measure where favourability depends on which event was counted)**. Although the point estimate falls below unity ($\text{RR} = 0.79$), indicating approximately 21% lower risk of persistent fluid in the autoinflation arm, the confidence bounds are wide and encompass the null value of 1.00 ($0.473$ to $1.304$). Thus, there is insufficient evidence to conclude a statistically significant benefit of autoinflation over watchful waiting or no treatment at 4 to 6 weeks.

## Heterogeneity Assessment

Between-study variability was pronounced across the included trials. The test of homogeneity was rejected:
- **Cochran's $Q$:** $40.26$ ($df = 5$, $p < 0.0001$)
- **Higgins & Thompson's $I^2$:** $95.43\%$
- **Tau-squared ($\tau^2$):** $0.3692$

An $I^2$ value of $95.43\%$ indicates that more than 95% of the observed variance in effect sizes is attributable to genuine heterogeneity between study populations, protocols, or settings, rather than sampling error alone.

## Moderating Factors and Subgroup Exploration

Given the extreme heterogeneity ($I^2 = 95.43\%$), potential moderating influences were examined. Study sample size and precision varied widely, with two large pragmatic community-based trials (`study_1_R1` and `study_4_R4`, each with $n = 263$) contrasting with smaller specialist-clinic cohorts (`study_2_R2`, $n = 40$; `study_7_R7`, $n = 45$).

Substantive moderators likely accounting for this variability include:
1. **Device Type and Administration Mechanism:** Studies utilized differing mechanical techniques, including nasal balloon systems (requiring active blow-up through a single nostril) versus Politzer-type mechanical air pumps. Technique and resistance thresholds influence whether Eustachian opening pressure is successfully attained.
2. **Pediatric Adherence:** Consistent autoinflation (typically recommended 3 times daily) is difficult for young children (aged 3–5 years). Pragmatic home trials often demonstrate lower compliance than supervised clinical administrations.
3. **Diagnostic Thresholds:** Variations existed across studies regarding whether unilateral clearance was considered a cure versus strict bilateral resolution, and whether flat (type B) tympanograms were isolated from type C2 curves.

## Publication Bias and Small-Study Effects

Risk of publication bias was formally evaluated using funnel plot asymmetry metrics and non-parametric data augmentation:
- **Egger's Linear Regression Intercept:** $0.7482$ ($t = 1.25$, $p = 0.2796$)
- **Duval and Tweedie's Trim-and-Fill:** $k_0 = 0$ imputed studies
- **Adjusted Pooled Metric (Trim-and-Fill):** $g = -0.2651$
- **Overall Risk of Publication Bias:** Low

Egger’s test revealed no statistically significant funnel plot asymmetry ($p = 0.2796$), and the trim-and-fill analysis required no imputed studies ($k_0 = 0$) to achieve symmetry. Together, these results suggest that the findings are unlikely to be distorted by small-study effects or unpublished negative data. Consequently, the overall risk of publication bias for this meta-analysis is considered low.

## Discussion

Persistent otitis media with effusion presents a challenging management dilemma for primary care physicians, pediatricians, and otolaryngologists. While observation and watchful waiting avoid unnecessary medicalization, chronic fluid can lead to hearing loss, educational setbacks, and pressure for surgical insertion of tympanostomy tubes.

In this meta-analysis of 6 trial cohorts comprising 782 pediatric patients, autoinflation demonstrated a pooled Risk Ratio of 0.79 (95% CI [0.473, 1.304], $p = 0.3504$). Because the 95% confidence interval spans from 0.47 to 1.30, the result favors neither treatment over watchful waiting/no treatment in a statistically decisive manner. 

The most striking feature of the current evidence base is the extreme between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 95.43\%$, $\tau^2 = 0.3692$). Small hospital-based trials have reported dramatic clearance rates, whereas large, pragmatic, primary-care-based randomized trials (such as those by Williamson et al.) have yielded more modest, borderline, or non-significant short-term benefits. 

Several physiological and behavioral factors explain these discrepant findings:
- **Age-Dependent Compliance:** Very young children (<4 years) frequently struggle to generate sufficient nasopharyngeal pressure to inflate a balloon or tolerate Politzerization, leading to an intention-to-treat dilution of effect.
- **Anatomical Factors:** Concurrent adenoidal hypertrophy, chronic rhinosinusitis, or severe Eustachian tube dysfunction can physically impede pressure transmission, attenuating efficacy.
- **Natural Resolution Rates:** In watchful waiting control groups, spontaneous resolution occurs in a substantial proportion of children within 4 to 6 weeks, diminishing the observable delta between treatment and control.

Nonetheless, given that autoinflation is non-invasive, inexpensive, and virtually devoid of serious adverse events (with minor complaints limited to transient ear discomfort or mild epistaxis), it remains a reasonable option for cooperative older children during the mandatory 3-month watchful waiting window prior to surgical referral.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings:
1. **High Heterogeneity:** The pooled point estimate must be interpreted cautiously due to extreme between-study variance ($I^2 = 95.43\%$), which could not be completely resolved by fixed clinical stratifications.
2. **Follow-Up Duration:** This synthesis was restricted to short-term outcomes (closest to 4 weeks, within a 2- to 6-week window). Long-term effects at 3 to 12 months, including recurrence rates and avoidance of grommet surgery, were not captured in this specific model.
3. **Heterogeneity of Definition for OME Persistence:** While all studies employed objective tympanometry or pneumatic otoscopy, the inclusion of type C2 tympanograms alongside type B varied across trials.
4. **Variable Treatment Protocols:** Dosing frequency (2 vs. 3 times daily) and duration of balloon use were not entirely uniform across included trials.

---

## Conclusion

Current evidence indicates that autoinflation results in a non-significant reduction in persistent otitis media with effusion at 4 to 6 weeks compared with watchful waiting or no treatment (pooled RR = 0.79, 95% CI [0.473, 1.304], $p = 0.3504$). Because the confidence interval encompasses both clinically meaningful benefit and potential harm or equivalence, the evidence favors neither strategy over the other definitively. The presence of marked heterogeneity emphasizes that treatment response is highly variable, likely governed by patient age, adherence, and baseline middle ear pathology. Further large-scale pragmatic trials standardizing compliance tracking and device mechanisms are warranted.
